# Acacia {#cathen.acacia}

**Acacia** (in Hebrew shíṭṭah, plural shíṭṭîm; Theod. πύξος; Vulgate, *spina*, thorn). The Hebrew *shíṭṭah* is probably a contraction of *Shintṭāh*, and thus identical with the Egyptian *shent*; the Coptic *shonte*, thorn; the Arabic *sunṭ*. Hence the Greek name ἄκανθα, thorn, the Latin, *acanthus* for the Egyptian *acacia*. Acacia wood is designated, ξύλον ἄσεππον "incorruptible wood", in the Septuagint, and *lignum setim*, "setim-wood" in the Vulgate. The Biblical Acacia belongs to the *genus Mimosa*, and is no doubt identical with the *Acacia seyal* (Del.) or the *Acacia tortilis* (Hayne); both are called *seyyal*, or torrent trees, *sayl* meaning torrent. They grow in the desert *wadis*, or torrent valleys, of Sinai. The wood is light, hard, and durable, and grows almost as black as ebony with age. The ark of the covenant, the table of the loaves of proposition, the altar of holocausts, the altar of incense, the wooden parts of the tabernacle, were made of setim-wood (Ex. xxv, 5). (See [[Plants in the Bible|Plants of the Bible]].)

Vigouroux, in *Dict. de la Bible* (Paris, 1895); Chapman in Hastings, *Dictionary of the Bible,* art. *Shittah Tree* (New York, 1902).

A.J. Maas.
