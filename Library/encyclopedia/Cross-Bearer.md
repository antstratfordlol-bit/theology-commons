# Cross-Bearer {#cathen.cross-bearer}

**Cross-Bearer**, The [[Cleric|cleric]] or [[Minister|minister]] who carries the processional [[Cross and Crucifix|cross]], that is, a crucifix provided with a long staff or handle. An archbishop's cross is borne with the figure of the crucifix towards the prelate, but in all other cases the figure should be turned forward. The cross-bearer should, whenever possible, be a cleric ([[Archdiocese of Milan|Council of Milan]], seventeenth century), but in lay [[Processions|processions]] the most worthy of the [[Laity|laity]] should be selected for the office. In the more solemn processions such as those of the [[The Blessed Eucharist as a Sacrament|Blessed Sacrament]], [[Cross-Bearer/Palm Sunday|Palm Sunday]], and Candlemas Day, the cross should be borne by a [[Subdeacon|subdeacon]] vested in [[Amice|amice]], [[Alb|alb]], and [[Tunic|tunic]]; on less solemn occasions by a clerk in [[Surplice|surplice]]. The staff is held with both hands so that the figure is well above the head. The cross-bearer and the two [[Acolyte|acolytes]] by whom he is accompanied on the more solemn occasions should walk at the head of the procession, except when the [[Incense|thurifer]] is there, and should not make any reverences whilst engaged in this function.

*Cæremoniale Episcoporum, passim*; DE HERDT, *Praxis Liturgiæ Sucræ* (Louvain, 1904), III, 318; LE VAVASSEUR, *Cérémonial Romain* (Paris, 1876), I, 680.

Patrick Morrisroe.
