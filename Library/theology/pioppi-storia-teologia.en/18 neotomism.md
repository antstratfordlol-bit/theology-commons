## Neotomism {#pioppi-storia-teologia.18}
The neotomist movement continues to thrive and grow stronger because it is seen as a wall of counter-opposition to modernism. It runs out towards the 1950s. It also produced an exceptional flowering of historical studies on scholastic theology.

The main exponents in the century. XX are the Dominicans Antonin-Gilbert SERTILLANGES (1863-1948) and Reginald GARRIGOU-LAGRANGE (1877-1964, very important also for spiritual theology), the laity Jacques MARITAIN (1882-1973) and Étienne GILSON (1884-1978), all French. Maritain is among other things the one who wrote of Paul VI's Creed of the People of God (1968); he also published a book denouncing the post-conciliar drifts that have already begun and are on the horizon (La paysan de la Garonne, 1966). Gilson is very important for historical studies and founded in Toronto in PIMS (Pontifical Institute of Me-dieval Studies).

In addition, the Belgians Desiré-Joseph MERCIER (1851-1926), cardinal and bishop of Malines, founder of the Higher Institute of Philosophy in Louvain; and the Jesuit Joseph MARÉCHAL (1878-1944), who attempted a development of the doctrine on Thomist knowledge in a Kantian key. Finally, the Spanish Dominican Juan GONZÁLEZ ARINTERO (1860-1928).

Linked to Neotomist thought is card. Charles Journet (1891-1975), a great friend of Paul VI and Maritain. Born in Geneva, he was a professor in Friborg. He is an important ecclesiologist (L ’Église du Verbe in-carné, unfinished; La primauté de Pierre). He collaborated conspicuously in the writing of Lumen gen-tium; founder of the magazine "Nova et vetera". In 1965 he was created a cardinal by Pope Montini.
