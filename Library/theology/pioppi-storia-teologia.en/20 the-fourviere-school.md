## The Fourvière School {#pioppi-storia-teologia.20}
In Fourvière, a hill in Lyon, there was the studentate of the Society of Jesus. Here - and in the Insti-tuts Catholiques de Lyon - the Jesuit Henri DE LUBAC (1896-1991) taught. He re-proposes the centrality of the history of theology studies (he wrote a conspicuous history of medieval exegesis). His first great work was Catholicisme (1938). In it, among other things. The Jesuit goes beyond the traditional concept of the Church's catholicity as a socio-geographical concept, to give it a Christological foundation. In his other great work Supernaturel (1946), de Lubac criticizes the concept of the state of

pure nature, which had influenced all theology starting from the modern age, placing a division between creation and redemption. De Lubac advocates a return to the unity of God's saving plan, above all by starting over from the fathers of the Church: for this reason he founded the "Sources Chré-tiennes" series in 1942. He was perished at the Second Vatican Council. John Paul II made him cardinal in 1983.

Jean DANIÉLOU (1905-1974) also belonged to the Society of Jesus; together with de Lubac he founded the patristic series “Sources Chrétiennes”; he taught from 1943 at the Catholic Institutes of Paris; he was perished at the Second Vatican Council; in 1969 he was appointed cardinal.

Historians of medieval theology

Neo-scholasticism favors historical studies on medieval theology: among these scholars - in addition to Chenu, de Lubac and Gilson - we remember the Germans Martin GRABMANN (1875-1949), with his Die Geschichte der scholastischen Methode; and Arthur M. LANDGRAF (1859-1958), with his studies on Frühscholastik; the Belgians Joseph DE GHELLINCK (1872-1950), Jesuit and scholar of the century. XII; and Fer-dinand VAN STEENBERGHEN (1904-1995), with his research on the century. XIII; the French Cistercian Jean LECLERCQ (1911-1993), scholar of Saint Bernard.

Belgian theologians

Gustave THILS (1909-2000), with his famous work Théologie des réalités terrestres (1947-1949).

Gérard PHILIPS (1899-1972), ecclesiologist, made an important contribution to the drafting of Lumen gentium.
