## The School of Le Saulchoir {#pioppi-storia-teologia.19}
The School of Le Saulchoir was born in Belgium in 1904, founded by French Dominicans expelled from their country at the time of the anti-Congregation laws. They settled in an ancient Cistercian monastery, precisely called Le Saulchoir. In 1939 the school returned to France, near Paris.

Marie-Dominique CHENU (1895-1990): Dominican since 1913; he was director of Le Saulchoir until 1942, when he had to resign because one of his works had been put on the Index; he taught at the Sorbonne from 1946 to 1953, and later returned to Le Saulchoir. He proclaims himself a Thomist, but he understands this more as an attitude and a spirit than as adherence to a method or doctrine. So in the contemporary era, Chenu argues, being a Thomist means facing the challenges and problems of the time as Aquinas would have done.

Research on the nature of theology led him to two important historical works, one on the theology of the century. XII (La théologie au XIIe siècle, 1957), the other on that of the century XIII (La théologie comme science au XIIIe siècle, 1927). With these studies, the French Dominican manages to describe the evolution of Thomasian thought and also its dependencies on sources, with a certain scandal in some circles of his order.

Chenu strongly underlines the link between theology and history, between the Gospel and time; this interest led him to move towards socio-cultural and therefore political studies; according to him the theologian should be very attentive to the "signs of the times". This attitude led him in some cases to an acceptance of elements coming from the Hegelian and Marxist philosophies. It is also important for the attempt to structure a theology of work.

In the early 1930s he founded an Institute of Medieval Studies in Ottawa.

However, the most important exponent of the School of Le Saulchoir is Yves CONGAR (1904-1995): he too was a Dominican, he taught at Le Saulchoir and Strasbourg; he was a conciliar expert in Vatican II, and in this task he played a very important role, especially in the drafting of Lumen gentium and Presbyterorum ordinis. His most important areas of work are ecclesiology and ecumenism. His theological method has a conspicuous historical-positive structure. In 1937 it gives start to the “Unam Sanctam” col-lana with his famous book Chrétiens désunis. He is the most important ecclesiologist of the century, even if he did not leave an orderly and coherent treatise on the Church, but a set of studies on sectoral themes. For example, in the context of the concept of catholicity, its definition of it as "property of the Church thanks to which the reality of the multiple, in it, harmonizes with the reality of unity" is interesting. In 1994 he was created a cardinal by John Paul II.
