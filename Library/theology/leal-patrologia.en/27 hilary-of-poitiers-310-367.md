### HILARY OF POITIERS (310-367) {#leal-patrologia.introduction-to-patristi.hilary-of-poitiers-310-3}
Hilary of Poitiers, coming from a well-known pagan family, was elected bishop of Poitiers around the year 350. He did not participate in the Synod of Arles (353) nor in that of Milan (355) which, by the will of Emperor Constantius, attempted to resolve the Arian question by deposing Athanasius, and for this reason, he was exiled to Asia Minor. A good connoisseur of the Scriptures, his thought is profound; he introduced numerous concepts of Eastern theology into the West. Among his dogmatic works, the treatise On the Trinity stands out, completed before 360, during his exile in Asia. This work is completed by the On Synods and the Faith of the Orientals, where he collects historical arguments. His exegetical works are: a Commentary on Saint Matthew, Treatises on the Psalms, Treatise on the Mysteries.

Hilary usually superimposes a second spiritual level onto the literal level. This procedure is extended to the entire Gospel, both to the words and to the events of Jesus' life. The connection between the two levels of reading is dictated by similarity, so that one recalls the other without forcing it. The relationship between the two levels is that of a material reality to a spiritual one, of an inferior to a superior one, in accordance with his interests in spiritual progress. But more frequently, this is a relationship between present and future: every episode of the Gospel recalls a future reality. The procedures are varied: an episode is interpreted based on the symbolism applied to another; an event in the life of Jesus is interpreted in connection with the words of Christ. In this allegorical interpretation, the literal sense is not denied. In general, Hilary interprets the Old Testament in a Christological sense.

#### Tract on the Mysteries
TREATISE ON THE MYSTERIES 1,1-5

The prefigurations of what found its full completion in the Lord

I 1. There are many ways (of interpreting Scripture) [...] while we fully understand these figures in the events. For these [unknown exegetes to us], however, it is sufficient to have applied to the interpretation of Scripture a typology that is vain and without any foundation, when instead the correlation must have its full completion in subsequent events and a solid starting point in present events. The whole work, contained in the sacred books, announces through words, reveals through deeds, establishes through typical figures, the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, who, sent by the Father, became man by being born of a Virgin through the work of the Holy Spirit. It is He, in fact, who throughout the duration of this created world, through true and clear prefigurations, generates, purifies, sanctifies, chooses, separates or redeems the Church in the Patriarchs: in the sleep of Adam, in the flood of Noah, in the blessing of Melchizedek, in the justification of Abraham, in the birth of Isaac, in the servitude of Jacob. In short, throughout the unfolding of time, the whole of the prophecies, put into operation by God's hidden design, has been given to us with benevolence for the knowledge of his future incarnation. And since our purpose has been to show, in this small treatise, that in every personage, in every epoch, in every event, each prophecy reflects as in a mirror the image of his coming, his preaching, his passion, his resurrection and our being gathered together in the Church, I will not limit myself to hastily recalling some passages, but I will treat everything, each thing in its time, starting with Adam, with whom our knowledge of the human race begins, so that it may be recognized that we find announced from the origin of the world, in a great number of prefigurations, what found its full completion in the Lord.

*Adam prefigures the birth of Christ*

2. Adam, with his very name, prefigures the birth of the Lord. In fact, the Hebrew term Adam, which is translated in Greek as ghe pyrra, means in Latin burnt earth, and Scripture usually indicates the flesh of the human body with the term earth. This flesh, born of the Virgin through the work of the Holy Spirit in the person of the Lord, transfigured into a new form and foreign to his nature, has been made capable of participating in the glory of the spirit (cf. Phil. 3:21), according to the words of the Apostle: "The second man comes from heaven, and is the heavenly Adam" (1 Cor. 15:47), since the earthly Adam "is a figure of the one who was to come" (Rom. 5:14). With all certainty, therefore, and with such authoritative confirmation, we also assume the name of Adam not without some prefiguration of the one who was to come.

3. The creation of Eve from the side and from a bone of the sleeping Adam follows. Upon his awakening, here is the prophecy we find: "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called woman, because she was taken out of man." (Gen. 2:23)

Here, no difficulty for me. The Apostle, in fact, after recalling this prophecy, says: "This mystery is great, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church." (Eph. 5:32). But we read that only one bone was taken from Adam: how then is it said "flesh of my flesh"?

This fact can certainly be explained in accordance with the reality of present events: the bone, in fact, which Almighty God took from Adam's side and covered with flesh to form the woman's body, this bone, taken from the flesh and covered again with flesh, became a body: as the bone was taken from the bone, so the flesh was taken from the flesh.

But the Lord in the Gospel, when the Jews tested him on the right to divorce, shows that this prophecy was pronounced for himself rather than for Adam, saying: "Have you not read that he who created them from the beginning made them male and female, and said, 'Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh'?" (Mt. 19:4)

*The prophecy follows the reality*

Now, this comes after the words "flesh of my flesh." The prophecy therefore follows the reality accomplished in Adam. When in fact the Lord, who created man and woman, spoke of "bone of his bones" and "flesh of his flesh," he announced himself, through Adam, what had been fully accomplished in Adam himself: he did not take away from the facts their credibility, but showed that what was accomplished in another was a prefiguration, of which he himself was the source. For indeed "the Word became flesh" (John 1:14) and the Church, which was generated from his side from water and made alive by blood, is a member of Christ (cf. John 19:34), since, on the other hand, the flesh in which the Word was born, subsisting before all ages as the Son of God, remains sacramentally among us. He has clearly taught us that Adam and Eve were figures of himself and the Church, since he makes us know that this Church has been sanctified after the sleep of his death through communion with his flesh.

He also tells us through the Apostle: 'For Adam was not deceived, but the woman was deceived and became a transgressor. Yet she will be saved through childbearing—if they continue in faith' 1 (1 Tim. 2:14). The Church, therefore, is composed of tax collectors, sinners, and Gentiles; since only the second and heavenly Adam does not sin, she, a sinner, will be saved by bearing children who persevere in faith. However, one must not think that the woman will not be redeemed from her sin by the Lord and that she will be baptized in vain, because she will have to be saved through the merit of having borne children: she will not be safe even by bearing children, since she will not be saved if those who have been born do not persevere in faith, and I do not know if it is right to be considered guilty or innocent for the merit or sin of others.

*The spiritual sense*

But with all certainty, comparing spiritual realities with spiritual realities (1 Cor. 2:13), we will fully understand why the Apostle affirms that from now on he refers to Christ and the Church the story of the great mystery accomplished in Adam and Eve. In reality, we must believe that he said these things in a spiritual sense, but this is not a valid reason not to apply his words also to present teaching and the formation of those he admonished. In fact, he who in the first letter to the Corinthians announced the multiplicity of the resources of divine mercy in the sanctification of unbelievers through marriage with believers (cf. 1 Cor. 7:12-15), also taught us that divine generosity would grant the same sanctification through the generation of believing children, so that, just as the union of one believer was useful to the unbelieving spouse through marriage, so the generation of believing children would be helpful to unbelieving parents.

It must be considered that in the sleep of Adam and in the creation of Eve, there is also the revelation in figure of the hidden mystery concerning Christ and the Church: this revelation, in fact, offers valid reasons to believe in the resurrection of bodies. In the creation of the woman, in fact, no more is taken from the mud, the earth is no longer shaped to form a body, the breath of God no longer transforms inanimate matter into a living soul, but flesh grows on bone, flesh is given the perfection of the body, and to the perfection of the body is added the strength of the spirit. God announced this order to the resurrection through the prophet Ezekiel (Ez. 37:4-11), teaching about future realities, what his power can do. Everything, in fact, contributes to it: there is the flesh, the spirit supervenes, none of his works are lost for God, who, to animate the human body, which is his work, found these things present, which did not exist.

Now, according to the Apostle, this is 'the mystery hidden for ages in God' (Col. 1:26; Eph. 3:9), that is, that the Gentiles are called to share the same inheritance, to form the same body, and to be partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus' (Eph. 3:6), 'who has the power,' according to the same Apostle, 'to conform our lowly body to his glorious body' (Phil. 3:21).

*The sleep of Adam prefigures the death of Christ*

Therefore, after the sleep of his passion, the heavenly Adam, at the resurrection of the Church, recognizes in her his bone, his flesh, no longer created from mud and made alive by breath, but which grows on bone and, made body from body, reaches its perfection under the breath of the Spirit. For those who are in Christ will rise like Christ (cf. 1 Thess. 4:16), in whom the resurrection of all flesh has already been accomplished, since he himself was born in our flesh by the power of God, in whom his Father generated him before the ages. And since Jew and Greek, Barbarian and Scythian, slave and free, man and woman, all are one in Christ (Gal. 3:28; Col. 3:11), since flesh is born from flesh, the Church is the body of Christ, and the mystery that is in Adam and Eve is a prophecy concerning Christ and the Church, all that has been prepared by Christ for the Church for the consummation of time, has already been accomplished in Adam and Eve at the beginning of the world.

*The Samaritan woman and the well of Jacob*

There are many ways of interpreting Scripture... while we have the intelligence of these figures in fact. But it is enough for these people to have applied to the interpretation of Scripture a semblance of a futile typology when the comparison must receive its full completion of later events and a solid starting point of the events now being told. All the work contained in the Holy Books announces with words, reveals with facts, establishes with examples the coming of Our Lord Jesus Christ, who, sent by his Father, became man by being born of a virgin through the operation of the Holy Spirit. It is He, in fact, who, throughout the present century, by true and manifest prefigures, generates, washes, sanctifies, chooses, separates or redeems the Church in the Patriarchs: by the sleep of Adam, by the flood of Noah, by the blessing of Melchizedek, by the justification of Abraham, by the birth of Isaac, by the bondage of Jacob. During all the course of time, in a word, all the prophecies, implementation of God's secret plan, were given to us out of benevolence for the knowledge of his Incarnation to come. And since our intention was to show, in this little treatise, that in each person, each epoch, each fact, the whole of the prophecies, projects as in a mirror the image of his coming, his preaching, his Passion, his Resurrection and our society. In the Church, I will not hastily recall a few passages, but I will deal with everything, everything in its time, beginning with Adam, the starting point of our knowledge of the human race, so that we may recognize that we find announced from the origin of the world in a great number of prefigures what has received in the Lord its total fulfillment.

Adam, by his very name, prefigures the birth of the Lord; for the Hebrew Adam, which is translated into Greek as "Gè pyrra", means in Latin "burning land" and it is a custom of Scripture to give the name of land to the flesh of the human body. This flesh, born of the Virgin by the operation of the Holy Spirit in the person of the Lord, changed into a new form and foreign to his nature, was made capable of sharing the glory of the spirit according to the words of the Apostle: "The second man is of heaven and is the heavenly Adam", because the earthly Adam "is the type of Him who is to come. " In complete safety, therefore, under such a high sanction, we draw from the very name of Adam the subject of some reflection on the One who is to come.

Then comes the creation of Eve from the flank and a sleeping Adam bone. When she awakens, here is the prophecy we find:

"This is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh. She shall be called wife, because she was taken from her husband, and they shall be two in one flesh. "

Here, no difficulty for my thesis; the Apostle says indeed, after recalling this prophecy :

"It is a great mystery, I mean in regard to Christ and the Church.

But we read that only one bone was taken away from Adam; how then can we explain this word "flesh of my flesh"? We can explain this fact in accordance with the reality of the present events: the bone, in fact, that God, who can do anything, after having taken it from the side of Adam, clothed it with flesh to make it the body of the woman, this bone drawn from the flesh and clothed again with flesh became a body; thus, as we see it, just as bone was drawn from bone, flesh was drawn from flesh. But the Lord in the Gospel, when the Jews tempted Him on the right of repudiation, shows by these words that this prophecy was spoken by Him rather than by Adam: "Have you not read that He who made man in the beginning made them male and female, and said, 'For this cause a man shall leave his father and mother, and they shall be two in one flesh. "For this comes after this saying, "flesh of my flesh. The prophecy is therefore a continuation of the reality fulfilled in Adam. When the Lord, who made man and woman, spoke of "bones of his bones" and "flesh of his flesh," He Himself announced through Adam what had been fulfilled in Adam Himself. He did not take away from the facts their credibility, and He showed that what was accomplished in another was a prefiguration of which He was the source. Since the Word was indeed made flesh and the Church is a member of Christ, she who was begotten by water and vivified by blood, since the flesh in which the Word was born subsists before all the ages, as the Son of God, subsists among us sacramentally, He taught us clearly that Adam and Eve were the type of His person and His Church, for He makes known to us by the communion of His Flesh that this Church was sanctified after the sleep of His death. He also tells us through the Apostle "that Adam did not sin, but that the woman by her sin belonged to transgression. But she will be saved because of the procreation of her sons, provided they remain in the Faith. "The Church is therefore composed of publicans, sinners, and Gentiles; whereas only her second and heavenly Adam did not sin, she, a sinner, will be saved by procreating sons who abide in the Faith. Moreover, it is not to be understood that a woman is not redeemed from her sin by the Lord, and that she will be baptized in vain, if it is rather by the merit of childbirth that she is to be set free; so it is not even by the generation of her sons that she will be safe, since she will not be saved if those whom she has begotten do not keep the Faith. And I do not know that it is right to be guilty or innocent by the sin or merit of another.

But, safely comparing spiritual things with spiritual things, we will understand fully that the Apostle tells us that he is now bringing back to Christ and to the Church the story of the great mystery fulfilled in Adam and Eve. These things, in fact, we must see that He said them in a spiritual sense, but this is no reason not to apply His words also to the present teaching and to the formation of those whom He warned. In fact, he who, in the First Epistle to the Corinthians, announced the multiplicity of the resources of divine mercy in the sanctification of the unbelievers through marriage to the faithful, taught us here too that divine generosity would grant sanctification of the same order through the procreation of sons, provided that they are believers, so that, just as the society of one faithful was useful to the unfaithful spouse through marriage, so the procreation of faithful children was a help to unfaithful parents.

We must also consider in Adam's sleep and Eve's creation the figurative revelation of the hidden mystery which had Christ and the Church as its object; this revelation in fact offers us motives to believe in the resurrection of the bodies at the same time as its figure. In fact, in the creation of woman, it is no longer silt that is taken, the earth is no longer shaped to take the form of a body, the breath of God no longer transforms inanimate matter into a living soul; but flesh grows on bone, the perfection of the body is given to the flesh, and the power of the spirit is added to the perfection of the body. God announced this ordinance of resurrection through Ezekiel, teaching about future realities what His power can do. Everything indeed contributes to it: the flesh is there, the spirit flies, none of its works are lost to God who, for the animation of the human body which is his work, has found present those things which were not. Now, according to the Apostles, it is a "design hidden in God since the beginning of time" that "the Gentiles be co-heirs and members of the same body and partakers of his promise in Christ", "who has the power, according to the same Apostle, to reform the body of our humility in the likeness of the body of his glory". Thus, after the sleep of his Passion, the heavenly Adam, on awakening from his Resurrection, recognizes in the Church his bone, his flesh no longer created from silt and coming to life under the breath, but growing on bone and, as one body, reaching perfection under the flight of the spirit. For those who are in Christ will rise again according to Christ, in whom the resurrection of all flesh is now consummated, because He Himself is born in our flesh with the power of God in whom His Father begot Him before the ages. And since the Jew and the Greek, the barbarian and the Scythian, the slave and the free man, the man and the woman, are all one thing in Christ, since the flesh is recognized as coming from the flesh, since the Church is the Body of Christ, and since the Mystery that is in Adam and Eve is a prophecy concerning Christ and the Church, all that has been prepared by Christ for the Church for the consummation of the times has already been fulfilled in Adam and Eve at the beginning of the present age.
