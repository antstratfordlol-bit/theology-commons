### JEROME (347-420) {#leal-patrologia.introduction-to-patristi.jerome-347-420}
Born in 347 in Stridone, in the bosom of a Christian family, he studied in Rome with the grammarian Aelius Donatus. At the age of twenty he moved to Treveri to follow his political career. It was there that he discovered his monastic vocation and the writings of the Fathers, especially the biblical commentaries. He retired to the desert and read the Scriptures, perfecting his knowledge of Greek and Hebrew. In Constantinople he met Gregory of Nazianzus and undertook the Latin translation of Origen. He achieved great prestige as a biblical scholar, participated in the Council of Rome in 382 and remained in Rome, where he was commissioned by Pope Damasus of the Latin translation of the Bible. When the pope died, he retired to Bethlehem where he continued his biblical work. He wrote many biblical commentaries, homilies, letters, historical works, polemical treatises [...]

His guide in exegetical work was Origen. Like him and many others, he sticks to the literal interpretation when he has to examine the letters of Paul, without excluding the allegorical interpretation. In this Origenian phase, Jerome will proceed in an orderly manner: first he will give the headwords translated from Greek and Hebrew, then he will do the literal interpretation and after the allegorical interpretation. But he will usually employ the usual overlapping of two levels of reading, but he will blame excessive allegoricism. In reality, the Origenian controversy did not affect the exegetical *ratio* of Jerome.

#### Commentary on Isaiah I, 1
Having just completed, after a long time, the twenty books of expositions on the twelve prophets and the commentaries on Daniel, you, Eustochium, virgin of Christ, urge me to move on to Isaiah and to fulfill for you what I promised to your holy mother Paula while she was alive. Although I remember having made the same promise to your very learned brother Pammachius, yet loving you both in the same way, you are the one to take precedence, because at your command, Lord, I rush to my writings, leaving behind the concern for my sins. I write to you, God, saying: “Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find” (Mt. 7:7), because I do not know how to judge myself.

You say: “You are mistaken, not knowing the Scripture is not knowing the power of God” (Mt. 22:29). In fact, according to the Apostle Paul, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God (1 Cor. 1:24), and he who does not know Christ does not know the power of God and his wisdom, for ignoring the Scripture is ignoring Christ.

Therefore, I exhort you to gather your prayers, both day and night, to imitate the Law of God and his temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Cor. 1:8). Imitate the father of the family who from his treasure brings out new and old things (Mt. 13:52), and the bride in the Song of Songs says: “New and old, my beloved, I have kept for you” (Cant. 7:13), and as the commentator Isaiah says, for most, in fact, he is neither a prophet nor an evangelist nor an apostle (cf. Is. 2). If you wish, imitate the others who announce peace (Is. 52:7), and God says to an apostle: “Take a sickle in your hand, and gather this people” (cf. Mk. 4:29).

I do not think I can gather all the arguments of this work in a brief exposition, because in this book they are recounted all the mysteries of the Lord and in it his coming is announced as from a virgin, his miracles and prodigies, death and burial, resurrection from the dead and ascension to heaven. And what are the arguments of physics, of morality and of theology? In this volume is contained everything that is in the Sacred Scripture, everything that human language can express and mortal mind can receive. For the mysteries that contain the testimony of the same author, you write: “Search for the vision of these words as from a book” - that is, he who hears a word from the Lord and speaks, let him speak: “Read the Law” - and he who does not know says: “I have not read,” and answer to him: “You do not know the letter” (Is. 29:11). So, you give this book to a pagan population that does not know the Law, you answer: “I have not read,” because he who has not learned the letters of the Scripture; so you write to scribes and Pharisees, that if they want to know the letters of the Law, they answer: “We have not read,” because he who reads the book is silent. Why is he silent? Because he who has received the book for reading, he who despises the words of David, who says and warns: “Come, children, listen to me: I will teach you the fear of the Lord” (Ap. 3:7).

He does not come, like a vane woman immersed in idleness, he who professes to speak from the mountain without knowing what he says. Indeed, if he ignores those who say: “Do not understand the things that come from the mouth of God” (1 Tim. 1:7), he is forced to prove what Solomon says in the book of Proverbs: “A sapient caprice is born from his mouth, and a fool’s lips swallow him” (Prov. 16:23), and the prophets say that they were chosen. Therefore, if you do not know the Scriptures, do not speak; if you have learned, from the mouth of the Lord and his response, as Daniel said to the prince of Tyre: “Perhaps you know more than Daniel?” (Ez. 28:3); if you are wise, David says, as he said to his soul: “I will set my eyes on the Lord always, for he is at my right hand, I will not be shaken” (Sal. 15:8), as brute animals filled with rage and ignorant of what they say, they are forced to make ignorant questions.

In the passage of the Apostle we read: “The spirits of the prophets are subject to prophets” (1 Cor. 14:32), so that they have the power to keep silent or speak. If anyone is stubborn and considers the words of the Apostle: “God speaks to the apostles in two or three ways, and the others judge” (1 Cor. 14:29 ss.), how can they know, as the prophet does, what the Spirit says through the prophet?

Indeed, everyone knows that what has been said is full of allegory and discernment. It was not lost on him that the woman who grieved for her error, but God spoke to her in human language, as he says: “And he called to him, saying: ‘Abba, Father!’” (Gal. 4:6) and “God exalted him as a sign of his glory” (Sal. 84:9). Therefore, according to the literal truth, everything written in the spiritual sense is interpreted. Judaea and Jerusalem, Babylon and the Philistines, Moab and Damascus, Egypt and the desert, Idumea, Arabia, the valley of Sion and infinite Tyre and the vision of the four-footed can be interpreted in a way that considers only its spiritual meaning and in the way the Apostle Paul, as a sapient architect, so that everything is founded on Christ. They are silent who do not know Christ.

#### Commentary on the Gospel of St. Mark I, 1-12
The four-faced beast that we met in the Revelation of John (Rev. 4:6) and at the start of Ezekiel’s prophecy, that had the face of a man, a calf, a lion and an eagle, has also special significance for the section we are considering. In Matthew, this human has the face of a man; in Luke, it is an ox; in John, an eagle; in Mar, it is the lion calling out in the desert.

“The beginning of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God. As it is written by Isaiah the prophet. “The voice of one calling out in the desert, “Make ready the way of the Lord, straighten his paths.”” The one calling out in the desert, truly is the lion whose roar fills every other animal with terror and makes them huddle together in a paralyzed fear. At the same moment, consider that John the Baptist is referred to the voice but our Lord Jesus is the word, for the servant precedes the master.

“The beginning of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God,” and, so not the son of Joseph. The start of the Gospel is the conclusion of the Law. The Law finishes and the Gospel starts. “As it is written by Isaiah the prophet, “See, I send my messenger before you, who will prepare your way.””

“As it is written by Isaiah.” Now, as far as I remember going back through the work of the seventy translators and the Hebrew manuscripts, I have never been able to find in Isaiah, the words, “See I send my messenger before you.” However I do find them written near the end of the prophecy of Malachi, (Mal. 3:1) If then, this statement is written at the conclusion of the prophecy of Malachi, why does Mark the evangelist say, “As it is written by Isaiah the prophet?” The words of the evangelists are the workings of the Holy Spirit. This Mark that writes is not to be taken lightly. In fact, Peter the apostle said in his letter, “The Church chosen with you, greets you, and so as well does my son Mark. (1 Peter 5:13) O Apostle Peter, Mark is your son, not by the flesh, but through the Spirit, since he is so knowledgeable in spiritual affairs, yet is ignorant here, and credits to a prophet from Holy Writ what was written by another. “As Isaiah the prophet wrote, ‘See I send my messenger before you.’” This is the passage that the impious Porphyry, who has vomited out his venom in so many books wrote against us, attacking with his fourteenth book. “The evangelists,” he claims, “were such uneducated men, not just in worldly affairs, but also in Holy Writ, that they cited the witness of one prophet and assigned it to another.” That is what he throws at us. Now what will we answer him? I think this is the answer brought about by your prayers, “As it is written in Isaiah.” What is written by Isaiah the prophet? “The voice of one calling out in the desert, “Make ready the path of the Lord, make straight his way.”” This is written by Isaiah, but there is a plain explanation of this passage by another prophet, and the Evangelist is actually saying, “This is John the Baptist, of whom Malachi said, “See, I send my messenger before you, who will make ready your way.” The verse, “it is written,” refers just to the following passage, “The voice of one calling out in the desert, “Prepare the way of the Lord, make straight his paths.”” To prove that John the Baptist was the messenger who was sent, Mark did not elect to speak his own words, but to prove it from the word of a prophet.

“John has been in the desert, baptizing and preaching.” (Mark 1:4) John has been: however God was. That which has been, no longer is. That which was, before it was, was not, but He who was, was before and always was. He was existing without ever having a beginning. About John the Baptist, it is said, “He was.” When it says, “He was,” He was always without beginning. It is He who said, “He who is has sent me,” because a being never had a beginning. “John has been in the desert baptizing and preaching.” The voice that is soon to declare the Lord was in the desert. Its first and primary goal was to announce the coming of the Savior. “John was in the desert.” Blessed is that manner of life, to forsake men, to seek out angels, to leave cities and, in the desert, to find Christ.

John was in the wilderness baptizing and preaching, baptizing with his hand, teaching by his word. The baptism of John was before the baptism of the Savior. Even as John the Baptist was before the Lord Savior, so was the baptism of John the Baptist before the baptism of the Savior. The first was provided for repentance, the latter by grace. At that time, repentance is given, on that occasion, pardon, and on this, victory is given.

“All the region of Judea went to him.” Judea comes to John in a group of crowds. Jerusalem runs to him. The whole world flocks to Jesus the Lord and Savior. “God is famous in Judea, in Israel great is his name.” (Ps. 75:2) To John, hastens Judea and Jerusalem. However to the Savior, the entire world.

All the inhabitants continued to come, “and they were baptized by him in the river Jordan, confessing their sins.” They were baptized by John. John the Baptist provides them the protection of the Law. So the Jews were baptized only with regard to the Law. They were leaving to go to Jerusalem and were baptized by him in the Jordan, in the river flowing downstream. The Law is also downstream. Even though it baptizes it is still downhill. That is what Jordan means. It is the river flowing downhill. However our Lord and the mystery of the Trinity is uphill. Someone might object and say, “if the baptism of the Law is from below, then, is the Lord from below who received his baptism in the Jordan, for He kept the ordinances of the Law? He was circumcised by the Law and He was baptized by the Law.”

“John was clothed with camel’s hair, with a leather girdle about his waist, and he ate locusts and wild honey.” In the same manner that the apostles provided an example for the priesthood, so John the Baptist provided an example for monks.

So far as tradition goes in the writings of the Hebrews and in the memory of his day, the name of John appears with the names of the priests and of the chief priest. It is clear, then that he was a holy man and he was a priest. Moreover we read in Luke’s gospel that John had been born from a priestly case. “There was a priest,” Luke says, “whose name was Zachariah, and in the order of his course…” (Luke 1:5,8) Not then was his privilege well allotted save to the first of the priests, that is to the head priests. Now, why have I related all this? That we should know that this was a head priest who knew that Christ was coming, and he was not seeking Christ in the temple, but had left the crowds for the desert. It is only fitting that the eyes looking to see Christ should look on nothing but Christ. John was clothed in the hair of a camel, not of a sheep. Do not think that he had soft clothing. Our Lord testifies to this in the Gospel about his asceticism. “Look” He says, “those who are clothed with soft clothing are in the mansions of kings.” (Matt. 11:8)

Now with the help of your prayers, may we turn to the spiritual message of these word. “John was robed in camel’s hair, girded about with leather.” It is the same John who declares, “He must increase, but I must decrease. He who has the bride is the bridegroom. But the friend of the bridegroom exults greatly if he sees the bridegroom.” (John 3:30) And also “one greater than I is coming after me, the strap of whose sandals I am unworthy to stoop down and loose.” (Mark 1:7)

The meaning of the words, “He must increase, I must decrease,” is that the Gospel must increase, but I the Law, must decrease.

John, that is, the Law in John, was clothed, then, in the hair of a camel. He could not wear the clothing of the lamb of whom it is said, “See the lamb of God who takes away the sins of the world.” (John 1:29) And also, “He is led as a lamb to the slaughter.” (Is. 53:7)

In the Law, we cannot wear clothing from that lamb. In the Law, John had a leather girdle for the Jews thought that to sin in deed was the only sin. On the other hand, in Revelation (Rev. 1:13) our Lord Jesus, who was seen in the middle of the seven lamp-stands, and was girded with a gold sash. But it was not about His waist, but about His chest. The Law is girded about the waist, but Christ, which is, the Gospel and the strength of monks, is binding, not just in dissolute lust, but also the mind and heart. In the Gospel, it is necessary for one not even to think evil. However in the Law, the fornicator is condemned. “Truly, truly, I tell you, that anyone who looks with lust at a woman has already committed adultery with her in his heart.” (Matt. 5:27) This is the leather girdle wrapped about the waist. “I say to you, anyone who looks with lust at a woman has already committed adultery with her in his heart.” This is the golden girdle that is wrapped about the mind and heart.

Clothed with camel’s hair, “he ate locusts and wild honey.” The locust is a small creature halfway between a bird and a reptile. It does not elevate itself above the earth to be a bird, because if it rises ever so little, it is not flying but rather jumping. When it rises it falls back again, because its wings fail. So also with the Law. It seemed to leave the errors of idolatry, but it cannot fly up to heaven. Truly we never read of the kingdom of heaven in the Law. Would you like proof that the kingdom of heaven is announced just in the Gospel? “Repent,” it says, “because the kingdom of heaven is at hand.” (Matt. 3:2) The Old Law elevated men a small amount from the earth, but it could not bear them to heaven. “Wherever the body is, there will the eagles be gathered.” (Matt. 24:28).
