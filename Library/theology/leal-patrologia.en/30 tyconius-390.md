### TYCONIUS († 390) {#leal-patrologia.introduction-to-patristi.tyconius-390}
The activity of Ticonius takes place between 370 and 390. Although he was a Donatist, he was admired and partially followed by Augustine in *De doctrina christiana*. His *Liber regularum* is the oldest manual of biblical hermeneutics in the West, which in seven rules offers indications on the interpretation of the most difficult passages. Its exegesis is systematically allegorical and has the tendency to interpret spiritually those passages that other authors (such as Victorinus) literally interpret.

#### Seven Rules for the Scriptures: Second Rule
*The bipartite body of the Lord*

Even more important is the rule of the bipartite body of the Lord and it is our duty to examine and study the Sacred Scriptures all the more accurately. In fact, as has been stated previously, reason alone has the faculty to distinguish the relationships between the head and the body, so it also happens for what concerns the relationships between one part and another of the body, between the right and the left or between the left and the right.

To a single body, in fact, the Scripture says: “I will give you hidden treasures, so that you may know that I am the Lord” (Is. 45:3). Then it adds: “But you have not known me, I am God and there is no God besides me and you did not know me” (Is. 45:4s.). Even admitting that he speaks to a single body, if perhaps the expressions “I will give you hidden treasures, so that you may know that I am the Lord God for the love of my servant Jacob,” and: “But you have not known me” are referred to a single person? Perhaps Jacob did not receive what God promised? Can the expressions “But you have not known me” and: “You did not know me” be referred to a single person? “You did not know me” in fact refers to those who now know; “you have not known me” instead refers to those who, having been called to know and having visibly despised my body, he approaches the Lord with his heart. And the one who clings to the Lord is one spirit with him” (1 Cor. 6:17). And elsewhere he says: “The Lord is near” (Phil. 4:5).

Jerome says: “Conduct me to those who do not know, guide me toward unknown paths and transform my darkness into light. But I will not abandon my spirit in prayer. Reshape these promises and do not abandon me. But if they are fulfilled, what are you going to say?” (Is. 42:16s.). Which ones? “I will make the blind walk” are those fulfilled; “I will make those who sit in darkness walk” are those not fulfilled?

Again, Jerome says to Jacob: “Turn to me, you who are far away, and I will lead you to your land of origin.” And elsewhere he says: “Do not be afraid to return; I will turn my face to you.” Return to me and in the middle of the journey: “Gather your children from a distant land and gather your offspring from the ends of the earth.” All those whom I have created and formed and composed to my glory, and whom I have called and formed and composed to my glory, are my people and the works of my hands (Is. 43:5-8). God says these things to those he created for his glory.

Isaiah affirms: “Appoint your fathers and your princes as commissioners to rule over me; for your fathers have profaned my sanctuary, for your fathers have brought forth against me Jacob and Israel for insults. Now Jacob and Israel, make me your God, and I will make you my God” (Is. 43:27-44:1). God has called those who were called Jacob and all those who were called Israel who were not yet elect.

Jerome says: “I have formed you to be my servant, you Israel; do not forget me. Bring me your iniquities and your sins. Return to me and I will redeem you” (Is. 44:21s.). God has forgiven the sins of those to whom he says: “Turn to me,” and remembers sins that did not diminish the call of those to whom he says: “Do not forget me.” Perhaps they cannot be forgiven unless the one who had turned away returns to the Lord?

Jerome says: “For I know what you will do to me for my name and I will show you my glory and I will give you your rich treasures” (Is. 48:8s.). And he who is reproved by God shows his glory and his love for those who are reproved?

Jerome says: “Neither a prince, nor an angel, but I myself have saved them; for I have loved them and I have had compassion for them. He himself has raised them up, he has lifted them up and carried them all the days of old.” If he remembers and contradicts the Holy Spirit (Is. 63:9s.). But what color did he bring on the day of the Lord? […]. If he remembers and contradicts the Holy Spirit?

God manifestly puts a unique stability into the body of the Lord saying: “Jerusalem, like a city that is compact and closely joined together, from which tribes have gone up, the tribes of the Lord, your tribes will not be torn apart” (Ps. 122:30). Immediately after adding: “Strive to have your peace, because the fineness of your ship has been restored to you, so that you will no longer see the other shore abandoned to perdition” (Is. 23:23).

Jerome says that the body of the Lord appears as bipartite in the following: “I am black and beautiful” (Cant. 1:5). Not that he is black and has no blemishes but says, “My sister, my spouse, is a garden enclosed” (Cant. 5:1). Jerome says that the Lord himself has spoken with his tongue, that is, in one part he is in a certain way, because the cause of “what name of God is better imitated by the Gentiles” (Rom. 2:24). As for the rest, he says to the bride: “You are all beautiful, my love, there is no blemish in you” (Cant. 4:7). All these things are said for the reason of your name and black and beautiful: “Like the tents of Kedar, like the pavilions of Solomon” (Cant. 1:5). There are therefore two tents, one rule and the other rule: between all the tribes of Abraham; Kedar indeed is the long stay. But in the other passage the Church is lamented in the long permanence with Kedar, that is, with the service of Abraham and says: “Woe is me, because my sojourn has been prolonged, my dwelling has been among the tents of Kedar.” This Kedar was the one who delayed the people in the desert for a long time, when they did not want to believe in the war. (Sal. 120:5-7). No one can say that the tents of Kedar are those of the people of the Jews. One text speaks of the tents of Kedar and of the pavilions of Solomon, for which it affirms: “I am black and beautiful” (Cant. 1:5). The Church is not black in those who are not of faith.

In referring to such mysteries Jerome calls the Apocalypse of the same angel - that is, the Church herself - showing herself to all saints and custodians of precepts and a great number of crimes and penances. In the Gospel John speaks of a unique body of diverse merit so that he who is in the first part is urged to say, according: “Behold what merit the patron has who has striven to reach this merit” (Mt. 24:46). And he proposed the following saying: “Whoever is dissolved from the body of the Lord is dissolved in two parts” (Mt. 24:48,51). We must understand therefore that God divides or separates in two parts all bodies will place him among the hypocrites” (Lk. 12:46), indeed in the unique servant he means the whole body.

Therefore, in relation to this mystery, it is necessary to understand through the Scriptures when God says that if he is at the base of merits he is destined to perish all Israel with its execrable heritage. The Apostle in fact clarifies extensively, especially in the Letter to the Romans, how one part of what is said about the body should be referred: "What does Israel say? All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient people” (Rom. 10:21). To make it even more evident that he was speaking of a part the text affirms: "I ask: Did God reject his people? By no means! I myself am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, from the tribe of Benjamin. God did not reject his people whom he chose long ago” (Rom. 11:1s.). After explaining what is the true meaning of such expressions, in the same way he clarifies how a single body can be good and evil and says: “As regards the gospel, they are enemies for your sake; but as regards election, they are beloved for the sake of their forefathers” (Rom. 11:28). Can these same beloved ones who are also enemies refer to Caiaphas?

Thus the Lord of all the Scriptures testifies how a single body of the descendants of Abraham in all grows, becomes vigorous and perishes.
