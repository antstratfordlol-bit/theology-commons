### PREFACE {#leal-patrologia.introduction-to-patristi.preface}
The manuals of Patrology available to students in the early years of ecclesiastical faculties and institutes of ancient Christian literature usually contain a detailed description of life, environment, works and critical and theological issues and, in addition, a selection of patristic texts for each author. It is composed, in smaller characters, of a collection of very short passages, or of an exuberant abundance of texts. From this general approach two opposing consequences are derived, respectively: either one does not read the texts of the Fathers or one has to buy three, four and up to five volumes, which neither guarantee an overall reading nor are within the students' reach.

Instead, the Congregation for Catholic Education, in its *Instruction on the Study of the Fathers of the Church*, n. 53, states: "It is, in fact, through the direct contact of the teacher and the student with the sources that Patristics must be taught and learned especially at the academic level and in special courses.” Italo Calvino, in his book *Perché leggere i classici*, affirmed: "[...] the direct reading of the original texts will never be recommended enough, avoiding as much as possible critical bibliography, comments, interpretations. The school and the university should serve to make people understand that no book that talks about a book says more than the book in question; instead they do everything to make people believe the opposite. There is a widespread reversal of values so that the introduction, the critical apparatus, and the bibliography are used as a smokescreen to hide what the text has to say and that it can say only if you let it speak without intermediaries who pretend to know more about it.” The recommendation is therefore clear and proceeds from different areas: the Fathers of the Church, classics of Christian thought, must be read. The reader will not, therefore, find a critical apparatus in these pages: the clarifications that we thought appropriate have been made in the introductory section.

Our purpose with this work is to offer a brief introduction to some Fathers, accompanied by selected texts. Each introduction has a reading duration equivalent to one lesson. Our choice of texts has been made from an exegetical perspective, that is, trying to emphasize the biblical aspects of the patristic texts which, while representing a natural continuation of Scripture and often contemporary to it, have not entered the canon because they are not inspired. It is, therefore, a thematic choice, which is one of the different ways of presenting the subject, as indicated in the *Instruction on the Study of the Fathers of the Church,* in n. 58b. A beginning, therefore, as we have indicated in the title, which encourages the student to deepen the reading of the patristic texts.

These pages are fundamentally based on some reference works with a well-rooted tradition. In the elaboration of the text we followed in particular the work of J. Quasten, *Patrologia* (1983-2000), which is a collective work prepared in the *Patristic Institute "Augustinianum"* in Rome under the direction of Angelo di Bernardino and which follows the previous one that appeared in Italian in 1978. It is also worth mentioning the *Nuovo Dizionario Patristico e di Antichità Cristiane* (NDPAC) also directed by A. di Bernardino, Casale Monferrato 2006-2008, translated into several languages. The final bibliography of each chapter is designed to offer a purely exegetical in-depth examination.

Our thanks go, in the first place, to Prof. Manuel Mira who with infinite patience has made suggestions for improving the manuscript; to the students who have followed year after year the evolution of these pages and have contributed, perhaps without their knowledge, with questions and requests for clarification; and particularly to Prof. Laurent Touze, who, when, several years ago, we were going to choose the texts to read in class, and we were almost convinced of the goodness of the exegetical choice, one day he told us "how beautiful it would be to study the way the Fathers of the Church read the Bible!”
