### JOHN CASSIAN (360-435) {#leal-patrologia.introduction-to-patristi.john-cassian-360-435}
John Cassian is the main western leader of the monastic tradition. Probably originally from Scythia Minor, near the mouth of the Danube. Still young he entered a monastery in Bethlehem and visited the monks of Egypt. Around 415 he was ordained a priest in Marseille, where he founded two monasteries, one for men and one for women. In the years 420-428 he wrote the *Instrutiones* and the *Collationes*, to transmit the authentic tradition of Eastern monasticism to the West.

The *Collationes*, published in three volumes, are twenty-four in total (like the Old Ones of the Apocalypse) and have the form of dialogues with famous monks of antiquity and, as a complement to his previous work, still deal with different aspects of monastic life. They were composed as spiritual exercises in the manner of Plato's or Cicero's dialogues, dealing with different themes: prayer, friendship, biblical interpretation, discretion and even Pelagianism, showing a different position from that of Augustine. His use of Scripture is rather focused on the search for examples to be proposed according to the themes dealt with.

#### Spiritual Conference II, 2-4; 9-16
*II. What discretion alone can give a monk; and a discourse of the blessed Anthony on this subject.*

And so I remember that while I was still a boy, in the region of Thebaid, where the blessed Antony lived, the elders came to him to inquire about perfection: and though the conference lasted from evening till morning, the greatest part of the night was taken up with this question. For it was discussed at great length what [virtue](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm) or observance could preserve a [monk](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10487b.htm) always unharmed by the snares and deceits of the [devil](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04764a.htm), and carry him forward on a sure and right path, and with firm step to the heights of perfection. And when each one gave his opinion according to the bent of his own [mind](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10321a.htm), and some made it consist in [zeal](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15753a.htm) in [fasting](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05789c.htm) and [vigils](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05647a.htm), because a [soul](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14153a.htm) that has been brought low by these, and so obtained purity of heart and body will be the more easily united to [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm), others in despising all things, as, if the mind were utterly deprived of them, it would come the more freely to [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm), as if henceforth there were no snares to entangle it: others thought that withdrawal from the world was the thing needful, i.e., solitude and the secrecy of the hermit's life; living in which a man may more readily commune with [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm), and cling more especially to Him; others laid down that the duties of charity, i.e., of kindness should be practiced, because the Lord in the gospel promised more especially to give the kingdom to these; when He said Come you blessed of My Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was an hungry and you gave Me to eat, I was thirsty and you gave Me to drink, etc.: [Matthew 25:35-36](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/mat025.htm#verse35) and when in this fashion they declared that by means of different [virtues](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm) a more certain approach to God could be secured, and the greater part of the night had been spent in this discussion, then at last the blessed Antony spoke and said: All these things which you have mentioned are indeed needful, and helpful to those who are thirsting for [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm), and desirous to approach Him. But countless accidents and the experience of many people will not allow us to make the most important of gifts consist in them. For often when men are most strict in [fasting](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05789c.htm) or in [vigils](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05647a.htm), and nobly withdraw into solitude, and aim at depriving themselves of all their goods so absolutely that they do not suffer even a day's allowance of food or a single penny to remain to them, and when they fulfil all the duties of kindness with the utmost devotion, yet still we have seen them suddenly deceived, so that they could not bring the work they had entered upon to a suitable close, but brought their exalted fervor and praiseworthy manner of life to a terrible end. Wherefore we shall be able clearly to recognize what it is which mainly leads to [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm), if we trace out with greater care the reason of their downfall and deception. For when the works of the above mentioned [virtues](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm) were abounding in them, discretion alone was wanting, and allowed them not to continue even to the end. Nor can any other reason for their falling off be discovered except that as they were not sufficiently instructed by their elders they could not obtain judgment and discretion, which passing by excess on either side, teaches a [monk](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10487b.htm) always to walk along the royal road, and does not allow him to be puffed up on the right hand of [virtue](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm), i.e., from excess of [zeal](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15753a.htm) to transgress the bounds of due moderation in foolish presumption, nor allows him to be enamored of slackness and turn aside to the [vices](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15403c.htm) on the left hand, i.e., under pretext of controlling the body, to grow slack with the opposite spirit of lukewarmness. For this is discretion, which is termed in the gospel the eye, and light of the body, according to the Savior's saying: The light of your body is your eye: but if your eye be single, your whole body will be full of light, but if your eye be [evil](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm), your whole body will be full of darkness: [Matthew 6:22-23](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/mat006.htm#verse22) because as it discerns all the thoughts and actions of [men](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09580c.htm), it sees and overlooks all things which should be done. But if in any man this is [evil](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm), i.e., not fortified by sound judgment and [knowledge](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08673a.htm), or deceived by some [error](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05525a.htm) and presumption, it will make our whole body full of darkness, i.e., it will darken all our mental vision and our actions, as they will be involved in the darkness of [vices](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15403c.htm) and the gloom of disturbances. For, says He, if the light which is in you be darkness, how great will that darkness be! [Matthew 6:22-23](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/mat006.htm#verse22) For no one can [doubt](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05141a.htm) that when the judgment of our heart goes wrong, and is overwhelmed by the night of [ignorance](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07648a.htm), our thoughts and [deeds](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01115a.htm), which are the result of deliberation and discretion, must be involved in the darkness of still greater [sins](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14004b.htm).

*III. Of the error of Saul and of Ahab, by which they were deceived through lack of discretion.*

Lastly, the man who in the [judgment of God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08549a.htm) was the first to be worthy of the kingdom of His people [Israel](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08193a.htm), because he was lacking in this eye of discretion, was, as if his whole body were full of darkness, actually cast down from the kingdom while, being deceived by the darkness of this light, and in [error](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05525a.htm), he imagined that his own offerings were more acceptable to God than [obedience](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11181c.htm) to the command of Samuel, and met with an occasion of falling in that very matter in which he had hoped to propitiate the Divine Majesty. [1 Samuel xv](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/1sa015.htm) And [ignorance](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07648a.htm), I say, of this discretion led Ahab the king of [Israel](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08193a.htm) after a triumph and splendid victory which had been granted to him by the favor of God to fancy that mercy on his part was better than the stern execution of the divine command, and, as it seemed to him, a cruel rule: and moved by this consideration, while he desired to temper a bloody victory with mercy, he was on account of his indiscriminating clemency rendered full of darkness in his whole body, and condemned irreversibly to death. [1 Kings xx](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/1ki020.htm)

*IV. What is said of the value of discretion in Holy Scripture.*

Such is discretion, which is not only the light of the body, but also called the sun by the Apostle, as it said Let not the sun go down upon your [wrath](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01489a.htm). [Ephesians 4:26](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/eph004.htm#verse26) It is also called the guidance of our life: as it said Those who have no guidance, fall like leaves. It is most [truly](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm) named counsel, without which the authority of Scripture allows us to do nothing, so that we are not even permitted to take that spiritual wine which makes glad the heart of man without its regulating control: as it is said Do everything with counsel, drink your wine with counsel, and again like a city that has its walls destroyed and is not fenced in, so is a man who does anything without counsel. And how injurious the absence of this is to a [monk](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10487b.htm), the illustration and figure in the passage quoted shows, by comparing it to a city that is destroyed and without walls. Herein lies wisdom, herein lies intelligence and understanding without which our inward house cannot be built, nor can spiritual riches be gathered together, as it is said: A house is built with wisdom, and again it is set up with intelligence. With understanding the storehouses are filled with all precious riches and [good](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06636b.htm) things. This I say is solid food, which can only be taken by those who are full grown and strong, as it is said: But solid food is for full grown men, who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern good and [evil](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm). [Hebrews 5:14](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/heb005.htm#verse14) And it is shown to be useful and necessary for us, only in so far as it is in accordance with the word of [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm) and its powers, as is said For the word of God is quick and powerful, and sharper than any two-edged sword, and reaching even to the dividing asunder of [soul](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14153a.htm) and spirit, of both joints and marrow, and a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart: [Hebrews 4:12](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/heb004.htm#verse12) and by this it is clearly shown that no [virtue](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm) can possibly be perfectly acquired or continue without the [grace](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm) of discretion. And so by the judgment of the blessed Antony as well as of all others it has been laid down that it is discretion which leads a fearless [monk](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10487b.htm) by fixed stages to [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm), and preserves the [virtues](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm) mentioned above continually intact, by means of which one may ascend with less weariness to the extreme summit of perfection, and without which even those who toil most willingly cannot reach the heights of perfection. For discretion is the mother of all [virtues](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm), as well as their guardian and regulator.

*IX. A question about the acquirement of true discretion.*

To this Germanus responds: It has been fully and completely shown both by recent instances and by the decisions of the ancients how discretion is in some sense the fountain head and the root of all [virtues](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm). We want then to learn how it ought to be gained, or how we can tell whether it is genuine and from [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm), or whether it is spurious and from the [devil](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04764a.htm): so that (to use the figure of that gospel [parable](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11460a.htm) which you discussed on a former occasion, in which we are bidden to become good money changers ) we may be able to see the figure of the [true](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm) king stamped on the coin and to detect what is not stamped on coin that is current, and that, as you said in yesterday's talk using an ordinary expression, we may reject it as counterfeit, under the teaching of that skill which you treated of with sufficient fullness and detail, and showed ought to belong to the man who is spiritually a good money changer of the gospel. For of what good will it be to have recognized the value of that [virtue](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm) and [grace](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm) if we do not [know](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08673a.htm) how to seek for it and to gain it?

*X. The answer how true discretion may be gained.*

Then [Moses](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10596a.htm): True discretion, said he, is only secured by [true](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm) humility. And of this humility the first [proof](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12454c.htm) is given by reserving everything (not only what you do but also what you think), for the scrutiny of the elders, so as not to trust at all in your own judgment but to acquiesce in their decisions in all points, and to acknowledge what ought to be considered good or bad by their traditions. And this habit will not only teach a young man to walk in the right path through the [true](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm) way of discretion, but will also keep him unhurt by all the crafts and deceits of the enemy. For a man cannot possibly be deceived, who lives not by his own judgment but according to the example of the elders, nor will our crafty foe be able to abuse the [ignorance](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07648a.htm) of one who is not accustomed from false modesty to conceal all the thoughts which rise in his heart, but either checks them or suffers them to remain, in accordance with the ripened judgment of the elders. For a wrong thought is enfeebled at the moment that it is discovered: and even before the sentence of discretion has been given, the foul serpent is by the power of confession dragged out, so to speak, from his dark under-ground cavern, and in some sense shown up and sent away in disgrace. For [evil](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05649a.htm) thoughts will hold sway in us just so long as they are hidden in the heart.

*XIV. Of the call of Samuel.*

Lastly so far has this opinion been shown to be pleasing to God that we see that this system not without reason finds a place in [holy](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07386a.htm) Scripture, so that the Lord would not of Himself instruct by the method of a Divine colloquy the lad Samuel, when chosen for judgment, but suffered him to run once or twice to the old man, and willed that one whom He was calling to converse with Him should be taught even by one who had offended [God](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06608a.htm), as he was an old man, and preferred that he whom He had deemed worthy to be called by Him should be trained by the Elder in order to test the humility of him who was called to a Divine office, and to set an example to the younger men by the manner of his subjection.

*XV. Of the call of the Apostle Paul.*

And when Christ in His own Person called and addressed [Paul](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11567b.htm), although He might have opened out to him at once the way of perfection, yet He chose rather to direct him to Ananias and commanded him to learn the way of [truth](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15073a.htm) from him, saying: Arise and go into the city and there it shall be told you what you ought to do. [Acts 9:6](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/act009.htm#verse6) So He sends him to an older man, and thinks good to have him instructed by his teaching rather than His own, lest what might have been rightly done in the case of [Paul](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11567b.htm) might set a bad example of self-sufficiency, if each one were to persuade himself that he also ought in like manner to be trained by the government and teaching of God alone rather than by the instruction of the Elders. And this self-sufficiency the apostle himself teaches, not only by his letters but by his acts and [deeds](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01115a.htm), ought to be shunned with all possible care, as he says that he went up to Jerusalem solely for this reason; viz., to communicate in a private and informal conference with his co-apostles and those who were before him that [Gospel](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06655b.htm) which he preached to the [Gentiles](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06422a.htm), the [grace](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06689a.htm) of the [Holy Spirit](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07409a.htm) accompanying him with powerful signs and wonders: as he says And I communicated with them the [Gospel](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06655b.htm) which I preach among the [Gentiles](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06422a.htm) lest perhaps I had run or should run in vain. [Galatians 2:2](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/gal002.htm#verse2) Who then is so self-sufficient and blind as to dare to trust in his own judgment and discretion when the chosen vessel confesses that he had need of conference with his fellow [apostles](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01626c.htm). Whence we clearly see that the Lord does not Himself show the way of perfection to anyone who having the opportunity of learning depises the teaching and training of the Elders, paying no heed to that saying which ought most carefully to be observed: Ask your father and he will show it to you: your Elders and they will tell you. [Deuteronomy 32:7](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/deu032.htm#verse7)

*XVI. How to seek for discretion.*

We ought then with all our might to strive for the [virtue](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15472a.htm) of discretion by the power of humility, as it will keep us uninjured by either extreme, for there is an old saying ἀκρότητες ἰσότητες, i.e., extremes meet. For excess of [fasting](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05789c.htm) and [gluttony](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06590a.htm) come to the same thing, and an unlimited continuance of [vigils](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05647a.htm) is equally injurious to a [monk](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10487b.htm) as the torpor of a deep sleep: for when a man is weakened by excessive abstinence he is sure to return to that condition in which a man is kept through carelessness and negligence, so that we have often seen those who could not be deceived by [gluttony](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06590a.htm), destroyed by excessive [fasting](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05789c.htm) and by reason of weakness liable to that passion which they had before overcome. Unreasonable [vigils](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05647a.htm) and nightly watches have also been the ruin of some whom sleep could not get the better of: wherefore as the apostle says with the arms of righteousness on the right hand and on the left, [2 Corinthians 6:7](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/2co006.htm#verse7) we pass on with due moderation, and walk between the two extremes, under the guidance of discretion, that we may not consent to be led away from the path of continence marked out for us, nor fall by undue carelessness into the pleasures of the palate and belly.
