### BEDE (673-735) {#leal-patrologia.introduction-to-patristi.bede-673-735}
Bede, born in Northumbria, was entrusted at the age of seven to the monks of Jarrow, with whom he remained forever. Throughout his life, which was dedicated to study and prayer, he was fascinated by the Fathers of the Church, especially Augustine and Isidore, and prepared a patristic anthology intended to facilitate the interpretation of Scripture. In a Latin of great quality, he wrote several historical works and treatises on orthography, metrics, rhetoric, astronomy, and cosmography. Like Isidore of Seville, he contributed greatly to the transmission of ancient knowledge to the medieval world, to which he already fully belonged. Countless copies of his works are preserved, an indication of his great influence on posterity. His *Historia anglorum* is particularly well known.

Bede was a man of Scripture and claimed to have made the effort to meditate on it. Most of Bede's literary output consists of biblical and exegetical commentaries: he commented on almost every biblical book. Then, he also wrote the *Homilies on the Gospel*. In these treatises one finds the substance of the Fathers (Ambrose, Jerome, Augustine, Gregory the Great), but he is not a repetitive compiler, because he critically elaborates a personal letter.

In his homilies, Bede repeatedly distinguishes between a literal sense and another, deeper sense, and he often employs the term *typology*, but this procedure should not be confused with that of the early patristic era: it is a *mystical* interpretation, as he says, that is, the person or object of the Old Testament is symbolic of some aspect of the spiritual life of the listeners present. In this sense it would be identified rather with allegorical-spiritual interpretation.

#### Homilies on the Gospel I, 21: In Lent (Mt. 9:9-13)
We have read the passage where the apostle says: “For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and all are justified freely by his grace” (Rom. 3:23-24). Then he recalls again the immeasurable greatness of his grace: “Where sin increased, grace increased all the more” (Rom. 5:20), because certainly the more serious the Lord has healed the disease of sins in his elect, the greater he has shown to all the power of healing grace. In this sense we have heard from the Gospel reading that the Lord, having had compassion on Matthew sitting at the tax collector's booth intent on material dealings, suddenly called him, and from a tax collector made him a righteous man, from a publican a disciple. Then, increasing his grace, he promoted him from the number of common disciples to the rank of apostle and entrusted him not only with the task of preaching but also of writing the Gospel, so that he who had not been a dispenser of earthly goods might begin to be one of heavenly talents. Divine providence has thus arranged that the enormity and great number of crimes should not deter anyone from hoping for forgiveness, since Matthew was freed from so many bonds of the world and progressed so much as to be and be called an evangelist, a word he shares with the angelic spirits.

“Jesus saw a man named Matthew, sitting at the tax collector’s booth, and said to him, ‘Follow me.’” He saw not so much with the sight of the body as with the gaze of inner compassion, by which he deigned to look also upon Peter who denied him, so that he might recognize and weep for his sin, by which he had observed his people to snatch them from the slavery of Egypt from which they were oppressed, when he said to Moses: “I have observed the affliction of my people who are in Egypt, I have heard their groaning, and I have come down to deliver them” (Acts 7:34). He saw the man and had compassion on him because, devoted only to the affairs of this earth, he was not yet worthy of the name of angels. He saw him sitting at the tax collector's booth with his mind greedy for earthly gains, named Matthew. The Hebrew Matthew in Latin means “given”, and the name fits well for him who has received so great a gift of divine grace. Nor should we overlook that Matthew had two names and was also called Levi, a name which also confirms the grace he had received. In fact, Levi means “added” or “assumed”, indicating that he was assumed by the Lord by election and added to the number of apostles. Mark and Luke in this reading wanted to call him rather Levi, not distinguishing him and not making him participate in the evangelical work from this first colloquy. But when they report the list of the twelve apostles, they are silent about Levi and openly call him Matthew. As for Matthew himself, according to what is written: “The righteous is the first to accuse himself, then comes his friend and examines him” (Prov. 18:17), he calls himself by his usual name when he narrates that he was called from the tax collector's booth, but in the catalog of the apostles he names himself with the addition of “publican” - Thomas, he says, and Matthew the publican -, to instill greater confidence in publicans and sinners to obtain salvation. Paul follows this very method of teaching when he says: “Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the worst. But for that very reason I was shown mercy so that in me, the worst of sinners, Christ Jesus might display his immense patience as an example for those who would believe in him and receive eternal life” (1 Tim. 2 1:15-16). He saw the publican and because in seeing him he had compassion on him, he chose him and said: “Follow me.” [Vidit ergo publicanum et quia miserando atque eligendo vidit ait illi: sequere me.]. He told him to follow him in the sense of imitating him; he told him to follow him not so much with the movement of his feet as with his way of life. In fact, “Whoever claims to live in him must live as Jesus did” (1 John 2:6), that is, not to desire earthly things, not to pursue fleeting gains, but to flee earthly honors, willingly despise the whole world for the glory of heaven, help everyone, not to insult anyone and patiently endure those who insult him, ask forgiveness from the Lord for those who inflict them, always seek his own glory in the Creator, to rise as much as possible to the love of heavenly things. To act in this way means to follow the footsteps of Christ.

“And he got up and followed him.” We should not be surprised that he, at the first command of the Lord’s voice, left the earthly gains he was attending to and, no longer caring about riches, joined the group of disciples of him whom he saw possessed no riches. The Lord who had called him outwardly with his word, inwardly urged him with invisible force to follow him, infusing into his mind the light of spiritual grace, so that he might understand that he who was diverting him from temporal goods on earth, was capable of giving incorruptible treasures in heaven. “He got up and followed him.” He got up to follow him, he left the fleeting goods he had to obtain the eternal ones, to which the truth invites him, as Isaiah says: “Awake, sleeper, rise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you” (Isaiah 60:1; Ephesians 5:14).

#### Homily I, 14 After the Epiphany (Jn. 2:1-11)
There were six stone water jars, prepared for the usual Jewish purifications, each holding from two to three measures.” The vessels prepared to receive water are called hydriae. In Greek, water is called hydor. But water indicates the knowledge of the Sacred Scriptures, because it washes those who listen to it from the stain of sins and quenches them at the fountain of divine science. The six vessels, which contained it, are the devoted hearts of the saints, whose perfection of life and faith was proposed to humankind as an example of how one should believe and live rightly during the six ages of the passing world, that is, up to the time of the Lord’s preaching. Rightly the vessels are of stone, because the hearts of the righteous are strong, consolidated by the faith and love of that stone that Daniel saw cut out from the mountain without human hands, and which had become a great mountain that filled the whole earth, and of which Zechariah says: “On one stone there are seven eyes” (Zechariah 3:9), that is, in Christ dwells all the knowledge of the Spirit. The apostle Peter remembers this when he says: “As you come to him, the living Stone—rejected by humans but chosen by God and precious to him— you also, like living stones, are being built into a spiritual house” 1 (1 Peter 2:4-5). HOMILY I, 14 AFTER EPIPHANY (JOHN 2:1-11)

(…) There were six stone water jars, prepared for the usual Jewish purifications, each holding from two to three measures.” The vessels prepared to receive water are called hydriae. In Greek, water is called hydor. But water indicates the knowledge of the Sacred Scriptures, because it washes those who listen to it from the stain of sins and quenches them at the fountain of divine science. The six vessels, which contained it, are the devoted hearts of the saints, whose perfection of life and faith was proposed to humankind as an example of how one should believe and live rightly during the six ages of the passing world, that is, up to the time of the Lord’s preaching. Rightly the vessels are of stone, because the hearts of the righteous are strong, consolidated by the faith and love of that stone that Daniel saw cut out from the mountain without human hands, and which had become a great mountain that filled the whole earth, and of which Zechariah says: “On one stone there are seven eyes” (Zechariah 3:9), that is, in Christ dwells all the knowledge of the Spirit. The apostle Peter remembers this when he says: “As you come to him, the living Stone—rejected by humans but chosen by God and precious to him— you also, like living stones, are being built into a spiritual house” 1 (1 Peter 2:4-5). Rightly the hydriae had been placed for the purification of the Jews, because the Law had been given by Moses only to the Jewish people, while the grace and truth of the Gospel have been given by Jesus Christ to both the Gentiles and the Jews. “Each holding from two to three measures,” because the prophets, authors and ministers of the Sacred Scripture, speak sometimes only of the Father and the Son, as in the passage “in wisdom you have made them all” (Psalm 103:24) (indeed Christ is the power and wisdom of God); at other times, however, they also mention the Holy Spirit as in this passage of the psalm: “By the word of the Lord the heavens were made, their starry host by the breath of his mouth” (Psalm 32:6). By the word and spirit of the Lord, understand the whole Trinity, which is one God. But how great is the difference between water and wine, so great is the difference between the sense with which the Scriptures were understood before the birth of the Lord, and the sense that he, coming to earth, revealed to the apostles, and which has always been followed by their disciples. Certainly the Lord could have filled the empty vessels with wine, he who at the beginning of the creation of the world had created all things from nothing, but he preferred to transform water into wine to teach symbolically that he had come not to abolish and blame but rather to fulfill the Law and the Prophets, nor did he do or teach anything with the grace of the Gospel other than what the Law and the Prophets had indicated he would do and teach. Let us therefore see, brothers, the six vessels full of the water of the Scriptures, and let us see this water changed into the sweetest fragrance and taste of wine.

In the first age of the world, the envious brother killed righteous Abel, and therefore Abel, blessed by the eternal glory of martyrdom, received praise for justice also in the Gospels and letters of the apostles, while the impious fratricide paid the penalty of eternal damnation. Whoever, having heard these facts, in fear of being condemned with the impious while desiring to be blessed with the pious, removes every occasion for hatred and envy, strives to please God with the sacrifice of justice, modesty, innocence, patience, these find in the Scripture the vessel full of water, by which they rejoice to be purified and quenched by the water of salvation. If they then understand that Cain the murderer signifies the perfidy of the Jews and the killing of Abel the passion of the Lord; and that the earth that opened its mouth and received the blood from Cain's hand signifies the Church that has received in the mystery of its rebirth the blood of Christ shed by the Jews, then certainly these find the water changed into wine, since they understand the sacred words of the Law in a more sacred sense.

At the beginning of the second age of the world, the world was destroyed by the waters of the flood because of the greatness of sins and only Noah was saved in his house in the ark, because he was a righteous man. Having heard the horrible devastation of this plague and the miraculous salvation of a few, whoever has begun to live more holily, in the hope of being saved with the elect and in fear of being exterminated with the reprobate, has received the hydria of water to be purified and restored. But when he has begun to look more deeply and has seen in the ark the Church, in Noah Christ, in the water that destroys sins the water of baptism that washes away sins, in all the animals that the ark contained the multiple diversity of the baptized, in the dove, which after the flood brought the olive branch to the ark, the anointing of the Holy Spirit that the baptized receive, then he will marvel that the water has become wine, because in the account of the ancient fact he contemplates the prophecy of his purification, sanctification, justification.

In the third age of the world, God, testing Abraham's obedience, commands him to offer as a burnt offering his only beloved son. Abraham does not hesitate to do what he is commanded, but instead of his son, a ram is immolated. He, however, by virtue of this extraordinary obedience, receives as a gift the inheritance of eternal blessing. Behold the third vessel. Indeed, to hear with what great reward such great virtue of obedience was rewarded, you also desire to learn to practice obedience. If then in the sacrifice of Abraham's only son you understand the passion of him of whom the Father says: “This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased” (Mt. 3:17); and in this regard the son is offered but the ram is sacrificed to indicate that the divinity remained impassible and only humanity suffered pain and death; if you understand that the gift of blessing, which was promised to Abraham, was realized in you, because the Gentile nations have believed, certainly Christ has transformed water into wine for you, because he has revealed to you the spiritual meaning of Scripture, with whose new fragrance you are inebriated.

In the fourth age of the world, David obtains the kingdom of the people of Israel instead of Saul, humble, innocent, meek, exiled, from the one unjustly persecuted. Behold the fourth vessel filled with the water of salvation. Whoever, listening to this story, begins to practice innocence and humility and to reject pride and envy from his heart finds, so to speak, a sip of very clear water to refresh himself. If he then understands that in Saul are signified the persecuting Jews, in David Christ and the Church, and that for their perfidy their empire, both carnal and spiritual, was destroyed while the kingdom of Christ and the Church will always remain, he will realize that the glass of water has become wine, since he knows how to read about himself, about his life, about his kingdom, and also about his king, where before he read an old story, which seemed to concern other people.

In the fifth age of the world, the sinful people, taken prisoner by Nebuchadnezzar, go into exile to Babylon, but after seventy years, repentant and corrected, they are led back to their homeland by Joshua, the great priest, and here he rebuilds the house of the Lord, which had been burned down, and the holy city, which had been destroyed. Whoever, reading or hearing these facts, is seized by the fear of sinning, and takes refuge in penance, washes himself with the water of the purifying hydria. If he then understands that Jerusalem and the temple of God indicate the Church of Christ, Babylon the confusion of sins, Nebuchadnezzar the devil, the priest Joshua the great, true, eternal priest Jesus Christ, the seventy years the fullness of good works, which are bestowed as gifts by the Holy Spirit by virtue of the Decalogue and the sevenfold grace of the Spirit; if he sees that all this happens every day because some are snatched from the Church by the devil because of sin, while others, repenting and doing penance, are reconciled in Christ by the grace of the Holy Spirit, he has the water transformed into wine, because he understands that the facts, which have been written, refer to him; he is therefore warmed, as by must, by a great ardor of compunction, and asks to be freed by the grace of Christ from every trace, which he has perceived in himself, of the sin that makes us prisoners.

At the beginning of the sixth age of the world, the Lord, appearing as a man, was circumcised according to the Law on the eighth day after his birth, was brought to the temple thirty-three days later, and offerings were made for him according to the Law. Considering these facts according to the letter, we learn with clarity how diligently we must practice the sacraments of evangelical faith, since he who gave the letter of the Law brought the blessing of grace, he who consecrates everything with his divinity was consecrated with the consecrated rite of the ancient ceremonies, and thus he both received and transmitted the new sacraments of grace. Behold the sixth vessel, which carries water purer than the others, to wash away the contagion of sin and to drink the joys of life. If then in the circumcision of the eighth day you understand baptism, which with the mystery of the Lord's resurrection redeemed us from the death of sins; if you understand that in the visit to the temple and in the offering of the purifying victim are symbolized all the faithful, who from the baptistery must access the altar and be consecrated by that very special victim which is constituted by the body and blood of the Lord, then you have received the gift of wine, made from water and moreover very pure. If you also interpret the day of circumcision in function of the general resurrection of the human race, when the human lineage will cease and all mortality will be changed into immortality, and you understand that the circumcised who are led into the temple with the victims are the saints who, after the resurrection and the universal judgment, having become incorruptible, will enter to contemplate forever the sight of the divine majesty with the reward of good works, then you will see that the water has been changed into extraordinary wine, and you will bear witness to the Creator saying: "How beautiful is your cup, which inebriates" (Psalm 22:5). Therefore, the Lord for the joy of the wedding did not want to make wine from nothing but after commanding that the six vessels be filled with water, he wonderfully transformed it into wine, as he gave to the six ages of the world the abundance of wisdom, which gives salvation, and then, coming to earth, enriched it with a higher meaning: those truths that carnal men understood only according to the flesh, he revealed that they were to be understood according to the spirit.

#### Homily II, 2 in Lent (Jn. 6:1-14)
(…) The five loaves of bread with which he satisfied the multitude of people are the five books of Moses. If they are opened up by spiritual understanding, and then multiplied by [penetration of] their deeper meaning, they daily refresh the hearts of the believers who hear them. It is appropriate that they are reported to have been made of barley, because of the stricter ordinances of the law, and the thicker outer husks of its literal [interpretation] which, as it were, cover the inner pith of its spiritual sense. The two fishes which he added not inappropriately signify the writings of the psalmists and prophets. One [of these] by chanting, and the other by talking, to those who listen, tell of future sacramental [mysteries] of Christ and the Church. And it is also appropriate that it was animals that live in the water that prefigured the heralds of that age in which faithful people could in no way live without the water of baptism. The boy who had the five loaves and two fishes, who did not distribute them to the hungry crowd but brought them to our Lord for him to distribute, is the Jewish people. They were childlike [in their understanding] of the literal sense, and they kept the words of the scriptures shut up within themselves. Nevertheless, our Lord, on his appearance in the flesh, took them and showed what usefulness and sweetness they had in them. By the manifold grace of the Spirit he disclosed how matters that seemed insignificant and unworthy of attention might be fruitful, and he handed these things over to be administered to all nations by the apostles and their successors. Hence it is appropriate that the other evangelists relate how our Lord administered the loaves and fishes to his disciples, and the disciples then administered them to the crowd. Although the mystery of human salvation received its start by being declared by our Lord, it was confirmed in us by those who heard it [from him]. He broke up the five loaves and two fishes, and distributed them to his disciples, when he opened their minds to understand everything that had been written about him in the law of Moses and in the prophets and the psalms. The disciples set them before the crowd when *they preached everywhere, out Lord working with them, and strengthening their utterance by the signs that followed it.*

The grass on which the crowd reclined while it was being refreshed is understood [as indicating] bodily concupiscence, which everyone who desires to be satisfied with spiritual nourishment ought to tread under foot and repress. *For all flesh is* [as] *grass, and all its glory like the bloom of grass*. Let all who long to be refreshed by the sweetness of the living bread, all who love to be renewed by the banquet of heavenly grace, sit down upon the grass, let them trample upon the bloom of the grass. Let them chastise the body and subject it to slavery, let them repress the pleasures of the flesh, let them restrain the changeable [urgings] of self-indulgence. Let them beware let they faint away through [being dragged down] by a degrading [return] to their former self. The five thousand men who ate suggest the perfection of those who are refreshed with the word of life. By the word “men” scripture customarily indicates all those more perfect ones whom no feminine softness corrupts, the sort of people the Apostle longs for those to be whom he tells, *Be watchful, stand* [firm] *in faith, act manfully and take courage*. The number one thousand, beyond which no calculation of ours extends, ordinarily indicates the fullness of the things which are being treated of; by the number five the well-known senses of the body are represented, namely, sight, hearing, taste, smell, and touch. Individuals from among [these five thousand] who attempt to act manfully and take courage by living soberly and righteously and piously, so that they may deserve to be renewed by the sweetness of heavenly wisdom, are those indicated by the five thousand men whom our Lord satisfied by this mystical banquet. Nor must we overlook the fact that as he was on the point of refreshing the multitude, he gave thanks. He gave thanks in order to teach us always to give thanks for the favors we have received from heaven, and in order to impress [upon us] how much he himself gives thanks for our progress, how much he himself rejoices at our spiritual refreshment. For do you indeed wish to know, brothers, how much he rejoices with us at our salvation? The evangelist Luke tells how, after he gave his disciples the power of treading upon all might of the enemy, he indicated that their names were written in heaven, and [Luke] then concludes, *At that hour he exulted in the Holy Spirit and said, “I give praise to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that you have kept these things secret from the wise and prudent and have revealed them to little ones.”* It is therefore apparent that he rejoices with the faithful at their salvation and life, since he praises the Father with them by giving [him] thanks for revealing these secrets to the humble of spirit after keeping them secret from the proud.

But as for his ordering the disciples, after the crowd had been satisfied, to gather up whatever fragments were left over, so that they might not perish, this unquestionably signifies that there are many hidden mysteries of the divine tidings that the minds of ordinary people do not grasp. There are some that those less learned are unable to assimilate on their own, but which they are able to understand once they are explained by teachers. Those more able, then, should, by probing them more diligently, gather these [mysteries] up, and, by their speaking or writing, make them a guide for the less [learned], so that the nourishment of the word may not perish because of their inertia and be kept from the people [by those] who, through God’s gift, know how to gather these [mysteries] up by interpreting [them]. *They gathered* [the fragments] *up, and filled twelve baskets with the fragments*. Because the height of anything’s perfection is customarily indicated by the number twelve, the complete concurrence of spiritual teachers is correctly represented by the twelve baskets full of fragments. They are ordered both to gather up by meditating [on them] the obscure points of the scriptures which the crowd is unable [to understand] on its own, and to preserve the results of their meditation and [the Lord’s] commandments in their writings for their own use as well as that of the crowds.
