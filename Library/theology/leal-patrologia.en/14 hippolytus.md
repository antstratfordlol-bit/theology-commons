### HIPPOLYTUS {#leal-patrologia.introduction-to-patristi.hippolytus}
Under the name of Hippolytus there are several historical and/or historical-literary entities: the writer Hippolytus, remembered by Eusebius of Caesarea, Jerome's *De viris illustribus* and other eastern testimonies, ignored instead in the West, author of a work entitled *Contro Noeto* and a group of exegetical writings; the author of the *Refutatio omnium haeresium* and related writings, active in Rome, in opposition to the popes Zephyrinus and Callistus, in the first decades of the third century; the presbyter and martyr Hippolytus, who died in Sardinia with Pope Pontian and was venerated in Rome and Porto. The relationships between these three entities have been explained in various ways. Tradition identifies them in a single character, Hippolytus of Rome. The Loi-Simonetti hypothesis, which we follow here, speaks of two historical figures. An important part of the discussion is the fact that in the mid-sixteenth century, a statue of Hippolytus, which is now in the Vatican Library, was discovered with the titles of his works engraved on it. But then it was shown that the statue did not depict Hippolytus and the list of works had to be considered unreliable. For this mysterious figure we refer to the studies of M. Simonetti and the Patristic Institute "Augustinianum".

By the name of Hippolytus we mean here the author of a work entitled Against Noetus and of a group of exegetical writings: a *Commentary on David*; a *Commentary on the Song of Songs*; a *Homily on the Story of David and Goliath*; a *Homily on the Psalms*; and a *Homily on Easter*. The names of seventeen lost works of exegesis are known, the majority belonging to the Old Testament. Some scholars believe that Hippolytus is the initiator of exegesis as an autonomous literary genre.

This group of exegetical treatises transmitted under the name of Hippolytus follows the allegorical method, albeit in moderation, and centering on Christology. We can speak of an extension of the typology, since Hippolytus is the first Christian writer to make a systematic commentary on a book of the Bible: thus it becomes an autonomous literary genre (not a polemical one, as might have been the case up to this time). We find in Hippolytus some straining of the text for allegory: for example, *The Blessings* are merely allegory and it seems that Hippolytus is not interested in a literal sense. This is not to say that he disputes the historicity of the facts, but that these facts were historically realised to constitute an anticipation of Christ and the Church. The criteria employed by Hippolytus, therefore, are: rejection of the literal sense for the total benefit of the exegetical one; making allegory spring from etymology; reinforcing the interpretation of a passage with the citation of other passages connected to it. This criterion is not applied systematically and sometimes the allegorical interpretation appears little justified. Thus, the exegetical rationale of Hippolytus appears approximate and even defective. Prinzivalli speaks of a-regulated alternation of literal and allegorical interpretation.

#### Blessings of Jacob 15-19
*An image of the cross of Christ*

Jacob, blessing Judah, says thus:

"Judah, praise thy brothers. Let your hands stand on the back of your enemies, and your father's children will worship you. Judah is a lion's cub; from a bud, my son, you have grown up. Lay down, you have slept like a lion and like a lion’s cub: Who will wake him up? There shall not lack a prince from Judah, nor a leader from his femur, until the one to whom the command has been reserved comes, and this will be the expectation of the people. He will tie his donkey to the vine and his donkey to the branch. He will wash in wine his robe and in the blood of the grape his petticoat. His eyes are bright with joy for the wine and his teeth are whiter than milk.” (Gen. 49:8-12)

How can these words be of the same kind as those said above about Ruben? (...) But one can say: For what reason did the prophet feel he had to give Judah such a blessing, when he did not do the same with the former? Learn it. Since from the tribe of Judah would be born David and from David would be born Christ according to the flesh, the prophet, knowing in advance in spirit what would happen, blessed David who would be from Judah and Christ who would be born from David according to the flesh, so that he would receive from God not only the blessing according to the spirit but also the blessing according to the flesh. (...)

16. Jacob says thus: "Judah, let your brothers praise you. May your hands be on the back of your enemies, and your father's children will worship you." Who then are the brothers who praise and worship him, if not the apostles to whom the Lord said: "You are my brothers and coheirs"? (Mt. 12:50) The words: "Let your hands be on the back of your enemies," indicate both that Christ extending his hands on his cross in the fight against his enemies had the strength to triumph over the opposing powers, and that he became lord and master of those who are his enemies according to the flesh and judge of all established by the Father (Jn. 5:22).

*Images about the Son of God*

"Judah is a lion cub of a lion; from a bud, my son, you have grown." By naming a lion and a lion cub the text clearly indicated the two persons, that of the Father and that of the Son. He then said: "From a bud, my son, you have grown" to indicate the birth according to the flesh of Christ, who incarnated in the womb of the Virgin by the Holy Spirit, sprouted in her and entered the world as a flower and as the scent of sweetness manifested itself. As he then called him a lion's cub, he indicated his birth from God according to the spirit, as a king born of the king. Of him, then, she has not even silenced the birth according to the flesh, but says: "From a bud, my son, you have grown. For Isaiah says, "A rod of Jesse was from the lineage of the fathers, almost a root planted in the ground;” the rod that sprang forth and manifested itself from them was Mary, for it was of the house and family of David (Cf. Lk. 2:4); the flower that sprang forth from her was Christ, and this prophesying said Jacob, "From a bud, my son, you have grown.”

The words, "Lay down you slept like a lion and like a lion’s cub," indicate that he lay for three days in the grave and rested in the heart of the earth, as the Lord himself testified, saying, "Just as Jonah spent three days and three nights in the belly of the whale, so too shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth" (Mt. 12:40); and David foretold this with these words, "I lay down and fell asleep. I have awakened, because the Lord will come in my defence." (Ps 3:6) Likewise Jacob also says, "Who will wake him up?” He did not say, "no one will wake him," but, "who will wake him?" so that we would not understand the Father, who has awakened the Son from the dead, as the apostle says: " [...] and God the Father, who has awakened him from the dead" (Gal. 1:1); and Peter says, "that God has awakened him, having put an end to the pains of death, because it was not possible that he would be overcome by death” (Acts 2:24). *Types of redemption*

18. Then, the text says: "He will bind his ass to the vine and his donkey to the branch", to indicate the two calls linked to him as a vine and led to unity by his love: they are the ass and the donkey purified at the same time by the Savior's word and on which he ascended to enter Jerusalem (cf. Mt. 21:2,7 ff.). He then adds: "He will wash his garment in wine. How mysteriously here the prophet also indicated the Baptism of Christ when, after ascending from the Jordan and having purified the waters, he received the grace and the gift of the Holy Spirit (cf. Mt. 3:13-17 ff.). As a garment he indicated the flesh; as wine the Spirit of the Father who descended upon him into the Jordan. "And in the blood of the grape his petticoat": the petticoat of the Logos indicates the pagan people, who were regarded with respect to him as a petticoat, according to what Scripture says through the prophet: "For my life," says the Lord, "I will clothe all of them as a cloak” (Is. 49:18). Because he was the cluster of grapes hanging from the wood that pierced his side made blood and water flow (Jn. 19:34) - this one for washing, the one for redemption -, what the prophet rightly said: "He will wash his garment in wine and his petticoat in the blood of grapes".

19. Then, to indicate his prophets and his apostles he says: "His eyes are bright with wine, and his teeth are whiter than milk". The eyes of Christ were the prophets, who rejoiced at the power of the Spirit and foretold the sufferings that he would suffer, and that would be useful also to the succeeding generations, so that every man, believing in him, might attain salvation. As then to the words: "his teeth are whiter than milk", they either pointed to the apostles who were sanctified precisely by the Logos, and became like milk, and gave us spiritual and heavenly nourishment, in other words, they refer to the commandments of the Lord, which were issued by a holy mouth and for us became milk, so that we might be nourished by them also as a part of the heavenly bread.
