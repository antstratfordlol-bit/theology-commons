### THEODORET OF CYRUS (393-458/466) {#leal-patrologia.introduction-to-patristi.theodoret-of-cyrus-393-4}
Theodoret was born in Antioch around 393. He received a Christian education and entered a monastery in 416, where he read abundant patristic commentaries that guided him in his future exegetical work. In 423 he was appointed bishop of Cyrus where he became involved in controversies against heresies. In this task exegesis had a great space. He died in 458/466.

Although rooted in the Antiochian environment, his exegesis presents a certain novelty, making use of the authors of the second and fourth century: it is with him that begins the manual system. Moreover, in Theodoret we find a clear reaction against the exaggerations of literal interpretation. We have therefore an Antiochian who tries to recover allegorical procedures, avoiding however the terminology, for the interpretation of the symbolic passages. Theodoret limits the typological interpretation to the traditional places and makes a Christological reading of the Old Testament, with a consequent allegorization of the details and an increase in the number of Christological psalms, interpreted on two or often three levels.

#### Comments on Daniel II,34-35
*The stone rejected by the builders*

You kept gazing and saw how a stone was hewn out of a mountain without hands being used, and it struck the image on its iron and clay feet, and smashed them to pieces. Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver, the gold were crushed in one go and became like dust from a threshing floor in summer. The wind carried them away, and no trace of them was found. The stone that struck the mountain became a huge mountain, and filled the whole earth (vv. 34–35). Such was the end of the dream; we must begin the interpretation starting at the end. So let us enquire first who is given the name stone, seeming first to be small, later shown to be immense and covering the whole world. Let us listen, then, to God himself speaking through the prophet Isaiah, “Lo, I put in Sion a precious stone, a corner stone, honorable, special, as its foundation; anyone believing in it will not be ashamed.” ( Let us listen also to blessed David prophesying in a loud cry, “The stone which the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.” (Ps. 117:22) Christ the Lord himself in the sacred Gospels also cited this text to Jews, “Did you not read the text, The stone which the builders rejected has become the cornerstone. It was done by the Lord, and is a marvel in our eyes?” (Mt. 21:42) Blessed Peter preaching among the Jews and focusing on the prophecy of the Lord says, “He is the stone despised by you the builders, which has become the cornerstone.” (Acts 4:11) Blessed Paul says, “Built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Jesus Christ himself as a cornerstone.” (Eph. 2:20) Elsewhere he says, “No one can lay any foundation stone other than the one laid, which is Jesus Christ.” (1 Cor. 3:11) And again, “They drank from the spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ.” (1 Cor. 10:4)

So we learn from Old and New Testament that our Lord Jesus Christ was called stone: it was cut from a mountain without hands being used, being born of a virgin independently of marital intercourse.58 Now, the divine Scripture is in the habit of calling preternatural birth “quarrying”: in reminding Jews of the begetting of children that happened preternaturally in Abraham’s case, Isaiah said, “Look to the solid rock from which you were quarried.” (Is. 51:1) So mountain means the Davidic tribe, and stone Christ in his humanity, not hewn according to the law of nature: formerly insignificant in appearance on account of the nature with which he was invested, he was suddenly shown to be a mighty mountain, filling the whole world. “The whole world was filled with the knowledge of God,” Scripture says, remember, “like a great volume of water covering the seas.” (Is. 11:9) He it is who will strike the image in its feet of clay and iron, that is, he will bring the last kingdom to an end, and make it futile and evanescent: far from any other kingdom succeeding it, he will reveal his own one and make it obvious to everyone, and will even wipe out the very memory of all those kingdoms like dust stirred up from the threshing floor and scattered by the wind.
