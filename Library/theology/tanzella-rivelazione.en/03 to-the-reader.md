### To the Reader {#tanzella-rivelazione.1.to-the-reader}
This volume *Revelation and its credibility* is the new, totally revised and expanded edition of the previous *Fundamental Theology Lessons* published almost ten years ago.1 The work is conceived as a manual for the Fundamental Theology course of the First Institutional Cycle of Theological Studies. The course, taught over two semesters, is designed in its traditional form as a theology of Revelation and a theology of credibility. As a necessary juncture, the program hosts a short treatise on faith and an exposition on the transmission of Revelation in the Church, and then offers in closing some elements of the relationship between Christian Revelation and religions.

For reasons of space the course can only hint at a *theological* reflection on religion, a broader discussion of which would have been appropriate to understand both man's openness to Revelation and the anthropological situation of man before God. Again because of the limited number of hours allocated to the present course by the university *curricula,* it was not possible to include a treatment of non-belief, in its various forms of the past and the present, which would also include today the situation of religion in a post-modern society. For both topics, the absence of adequate development in these pages can be made up for by consulting the Bibliography proposed at the end of the volume.

Greater extension would also have deserved the relationship between faith and reason, especially with regard to a contemporary re-proposal of the preambles of faith, which is necessary today so that the appeal of Revelation may be recognized as fully significant by a reason that has become weak and incapable of metaphysical petition: we refer, however, to preambles not only of a metaphysical order, but also anthropological, historical and existential. In this sense, a useful complement to *Revelation and its credibility* is provided by our wise *Philosophy and Revelation. Expectations of reason, surprises of the Christian announcement* (San Paolo, Cinisello Balsamo 2008). It is, however, in my treatise *in* 4 volumes *Fundamental Theology in the Scientific Context,* of which only the first two are currently available (*Theology of Credibility*: vol. 1. *La teologia fondamentale e la sua dimensione di apologia*, vol. 2. *La credibilità del cristianesimo*, Città Nuova, Rome 2015), which I refer the reader to, although it should be noted that this treatise was designed for specialized licentiate courses and not for the first cycle. We recall that, in the cycle of institutional theological studies, the *area of Fundamental Theology* also develops the courses of *Introduction to the Christian Mystery* and *Introduction to Theology*. Two homonymous manuals, soon to be published, correspond to them and together they constitute a "Path of Fundamental Theology" whose contents harmonize with those of the present volume.

In drafting this manual I have tried to privilege the didactic perspective, both in the language used and by introducing, when convenient, bulleted lists and synthetic concepts. Compared to the previous edition, I preferred not to overburden the critical apparatus, although some new references were necessary; instead, the Bibliography proposed at the end has been enriched, again for teaching purposes. Although the *web* today allows us to access a great number of texts, I preferred to keep as an appendix to the book a Documentary Anthology, also enriched with some significant passages. In this way, along the pages of the volume we continue to make explicit reference to Anthological texts whose reading is considered fruitful for a better theological-contextual framework of the topics dealt with: the suggested passages are introduced by the symbol, as well as

biblical pages whose reading - I would say careful meditation - is proposed as an integral part of the topics dealt with. Instead, I have published on the *web,* in the didactic section of my personal page, further aids for the course and material in languages other than Italian, considering the international provenance of the students of our ecclesiastical universities.

To make it easier to recognize them, in the notes of each chapter I have reported the complete bibliographic data of the volumes and articles mentioned there for the first time, even if already mentioned in a previous chapter. Where I was aware of them, I preferred to mention the Italian edition of the works of foreign authors. I generally used capital letters to indicate the Hebrew-Christian Revelation, in order to keep it distinct from the generic noun. I also used capital letters in pronouns and adjectives referring to God or one of the Divine Persons, when ambiguities might arise in reference to other subjects.

I am grateful to Nicola Commisso for the careful revision of the text. Sincere thanks are addressed to all my students, whose questions have always been enriching, perhaps more than they could have imagined. Finally, I would like to thank the reader for the observations and corrections that he will point out to me, appealing to his understanding of the incompleteness and the inevitable inaccuracies for which I am, in any case, solely responsible.

The Author
