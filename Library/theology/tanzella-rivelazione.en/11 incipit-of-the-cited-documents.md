1. For the Greeks, to understand, to know, meant "see inside", to understand after looking. To know is the seeing of the Spirit. To it corresponds to what is seen: the idea, the eidós, the truth in the figure of the alétheia, of non-hiding. For the Greeks the vision of the spirit was considered the refinement of human activity. Contemplating the divinity was the state of bliss. In the Old Testament, instead, "knowing" refers to the sphere of hearing: it is understood after hearing the word, or the call. Unlike what we find among the Greeks, here understanding is not related to a timeless idea, but to a historical experience, to the person, his word and his will. Consequently, to know here also means to get acquainted, but to arrive at a re-knowledge. Here knowledge becomes the way of meeting, of being together in the communion of life", H. Fries, Fundamental Theology, Queriniana, Brescia 1987, 137-138. ↑
