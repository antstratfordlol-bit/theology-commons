## Afterword: Saint Thomas Aquinas’ Sixteen Precepts to Acquire the Treasure of Knowledge {#sertillanges-intellectual.afterword-saint-thomas-a}
Because you asked me, in Christ, my dearest John, how you should study to acquire the treasure of knowledge, I offer you the following advice on this matter.

1. Choose to enter the sea by the streams, not directly, because it is necessary to reach the difficult through the easier. This is my advice, and your instruction.
2. Be slow to speak and slow to approach the parlor.
3. Embrace the purity of conscience.
4. Do not cease to devote yourself to prayer.
5. Love your cell frequently if you wish to be introduced to the wine cellar.
6. Show yourself lovable to everyone.
7. Do not inquire at all about the deeds of others.
8. Do not show yourself overly familiar with anyone: because excessive familiarity breeds contempt and provides an occasion for withdrawing from study.
9. Do not get involved in any way with the words and deeds of secular people.
10. Avoid useless outings above all.
11. Do not omit to imitate the footsteps of the saints and the good.
12. Do not consider from whom you hear things, but whatever good is said, entrust it to your memory.
13. Make sure you understand what you read and hear.
14. Certify yourself about any doubts.
15. And strive to store in the little cabinet of your mind whatever you can, like someone desiring to fill a vessel.
16. Do not seek what is above you.

Following these steps, you will bring forth useful leaves and fruits in the vineyard of the Lord of Hosts for as long as you live. If you follow these pieces of advice, you will be able to achieve what you desire. Farewell.
