## Preface to the First Edition {#sertillanges-intellectual.preface-to-the-first-edi}
We find among the works of St. Thomas a letter to a certain friar John, in which are enumerated sixteen precepts to acquire the treasure of knowledge. This letter, authentic or not, deserves to be studied in itself; it is priceless; its phrases should all be impressed in the innermost part of the Christian thinker. We have just republished it once more at the end of our edition of the Prayers of the same doctor, in which his religious thought is condensed, and his soul is glimpsed.[1](part0018.xhtml#a1HW)

We first thought of commenting on the sixteen precepts, extracting from them everything that can be useful to recall to current men of study. In practice, this procedure seemed a bit limited to us; we preferred to proceed with more freedom; but the substance of this little volume remains entirely Thomistic; in it, one finds what in the sixteen precepts and in other places the master suggests regarding the conduct of the spirit.

This booklet does not intend to replace Fr. Gratry’s Sources, and it refers to many of them. The author has not forgotten, as undoubtedly many others have not forgotten, the enthusiasm of his twenties, when Father Gratry stimulated in him the fervor of knowledge.

In a time that so desperately needs light, we must frequently recall the conditions that allow us to acquire light and prepare its diffusion through works.

We will not address production itself here: it will be the subject of another work. But the spirit acts similarly when seeking to enrich itself and when making a prudent expense.

As we will mention later that this expense is in this case one of the means of acquisition, we cannot doubt the identity of the principles that, both in one and in the other, make our intellectual activity fruitful.

And it is a reason to have hope of being useful to everyone.

Chandolin, August 15, 1920.
