# *Missale Romanum* — sigla `misal` (tracker #92)

One work in three languages (Alex, 27–28 August 2026): the Latin *editio typica tertia*, the Spanish of the Conferencia Episcopal Española, and the English of England and Wales. The Latin is the original. Every language carries the Latin file's anchors, `misal.<latin-celebration-slug>.<part-of-the-mass>`; a celebration that exists in one language's book and not another's (the calendars differ, and several celebrations on one date are part of the design) has its anchor only where its text is, and the file says so. A celebration the 2002 book does not have at all (a particular calendar's saint, the Prelature's propers, a celebration added after 2002) has a Latin-style slug in the same space, declared in the Latin file's final section (EF-2, below).

## The Eucharistic Prayers (15 September 2026)

The Latin numbers the Ordo Missae's paragraphs 1–146 through Prayers I–IV (`misal.ordo-missae.83`–`123`) and each Prayer of the Appendix by its own run (`misal.prex-eucharistica-de-reconciliatione-ii.4`). Neither vernacular prints those numbers, and neither paragraphs its text as the Latin does, so a vernacular paragraph is put on a Latin number **only where its content shows it is that paragraph's**, never by its position.

- **The rule.** A vernacular unit runs from the paragraph whose opening renders the opening of a Latin numbered paragraph to the paragraph before the next such opening, and carries that number's anchor. A Latin paragraph whose content the vernacular spreads over several paragraphs is one unit under one anchor (the English 86, *Communicantes*: three paragraphs; the English 105 runs through its insert for Masses for the dead, as the Latin 105 does). A vernacular paragraph that renders two Latin paragraphs is not split: it carries the first anchor, and the second is declared inside it (101, *Hæc ergo dona*, inside 100 in both languages). A Latin paragraph the vernacular section does not print has no anchor there. The heading of a unit is the vernacular's own opening words, not the Latin number. A Prayer's own Latin heading anchor (the Appendix Prayers') goes on the vernacular Prayer's heading.
- **The evidence** (`ep-content` in the alignment TSVs): `99. Tools/missale/eucharistic_prayers.tsv` pairs each Latin unit's opening words with each language's, read in both texts (*Te igitur, clementissime Pater* = "To you, therefore, most merciful Father" = *Padre misericordioso, te pedimos humildemente*). The converters stop unless the Latin words are in that unit and in no other unit of the same Prayer, the vernacular words begin exactly one paragraph of that Prayer's section, the units follow the Latin order (the one declared exception: the English prints 115, the insert for Masses for the dead, before 114, the doxology), and, in the English, every Latin incipit or line the book prints lies in the unit of the English beside it.
- **Order and grouping as printed.** The English Prayers are those of `Order_of_the_Mass.html`, after a shared opening (dialogue and Sanctus printed once for every Prayer, unanchored). The Spanish are decode record 0074, in its order: I–IV, the four for various needs, the two of Reconciliation; the rubric the record prints over the Reconciliation Prayers stays under the heading of the first, before its unit 1, where the Latin prints it.

## Why not odtake

odtake's PDF route reads paragraphs by shape and mints chapter and section anchors; the Missal's unit is the celebration and the part of the Mass, which only the book's typography carries. For the English and the Spanish, odtake would anchor each source by its own chapters or records, where the task is to put their texts under the Latin's anchors with evidence. The full reasons are in `99. Tools/missale/README.md`.
