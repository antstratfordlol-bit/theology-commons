# Doctors of the Church — the register

Hand-written map of the corpus. Begun 2026-08-25 (tracker #70), on Alex's instruction: *all* the Doctors of the Church as a source folder, with the writings of each pulled from the internet — **in English and Spanish, and in Latin where the original is Latin** — taking only what is not already held elsewhere in the library.

**There are 38 Doctors.** John Henry Newman was proclaimed the 38th by Leo XIV on 1 November 2025 (All Saints; vatican.va homily `20251101-messa-giubileo-formatori`), and named co-patron, with Aquinas, of the Church's educational mission. Verified 2026-08-25.

## What this folder is, and is not

- **One copy of a source.** Fifteen doctors already live elsewhere in this library and are **not duplicated here**: the fourteen patristic doctors held in `Library/fathers/` (the ANF/NPNF set, 2,841 files, English), with critical Greek and Latin texts for some in `Library/fathers/critical/` (10 Greek, 31 Latin files) and Spanish excerpts of seventeen doctors in the anonymous anthology `Library/fathers/padres-de-la-iglesia/` (translator unknown — read its README before citing), and **Aquinas**, held trilingually in `Library/aquinas/` (190 files: 78 en, 35 es, 75 la, 2 la-en). **Peter Damian's** two MGH works sit in `Library/latin-west/`. **Augustine's Spanish *Ciudad de Dios*** is the one exception filed here (`augustinus-hipponensis/`, 14 Sep): the Spanish was held nowhere, and the file shares the Latin's anchors whatever folder it sits in. This folder holds the doctors, and the works, the library did not have. The table below says where everyone lives.
- **Folder names** are kebab-case (#56), the author's name in his or her principal language, Latin for pre-modern Latin/Greek authors on the pattern of the Latin pope directories: `anselmus-cantuariensis/`, `teresa-de-jesus/`, `john-henry-newman/`.
- **File names** carry the work's own title in its original language with the language suffix: `proslogion.en.md`, `camino-de-perfeccion.es.md`.
- **Relation to #54, which landed on 25 Aug (od-6c).** `Library/` is now split: `aquinas/`, `fathers/` (the two Fathers folders merged, the critical Greek and Latin editions inside as `fathers/critical/`), `encyclopedia/`, this folder, and the loose books by kind. Nothing of this corpus moved. The one open question it leaves is whether this folder's patristic additions — Cyril of Alexandria above all, who is absent from the Schaff set entirely — should later sit under `fathers/` per author instead; they are left here, since the doctors are a class of their own and the register is what makes either arrangement findable.
- **Licence.** Tranche 1 is CCEL ThML: public-domain translations by age, CCEL's proofing and markup granted for "personal, educational, or non-profit purposes", and every extracted file says so in its frontmatter, as in `Fathers/`.

## The tranches

- **Tranche 1 — CCEL, English (2026-08-25, this session) — LANDED.** 30 work-ids fetched as ThML XML by `99. Tools/patristics/fetch_ccel.py` (extended to take `author/book` ids; same politeness: serial, 12 s, identifying UA), cached durably at `~/Library/Caches/od-knowledgebase/ccel/xml/`. Of the 30: **4 were untranscribed page-image shells** (the two Cyril of Alexandria volumes, `damascus/icons`, `newman/apologia` — the ANF10 failure mode, caught by a body-text count before extraction), **1 duplicated a held text** (`ephraim/pearl` = NPNF-213 file 17), **1 was an overlapping older edition** (`anselm/meditations`), and `lawrence/practice` had already been excluded at selection as Brother Lawrence, not Lawrence of Brindisi. The remaining **24 books produced 31 files — 30 works, one of them (the Alphonsus *Uniformità*) in both English and its Italian original with aligned language-invariant anchors** — extracted by `99. Tools/doctors/extract_ccel_doctors.py`, every file within 0.97–1.03 character parity of its source (worst 1.0036 high, 0.9841 low), no markup leaks, no mojibake, translator apparatus excluded and declared per file in `omitted`. Division counts match the works' canonical structure where known: *Moradas* 7 mansions + 27 chapters, *Noche oscura* 14 + 25, *Monologion* 80, *Camino de perfección* 42, Dialogues II 38. Taylor's *Story of a Soul* was split into its component works: the *Histoire d'une âme* itself, *Conseils et souvenirs*, the *Lettres* and the *Prières*, with its Selected Poems left to the fuller dedicated *Poésies* volume. Sigla: 30, registered in `sigla.md`, all checked free by `check_sigla.py`.
- **Tranche 2 — the originals, and what the shells left missing (2026-08-25) — LANDED.** Three sources, each checked before use and each politely fetched by `99. Tools/doctors/fetch_doctors.py` (serial, 4–5 s, identifying user agent, durable cache):
  - **tertullian.org** (Roger Pearse's Additional Fathers; transcriptions placed in the public domain): the whole of **Cyril of Alexandria** in English — the commentaries on *Luke* (156 sermons, Payne Smith) and *John* (12 books, Pusey/Randell), *Adversus Nestorium*, *Quod unus sit Christus*, the *Scholia de incarnatione*, the fragments against Diodore, Theodore and the Synousiasts, and *Contra Iulianum* (partial: the address and Book II are all that is translated there, declared in the file) — and **Gregory the Great's complete *Dialogi*** (4 books, 148 chapters, Gardner 1911), which supersedes the single-book CCEL copy taken in tranche 1.
  - **newmanreader.org**: Newman's ***Apologia pro vita sua*** in the definitive **1865** recension, with the Preface, Notes A–G, Additional Notes and Supplemental Matter — replacing the CCEL page-image shell.
  - **The Spanish originals.** For Teresa, John of the Cross and John of Ávila the Spanish is the language of composition, so these are the primary texts of the corpus: **Teresa** — *Vida* (40 chapters), *Camino de perfección* (42), *Castillo interior* (7 dwellings, 26 numbered chapters), *Fundaciones* (29); **John of the Cross, the complete works** — *Subida* (3 books, 91 chapters), *Noche oscura* (2 books, 39), *Cántico espiritual* in both redactions plus the poem alone, *Llama de amor viva* in both, *Dichos de luz y amor*, *Cautelas*, *Grados de perfección*, the *Epistolario* (33 letters), the five collections of *Avisos*, the *Poesías*; and **John of Ávila's *Audi, filia*** (112 chapters) — doctor #28, whose only holding this is.

  **Anchors follow each work's own citation form**, which for all the Spanish works is chapter-and-paragraph: `juan-noche.1.9.2` is the second paragraph of the "three signs" chapter, `teresa-moradas.5.1.3` a paragraph of the Fifth Dwellings. Because sigla are language-invariant, the tranche-1 English and the tranche-2 Spanish of the same work **share one sigla**: `juan-subida.2.14` now resolves in both languages, and the Spanish adds the paragraph level beneath it.

  **Findings recorded rather than smoothed.** (a) *Audi, filia* is filed by Documenta Catholica Omnia under "1542-1591 Ioannes a Cruce"; it is **not John of the Cross's** — the volumes' own title page reads SAN JUAN DE ÁVILA — and it is filed here under its true author. (b) **Neither Spanish witness of the *Moradas* is complete**: the DCO transcription carries Sextas 1–7 and stops, mercaba's pages carry Sextas 5–11 and lack 1–4. The file is built from **both**, in that order, so the overlap is dropped and the union is the whole book; the single chapter of the Segundas Moradas is unnumbered in both witnesses and is therefore not anchored at chapter level (26 numbered chapters against a canonical 27). (c) mercaba's *Vida* needed two further pages (`vida_12`, `vida_13`) for chapters 38–40, which its own index does not link. (d) The *Cántico* prints each stanza twice — as the poem and as the commentary heading — and only the capitalised commentary headings are anchored (37 of 40 in CB, 36 of 39 in CA: the source does not mark the rest, and no number was invented). (e) The *Llama*'s commentary sections carry **no** heading at all; their stanza numbers are inferred from the restarts of the paragraph numbering and are labelled as inferred in the file.

Both are recorded in the tool.
