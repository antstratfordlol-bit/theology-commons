The first [heresy] is that of Simon the magician. This one, in his magical operations, appeared to the Jews in the form of the Father, to the Samaritans as Son, and to the Gentiles as Spirit, and he denied the resurrection of the dead. - 2nd Of Menander. This one was a disciple of Simon the magician, and he said to those who were baptized by him that they were more excellent than the angels. - 3rd Of Saturninus. This one said that seven angels made the world; it is to them that God said: Let us make man in our image and in our likeness, and it is they who bore the law. He said that marriage comes from the Wicked One. As the demons lent (their) aid to evil men, the Savior came to help the good. - 4th Of Basilides. This one began in the heresy of the serpent-worshippers who are named Gnostics and he placed 365 heavens according to the days of the year. He said openly that the unclean law must be fulfilled.

If it is not the light but the darkness that is the cause of his lie, the darkness will turn out to be a cause of justice, which is a good, namely that he says "I have sinned" when he has not sinned, which is absurd. - We will say that this argument, although it strengthens our opinion and overturns the adverse opinion, does not please us, because it is sophistic, seeing that the Manichaeans could answer and say that the creator of sin is the darkness and that confessing one's sin with justice is light; therefore we say that the first answer is the principal one. Fifth heresy. Of certain Muslims. Two parts. - First part. Their arguments of reason. - First objection. They say that God - glory to His grace


[p. 269] {#po.13.p269}
- has never been able to do the works that man can do, because man in all his actions serves either God or the world, that is to say either justice or sin; as God does not have another God whom he can please and as he cannot please, through the accomplishment of his desires, this despicable and material world; because of that, it is accurate, they say, that God cannot do the actions that man does in his freedom. It is therefore man (by himself alone) who does everything he does, whether good or bad, and divine Providence cannot do anything by his hands. And a man, confirming this objection for himself, said the very words of Our Lord: "A servant cannot serve two masters"; consequently God, since he has no other lord, cannot do what the slave, that is to say man, does. - Second objection. They say that everything man does, whether good or bad, he does by his freedom. If Providence also did the things he does, this action, if it proceeded from Providence, would annihilate freedom; but since we know with certain knowledge that we have the freedom that does voluntarily what is chosen, we know with certain knowledge that it is not Providence that does anything by our hands. - Third objection. They say that God cannot do what man can do, because many of man's actions are bad and God does not do evil, because he who does evil does it either without knowing it, or because he needs it, namely for the appeasement of passions, and he derives profit from it; but God, whose knowledge has no limit, who is accomplished in everything and needs nothing, cannot do evil, and one concludes from there that he cannot do everything that man does, but that it is the freedom of man that does the actions of man. One of our own, reinforcing this objection, cited the word of the prophet Habakkuk who says: "Your eyes are too pure to see evil and you cannot look upon iniquity."
