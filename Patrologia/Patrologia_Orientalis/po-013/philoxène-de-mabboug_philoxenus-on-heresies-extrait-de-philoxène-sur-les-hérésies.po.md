XVII. - Explanation of all the heresies by Mar Xenaias (Acsénaïa). Manes, Marcion and Eutyches deny that the Word God took a body from the holy Virgin Mary, and they regard the mystery of his incorporation as hallucinations and (as) an illusion (φαντασία). They say that the Word God passed through the Virgin as through a canal without taking anything from Mary. Valentinus and Bardesanes say that the Word had a body brought down from heaven and that the incarnation * of the Word did not take place from Mary. Apollinaris regarded the incarnation of God the Word as without (human) intelligence. Eunomius said that the Word had taken from Mary only the body and that he did not take the soul, but he says that the divinity takes the place of the soul. Diodore, Theodore, Theodoret, Nestorius, Irenaeus, Eutherius (of Tyana) and Barsauma (of Nisibis) say that Christ was a simple human being: a man who was justified by his good works; God loved him and dwelt in him

They say that this one died and that the one who dwelt in him raised him, and they divide him into two sons and into two natures and into two hypostases, (one) of God the creator and the other of the man created and formed. Arius, for his part, says that the Son of God is a creature. Paul of Samosata says that Christ is a simple man, like one of the prophets and the righteous. Then the addition that was made at Chalcedon speaks of a quaternity and introduces two Christs after the union. The Jews say that this Christ who came and whom the Christians adore is a lying and deceitful man, for while being a man he makes himself God [as for the true Christ, of whom they say sometimes that he will come] they have in view the Antichrist, the lying Christ.
