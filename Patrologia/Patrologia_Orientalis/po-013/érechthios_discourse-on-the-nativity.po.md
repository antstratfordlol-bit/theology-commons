B. - On Saint Erechthios, Bishop of Tarsus. On the Nativity (B: Bishop of Antioch in Pisidia. Homily on the Nativity of Our Savior). Virgin full of grace, I will never cease to name you Mother of God, for behold, God is born of you, Savior and Life-giver, Creator of heaven and earth, of the sea and the continent, and of all that is seen and unseen; who is born of his Father before the worlds and above time, forever and from always, without beginning and without end. But at the end, for the life and salvation of men, he humbled himself and was man entirely, without departing from his first honor and without being deprived of the glory of divinity. Being God, he did not make the form of a servant which he took deceptive; but he remained in what he was and he was what he willed. Have pity on me, O only-begotten Son, and do not let me worship another in another, God and man; but only one: King and only-begotten Son, immortal God who appeared among men as man. Glory to you, Our Lord! Your mercies are unlimited, your graces are ineffable, your Providence is inaccessible; who will be able to recount your wonders? Your Providence, Lord, is distinguished; it is for this that it is difficult for renegades to believe that God became man for men, that the Invisible was seen on earth and lived with men, and, concerning the body which became corrupt: The Word became body and dwelt among us. Because of the infidels, He who gives aid dwelt with the captives; He, the liberator of captives, and the sun of justice shone for those who were sitting in darkness. Add also something else if you wish: The redemption of those who are in need, the food given to the hungry, the light in the darkness, the deliverance of captives, the resurrection of the dead, the healing of the sick, the eyes opened (to the light), the conversion of the strayed, the search for those who are lost, the life returned to the dead, the resurrection of the deceased; and all these wonders, that is to say these graces, are the stumbling block of the renegades and the life of the faithful.

The heretics ask again: 'How was the unlimited God limited in the womb of the Virgin? How did the fearsome divinity dwell in the flesh of the Virgin without the Virgin suffering from it?' I will answer them: 'How did God dwell in the bush, and the fire burned in the bush and the bush was not consumed?'
