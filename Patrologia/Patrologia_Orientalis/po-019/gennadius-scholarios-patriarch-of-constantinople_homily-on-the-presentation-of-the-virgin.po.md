GEORGE SCHOLARIOS' ALLOCUTORY SERMON ON THE FEAST OF THE ENTRY IN THE MONASTERY [OF THE MOTHER OF GOD] FIRST READ, IN THE PRESENCE OF EMPEROR CONSTANTINE WITH THE ENTIRE SENATE; AFTERWARDS IT WAS PUBLISHED. 1. Those men who belong to the common people and are weak in spirit, and are not yet able to rise to the contemplation of the Word, think human happiness consists either in bringing forth good people, or in being born from good people, not holding this as a probable sign of virtue, but considering this same thing the limit and end of happiness. For we leave those who are poured out entirely toward matter, who opine that riches and pleasures and human honors are the happiness of man, and who proclaim those who enjoy these to satiety as blessed. For these not only admire things that do not exist in the place of those that subsist, but furthermore they subvert human society.

Τὰ αὐτὰ δέ φησι καὶ τοῖς ἀπαγγείλασιν ἔξω τὴν μητέρα καὶ τοὺς ἀδελφοὺς ἑστῶτας ἰδεῖν αὐτὸν βούλεσθαι.
For since it is difficult both to acquire and to possess such things with virtue, they consume their life in wickedness, and turn everything upside down, and dare everything to satisfy their concupiscence. To the more approved, however, as I said, the kinship of the best is a certain great incentive to emulation, and they think this is almost the greatest of all things in human life, inasmuch as they think lowly and humbly of human honor. The word of truth, however, explaining congratulation more clearly, and describing the nature of those things which are truly worthy of praise, says briefly: Blessed, it says, are those who hear the word of God, and keep it. The same is said by him who had announced that his mother and brothers were standing outside and wishing to see him.

For since that woman and those who reported these things seemed to say the same thing and to be moved by the same thoughts, our Lord also gives the same response, and similarly raises and perfects both in the knowledge of true and genuine happiness. For she proclaims the Mother of God blessed on account of consanguinity; but these, venerating kinship, announce to Jesus his mother and brothers, and attempt to introduce them, and to honor him by the entry. But he answered them more sharply, because they seemed to be drawing him away from his purpose, and moving him from the word of doctrine, hoping that he would grant them greater favor on account of their benevolence and reverence toward his relatives.

Therefore, he neither denies there that she who bore him is blessed, nor does he refuse their kinship here; but that word is more fitting to him who, while he concedes and agrees to these things, judges those who hear and keep or do his word to be more blessed and more kin. For kinship according to nature, he says, is not sufficient, unless there be similarity according to purpose. He does not, however, keep the mother away from genuine kinship, nor exclude her from true congratulation, nor does he prefer those who were present to her; for how could this be, when she exercised every human virtue, both before and after this birth? But instructing their infantile sense, he vindicates this praise for the mother at the same time, as if he were saying: "She is more blessed for this very reason, because she both heard and kept the word of God." For this very reason she is also more kin, just as truly to Martha:

O truly blessed Virgin on account of both, both by will and by nature the mother of God; for now I will turn the whole discourse to you. O woman most pure in soul and body, not only the flower of all women, but also of the whole of nature, the most pleasant flower among flowers, because you were made the root and principle of those predestined to salvation, just as the first woman was made and remains of the reprobate. For on account of her, infidelity and discord from God, and the darkness of souls, and blindnesses and passions of the flesh entered into human life; but from you, for those who had and have a good purpose, came the illumination of the mind and purification, and the chastity of the flesh, and the certain and firm hope of eternal life.

Then, therefore, hearing and doing the word of God, we shall be deemed worthy of kinship with God, doubly conformed to him, on the one hand by the fervor of the zeal which is according to him, and obedience to his laws; on the other hand, as if by a sacramental communion of his passions. We will no longer care for the clay-like nobility of the world that is introduced, which is quickly dissolved, or, to say it better, the horrible disgrace. How many are the nations of the earth, in which [that Rome] has reveled, repelling the glory which comes from virtues! Moreover, we are kin to God in another way because of the proportions divinely infused into nature, by which we are related to him in some way, those I say which are in our souls. Why was it necessary to cling so fervently to clay-like kinship and relation? We shall be blessed, not merely called so, when with such illustrious pledges of beatitude we shall ascend to heaven from there, where our true beatitude is, namely, the one God in the Trinity; and according to the species, not in a riddle, to be with him. To him be glory for ages. Amen.
