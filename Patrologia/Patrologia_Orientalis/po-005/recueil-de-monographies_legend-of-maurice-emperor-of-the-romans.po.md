Revolt of Phocas. - Two days later, a general of the Romans, named Phocas, revolted against him; he incited the Romans with (these) words: "Our emperor is weak and soft, he does not know how to govern the empire; you see with your own eyes that the enemies have taken a large part of our provinces because of his negligence * and his weakness. If we do not awaken and seek to save ourselves, we have nothing left but to perish entirely. As long as Maurice or one of his children lives, the empire of the Romans will be without strength and the enemies will not be subdued. All the Romans answered with a single voice and said to him: "Do what you want, we are with you."

When Maurice had made known his young son and the one who was nursing him, some Romans ran to look for the woman and the child; they brought him before the emperor and before his enemies and they said to him: "Teach us if this is your son?" Maurice looked and knew that it was the son of this woman. He recognized her love, blessed her, and said to her: "May God reward you in my place, for having had the thought of delivering your son to death in place of mine." Then this woman, as if the Romans had misinformed her, gave them the following excuse: "The messengers arrived at my place in great tumult, I came without knowing what it was about; send therefore with me and I will give the son of the emperor." She brought therefore finally this child and the emperor testified and said: "This one is my son * in truth." The Romans did not even have pity on this one and killed him upon his brothers.

10. Then Phocas and the nobles had a boat brought and arranged wood in it. They poured naphtha on the wood, then brought Maurice, his hands tied behind his back, and placed him in the middle of the ship. They set fire to the wood all around Maurice and abandoned the ship on the sea. There was a great fire. The fire increased behind him and burned the bonds that tied his hands; he raised them to heaven and he gave thanks to God in a loud voice that He had judged him worthy of this grace. 11. The inhabitants of Constantinople stood in their dwellings and heard the praises that Maurice (addressed to God) in a loud voice. It is thus that he ended his life and that his combat had a happy outcome. End of the history of Maurice, * emperor of the Romans, and continuous glory to God! Amen.
