As the reign of Sapor dragged on, and the persecution still raged against the Christians, and they had lived continually in hiding, the faithful of many countries, in such great numbers that God alone could count them, gathered together, hoping with confidence that the support of God would descend upon them against Sapor, so that they could finally show themselves. The pontificate of Barba'semin had lasted twelve years. In his time, the three fathers whom Sahdost had ordained were arrested. They were thrown into prison for some time and made to suffer. Then, in the time of Barba'semin, they were delivered from it with the help of God. They took flight and went to live in caves. Since then, there was no news of them. But in the region they inhabited, they applied themselves to Christianizing a considerable number of pagans; they broke an idol there that the crowd worshipped; and they died in their retreats. Later, a large monastery and a school were built there in their name, where masters and students gathered. In the time of Mar Narsai, these constructions were further enlarged.

Qardagh lived in the time of Barba'semin. He was counted among the great personages of Persia. || was famous for his bravery. Seeing his manly soul, his courage, and his skill in archery, Sapor invested him with the dignity of governor of the country that extends from Beit-Garmai to Nisibis. * || fixed his residence at Arbel. The Christians feared him greatly. He built himself a fortified castle on a high hill and called it by his name. He was then twenty-five years old. But God - may He be praised and exalted! - wanted to make him His choice. One night, Qardagh saw in a dream a handsome man who struck his side with a lance and said to him: "You will be killed for the love of Christ before finishing the construction of this citadel." Qardagh said to him: "Who are you, you who foretell this to me?" And the man replied: "I am George the martyr, disciple of Christ and master of all the Greek country. I come to inform you of what Our Lord has made known to me." Qardagh awoke frightened and paid no more attention to this vision. Now, on the mountain of Beit-Bagae lived a hermit by the name of 'Abdiso'.

Although he was the grandson of Constantine the Great, he had nevertheless revolted against him. He threw off the yoke of obedience, denied his faith, and served the idols, to which he offered sacrifices. He gathered the magicians and sorcerers who decided him to abandon the true belief to devote himself to their impious practices. He had an interview at Ephesus with the two saints Athanasius of Caesarea and Gregory of Nazianzus, while he was studying philosophy. XLIX. - History of Flavian (Flavianous) and his disciple Diodore (Diodourous). This saint was Greek. When Julian (Lelianous) forbade Christians the study of philosophy, he disobeyed him and sacrificed himself (for science). Diodore was at first a disciple of the learned and virtuous Silvanus (Selouanous), bishop of Tarsus. He studied philosophy thoroughly with him and acquired such a profound knowledge of it that no one could defeat him in controversies. After the death of Silvanus, Diodore remained with Flavian, became a religious, walked in the footsteps of his new master, lived with him, and was inspired by his science. He went to Antioch to fight some Arians there, entered into discussion with them, and unveiled the perversity of their doctrine. He showed the error of Eunomius, a rebel against the truth, who denied a soul to the Word incarnate of Mary, claiming that God did not need a soul. This perverse opinion stemmed from the doctrine of Arius. But Diodore unveiled the error of this heresiarch, whom the king drove out with his master.

LX. - History of Rabban Mar 'Abda of Dair-Qoni. This saint was a native of Dair-Qoni. His mother was of bad conduct. It is a thing bordering on the miraculous that a debauched woman should give birth to a saint! It was the same with Jephthah, through whom God delivered the Israelites from Ammon. From his birth, Mar 'Abda was thrown into the church by his mother. The Christians took care of him and raised him. He pursued excellent studies at the school of his village. Ordained a priest, he built a large monastery and a universal school, to which people came from all parts; for, in that time, there was no other monastery in the land of the Arameans (An-Nabat). This work prospered; about sixty learned men, among others Ahai and Yahballaha the Catholicos, illustrated it. Mar 'Abda became very famous for his purity. He did not cease to travel the country, baptizing and converting the Arameans to Christianity. LXIII. - History of Qayoma, the thirteenth Catholicos. At the death of the Catholicos Toumarsa, Qayoma succeeded him in the ninth year of Bahram Farmansah. He was a man advanced in age. Despite his old age and the weakness of his body, he did not cease to administer the affairs of his flock until the advent of Yazdegerd the Impious. He then chose Isaac (Ishaq), a relative of Toumarsa, to be Catholicos in his place. And, in concert with the Fathers and the faithful, he left the administration to him. Qayoma kept his cell and said: "As God restored tranquility to his subjects and removed exile and persecution from them, it is necessary to entrust the direction of their affairs to a man who is not old, capable of directing and governing them as is fitting." Then Qayoma died in the third year of Yazdegerd.
