### HOMILY VII
ܡܐܡܖܪ̈ܐ ܕܫܒܥܐ]. ܕܩܕܝܫܐ ܣܐܘܝܪ̈ܘܣ ܦܛܪ̈ܝܪ̈ܟܝܣ ܡܐܡܪ̈ܐ ܥܠ ܒܝܬ ܝܠܕܐ ܐܘܟܝܬ ܒܝܬ ܕܢܚܐ. ܐܬܐܡܖܪ̈ ܕܝܢ ܒܬܪ ܗܠܝܢ ܒܕܘܟܪ̈ܢܗ ܕܡܪܝ ܣܛܦܢܘܣ ܡܛܠ ܗܕܐ ܐܦ ܥܠܘܗܝ ܕܣܗܕܐ ܗܼܘ ܫܘܠܡܗ ܕܡܐܡܪ̈ܐ ܡܛܟܘ݀
From the Patriarch Saint Severus, homily on the Nativity, that is to say the Manifestation. It is spoken then of the commemoration of Mar Stephen: this is why the end of the homily also treats of the martyr.
ܟܕ ܢܨܝܚܐ ܚܳܙܐ ܐܢܐܘ ܠܟܢܘܫܝܐ ܕܝܠܟܘܢ ܚܿܕܐ ܐܢܐ ܡܶܢ ܘܪ̈ܘ̈ܙ ܐܢܐ ܒܢܦܫܝ. ܠܐ ܕܝܢ ܝ̇ܕܥ ܐܢܐ ܒܪܡ ܐܢ ܐܢܫ ܡܢܟܘܢ. ܗܒܘ ܠܝܓܝܪ ܕܥܡ ܫܘܒܩܢܐ ܐܿܡܪ. ܬܪܥܝܬܐ ܕܫܘ̈ܝܐ ܢܣܼܒ. ܠܚܓܐ ܗܢܐ ܕܩܪ̈ܝܒ. ܐܝܕܐ ܗܺܝ ܓܝܪ̈ ܬܪ̈ܥܝܬܐ ܕܗܟܢܐ ܪ̈ܘܝܚܐ. ܘܡܢ ܥܡܛܢܐ ܕܚ̈ܫܐ ܕܟܝܐ. ܘܕܟܕ ܒܝܕ ܬܐܘܪ̈ܝܐ ܡܡܫܚܐܝܬ ܟܝܬ ܠܡܬܩ̇ܪ̈ܒܘ ܡܫܟܚܐ ܠܘܬ ܗܠܝܢ ܠܐ ܡܬܕܪ̈ܟܢܝܬܐ. ܬܨܒܐ ܡܶܢ ܫܘܝܐܝܬ. ܬܬܡܨܐ ܕܝܢ ܟܕ ܬܨܒܐ ܕܬܬܢܗܖ ܒܠܚܘܕ ܒܬܕܡܘܪ̈ܬܐ ܕܝܘܡܐ ܗܢܐ ܕܩ̇ܐܡ. ܘܕܚܕ
1 At the sight of your illustrious assembly, I rejoice and exult within myself. But I truly do not know if anyone among you—allow me to express myself by (recourse to your) indulgence—has received a spirit that is worthy of the present solemnity. For what spirit is vast enough and free enough from the clouds of passions to be capable, through contemplation, of approaching these inaccessible subjects as is fitting, of having a sufficient desire for them, and of being susceptible, even if it has this desire, to be enlightened only by the wonder of this day which is dawning, and to receive even a single ray of the Nativity of Christ according to the flesh?
ܙܠܝܩܐ ܬܩ̇ܒܠ ܕܝܠܝܕܘܬܐ ܕܒܒܣܪ ܕܡܫܝܚܐ. ܠܘܬ ܓܝܪ ܟܠܝܗ̇ ܡܕܒܪ̈ܢܘܬܐ. ܘܐܦܠܐ ܠܡܠܐ̈ܟܐ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇ ܡܬܡܨܝܢܝܬܐ ܠܡܥܚܪ̈. ܫܒܘܩ ܠܢ ܕܝܠܢ. ܠܗܿܢܘܢ ܕܥܡ ܛܝܢܐ ܐܣܝܪ̈ܝܢܢ. ܕܗܘ ܓܝܪ ܕܠܐ ܙܒܢܐ ܘܕܠܐ ܫܘܪ̈ܝܐ ܘܠܐ ܒܪ̈ܝܐ ܡܥܕܥܕܝܢܢ ܥܐܕܐ ܕܒܝܬ ܝܠܕܐ ܗܺܝ ܕܐܦ ܬܡܝܗܐ. ܕܗܼܘ ܟܕ ܗܼܘ ܐܦ ܡܬܚܙܝܢܘܬ ܐܠܗܐ ܡܫܬܿܡܗ. ܐܝܟܢܐ ܕܐܡܬܚ ܕܢܒܥܐ ܠܥܠܬܐ ܗܺܝ ܕܡܛܠܬܗ̇ ܗܘܼܐ ܗ̇ܘ ܕܠܐ ܗܘ̈ܝܐ ܟܕܓܝܪ ܡܠܬܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܗܘܐ ܗܘܐ ܒܣܪܐ ܟܕ ܘܠܐ ܠܗ̇ܺܝ ܕܢܗܘܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܡܠܬܐ ܫܚܠܦ ܘܐܦ ܠܗ̇ܝ ܕܢܗܘܐ ܒܪܢܫܐ
2 Indeed, from the point of view of the entire Economy, it is not even possible for the angels—much less for us who are mired in the mud—to consider that, in honor of Him who is timeless, without beginning, and uncreated, we celebrate the feast of the Nativity, and—a thing even more surprising—that the same feast is called the Apparition of God: to the point that I dare to ask the reason for which He who does not become has become. For, while He is the Word, He became flesh, without changing from what He was, the Word, and He assumed in addition this fact of becoming man.
ܐܘܣܦ ܢܣܼܒ. ܕ ܢܐܠܦ ܡܢ ܗܠܝܢ ܬܪ̈ܝܢ ܫܡ̈ܗܐ. ܕܡܛܠ ܗܕܐ ܠܝܠܝܕܘܬܐ ܕܒܒܤܣܪ ܐܬܪ̈ܡܝ. ܐܝܟܢܐ ܕܐܠܗܐ ܠܒܢܝ̈ܢܫܐ ܢܬܚܙܐ. ܐܐܝܟܢܐ ܕܒܕܘܟ ܩ̇ܕܡ ܗܘܐ ܡܟܪ̈ܙ ܢܒܝܐ ܐܠܗܝܐ ܕܘܝܕ ܟܕ ܐܡ̇ܪ. ܐܠܗܐܓܠܝܐܝܬ ܢܐܬܐ. ܐܠܗܐ ܕܝܠܢ ܘܠܐ ܢܫܬܘܩ. ܗ̇ܝ ܕܝܢ ܕܐܠܗܐܓܠܝܐܝܬ ܢܐܬܐ. ܡܥܚܘܝܐ ܢܗܝܪ̈ܐܝܬ. ܕܗܘ ܕܠܐ ܡܟ̣ܢ ܠܡܬܚܙܝܘ. ܠܐܠܗܐ ܓܝܪ ܘܠܐ ܐܢܫ ܡܬܘܡ ܘ ܚܼܙܐ ܐܝܟ ܒܪܬ ܩܠܐ ܕܝܘܚܢܢ. ܠܥܝ̈ܢܐ ܦܓܪ̈ܢܝܬܐ ܢܗܘܐ ܡܬܚܿܙܝܢܐ. ܗܺܝ ܕܝܢ ܕܠܐ ܢܫܬܘܩ. ܗܕܐ ܡܘܕܥܐ. ܕܟܝܢܐ ܡܶܢ ܐܢܫܝܐ ܘܓܢܝܣܐ ܕܝܠܢ. ܒܝܫܐܝܬ ܣ̈ܥܪܪ ܗܘܐ. ܘܠܘܬ ܩܪ̈ܩܦܬܐ ܖܪ̈ܡܬܐ ܕܒܝܫܘܬܐ ܢܦܸܩ ܢܦ̣ܠ. ܘܐܦܠܐ ܚܕ ܐܕܫܐ ܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܀ ܕܣܥܘܪ̈ܘܬܐ ܫܟܝܪܬܐ. ܗܿܘ ܕܠܐ ܐܪܟܢ ܠܗ ܨܘܪܗ.
3 Let us learn by these two names why He lowered Himself to the nativity according to the flesh: it is so that God might appear to men, as the divine prophet David had predicted somewhere by saying: God will come visibly, our God, and He will not be silent. These words: God will come visibly, show clearly that He who cannot be seen—for God, no one has ever seen, according to the word of John—became visible to bodily eyes. And these words: He will not be silent, indicate that human nature and our race were acting badly and had come to fall into deep abysses of malice, and that there was not even a sort of impious action that did not make it lower its head to the point of bowing

ܐܠܗܐ ܕܝܢ ܡܠܬܬܐ ܟܕ ܕܫܿܘܝܢ ܐܝܿܬܝܢ ܗܘܝܢ ܠܗܿܝ ܕܢܬܒܣܐ ܪ ܘܢܫܬܼܬ ܩ ܘܕܐܦܠܐ ܟܠܟܠܗ ܡܢ ܒܛܝܠܘܬܐ ܕܝܠܗ ܢܣܬܥܖ̈ ܡܛܠ ܠܐ ܡܬܬܟܝܠܢܘܬܐ ܗܺܝ ܕܒܝܫ̈ܬܢ ܟܕ ܐܙܕܟܝ ܡܢ ܒܣܝܡܘܬܐ ܗ̇ܝ ܕܡܟܿܢܐ ܒܗ. ܠܐ ܐܗܡܝ ܡܢܢ ܘܠܐ ܫܬܩ. ܐܠܐ ܐܬܼܐܓܠܝܐܝܬ. ܘܬܿܪܺܝܨ ܠܟܝܢܐ ܗ̇ܘ ܕܐܬܟܪܗ.
4 But God the Word, although we had come to deserve to be despised and reduced to silence, so that no more sign of His providence might be manifested, because our malice had made trust disappear, (God the Word), nevertheless dominating by this sweetness which dwells in Him, did not turn away from us and did not keep silence, but He came visibly and healed the nature which was sick.

5 Now He came and descended to us, not by passing from one place to another—how, indeed, (could He) who is present everywhere, who fills the Universe and who is not contained by anything, but rather who Himself contains everything—but, while He is entirely in God and in the Father, He is the same entirely in the womb of the Virgin, and He also consented to be grasped, while by His nature He remains ungraspable.

6 As for the 'how', speech cannot explain it: indeed those who preached the gospel transmitted the wonder, but they were not charged with indicating the 'how'. For He who said: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God, also said this: The Word became flesh, and dwelt among us. Now, after having until here attested His proclamation by these words, not having the means to explain the mode of it, in its place, He proposed faith, and faith, not that which is without words nor even that which sticks to words, but that which (passes) into acts and which is confirmed by the brilliance of actions. What did He say indeed? We have seen His glory, glory as of the only (Son) who (is) from the Father, who is full of grace and truth, as if He were saying: This fact that the Word became flesh without change is the sign and the manifest and very clear proof for us that with our eyes we have seen His glory and that we have seen Him accomplish wonders worthy of God and that He has healed the diseases of our nature.
ܘܢܚܼܙܝܘܗܝ ܕܦܐܝܘܬ ܐܠܗܐܝܬ ܡ̇ܥܒܕ ܬܕܡܪ̈ܬܐ. ܘܠܟܘܪ̈ܗܢܐ ܕܟܝܢܐ ܕܝܢ ܡܬ̇ܪܨ. ܟܕ ܓܝܪ ܒܝܡܐ ܘܒܪ̈ܘܚܐ ܟܳܐܐ ܗܘܐ ܘܠܣܛܘ̈ܟܝܐ ܐ ܡܫ̇ܚܠܦ ܡܠܚܡ ܗܘܐ ܒܨܒܝܢܐ ܒܠܚܘܕ ܘܠܣܡܝܐ ܕܡܢ ܝܠܝܕܘܬܗ ܒܒ̈ܝܬܐ ܕܥܝ̈ܢܐ ܝܗ̇ܒ ܗܘܐ ܗܠܝܢ ܕܠܐ ܫܩܼܠ ܡܢ ܟܝܢܐ. ܡܥܿܘܐ ܗܘܐ ܗܼܘ ܠܗ ܕܥܒ̈ܘܕܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܕܗܢܐ ܟܠ ܘܡܪܐ ܕܟܝܢܐ ܘܐܠܗܐ ܕܫܪܖ̈ܪ̈ܐ »ܐ ܘܕܡܛܠ ܪܚܡܬ ܐܢܫܘܬܐ ܠܐ ܡܫܬ̇ܚܠܦܢܐܝܬ ܗܘܼܐ ܒܪ̈ܢܫܐ. ܘܠܘ ܗܺܝ ܕܠܩܘܒܠܐ. ܕܟܕ ܒܪ̈ܢܫܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ. ܘܡܛܠ ܬܖ̈ܒܝܬܐ ܕܡܝܬܪ̈ܘܬܐ. ܐܝܟ ܫܛܘܪ̈ܘܬܐ ܕܢܣܛܘܪ̈ܝܘܣ ܠܐܝܩܪ̈ܐ ܕܐܠܗܘܬܐ ܫܘܪ ܐܬܥ̇ܠܥ ܝ ܐܟܙܢܐ ܕܐܠܗ̈ܐ ܗܢܘܢ ܕܡܬ̈ܠܐ ܕܚ̈ܢܦܐ ܓܿܒܠܝܢ.
7 Indeed, when He rebuked the sea and the winds, when He transformed the elements by His sole will, and when He gave the man blind from birth the pupils for his eyes without taking them from nature, He showed Himself as the author of this Universe, the Lord of nature and God, in truth, become man without change thanks to His charity; and not the opposite, according to the ineptitudes of Nestorius, namely that being man and having grown in perfection, He would have leaped up to the honors of divinity, in the manner of the gods that the myths of the pagans forged.

ܡܙܥܩ ܓܝܪ ܐܘܢܓܠܝܣܛܐ. ܕܚܙܝܢ ܫܘܒܚܗ. ܫܘܒܚܐ ܐܝܟ ܕܝܚܝܕܝܐ ܕܡܢ ܐܒܐ. ܕܡܠܐ ܛܝܒܘܬܐ ܘܫܪܪܐ. ܝܚܝܕܝܐ ܕܝܢ. ܒܐܘܣܝܐ ܘܒܟܝܢܐ ܗܘ ܕܐܒܐ ܐܝܬ ܠܗ ܫܘܒܚܐ ܗܘ ܒܗ. ܘܠܐ ܗܘܐ ܐܝܟ ܚܕ ܡܢ ܒܢܝܢܫܐ ܙܕܝܩܐ ܡܫܘܬܦܐܝܬ ܘܐܝܟ ܕܒܡܢܬܐ ܕܛܝܒܘܬܐ ܘܕܡܘܗܒܬܐ ܥܬܪ ܣܘܥܪܢܐ ܡܬܩܢܝܢܐ. ܘܕܦܐܐ ܗܘܐ ܠܐܠܗܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܗܘܐ ܗܘ ܫܘܒܚܐ. ܡܠܝܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܗܘܐ ܡܢ ܛܝܒܘܬܐ ܘܫܪܪܐ. ܕܐܠܗܐ ܓܝܪ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇ ܗܘܬ ܫܪܝܪܐܝܬ. ܗܝ ܕܗܠܝܢ ܕܐܝܟ ܗܟܢ ܢܫܟܢ. ܘܕܠܐ ܡܬܥܗܕܐ ܒܝܫܬܐ ܢܗܘܐ ܠܘܬܢ. ܘܢܦܪܘܩ ܠܢ ܠܗܢܘܢ
8 For the evangelist cries out: We have seen His glory, glory as of the only (Son) who (is) from the Father, who is full of grace and truth. Now the only (Son), it is in the essence and nature of the Father that He has the glory in Himself, and not like one of the just men, by participation and as if by a part of grace and gift, that He enriched Himself with a reality that can be acquired. Now, it is because the glory befitted God that He was full of grace and truth. For it was to God that it truly belonged to grant such grace, not to remember our malice for us, and to save us, we who had sinned and had become ungrateful.

23 That is why, rewarding him because of his imitation so faithful and so exact, it is very close to his (feast) that (God) caused the memory of the day and the feast of the Nativity to be made; that to us also, who are so far away because of our sins—for our sins, as the prophet says, make a separation between us and him—that this (memory) may obtain for us all to be very close to him, once purified by penance, since he can do everything, and for him there is nothing impossible. To him befits praise, glory, and power, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen!
