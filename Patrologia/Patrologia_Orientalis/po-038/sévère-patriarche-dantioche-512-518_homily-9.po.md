# Homily 9

### HOMILY IX
ܡܐܡܖܪܐ ܕܬܫܟܥܐ ܥܠ ܒܣܝܠܝܘܣ ܘܓܪ̈ܝܓܘܪܝܘܣ ܐܦܝܣܩ̈ܘܦܐ. ܕܐܬܐܡܪ ܒܗܝܟܠܐ ܕܩܕܝܫܐ ܐܝܓܢܐܛܝܘܣ.
On Basil and Gregory, bishops. It was pronounced in the sanctuary of Saint Ignatius.
ܐܝܟ ܙܢܐ ܕܢܒܝܐ ܕܘܝܕ ܛܘܒܬܢܝܬܐ ܦܣܩ ܕܐܝܬܝܗ̇ ܗܺܝ ܕܒܢܡܘܣܐ ܕܡܪܝܐ ܢܬܗܓܐ ܐܝܡܡܐ ܘܠܠܝܐ ܆ ܗܟܢܐ ܠܝ ܕܝܬܝܪ ܛܘܒܬܢܝܬܐ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇ ܡܬܚܙܝܐ ܇ ܗ̇ܝ ܕܥܘܗ̈ܕܢܐ ܕܗܢܘܢ ܕܫܦܝܪ ܚܝܘ ܒܥܠܡܐ ܗܢܐ ܒܟܠ ܚܕܐ ܫܥܬܐ ܐܝܟ ܕܠܡܐܡܪ ܢܬܝܐ ܒܬܪ̈ܥܝܬܐ ܇ ܘܢܨܚ̈ܢܐ ܕܗܢܘܢ ܕܚܠܦ ܫܦܝܪܘܬ ܕܚܠܬܐ ܐܬܟܬܫܘ ܇ ܡܠܠܝܢ ܕܝܢ ܐܦ ܗܕܐ ܠܗܢܘܢ ܕܚܬܝܬܐܝܬ ܡܬܚܫܒܝܢ ܆ ܕܢܬܗܓܐ ܒܢܡܘܣܐ ܕܡܪܝܐ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇. ܗܘܓܝܪ ܕܒܚ̈ܝܐ ܕܗܢܘܢ ܕܢܡܘܣܐܝܬ ܐܬܕܒܪܘ ܡܬܕܡܪ ܆ ܟܣܝܐܝܬ ܡܢ ܆ ܝܬܝܪܐܝܬ ܡܪܝܐܝܬ ܕܝܢ ܇ ܘܐܦ ܒܢܡܘܣܐ ܘܐܦ ܒܗ ܒܣܐܡ ܢܡܘܣܐ
1 Just as the prophet David considered it a happiness for him to meditate day and night in the law of the Lord, so, and even more so, it is a happiness for me, it seems to me, to present to your minds, so to speak at every hour, the memories of those who have had a beautiful life in this world, and the triumphs of those who have fought for piety; or rather, for those who reflect seriously, this is also meditating in the law of the Lord. For he who admires the life of those who have conducted themselves according to the Law, it is on the one hand in a figurative manner, and on the other hand very literally, that he has admired both the Law and the Lawgiver himself.
ܐܬܕܡܪ. ܠܡܢܘ ܗܟܝܠ ܢܫܟܚ ܇ ܕܝܬܝܪ ܡܢ ܒܣܝܠܝܘܣ ܗ̇ܘ ܪܒܐ ܘܓܪ̈ܝܓܘܪܝܘܣ ܗ̇ܘ ܡܡܠܠ ܐܠܗ̈ܝܬܐ ܇ ܕܢܡܘܣܐܝܬ ܚܝܘ ܘ ܇ ܘܠܢܡܘܣܐ ܘܠܣܐܡ ܢܡܘܣܐ ܡܙܡܢܝܢ ܫܘܒܚܐ ܡܢ ܥܘܗܕܢܗܘܢ ܀ ܘܠܢ ܗܢܘܢ ܕܡܬܥܗܕܝܢ ܠܗܘܢ ܝܘܬܪܢܐ. ܗܠܝܢ ܓܝܪ ܗܠܝܢ ܐܝܟ ܕܒܫܪ̈ܪܐ ܐܝܬܝܗܘܢ ܗܘܘ ܪ̈ܥܘܬܐ ܡܠܝܠܐ ܕܡܪܥܝܬܐ ܡܠܝܠܬܐ ܇ ܘܪ̈ܒܝ ܒ̈ܬܐ ܕܪ̈ܐܙܐ ܕܪ̈ܘܚܐ ܇ ܘܡܫ̈ܡܫܢܐ ܫܪ̈ܝܪ̈ܐ ܕܐܠܗܐ ܘܡܠܬܐ ܘܕܡܛܠܬܢ ܪܝܫ ܟܗ̈ܢܐ ܡܫܝܚܐ ܇ ܗ̇ܘ ܕܐܪ̈ܟܢ ܫܡܝܐ ܘܥܕܡܐ ܠܗܕܐ ܢܚܬ ܇ ܕܢܣܒ ܠܐ ܡܫܬܚܠܦܢܐܝܬ ܕܡܓܘܬܐ ܕܥܒܕܐ ܇ ܘܕܢܗܘܐ ܒܪ ܐܢܫܐ ܇ ܘܠܐ ܢܫܚܠܦ ܠܗ̇ܘ ܡܐ ܕܐܝܬܘܗܝ
2 Whom shall we find then who, better than Basil the Great and Gregory the Theologian, having lived according to the Law, procure, for the Law and the Lawgiver, glory by their memory, and, for us who remember them, profit? Those men, indeed, those men were truly spiritual pastors of a spiritual flock, dispensers of the mysteries of the Spirit and true ministers of God the Word and of Christ the high priest for us—he who bowed the heavens and descended to the point of taking without change the form of a slave and of becoming man without changing from what he was, and who, after his voluntary emptying, returned to heaven in a more sublime manner.
ܗܘܐ ܇ ܘܬܘܒ ܥܒܪ ܠܫܡܝܐ ܡܢ ܒܬܪ ܣܘܪ̈ܩܐ ܗ̇ܘ܀ ܨܒܝܢܝܐ ܝܬܝܪ ܡܪܡܪܡܐܝܬ ܀ ܡܛܠܗܕܐ ܓܝܪ ܐܦ ܚܝ̈ܠܘܬܐ ܡ̈ܠܐܟܝܐ ܟܕ ܡܢ ܒܬܪ ܫܘܠܡܐ ܕܡܕܒܪ̈ܢܘܬܐ ܦܢܐ ܗܘܐ ܘ ܣܠܩ ܠܐܦ̈ܝ ܠܥܠ ܦܓܪ̈ܢܐܝܬ ܘ ܟܕ ܡܢܩܪܝܢ ܟܝܬ ܘܡܠܘܝܢ ܆ ܠܘ ܒܠܚܘܕ ܐܡܪ̈ܝܢ ܗܘܘ ܠܪ̈ܘܚܬܐ ܡܬܗ̈ܘܢܢܝܬܐ ܘܒܢ̈ܬ ܬܫܡܫܬܐ ܕܝܠܗܘܢ ܐܪܝܡܘ ܬܪ̈ܥܐ ܪ̈ܝܫܢܝܐ ܕܝܠܟܘܢ ܐܠܐ ܡܘܣܦܝܢ ܗܘܘ ܆ ܐܦ ܗܺܝ ܕܐܬܬܪ̈ܝܡܘ ܬܪ̈ܥܐ ܕܡܢ ܥܠܡ. ܗܺܝ ܕܝܢ ܕܐܬܬܪܝܡܘ܆ ܠܬܫܒܘܚܬܐ ܕܝܬܝܪ ܪܡܐ ܡܝܥܢܘܝܐ. ܠܘ ܓܝܪ ܗܼܘ ܝܬܝܪ ܡܢ ܢܦܫܗ ܗܼܘܐ ܕܝܬܝܪ ܪܡ ܟܕ ܡܢܥܝܐܓܝܪ ܢܘܣܦ ܐܢܫ ܠܗ̇ܘ ܕܡܢ ܫܘܪ̈ܝܐ ܘܒܐܘܣܝܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܡܫܡܠܝܐ ܇ ܢܦܣܘܩ ܕܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܝܬܝܪ ܡܫܡܠܝܐ ܆ ܐܠܐ ܐܢ ܕܩ ܕܗ̇ܝ ܕܫܪܝܪ̈ܐ ܢܐܡܪ ܆ ܒܬܪ̈ܥܝܬܐ ܕܝܠܢ ܆ ܘܒܟܠܗ̇ ܒܪܝܬܐ ܡܠܝܠܬܐ ܇
3 That is why, when after the end of the Economy he was ascending toward the heights with his body, the angelic armies who honored him and accompanied him, were not content to say to the spiritual spirits and to their co-ministers: Lift up your gates, princes, but added also these words: Lift yourselves up, eternal gates; now these words: Lift yourselves up, indicate a more sublime praise. For he did not become higher than he is in himself—what could one indeed add to him who, from the beginning and by essence, is perfect; would one estimate that he has become more perfect?—But to tell the truth, it is in our minds and in all the rational creation that he has come to be more sublime, after that in ourselves


[p. 338] {#po.38.p338}
ܗܼܘܐ ܕܝܬܝܪ ܪܡ ܇ ܟܕ ܒܢ ܟܝܬ ܘܒܚܝ̈ܠܘܬܐ ܕܠܐܓܫܘܡ ܣܡ ܠܝܕܥܬܐ ܗܝ ܕܡܛܠܬܗ ܇ ܝܬܝܪ ܪܡܬܐ ܘܝܬܝܪ ܡܫܡܠܝܬܐ ܀
and in the incorporeal armies, he gave a knowledge of himself more sublime and more perfect.
ܗܝܓܝܪ ܕܐܬܬܪܝܡܘ ܬܪ̈ܥܐ ܕܡܢ ܥܠܡ ܆ ܡܛܠ ܕ ܬܪ̈ܥܐ ܫܡ̈ܝܢܐ ܐܝܟ ܣܘܟܠܐ ܡܢ ܒܪ̈ܝܐ ܘܐ ܘܕܩܕܡ ܐܝ̈ܕܝܐ ܐܡܝܪ̈ܐ ܆ ܠܘܬ ܬܪ̈ܥܝܬܐ ܕܝܢ ܡܝܬܪ̈ܬܐ ܒܪܡ ܡܥܠܝܐ ܠܗ̇ܘ ܕܫܡܥ. ܕܟܠܗ ܓܝܪ ܟܝܢܐ ܡܠܝܠܐ ܦܬܚܐ ܠܬܪ̈ܥܐ ܡܬܗ̈ܘܢܢܐ ܘܡܪܝܡܐ ܘܐܝܟ ܗܘ ܕܡܪܡܪܡܐ ܇ ܠܘܬ ܗܺܝ ܕܢܩܒܠܘܢ ܠܗ̇ܘ ܪܡܐ ܇ ܘܕܢܬܪܥܘܢ ܡܛܠܬܗ ܇ ܕܝܬܝܪ ܦܐܝܢ
4 Indeed these words: Lift yourselves up, eternal gates, I apply on the one hand to the heavenly gates according to a literal and obvious sense, but on the other hand they elevate him who hears them to a higher thought. For, it is to all rational nature that it opens and elevates the intellectual gates, so that they may receive him who is higher, given that he is sublime, and that concerning him they may think what is more worthy of God and more sublime.
ܠܐܠܗܐ ܟܝܬ ܘܕܝܬܝܪ ܪ̈ܡܢ ܀ ܐܠܐ ܠܐ ܝܕܥ ܐܢܐ ܡܢܐ ܚܫܬ. ܟܕܓܝܪ ܠܘܬ ܡܠܐ ܡܩ̈ܠܣܢܝܬܐ ܕܡ̈ܠܦܢܝܐ ܕܥܕܬܐ ܦܢܐ ܗܘܝܬ ܆ ܠܐ ܝܕܥ ܐܢܐ ܐܝܟܢܐ ܠܘܬ ܡܡܠܠܘܬ ܐܠܗܘܬܐ ܥܒܪܬ ܢܦܠܬ ܇ ܘܠܘܬ ܪ̈ܐܙܐ ܪ̈ܒܐ ܕܡܕܒܪ̈ܢܘܬܐ. ܒܪܡ ܐܦܢ ܠܐ ܨܒܝܢܐܝܬ ܠܗܕܐ ܐܬܬܝܬܝܬ ܆ ܡܦܣ ܐܢܐ ܆ ܕܠܘ ܡܢ ܨܒܘܬ ܢܦܫܝ ܆ ܐܠܐ ܡܢ ܡܬܬܙܝܥܢܘܬܐ ܐܠܗܝܬܐ ܕܡܘܕܥܐ ܓܠܝܐܝܬ ܇ ܕܐܦ ܗܝ ܗܺܝ ܕܢܬܥܗܕ ܒܠܚܘܕ ܠܒܣܝܠܝܘܣ ܘܠܓܪ̈ܝܓܘܪܝܘܣ ܇ ܡܡܠܠ ܐܠܗ̈ܝܬܐ ܡܫܡܠܝܐ ܠܗ ܠܗ̇ܘ ܕܡܬܥܗܕ ܠܗܘܢ .ܐܟܙܢܐ ܕܐܦ ܠܗܢܘܢ ܕܠܢܘܪܐ ܡܬܩܪ̈ܒܝܢ ܇ ܟܠܗ̇ ܐܢܢܩܐ ܕܢܫܬܘܬܦܘܢ ܒܫܚܝܢܘܬܐ ܟܝܬ ܘܢܗܝܪ̈ܘܬܐ ܕܡܢܗ̇. ܒܪܡ ܕܝܢ ܗܺܝ ܨܒܝܐ ܗܘܬ ܡܠܬܐ ܠܡܐܡܪ ܆ ܕܟܕ ܒܡܟܝܟܘܬܐ
5 I do not know what I felt: when indeed I prepared myself to praise the doctors of the Church, I do not know how, I came to end up at Theology and the great mystery of the Economy. However, even if I was led there involuntarily, I know that it is not of myself, but by a divine movement, which indicates clearly that the simple fact of remembering Basil and Gregory the Theologian brings to perfection the man who makes memory of them; just as it is quite necessary that those who approach the fire receive a share in truth of the heat and the light that (come) from it. This is however what the word meant to say, namely that after they imitated the humility of our God and Savior Jesus Christ, they arrived at the summit of perfection.
ܕܐܠܗܐ ܘܦܪ̈ܘܩܐ ܕܝܠܢ ܝܫܘܥ ܡܫܝܚܐ ܐܬܕܡܝܘ ܗܠܝܢ ܇ ܠܘܬ ܪܘܡܐ ܕܡܝܬܪ̈ܘܬܐ ܐܬܘ ܀ ܟܕܓܝܪ ܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܠܗܘܢ ܢܨܝܚܘܬܐ ܗ̇ܝ ܕܡܢܓܢܣܐ ܆ ܘܒܥܘܬܪܐ ܥܒܝܛܝܢ ܗܘܘ ܇ ܘܟܠܗ̇ ܚܟܡܬܐ ܕܚܟܝ̈ܡܐ ܗܢܘܢ ܕܡܢ ܠܒܪ̈ ܐܬܥܢܘܝܘ ܡܘܫ̈ܚܬܐ ܕܓܪܡܛܝܩܝ ܘܐ ܘܫܦܝܪ̈ܘܬ ܡܠܚܡܘܬܐ ܟܝܬ ܘܫܦܝܪ̈ܘܬ ܛܘܗܡܐ ܕܡܠܬܐ ܘܪܘܪܒ ܡ̈ܠܐ ܇ ܘܡܦܝܣܢܘܬܐ ܕܪ̈ܝܛܘܪܝܩܝ ܘܪ̈ܝܫܝܘܬܐ ܘܣܓܝܐܘܬ ܡܢܣܝܢܘܬܐ ܕܡܟܬܒܘܬ ܬܫ̈ܥܝܬܐ ܘܗܘܦܟ ܚܘܫ̈ܒܐ ܘܥܘܪ̈ܙܠܐ ܕܦܝܠܘܣܦܝܐ ܦܐ ܘܬܚ̈ܘܡܐ ܕܓܐܘܡܐܛܪܝܐ ܘܩܛܝܢܘܬܐ ܕܪ̈ܘܟܒܐ ܕܡܢ̈ܝܢܐ ܘܣܝܡܐ ܘܡܬܬܙܝܥܢܘܬܐ ܕܟܘ̈ܟܒܐ ܘܟܠܗܝܢ ܐܝܠܝܢ ܕܠܘܬ ܡܡܠܠܘܬ ܐܠܗܘܬܐ ܣܡܝܬܐ ܡܝܬܝܢ ܘܠܘܬ ܝܕܥܬܐ ܕܗܠܝܢ ܕܐܝܬܝܗܝܢ ܗܺܝ ܕܕܓܠܐܝܬ ܡܫ̈ܬܘܕܝܢ ܘܠܟܠܗܝܢ ܗܠܝܢ ܠܐ ܡܕܡ ܚܫܒܘ ܆ ܨܝ̈ܕܐ ܕܡܨܝܕܬܐ ܕܝܘܚܢܢ ܘܕܦܛܪܘܣ ܗܼܘܘ ܇ ܕܗܢܘܢ
6 They had indeed the illustration of birth, riches in abundance, they had devoted themselves to all the wisdom of the profane sages, to the different meters of grammar, to the study of a chosen and distinguished language, to eloquent speeches, to persuasive rhetoric, to the narratives of ancient and well-founded history, to the dialectic of opinions, to philosophical enigmas, to geometric definitions, to the subtle combinations of numbers, to the position and movement of the stars, to everything that leads to a blind science of God and a falsely assured knowledge of things; however, they considered all that as nothing and fell into the nets of John and Peter, those fishermen and ignorant men, and they considered Paul, the tentmaker, as the doctor and guide of the divine words.
ܨܝ̈ܕܐ ܘܠܐ ܣܦܝܪ̈ܐ. ܘܠܦܘܠܘܣ ܥܒܕ ܡܫ̈ܟܢܐ ܆ ܡܠܦܢܐ ܘܡܗܕܝܢܐ ܕܡ̈ܠܐ ܐܠܗ̈ܝܬܐ ܩܢܘܗܝ ܀ ܘܟܕ ܝܕܥܘ ܕܡܬܬܘܕܝܢܐܝܬ ܪܒܐ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܪ̈ܐܙܐ ܕܫܦܝܪܘܬ ܕܚܠܬܐ ܘܫܡܥܘ ܠܡܘܫܐ ܕܐܡܪ ܫܡܥ ܐܝܣܪܐܝܠ ܡܪܝܐ ܐܠܗܟ ܡܪܝܐ ܚܕ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܘܝܕܥܘ ܒܝܕ ܗܕܐ ܘܒܝܕ ܗܠܝܢ ܕܐܟ ܗܟܢ ܠܚܕܐ ܡܪ̈ܘܬܐ ܘܐܠܗܘܬܐ ܕܒܬܠܬܐ ܩܢܘ̈ܡܐ ܇ ܕܐܒܐ ܘܕܒܪܐ ܘܕܪ̈ܘܚܐ ܩܕܝܫܐ ܆
7 After having learned that without contradiction the mystery of piety is great and having heard Moses say: Hear, O Israel, the Lord your God, the Lord is one, and after having, by these words and others similar, recognized the only Lordship and the divinity in three hypostases of the Father, of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, they rejected

the polytheism of the pagans; and as soon as they had read these words: In the beginning God made the heaven and the earth, they mocked the opinions of the philosophers, one saying: "Fire is the principle of this world," another: "It is water," a third: "It is another element."

22 Let us therefore flee vain glory, O friends and brothers, and at every hour let us know ourselves. For those who know themselves are wise, as the proverb says, and let us despise these honors of this world which pass away, and let us look toward those which are to come and which do not disappear. Behold indeed Basil and Gregory, those theologians: they considered these ephemeral honors and, judging that they were not worthy of the honor of Him in whom they had placed their faith, after having escaped them here below, they became bishops and doctors of the whole earth.

23 You see that again we are being robbed of our propensity for violence in words; and as we abandon praises, we turn to teaching a reform concerning the conduct of life and the knowledge of ourselves; but do not fear what is dear to those who receive praises and who believe that this praise is their own good, but that it leads to progress. May we deserve it, all of us and in all things, by the grace and charity of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ, to whom belongs glory with the Father and the Holy Spirit now and at all times and for ages of ages! Amen!
