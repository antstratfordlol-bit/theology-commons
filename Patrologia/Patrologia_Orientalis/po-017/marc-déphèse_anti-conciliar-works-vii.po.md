Why is it that we contend so much about this kind of innovation, which has cut and torn the body of Christ, and has hitherto separated those who are called his disciples by the dissension of opinions? What is this long and lasting quarrel and unfilial contempt of brothers, and the alienation of those who suffer scandal? And we indeed, while we say the same things as before, both conspire with ourselves and with our Fathers and with you also, if you were willing to speak the truth; but you, since you have adopted more recent things, must necessarily dissent first from yourselves, then from the common Fathers, and finally from us also. Nay, by the Trinity itself! nay, by the common hope, in which we have placed our trust and you have placed yours, do not permit us to depart without fruit and with the matter undone. We perform an embassy for Christ, as if God were exhorting through us; do not bring ignominy upon the embassy; do not render labors vain, do not make prayers fruitless, do not comply with the wishes of enemies; do not allow our common enemy and adversary to mock us as before; do not cause God and his Holy Spirit to be saddened. Every soul and every ear hangs suspended in expectation of your sentence. But if you incline of your own accord toward peace to remove scandals from the midst, already the affairs of Christians turn for the better, those of the impious for the worse, those who hate us tremble, and foresee their own destruction. But if (which God forbid!) the contrary happens, and the perverse custom of dissenting prevails to the convenience of all, I indeed can no longer proceed, disturbed by anguish; but God, who can do all things, may restore his Church, which he redeemed with his own blood, and may he himself cause that what he has willed be completed, as in heaven, so also on earth, since glory, honor, and adoration befit him for ever and ever, amen.
