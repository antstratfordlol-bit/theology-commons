LETTER OF MARK OF EPHESUS TO THEOPHANES THE PRIEST ON THE ISLAND OF EUBOEA. TO THE MOST RELIGIOUS HIEROMONK AND SPIRITUAL FATHER, AND TO MY MOST BELOVED AND VENERABLE LORD AND BROTHER IN CHRIST, LORD THEOPHANES, AT EURIPUS. 1. Most religious hieromonk and spiritual father, and my most beloved and honorable lord and brother in Christ, I pray God that your holiness may also be well in body: by whose holy prayers I myself, God having mercy, am doing well enough. You should know that, as soon as I returned to Constantinople, when one of those supporters of the Latins who subscribed had ascended to the patriarchal seat, and was causing me trouble, I went away to my church, compelled by necessity. In which, since I had no rest either, but was seized by a serious illness, and was vexed by many losses and inconveniences from the infidels, because I had not received a mandate from the prince, I departed from there also with the intention of betaking myself to the Holy Mountain. And so, having crossed the strait to Callipolis, while I was passing through Lemnos, I was seized there by the emperor's order and surrounded by custody. But the word of God and the power of the truth is not bound in any way, but rather is carried with a flowing and prosperous course. For many brothers, incited by my exile, assail those wicked men and violators of the sound faith and ancestral customs with all reproaches, and

I also discovered that a certain servant of the Monembasian had been ordained metropolitan of Athens by the followers of the Latins, who, dwelling there, performs sacred rites promiscuously with the Latins and ordains illicitly whomever and as many as he finds. Wherefore I ask your holiness, that, kindled with zeal for God, as befits both a man of God and a friend of the truth and a genuine disciple of the holy Isidore, you urge the priests of God to avoid his communion clearly and entirely, not to perform sacred rites with him, nor in any way to make commemoration of him, holding him not for a bishop, but for a wolf and a hireling; and let them not perform the sacred rite in the temples of the Latins, lest the wrath of God, which erupted against Constantinople because of the crimes that are perpetrated there, should also turn upon you. 3. Also receive that that false union is already on the point, God favoring by His grace and power, of being utterly dissolved, and that the doctrine of the Latins has not only not become stronger through the pseudo-synod, to which they always directed their efforts, but is even more vehemently refuted and convicted, so much so that it is proscribed everywhere as blasphemous and impious, and those who confirmed it do not even dare to open their mouth to defend it. Indeed, that patron of your hireling, not shepherd, I mean the stupid Monembasian, having obtained the prefecture of the Prodromos from the emperor, is not even commemorated by his monks while the sacred rites are performed, nor is he incensed in any way like the other faithful, but they have commerce with him only in expediting business, as if he were a pure consul. Informed of this matter, the emperor says nothing, nay, he openly confesses that he regrets the deed, casting the blame on those who submitted and subscribed. Beware, therefore, you yourselves, brothers, that you do not communicate with the excommunicated, nor commemorate men who are not to be commemorated at all.
