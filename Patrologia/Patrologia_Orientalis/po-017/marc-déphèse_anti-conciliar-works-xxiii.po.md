Therefore, I wish the patriarch to be warned, lest perhaps, under the guise of showing me honor, he decides to send some of his bishops or his clerics or any other of those who communicate with him to the funeral of this vile body of mine or to the performance of sacred rites in my memory, so that they may offer prayers or celebrate the sacred mysteries together with the priests of our party who are to be invited to perform these things, with the persuasion that I, in whatever way it may be, even in secret, admit his communion. But lest my silence provide an occasion for those who do not fully and thoroughly know my purpose to suspect some compromise, I say and testify before many most honorable men present here, that I neither approve nor accept his communion or that of his followers in any way at all, neither while I live nor after my death, just as I do not accept the union that has been accomplished nor the dogmas of the Latins, which he and his supporters have accepted, and by defending which he obtained this dignity for himself to the ruin of the sound dogmas of the Church. For I hold most certainly that the further I am separated from him and men of that kind, the closer I approach God and all the saints; and to the extent that I am divided from them, to that extent I am united to the truth and to the holy Fathers, the teachers of the Church: just as I am likewise persuaded that their followers are at the greatest distance from the truth and the blessed doctors of the Church.

I have known him since his early youth, and I follow him with affection and love, and I cherish him as my son and friend and if anyone can devise anything else to reconcile friendship and charity. Having conversed familiarly with him up to this point in time, I am most fully aware of how much prudence and wisdom he possesses, and how much power of speech. Wherefore I have no doubt that he alone, of those who survive at this time, is able to extend a helping hand to the right faith, which is tossed about by the fury of those who have corrupted the sincerity of the divine dogmas, so that the Church may be reformed, God helping, and the orthodox faith may be strengthened, provided that he himself is willing not to be wanting to the work, nor to hide his lamp under a bushel. Indeed, I do not believe that he would be so affected, nor that he would so fail his own conscience, as to see the Church swept away by a storm and the faith resting on a weak man—I speak in the human manner—and knowing that he has what he might provide in its aid, not to prepare a defense with all zeal and great eagerness of spirit. Now, however, with God's help, I have already despised all these things, and I have established myself as the most sincere and most open defender of the truth, so that, by yielding nothing of the dogmas of my fathers and the truth of the orthodox faith, I may preach according to the purpose of your great holiness. I do not confess these things, however, because I see your great holiness departing from here: for we have not laid aside all hope, but rather, trusting in God, we believe that you, recovering from this illness, will still be with us and will carry out all these things together.
