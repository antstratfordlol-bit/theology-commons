# Book of the Laws of Countries (Liber legum regionum)

BOOK OF THE LAWS OF THE REGIONS
ܡܶܢ ܩܕ݂ܳܡ ܝܰܘ̈ܡܳܬ݂ܳܐ ܥܳܠܬܢ ܗ̱ܘܰܝܢ ܠܡܶܣܥܰܪ ܠܫܶܡܰܫܓܪܳܡ ܐܰܚܘܽܢ: ܘܐܶܬ݁ܳܐ ܐܶܫܟܚܰܝܢ ܬܰܡܳܢ ܒܰܪܕ݁ܰܝܨܳܢ. ܘܟܰܝܕ݂ ܓܰܫܶܗ ܘܰܚܙܳܐ ܕܫܰܦܝ݁ܪ ܥܒܝܼܕ: ܫܰܐܠܰܢ ܕܡܳܢܳܐ ܡܡܰܠ̱ܠܝܢ ܗ̱ܘܰܝܬܘܽܢ܆ ܫܶܡܥܶܬ݂ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܩܳܠܟܘܽܢ ܡܶܢ ܠܒܰܪ ܟ݁ܰܕ݂ ܥܳܐܶܠ ܐ̱ܢܳܐ. ܡܥܳܕ݂ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܕܐܶܡܰܬܝ ܕ݁ܡܶܫܟ݁ܰܚ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܠܰܢ ܕܰܡܡܰܠ̱ܠܝܢ ܗ̱ܘܰܝܢ ܡܶܕ݁ܶܡ ܡܶܢ ܩܕ݂ܳܡܰܘܗ̱ܝ: ܕܰܢܫܰܐܠܰܢ ܡܳܢܳܐ ܐܳܡܪܝܺܢ ܗ̱ܘܰܝܬܘܽܢ܆ ܕܰܥܠܰܘܗ̱ܝ ܢܡܰܠܠ܆ ܥܰܡܰܢ. ܚܢܰܢ ܕ݁ܶܝܢ ܐܶܡܰܪܢ ܠܶܗ: ܥܰܘܝܺܕ݂ܳܐ ܠܰܡ ܗܳܢܐ ܐܳܡܰܪ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܠܰܢ: ܕܐܶܢ ܚܰܕ݂ ܗ̱ܘܽ ܐܰܠܳܗܳܐ: ܐܰܝܟܰܢܳܐ ܕܐܳܡܪܝܺܢ ܐܸܢܬܘܽܢ: ܘܗܽܘ ܐܳܟܝܢ ܠܰܒܢܰܝ̈ ܐ̱ܢܳܫܳܐ ܘܨܳܒ݂ܶܐ ܒܗܳܢܶܐ ܡܶܕܶܡ ܕܰܡܦܰܩܕܝܺܢ ܐܸܢܬܘܽܢ ܕܐܶܥܒܕܘܽܢ: ܡܶܛܽܠ ܡܳܢܳܐ ܠܳܐ܂ ܐܰܟܝܢ ܐܶܢܘܽܢ ܠܰܒܢܰܝ̈ ܐ̱ܢܳܫܳܐ: ܐܰܟܰܢܳܐ ܕܠܳܐ ܢܶܟܚܘܽܢ ܠܡܰܣܟܳܠܘܽ: ܐܶܠܳܐ ܕܰܒܟ݁ܽܠܙܒܰܢ ܕܛܳܒ ܗܘܰܘ ܥܳܒܕܝܺܢ܆ ܒܗܳܕ݂ܶܐ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܡܶܬܡܰܠܶܐ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܨܶܒܝܳܢܶܗ܀
1. A few days ago we had gone to visit our brother Semasgram: when he came and found us there, Bardesanes, who after he had tested him and saw that he was well, asked us: Of what matter were you speaking, for I heard your voice outside while I was entering. For he was accustomed, when he found us discussing some matter before him, to ask us: Of what matter were you speaking, so that he might confer about it with us. We said to him: Avida was saying to us with eagerness: If there is one God, as you have said, who himself created men wishing [you] to do that which you were commanded to do, why did he not create men so that they could not sin, but in all time would do what is good. In this indeed his will would have been fulfilled.

ܐܳܡܰܪ ܠܶܗ ܒܰܪܕ݁ܰܝܨܳܢ: ܐܶܡܰܪ ܠܝܺ ܒܶܪܝ ܥܰܘܝܺܕ݂ܳܐ: ܡܳܢܳܐ ܣܳܡ ܒܪܶܥܝܳܢܳܟ: ܕܠܳܐ ܗܘܳܐ ܚܰܕ݂ ܗ̱ܘܽ ܐܰܠܳܗܳܐ ܕܟ݂ܽܠ: ܐܰܘ ܕܚܰܕ݂ ܗ̱ܘܽ ܘܠܳܐ ܨܳܒܶܐ ܕܟܺܐܢܳܐܝܺܬ ܘܰܫܪܺܝܪܳܐܝܺܬ ܢܶܬܕܰܒܪܘܽܢ ܒܢܰܝ̈ ܐ̱ܢܳܫܳܐ܀ ܥܰܘܝܺܕ݂ܳܐ ܐܳܡܰܪ: ܐܶܢܳܐ ܡܳܪܝ ܠܗܳܠܶܝܢ ܗ̱ܘܽ ܒܢܰܝ̈ ܬ̈ܢܰܚܝܢ ܫܰܐܠܶܬ ܕܗܶܢܘܽܢ ܢܰܬܝܺܒܘܽܢܳܢܝ
2. Bardesanes says to him: Tell me, my son Avida, what is in your mind: either there is not one God of all, or there is one and he does not wish to govern men according to nature and order? / Avida says: I, Lord, asked these peers of mine in age to give me an answer.


[p. 538] {#po.0.p538}
ܦܶܬܓ݂ܳܡܳܐ܀ ܐܳܡܰܪ ܠܶܗ ܒܰܪܕ݂ܰܝܨܳ: ܐܶܢ ܬ݁ܰܐܠܰܦ ܗ̱ܘܽ ܨܳܒܶܝܬ: ܡܶܢ ܡܰܢ ܕܩܰܫܝܺܫ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܡܶܢܗܘܽܢ ܥܳܕ݂ܪܳܐ ܠܳܟ݂ ܨܒܘܽܬܳܐ ܕܬܰܐܠܰܦ: ܘܐܶܢ ܕܬܰܠܶܦ ܠܳܐ ܡܶܬܒܰܠܳܝܐ ܨܒܘܽܬܳܐ ܕܐ݇ܢ̱ܬ ܬܫܰܐܶܠ ܐܶܢܘܽܢ: ܐܶܠܳܐ ܕܐܰܦ݁ܺܝܣ ܠܗܘܢ ܕܗܳܢܘܽܢ ܢܫܰܐܠܘܽܢܳܟ݂ ܡܶܢ ܡܶܕ݁ܶܡ ܕܨܒ݂ܺܝ, ܡܰܠܦܳܢ̈ܶܐ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܡܶܫܬܰܐܰܠܘܽ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܡܶܫܬܰܐܠܝܺܢ: ܠܳܐ ܙܗܳܐ ܡܫܰܐܠܘܽ ܡܫܰܐܠܝܢ: ܘܐܶܡܰܬܝ ܕܰܡܫܰܐܠܝܢ ܕܰܢܬܰܪܨܘܽܢ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܪܶܥܝܢܳܐ ܕ݁ܰܡܫܰܐܠܳܢܳܐ ܕܫܰܦܝܪ ܢܫܰܐܶܠ: ܘܢܶܕ݂ܥܘܽܢ ܡܶܢܰܘ ܨܶܒܝܳܢܶܗ.
3. Bardesanes says to him: If you desire to learn, it is expedient for you to learn from someone older than these, but if you are teaching, it is not expedient for you to question them; but persuade them so that they themselves may question you about whatever matter they wish. For teachers when questioned are questioned, but they do not question, and if they sometimes question, [they do this] to direct the mind of the questioner, so that he may question well, and they may know what his intention is: for it is a good work that everyone should know the mode of questioning.


[p. 539] {#po.0.p539}
ܥܒܳܕ݂ܳܐ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܫܰܦܝܺܪܳܐ ܗܳܢܳܐ: ܕܢܶܚܕ݂ܰܥ ܐ݈ܢܳܫ ܐܝܟ݁ܰܢܳܐ ܢܫܰܐܰܠ܀ ܥܰܘܝܺܕ݂ܳܐ ܐܳܡܰܪ: ܐܶܢܳܐ ܠܡܺܐܠܰܦ ܗܘ ܨܳܒܶܐ ܐ݈ܢܐ: ܥܰܪܝܺܬ ܕܶܝܢ ܠܘܽܩܕ݂ܰܡ ܠܰܡܫܰܐܳܠܘܽ ܠܗܳܠܶܝܢ ܐܰܚܰܝ̈: ܡܶܛܽܠ ܕ݁ܡܶܢܳܟ ܢܳܟܶܦ ܐ̱ܢܳܐ܀ ܒܰܪܕ݁ܰܝܨܳ ܐܳܡܰܪ: ܐܶܣܟܺܡܬܳܢܳܐܝܺܬ ܡܡܰܠܶܠ ܐܰܢ̱ܬ. ܒܪܰܡ ܕ݁ܶܝܢ ܕܰܥ ܕܡܰܢ ܕܫܰܦܝ݁ܪ ܡܫܰܐܶܠ ܘܰܠܥܶܡܬܛܦܳܣܘܽ ܨܳܒܶܐ: ܘܰܕ݂ܠܳܐ ܚܶܪܝܳܢܳܐ ܡܶܬܩܰܪܰܒ݂ ܠܽܐܘܪ̈ܚܳܐ ܕܰܦܪܳܪܳܐ: ܠܳܐ ܬܫܒܰܚ ܘܡܶܬܢܟܶܝܦ: ܡܶܛܽܠ ܕܰܡܒܰܣܳܡܘܽ ܡܒܰܣܶܝܡ ܒ݁ܗܳܠܺܝܢ ܕܐܶܡܪܶܐ ܠܗܰܘ ܐܰܝܢܳܐ ܕܡܶܫܬܰܐܰܠ. ܐܶܢ ܗܘ ܗܳܟܝܶܠ ܒܶܪܝ: ܕܐܝܺܬ ܠܳܟ݂ ܡܶܝܕ݁ܶܡ ܒܪܶܥܝܢܳܟ݂ ܥܰܠ ܗܳܢܳܐ ܡܶܕ݁ܶܡ ܕܫܰܐܶܠܬ: ܐܶܡܰܪܳܝܗ̱ܝ ܠܟ݁ܽܠܰܢ܂ ܘܐܶܢ ܐܳܦ ܠܰܢ ܫܳܦܰܪ ܡܶܫܬܘܶܝܢܰܢ ܥܰܡܳܟ: ܘܐܶܢ ܠܰܢ ܠܳܐ ܫܳܦܰܪ: ܐܰܢܰܢܩܺܝܐ ܥܳܒ݂ܕ݂ܳܐ ܠܰܢ ܕܰܢܚܰܘܶܝܟ ܡܶܛܽܠ ܡܳܢܳܝܐ ܠܳܐ ܫܳܦ݁ܰܪ ܠܰܢ. ܘܐܶܢ ܗܳܕܶܐ ܗ̱ܘ ܡܶܠܬ݁ܳܐ ܒܰܠܚܘܽܕ݂ ܨܒܶܝܬ ܠܡܶܫܡܰܥ: ܟ݁ܰܕ ܒܪܶܥܝܢܳܟ ܡܶܕ݁ܶܡ ܠܰܝܬ ܠܳܟ݂ ܥܠܶܝܗ: ܐܰܝܟ݂ ܒ݁ܰܪܢܳܫܳܐ ܕܚܰܕ݂ܬܳܐܝܺܬ ܩܪܶܒ ܠܘܳܬ ܬܰܠܡܝ̈ܺܕܶܐ ܘܰܕܫܰܪܝܺܪܳܐܝܺܬ ܡܫܰܐܶܠ ܐܳܡܰܪ ܠܳܟ: ܐܰܝܟܰܢܳܐ ܕܰܣܪܝܺܩܳܐܝܺܬ ܠܳܐ ܬܶܦܪܰܩ ܡܶܢ ܠܘܳܬܰܢ: ܘܐܶܢ ܒܳܣܺܡܳܢ ܠܳܟ݂ ܐܰܝܠܶܝܢ ܕܐܶܡܰܪ ܐ̱ܢܳܐ ܠܳܟ݂ ܥܰܠ ܗܳܕ݁ܶܐ ܐܝܺܬ ܠܳܟ ܠܘܳܬܰܢ ܘܐܳܦ ܐ̱ܚܪܳܢܝܳܬܳܐ: ܘܐܶܢ ܠܳܐ ܒܳܣ̈ܡܳܢ ܠܳܟ: ܚܢܰܢ ܕܝܺܠܰܢ ܕܠܳܐ ܚܣܳܡ
4. Avida says: I desire to learn; I indeed began to question those brothers of mine because I was ashamed before you. Bardesanes says: You have spoken beautifully; know, however, that he who questions well wishing to be persuaded and who approaches the way of truth without strife is not to be condemned nor should he be ashamed, for it brings pleasure in these things that I have said to him who is questioned. If therefore, son, there is something in your mind about this which you have questioned, tell it to us all. And if it also pleases us, we will communicate with you; if, however, it does not please us, it will be necessary for us to show you why it did not please us. Even if you only wish to hear this discourse, when there is nothing about it in your mind, like a man who has very recently approached the disciples and who questions recently, I will say it to you, so that you may not separate yourself from us in vain; and if those things which I shall say to you about that matter are pleasant to you, there are also others with us for you; if, however, they are not pleasant, we will speak that which pertains to us without envy.


[p. 540] {#po.0.p540}
ܐܳܡܪܝܺܢܰܢ܀ ܐܳܡܰܪ ܥܰܘܝܺܕ݂ܳܐ ܐܳܦ ܐܶܢܳܐ ܛܳܒ ܐܳܪܰܥ ܐ̱ܢܳܐ ܕܐܶܫܡܰܥ ܘܐܶܬܛܦܝܰܣ ܡܶܛܽܠ ܗܳܕ݂ܶܐ ܡܶܠܬ݁ܳܐ ܠܳܐ ܙܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܡܶܢ ܐ̱ܢܳܫ ܐܚܪܝܺܢ ܫܡܰܥܬܳܗ: ܐܶܠܳܐ
5. Avida says: I also very much desire to hear so that I may be persuaded; for I have not heard this discourse from another, but I said these things from my own mind to these brothers of mine and they did not want to persuade me, but were saying: Believe by believing,


[p. 541] {#po.0.p541}
ܐܰܚܰܝ݁ ܘܠܳܐ ܨܒܰܘ ܠܰܡܦܳܣܘܽܬܳܢܝ: ܐܶܠܳܐ ܐܳܡܪܝܺܢ
and you will be able to know everything, but I cannot believe unless I am persuaded.
ܕܰܡܗܰܝܡܰܢ ܐܰܢ̱ܬ ܠܥܶܒܕܰܥ. ܘܐܶܢܳܐ ܠܳܐ ܡܶܫܟ݁ܰܚ ܐ̱ܢܳܐ ܠܰܡܿܗܰܝܡܳܢܘܽ ܐܶܠܳܐ ܐܶܢ ܐܶܬܛܦܝܣܶܬ܀ ° ܐܳܡܰܪ ܒܰܪܕ݁ܺܝܨܳܢ: ܠܳܐ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܥܰܘܝܺܕ݂ܳܐ ܒܰܠܚܘܽܕ݂ ܠܳܐ ܨܳܒ݂ܶܐ ܠܰܡܗܰܝܡܳܢܘܽ: ܐܶܠܳܐ ܐܳܦ ܣܰܓ̈ܺܝܶܐ. ܡܶܛܽܠ ܕܗܰܝܡܳܢܘܽܬܳܐ ܠܰܝܬ ܒܗܘܿܢ ܐܳܦ ܠܳܐ ܠܶܡܬܛܦܳܣܘܽ ܡܶܫܟܚܝܺܢ: ܐܶܠܳܐ ܒܟ݁ܽܠܙܒ݂ܰܢ ܣܳܬܪܝܺܢ ܘܒܳܢܶܝܢ: ܘܡܶܫܬ݁ܰܟ݂ܚܝܺܢ ܚܰܪ̈ܒܝܳܢ ܡܶܢ
6. Bardesanes says: Avida is not the only one who does not want to believe, but also many; because faith is not in them nor can they be persuaded, but at all times they withdraw and build [arguments] and are found bereft of all knowledge of truth.


[p. 542] {#po.0.p542}
ܟܽܠܳܗ ܚܶܕ݂ܰܥܬ݁ܳܐ ܕܰܫܪܳܪܳܐ. ܒܪܰܡ ܕ݁ܶܝܢ ܡܶܛܽܠ ܕܥܰܘܝܺܕ݂ܳܐ ܠܳܐ ܨܳܒ݂ܶܐ ܠܰܡܗܰܝܡܳܢܘ̇: ܗܳܐ ܐܳܡܰܪ ܐ̱ܢܳܐ ܠܟܘܽܢ ܕܰܡܗܰܝܡܢܝܺܢ ܐܢ̱ܬܘܽܢ ܥܰܠ ܗܳܢܶܝܢ
Nevertheless, because Avida does not want to believe, behold I will speak to you who believe about those things which he inquired, and he himself will hear something more.
ܡܶܕ݁ܶܡ ܕܫܰܐܶܠ: ܘܗܘ̈ ܡܶܕܶܡ ܝܰܬܝܰܪ ܢܰܫܡܰܥ܀ ܘܫܰܪܝܺ ܠܡܺܐܡܰܪ ܠܰܢ: ܣܰܓܝܺܐܝܺܢ ܐܶܢܘܽܢ ܒܢܰܝ̈ ܐ̱ܢܳܫܳܐ ܗܳܢܘܽܢ ܕܗܰܝܡܳܢܘܽܬ݁ܳܐ ܠܝܬ ܒܗܘܿܢ ܘܝܺܕ݂ܰܥܬܳܐ ܡܶܢ ܚܶܟܡܬ݁ܳܐ ܕܰܫܪܳܪܳܐ ܠܳܐ ܩܰܒܶܠܘ. ܘܡܶܛܽܠ ܗܳܢܳܝܐ ܠܳܐ ܠܡܺܐܡܰܪ ܘܰܠܡܰܝܠܳܠܘܽ ܣܳܦܩܝܺܢ: ܘܠܳܐ ܕܠܝܠܳܐܝܺܬ ܡܰܘܚܝܺܢ ܠܡܶܫܡܰܥ. ܠܳܐ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܫܶܬܐܳܣܬ݁ܳܐ ܕܗܰܝܡܳܢܘܽܬܳܐ ܐܝܺܬ ܠܗܘܽܢ ܕܢܶܒܢܘܽܢ ܥܠܶܝܗ: ܘܠܳܐ ܬܘܽܟܠܳܢܳܐ ܐܝܺܬ ܠܗܘܢ ܕܰܥܠܰܘܗ̱ܝ ܢܶܣܒܪܘܽܢ ܘܡܶܛܽܠ ܕܐܳܦ݂ ܥܰܠ ܐܰܠܳܗܳܐ ܡܶܬܦܰܠܳܓ݂ܘܽ ܡܶܬܦܰܠܓܝܺܢ: ܐܳܦ ܠܳܐ ܕ݁ܶܚܠܬܶܗ ܐܝܺܬ ܒܗܘܽܢ ܕܗܝ݇ ܡܦܰܪܩܳܐ ܠܗܘܽܢ ܡܶܢ ܟܽܠܗܶܝܢ ܕ݁ܶܚ̈ܠܳܬܳܐ. ܡܰܢ ܕܕ݂ܶܚܠܬܶܗ ܓܶܝܪ ܕܐܰܠܳܗܳܐ ܠܝܬ ܒܶܗ: ܡܫܰܥܒܰܕ݂ ܗ̱ܘ ܠܟ݁ܽܠܗܶܝܢ ܕ݁ܶܚ̈ܠܳܬ݁ܳܐ: ܐܳܦ ܠܳܐ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܗܰܘ ܡܶܕܶܡ ܡܶܕ݁ܶܡ ܕܠܳܐ ܡܗܰܝܡܢܝܺܢ ܝܳܕ݂ܥܝܺܢ ܕܫܰܦܝܰܪ ܠܳܐ ܡܗܰܝܡܢܝܺܢ: ܐܶܠܳܐ ܦܳܗܶܝܢ ܐܶܢܘܽܢ ܒܬܰܪ̈ܥܝܳܬܗܘܽܢ ܘܠܰܡܩܳܝܡ ܠܳܐ ܡܶܫܟܚܝܢ. ܘܦܰܟܝܗ ܒܦܘܽܡܗܘܽܢ
7T. And he began to say to us: There are many men in whom faith is not and they have not received knowledge from the wisdom of truth. Because of this they are not sufficient for speaking and defining, and they are not easily brought to hearing; for there is no foundation of faith for them upon which it may be built, and there is no hope for them upon which they may hope; and because they also doubt about God, there is not even the fear of Him which might snatch them from all fears; for he in whom the fear of God is not, is subject to all fears. Nor do they know those diverse things which they do not believe, that [they] do not believe them rightly; they err in their opinions and cannot remain in them. The taste of their thoughts is insipid in their mouth, wherefore at all times they fear, are moved, and become rebellious.

8. Concerning this which Avida says: Why did God not make us so that we would not sin nor condemn ourselves? If man had been made so, he would not be master of himself, but would be an instrument of him who would make him act. For as is evident, he who would move him, as he wished, he would move him either for good or for evil. How therefore would man differ from a lyre which another plays or from a chariot which another drives? But praise and blame are placed in the hand of the craftsman himself, and the lyre does not know that it is being played upon and it does not pertain to the chariot if it is driven and steered well; but they are instruments which were made for the use of him in whom there is intellect. God, however, in his goodness did not wish to create man so, but for the sake of liberty he enriched him with many things and equaled him to the angels. For observe the sun and the moon and the firmament and the remaining creatures which are greater than us in some thing, they were not given their own liberty, but all are fixed by a precept so that they may perform only that which is commanded and nothing else. If anyone says that it pertains to the nature of each one to act in this way, let him see that the matter is not so; for there are those who are unchaste and drunken, and when the discipline of good laws reaches them, they become honest and patient and despise the desires of their bodies. 20. Let us say, therefore, now and show concerning Fate that it is not ruling over all things. For that very thing which is called Fate is the order of the course which was given to the powers and elements by God, and, according to this course and this order, intelligences are changed when they come to the soul, and souls are changed when they come to bodies. That change is called Fate and the horoscope of this mixture which has been sifted and purified so that it might help those things which, through the clemency and grace of God, will be saved and are saved until the consummation of all things. Therefore, the body is led by nature while the soul suffers with it and perceives. Nor is the body compelled and not helped by Fate in all the things it does one after another. For a man does not become a father before fifteen years, and a woman is not a mother before thirteen years. So also for old age there is a law, so that women are deprived of birth and men are deprived of the power of generation. Animals which are also led by their nature do not beget before the years I have mentioned; but they also grow old and are unable to beget, just as also the bodies of men

21. But when the times and modes of nature are completed, then Fate comes, appearing among these and making things diverse from one another: sometimes it helps and strengthens nature, but sometimes it harms it and afflicts it with ignominy. From nature proceeds the growth and perfection of the body; but beyond nature, yet from Fate, come diseases and defects of the body. From nature proceeds the coitus of male and female, and the rest of the senses of both. From Fate proceed hatred and the denial of coitus and every pollution and abomination which men do under the pretext of coitus according to their concupiscence. From nature proceed generation and children; by Fate sometimes children perish, sometimes they are cast out, sometimes they perish before their time. From nature proceeds satiety sufficient for all bodies, and from Fate is the lack of food and the necessity of bodies, and likewise from Fate is gluttony and luxury which is not necessary. Nature commands the old to be judges to the young, and the wise to the foolish, and the powerful to be leaders to the weak, and the strenuous to the timid. Fate, however, makes it so that the young are leaders of the old, the foolish of the wise, and that in time of war the weak lead the strenuous and the cowardly the brave. Hold it for certain that as often as nature deflects from its rectitude, that deflection of it proceeds from Fate, because the leaders and governors from whom that change which is called the horoscope comes, are opposed among themselves. Those who are on the right are called helpers of nature and those who increase its beauty, as often as the course helps them and they stand in the high places which are in the sphere in their parts. 26. The laws of the Seres. — There are laws among the Seres not to kill, nor to commit fornication, nor to serve idols. And in the whole region of the Seres there are no idols, nor harlots, nor is there anyone who kills or is killed, even if they are born on any day and at any hour, and the terrible Mars does not hinder the liberty of the Seres, even if he is placed in the middle of the sky, so that a man might meet his companion with a sword of iron. And Venus in conjunction with Mars does not compel any of the Seres to be defiled with the wife of his companion or with another woman. Nevertheless, the rich and the poor and the sick and the healthy and the powerful and the subjects are there, because these are given to the authority of the governors.

27. The laws of the Brahmins who are in India. — So among the Indians are the laws of the Brahmins, who are thousands and myriads, not to commit killing, and not to adore idols, and not to commit fornication, and not to eat meat and not to drink wine, and among them none of these things are done. ܗܰܘ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܪܰܒܳܐ ܘܩܰܕ݂ܝܺܫܳܐ: ܠܳܐ ܡܶܕܶܡ ܕܡܶܫܟ݁ܰܚ ܠܡܶܟܠܝܽܗܝ. ܘܐܳܦ ܗܳܢܘܽܢ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܕܣܳܒܪܝܺܢ ܕܰܠܩܘܽܒܠܶܗ ܩܳܝܡܺܝܢ: ܠܳܐ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܒܚܰܝܠܳܐ ܩܳܝܡܺܝܢ: ܐܶܠܳܐ ܒܒܺܝܫܘܽܬܳܐ ܘܰܒܛܘܽܥܝܳܝ. ܘܗܳܕܶܐ ܡܶܫܟ݁ܚܳܐ ܕܬܶܗܘܶܐ ܙܰܒܢܳܐ ܙܥܘܽܪܳܐ: ܡܶܛܽܠ ܕܗܘܽ ܒܰܣܺܝܡ ܘܡܰܦ݁ܶܣ ܠܟܽܠܗܘܽܢ ܟ̈ܳܢܶܐ: ܕܢܶܗܘܘܽܢ ܩܳܝܡܝܳܢ ܒ݁ܡܳܐ ܐܺܝܬܰܝܗܘܽܢ ܘܡܶܬܕܰܒܪܝܺܢ ܒܨܶܒܝܳܢܶܗܘܽܢ. ܒܪܰܡ ܕ݁ܶܝܢ ܟܰܕ݂ ܐܶܣܚܪܝܺܝܢ ܒܰܡܒܺܝܕ݂ܳܐ ܕܐܶܬܥܒܶܕܘ: ܘܰܒܬܘܳܩܳܢ̈ܶܐ ܕܐܶܬܬܰܩܰܢܘ ܠܥܘܽܕܪܳܢܗܘܽܢ: ܗܘܽ ܓ݁ܶܝܪ ܛܶܟ݂ܣܳܐ ܘܕܘܒܳܪܳܐ ܕܐܶܬܝܳܗܶܒ: ܘܡܘܽܙܳܓ݁ܳܐ ܕܚܰܕ݂ ܒܚܰܕ݂ ܡܰܥܡܶܩ ܠܥܘܽܙܳܐ ܕܰܟܝ̈ܳܢܶܐ: ܕܠܳܐ ܓܡܝܺܪܳܐܝܺܬ ܢܶܗܘܘܽܢ ܢܟܶܝܢ: ܘܠܳܐ ܓܡܝܺܪܳܐܝܺܬ ܢܶܗܘܘܽܢ ܡܶܬܢܟܶܝܢ: ܐܰܝܟ݁ܰܢܳܐ ܕܢܳܟܺܝܢ ܗ̱ܘܰܘ ܘܡܶܬܢܟܺܝܢ ܡܶܢ ܩܕ݂ܳܡ ܒܪܝܺܬ݁ܶܗ ܕܥܳܠܡܳܐ. ܘܰܥܬܝܰܕ݂ ܗ̱ܘܳܐ ܕܢܶܗܘܶܐ ܙܰܒܢܳܐ ܕܐܳܦ ܗܳܢܳܐ ܢܶܟܝܳܢܳܐ ܕܩܳܐܶܡ ܒܗܘܽܢ ܢܶܓܡܰܪ: ܒܝܘܽܠܦܳܢܳܐ ܕܗܳܘܶܐ ܒܡܘܽܙܳܓ݂ܳܐ ܐ̱ܚܪܺܢܳܐ. ܘܒܰܩܝܳܡܳܐ ܕܥܳܠܡܳܐ ܚܰܕ݂ܬܳܐ ܗܰܘ: ܫܳܠܶܝܢ
can resist. Even those who believe they are rising against him do not rise because of strength but because of malice and error, and this can last for some time because he is good and allows all natures to remain in their essence and to be led according to their will. They are, however, bound in the deeds that have been done and in the structures that were established for their benefit. For that order and direction which is given and the mixing of one with another hinders the power of the natures so that they do not become entirely harmful and are not entirely harmed as they were harming and were being harmed before the creation of the world. A time will also come in which the harmfulness that remains in them will vanish because of the doctrine that will be in another mixture. And in the constitution of this new world, all evil motions will rest and all seditions will have an end. And the foolish will be persuaded and the deficiencies will be filled and there will be peace and rest by the gift of the Lord of all natures.
