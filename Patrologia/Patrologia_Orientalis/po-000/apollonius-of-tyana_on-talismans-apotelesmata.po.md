* The Book of Wisdom and Understanding, (that is:) of the Apotelesmata of Apollonius of Tyana, which he wrote and taught to Dustumus Thylassus, his disciple, saying to him: 1. My son, hear me, and I will show you the mystery of wisdom, which is neither known nor can be known, and is hidden from many concerning moments and seasons, hours, days and nights, and their names and powers, and concerning the true wisdom that is concealed in them. I will reveal to you, from the knowledge given to me by God, those apotelesmata by which all things that God made upon the earth are enchanted. For behold, I have acquired four books more precious than gold and precious stones: one of astronomy, the second of astrology, the third scholastic, and the fourth, which is more precious than all, in which are great and dreadful signs, wonders of power and mysteries, namely the book concerning the incantation of creatures created and set in motion by God. If anyone, approaching this book, wishes to work such wonders with success and skill, * he must abstain from every evil action, especially from the company and familiarity of women. Nor is it expedient to use the service of women for such illusions, since many extraordinary things are wont to arise from them. For if it happens that they are in their menstrual periods, the trees they approach become dry, and by the force of waters and the storms of winds they are uprooted and laid low. It is necessary, therefore, as has been said, to guard carefully against such things; whether one wishes, under the yoke of necessity, so to speak, to fashion all things that are on earth — loosing, that is, trees and birds and animals and creeping things and the blasts of winds and the courses of rivers — he must have in hand, to a nicety, that doctrine of constellations — I mean of the appellation and name of the great and almighty God — to choose each moment and season in which he ought to perform such incantations

IV. Concerning the hours of the day, how they are named and what work each hour performs. / The first hour of the day is called laek; in that hour it is good for men to pray. In the same hour every element of quadrupeds is enchanted: of the lion, panther, bear, wolf, and other animals. / The sixth hour is called Teechmul; in that hour the Cherubim intercede with God for the human race. / The seventh hour is called Berouki; in that hour the orders of angels praise before God. / The eighth hour is called Buran. / The ninth hour is called Chapbrum or Chapaurun. /
