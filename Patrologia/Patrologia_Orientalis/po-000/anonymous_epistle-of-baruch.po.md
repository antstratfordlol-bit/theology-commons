the river Euphrates, in which these things are written: Thus says Baruch the son of Neriah to the brothers led away into captivity: "Peace and mercy be to you!" I remember, my brothers, the love of Him who created us, who loved us from ancient times, and never held us in hatred, but rather chastised us. And truly I know that we are bound by one bond, just as we were born from one father. Therefore, it is my special care to leave you the words of this epistle before I die, so that you may be comforted regarding the calamities that have befallen you and also grieve for the misfortune that has happened to your brothers, and likewise that you may justify the judgment of Him who decreed against you that you should be led away captive. For what you have suffered is less than what you have done, so that in the last times you may be found worthy of your fathers. Therefore, if you reflect upon these things, which you have suffered now for your own good, lest you be judged and tormented in the end, you will receive eternal hope, if first you remove from your heart the vain error because of which you went away from here. For if you do these things, He who always promised for our sake to those who were more excellent than we, that He would never forget or abandon our seed, but would gather again with great mercy those who are dispersed, will immediately remember you.

VIII. Furthermore, know that in former times and ancient generations our fathers had the righteous and prophets and saints as helpers, but also that we were in our own land and they helped us, when we sinned, and besought for us Him who made us, because they trusted in their works, and the Most High heard them and was propitious to us. But now the righteous have been gathered and the prophets have fallen asleep, and we too have gone out from our land, and Zion has been taken from us, and we have nothing now except the Most High and his law. If, therefore, we direct and dispose our hearts, we will receive all that we have lost, which are twice as excellent as those which we have lost. For those which we have lost were of corruption, and those which we will receive will not be corrupted. I have indeed written thus to our brothers in Babylon, so that I might also testify these very things to them. Let all these foretold things be before your eyes at all times, because we are in the spirit and the power of our liberty. Likewise, also, the Most High will show himself long-suffering to us here, and he has indicated to us what is to come, nor has he hidden from us what will happen in the end. Before, therefore, his judgment requires what is his own and truth requires what is owed to it, let us prepare our soul, so that we may receive and not be consumed; and that we may hope, and not be confounded; and that we may rest with our fathers, and not be tormented with our enemies.
