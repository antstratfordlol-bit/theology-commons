### XXV
ܘܒܶܪܘ ܒܙܰܒ݂ܥܳܐ ܘܳܐ ܒܿܝܬ ܦܰܛܪܝܰܪܰܦܳܐ ܠܡܳܪܝ ܝܘܽܚܰܢܢ ܘܰܡܦܰܪܝܳܢܳܐ ܪܘܺܚܳܡܳܐ ܒܥܶܠܬ ܚܰܣܳܢܳܝ̈ܶܐ ܐܰܝܟ ܕܡܶܬܚܰܘܝܳܐ ܒܥܶܕܰܪ̈ܒܶܚܐ ܘܰܥܕܰܪ̈ܒܶܚܐ. ܘܰܒܥܶܕܪܶܝܢ ܬܘܒ ܡܰܠܦܳܢܳܐ ܐܰܝܟ ܣܰܡܝܳܐ ܕܰܫܡܶܗ ܡܰܪܩܳܘܣ ܒܰܪ ܩܘܽܢܒܰܪ ܫܰܪܝܺ ܕܢܰܟܪܶܙ ܡܶܛܽܠ ܘܰܡܘܕܝܳܢܘܽܬ݁ܳܐ ܕܚܰܛܳܗܶܐ: ܒܪܰܡ ܐܰܝܟ ܪܶܥܝܳܢܳܐ ܕܰܡܨܰܠܝܳܢܶܐ ܛܰܡ̈ܐܶܐ ܘܰܒܬܰܪܥܝܳܬ݁ܳܐ ܕܠܰܡܦܶܛܝܳܘܣ ܕܠܰܡ ܒ݁ܦܰܓ݁ܪܳܐ ܐܰܝܟ ܠܳܐ ܦܰܓܪ̈ܳܢܶܐ ܗܳܘܶܝܢܰܢ: ܘܰܠܠܳܐ ܚܳܫܘܽܫܘܽܬܳܐ

At the same time a dissension arose between the Patriarch and Mar John the Maphrian because of the Hassanites, as is shown in the Oriental Histories. And in Egypt likewise a blind doctor, whose name was Mark bar Konbar, began to preach about the confession of sins, yet according to the mind of the unclean Messalians and according to the opinion of Lampetius, that is to say that in the body we become incorporeal and everyone who has confessed his sins reaches impassibility. For this reason Mark, the Pope of Alexandria, began to reject confession entirely and a great schism was made in all

Egypt. And some justified Bar Konbar and condemned the Patriarch, others vice versa. And both parties gave letters to Mar Michael when he was at Antioch. The most blessed one indeed wrote a long volume and, arguing against the heresy of the Messalians of falsehood, deposed Bar Konbar, until he should persevere in his opinion, writing to the Pope himself: that it was not fitting for the confession to be rejected because of the error of Bar Konbar.
