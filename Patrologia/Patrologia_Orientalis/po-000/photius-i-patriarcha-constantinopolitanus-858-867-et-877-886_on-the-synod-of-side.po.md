The synod of Side held against the heresy of the Messalians, that is, the Euchites, or Adelphians, was read. Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, presided over the synod, with twenty-five other bishops sitting with him. In the same book was read the synodical letter of the same synod to the great Flavian, bishop of Antioch, rendering an account of the things done.

In this synod, they did not receive Adelphius, who was promising penance, nor did they admit him abjuring the heresy, for neither the abjuration of the heresy nor sincere penance were found. Now the authors of this heresy were Adelphius, so-called, not read into the order of monks or priests, but found among the laity, and Sabas, wearing the habit of monks, whom they called "the castrated" from the event, another Sabas, and Eustathius of Edessa, and Dadoes, and Symeones, tares of the evil one, and others adhering to them. Adelphius and those who were with him were condemned: although, as we said, they sought a place for penance, they did not find it, because those whom they had condemned with anathema as Messalians, they were found to be communicating with them in writing as if they were associates.

This Lampetius, however, with Hormisdas the bishop judging and Gerontius accusing, partly convicted by witnesses, partly guilty by his own mouth, was deprived of the priesthood by unanimous votes, with the support of Alypius of Caesarea himself, to whom it happened that he had been deceived into raising that wretched man to the rank of presbyterate. The same most wicked Lampetius, furthermore, composed a certain discourse which he surnamed "Testament," in which some inventions of his impiety were disseminated. This work Severus, who held the Antiochene see, while still having the rank of presbyterate, refuted. Nevertheless, a certain Alpheius, bishop of Rhinocorura, defended Lampetius, as if he had neither done nor said any of the things of which he was accused. But having published the discourse, even if he did not blaspheme anything, as it was possible to find out, he was nevertheless deposed, as agreeing with Lampetius. Another certain Alpheius, of the same name as his teacher, ordained by Timothy of Alexandria, was deposed for the same heresy, as the report of Ptolemy, bishop of Rhinocorura, to the aforementioned Timothy teaches.
