of Adelphius the Mesopotamian and Alexander, who a little earlier defiled the ruling Constantinople, we open the door of laziness, the veil of idleness from working, since they feigned to be perpetually free for prayer and, for young boys and men still swelling with bodily strength, which they ought to have tamed with many labors, they established by law to do no work, by the relaxation of the mind they even stirred up passions in them and provided license to the intellect by idly lingering on the materials of them, until the apparent prayer, when it was not what was said, perished altogether.
