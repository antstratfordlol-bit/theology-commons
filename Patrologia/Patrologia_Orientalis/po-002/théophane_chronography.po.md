Moved by divine zeal, the pious monks of Palestine came to Byzantium to fight Severus and the monks who were with him.

12 The same year, after the death of the bishop of Alexandria, Timothy, the impious Severus, that adulterous bishop of Antioch, and Julian of Halicarnassus fled to Alexandria. They disputed about corruptibility and incorruptibility, became enemies, both being hostile to the truth. One proposed Theodosius, the other Gaïnos, as bishop of Alexandria. Gaïnos occupied the episcopal throne for one year and Theodosius for two years. Justinian had them brought to Byzantium, ordered them to live separated from one another, and proposed as bishop of Alexandria a certain Paul, who appeared to be orthodox. The latter, having celebrated the memory of the impious Severus, was driven from the episcopate by the anger of the emperor and went to live in Jerusalem.
