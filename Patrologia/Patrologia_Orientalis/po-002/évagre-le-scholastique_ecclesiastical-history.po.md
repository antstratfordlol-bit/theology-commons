They allow one to see that at the beginning he continually delayed his departure for the imperial city, because he had left the throne of Antioch, but that in the end he went there. Severus wrote that after his arrival in the imperial city, he had an interview with Anthimus, and that having found in him opinions similar to his own and the same belief in God, he persuaded him to abandon his see. He related the details of this affair in his letter to Theodosius, the patriarch of Alexandria, where he boasts of having persuaded Anthimus, as has been said, to prefer these doctrines to earthly glory and to his see. One also possesses letters on this subject from Anthimus to Theodosius, as well as from Theodosius to Severus and Anthimus. I pass them over in silence and leave them to those who desire to read them, so as not to insert too considerable a matter into my work. Theodosius and Anthimus, going against the orders of the emperor and not receiving what had been agreed upon at Chalcedon, were driven from their thrones; Zoïlus received that of Alexandria and Epiphanius that of the imperial city... Justinian wrote a constitution in which he anathematized Severus and Anthimus as well as others, and where he threatened with the greatest penalties those who professed their doctrines.
