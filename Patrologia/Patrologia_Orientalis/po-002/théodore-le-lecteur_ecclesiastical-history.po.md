1 More than two hundred monks who were and were said to be schismatics, after having troubled all the Orient, also arrived in Constantinople. Seized with fear, the emperor (Anastasius) ordered them to be received with honor, and commanded great expenses to be made (for them). He summoned them to frequent interviews and proposed numerous measures against the Church. Certain members of the clergy, partisans of Eutyches, and certain monks ill-disposed toward Macedonius, frequented the enemies of the Church and conversed with Severus. 2 Monks zealous in their faith came up from Palestine against the monks who were with Severus. They were pious and virtuous men. 3 The emperor schemed many evil tricks against Macedonius. The schismatics on their side gathered a crowd of bribed people, and entered on Sunday into the chapel of the Archangel, which is found in the Palace.

As the chanters were singing the Trisagion, they responded by singing with the addition: He who was crucified for us. The following Sunday, they did the same thing in the great Church where they had entered with sticks. But the people became angry: they responded first to the shouts with shouts and to the insults with insults, then they overwhelmed them with blows and chased them away. From that time on, the emperor was openly hostile to Macedonius; he excited now the schismatic monks, now the magistrates, to overwhelm the bishop with unworthy shouts and insults. Of those were Julian, bishop of Halicarnassus in Caria, and the monk Severus, who became enemies of the faith and personal enemies.
