OF THE OPINIONS THAT EXIST REGARDING PARADISE AND THE KINGDOM
ܙܠܓܐ. ܡܢܗܘܢ ܕܡ̈ܠܦܢܐ ܓܘܫܡܢܝܐ ܐܡܼܪ̈ܘܗܝ ܠܦܪܕܝܣܐ. ܘܡܢܗܘܢ ܪ̈ܘܚܢܝܐ. ܘܡܢܗܘܢ ܬܪ̈ܝܢ ܐܡܼܪܘ ܦܪ̈ܕܝܣܐ. ܓܘܫܡܢܝܐ ܘܪ̈ܘܚܢܝܐ. ܘܡܢܗܘܢ ܚܕ ܓܘܫܡܢܝܐ ܨܐ ܪܐܙܐ ܕܒܘܣܡܐ ܗܘܢܢܝܐ. ܕܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܦܪܕܝܣܐ ܪܘܚܢܝܐ: ܘܟܕ ܬܪ̈ܥܝܬܐ ܗܕܐ ܪ̈ܒܝܥܝܬܐ. ܠܚܕܐ ܡܢ ܗܠܝܢ ܠܐ ܫܪ̈ܝܐ ܘܥܡܗܝܢ ܐܟܚܕܐ
Ray. Some among the doctors have said that Paradise is corporeal, some (that it is) spiritual; others have said that there are two paradises, corporeal and spiritual, others that there is only one, corporeal, forming the symbol of intellectual delectation, which is the spiritual Paradise. Since this fourth opinion does not contradict any of these (three preceding ones), but, at the same time as them, is confirmed, it is the truthful one.
ܡܫܬܖܪܪܐ ܗܝ ܫܖܪܝܪܐ ܀ ܫܘܪܪ̈ܐ. ܡܟܪ̈ܙܢܝ ܓܘܫܡܢܝܘܬܗ: ܩܕܝܫܐ ܐܝܘܐܢܢܝܣ. ܚܙܬܹܗ ܠܡ ܥܝܢܗ ܕܐܕܡ ܠܦܪܕܝܣܐ. ܘܫܡܥܬܗ ܐܕܢܗ. ܘܣܠܩ ܥܠ ܠܒܗ: ܘܩܕܝܫܐ ܐܬܢܣܝܘܣ ܘܩܘܪ̈ܝܠܠܘܣ. ܥܿܡܘܪ̈ܐ ܠܡ ܩܕܡܝܐ ܕܩܥܼܬ ܝܗܘܕ ܠܐܕܡ ܡܢ ܒܬܪ ܕܢܦܼܩ ܡܢ ܦܪܕܝܣܐ. ܟܕ ܓܝܪ ܒܕܘ̈ܟܝܬܐ ܣܓ̈ܝܐܐ ܥܡܼܪ. ܠܣܘܦܐ ܕܩܼܨܗ ܐܬܼܐ. ܐܬܩܼܒܪ ܒܛܘܪ̈ܐ
Confirmation. Those who proclaim that it is corporeal: Saint John (Chrysostom): "The eye of Adam saw Paradise and his ear heard it and it ascended to his heart." Saint Athanasius and Cyril: "The first inhabitant that Judea possessed, namely Adam after he went out of Paradise." Indeed, after having lived in many places, finally, when his end arrived, he was buried in the mountain of Jebus.
ܕܝܒܘܣ ܀ ܩܕܝܫܐ ܣܐܘܪܐ܇ ܠܦܪܕܝܣܐ ܥܝܢܗ ܕܐܕܡ ܚܙܬܗ. ܘܐܕܢܗ ܫܡܥܬܗ. ܫܡܥ ܗܘܐ ܓܝܪ ܕܡܢ ܟܠܗܘܢ ܐܝ̈ܠܢܝܐ ܕܒܦܪܕܝܣܐ ܡܐܟܠ ܬܐܟܘܠ. ܘܕܗ̇ܘ ܚܕ ܐܝܠܢܐ ܚܙܬܗ ܡܿܢ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇ ܗܘܬ ܠܗ.
Saint Severus: "Paradise, the eye of Adam saw it and his ear heard it. He had indeed heard: Of all the trees that are in Paradise you shall eat. And that it is a certain tree, he had the vision of it. But to eat of it, he was not permitted."
ܡܐܟܘܠܬܐ ܕܝܢ ܕܡܢܗ. ܠܐ ܡܿܦܣܐ ܗܘܬ ܠܗ ܀ ܝܘܚܢܢ ܝܚܝܕܝܐ. ܘܠܐ ܐܢܫ ܢܿܣܒܪ ܕܐܝ̈ܠܢܝܐ ܕܒܦܪܕܝܣܐ ܠܐ ܗܼܘܘ ܦܓܪ. ܡܠܬܐ ܐܠܗܝܬܐ ܡܢܿܗܪܐ ܠܝܕܥܬܗ. ܕܒܝܕ ܡܘܫܐ ܪܫܡܬ ܥܘܡܪܗ ܕܦܪܕܝܣܐ. ܫܩܠܼܬ ܠܡ ܚܘܐ ܦܐܖܐ ܘܐܟܠܼܬ. ܘܐܝܟܢܐ ܩܛܦܼܬ ܡܕܡ ܕܠܐ ܡܬܓܫܼܫ. ܘܫܩܠܼܬ ܡܢ ܡܕܡ ܕܪ̈ܘܚܢܝܐ ܗܘ. ܡܕܡ ܓܝܪ ܕܪܘܚܢܝܐ ܗܘ ܠܐ ܡܬܦܠܓܢܐ ܗܘܼ. ܡܛܠ
John the Solitary: "Let no one think that the trees in Paradise were not corporeal. It is the divine Word that clarifies the notion of it, by describing, through the intermediary of Moses, the sojourn of Paradise: Eve took the fruit and ate. How would she have picked something that was not palpable, and would she have taken something spiritual? For a spiritual thing is indivisible because of the perfection of its substance."

Confirmation. Those who proclaim its spirituality: Saint Gregory of Nyssa: "Therefore (concerning) the plantation of God that is in Eden, one must think of a fruit that is worthy of it. And James of Edessa: "It is in a parable that Moses spoke of Paradise."
ܢܣܬܿܟܠܝܘܗܝ ܠܕܘܝܪ̈ܐ ܗܿܘ ܕܒܦܪ̈ܕܝܣܐ ܘܝܥܩܘܒ ܕܐܘܪܗܝ ܦܠܐܬܢܐܝܬ ܟܝܬ ܡܿܠܠ ܡܘܫܐ ܥܠ ܦܪܕܝܣܐ ܀ ܫܘܪܪ̈ܐ. ܘܡܫܪ̈ܪ̈ܢܝ ܥܦܝܦܘܬܗ. ܩܕܝܫܐ ܬܐܘܠܘܓܘܣ. ܕܒܕܘܟ ܐܡܼܪ. ܩܝܣܐ ܠܘܩܒܠ ܩܝܣܐ. ܟܐܡܬ ܩܝܣܐ ܕܨܠܝܒܐ ܠܘܩܒܠ ܩܝܣܐ ܕܐܝܠܢܐ ܕܝܕܥܬܐ. Confirmation. Those who affirm that it is double. The holy Theologian who says in one (certain) place: "Wood opposed to wood, that is to say the wood of the cross opposed to the wood of the tree of knowledge." It is here that he demonstrates that it was corporeal: "He indeed who contained life, also contained death." In one (other) place, he says: "This one, he placed him in Paradise." It is Adam. And there he shows the spiritual meaning: "Whatever this Paradise may be, and he honored it with free will, so that the good of the one who chooses is not inferior to the one who provides the seeds." (The one who honored it), it is God; the farmer, it is man. "Farmer of immortal plantations, which are perhaps the divine thoughts, those that are simpler and those that are more perfect; naked in simplicity and in life without end, devoid of all clothing and garment, such must have been the one who was from the beginning." Saint Mar Ephrem, who made two commentaries on the Law, (according to the) corporeal (sense) and (according to the) spiritual (sense); and Saint Philoxenus who says: "For Paradise, it is a question of spiritual contemplations."
ܐܦܪܝܡ. ܕܬܪ̈ܝܢ ܦܘ̈ܫܩܐ ܓܘܫܡܢܝܐ ܘܪܘܚܢܝܐ ܥܒܼܕ ܠܐܘܪ̈ܝܬܐ ܘܩܕܝܫܐ ܦܝܠܠܘܟܣܢܘܣ ܕܐܡܼܪ. ܬܐܘܪ̈ܝܐܣ ܟܝܬ ܪ̈ܘܚܢܝܬܐ ܐܝܬ ܠܗ ܠܦܪܕܝܣܐ ܀ ܡܥܝܪ̈ܢܘܬܐ. ܘܡܢ ܡܣܬܟ̈ܠܢܝ ܪ̈ܘܚܢܝܘܬܗ ܕܦܪ̈ܕܝܣܐ. ܗܠܝܢ ܡܿܢ ܒܥܕܬܐ ܩܕܝܫܬܐ ܦܫܩܘܗܝ. ܗܠܝܢ ܕܝܢ ܒܕܝܬܝܩܝ ܚܿܕܬܐ. ܘܐܚܪ̈ܢܐ ܒܢܦܫܐ ܡܠܝܠܬܐ ܕܐܝ̈ܠܢܘܗܝ ܡܝܬܪ̈ܘܬܗܿ. ܘܡ̈ܘܗܝ ܚ̈ܝܠܝܗ̇: ܐܚܪ̈ܢܐ ܐܡܪܘ ܕܠܡܢܗܝܢ ܕܡ̈ܠܝ ܦܪ̈ܕܝܣܐ ܦܠܐܬܢܐܝܬ ܙܰܕܩ̇ ܕܢܣܒ. ܐܝܟ ܗܺܝ ܕܢܼܨܒ ܡܪ̈ܝܐ ܐܠܗܐ ܦܪ̈ܕܝܣܐ ܒܥܕܢ "ܡܢ ܩܕܝܡ7. ܘܣܼܡ ܬܡܢ ܠܐܕܡ ܕܓܒܼܠ *'. ܟܐܡܬ ܥܒܼܕ ܐܠܗܐ ܕܘܒܪ̈ܐ
Warning. Among those who understand Paradise (according to the) spiritual (sense), some have applied it to the holy Church, others to the New Testament, others to the rational soul, whose trees are the virtues and the waters are the (psychic) faculties. Others have said that certain words concerning Paradise must be taken symbolically, as (for example) that the Lord God planted Paradise in Eden from the beginning, and he put there Adam whom he had fashioned, that is to say that God established a conduct of spiritual life and the blessed life; and certain lives as they are, that is to say Adam and Eve and the law that was given to them(?).

Objection. If the tree of life and the tree of knowledge were corporeal, they would not be found together in the middle of Paradise. And as they are found in its middle, they are not corporeal. Taking the problem in reverse, the contradiction of the consequence entails the contradiction of the antecedent. This hypothesis is demonstrated, since the center of any place is unique, as also there is a single center for any circle. Since two bodies cannot be found together in a single place, the absurdity of the argument is confirmed.

Warning and confirmation. Of the tree of life (understood in the) spiritual (sense), some have thought that it is the (state of) union of the intellect that attaches itself solely to the contemplation of its Creator. Now, it is manifest that it is the Kingdom of God. Concerning the tree of knowledge, some have said that it is the science of creatures, since their goodness is mixed with wickedness. Others have said that it is the type of the Law of Moses which is the science of the pure, which is the good, and of the impure, which is the evil. Gregory of Nyssa teaches that it is corporeal concupiscence. He says indeed: "This food was the mother of death for men. It was indeed mixed fertility. Manifestly, this word makes explicit the reason that is there. This tree is named (that) of the knowledge of good and evil, for it is in the image of the malignancy of this poison that is mixed with honey; from the very fact that it makes concupiscence sweet, it is considered as good; from the fact, on the other hand, that it destroys the one who approaches it, it is the last of all evils." And that we adhere to this idea, we will show at the end.

### OF THE MODE OF LIFE THAT IS IN PARADISE

Ray. It is only by the virtue of the divine Scriptures and not by our own that we must treat this question, and we will say: Certainly, if the family of Adam had observed the Law, and had not transgressed the commandment, it would have lived far from miseries, anxieties, fears, and constraints. It would have been nourished by the goods of the soil of Paradise without work and without labor. From the tree of knowledge, it would have had its share in its time with permission, legally. Then, freed from the death of sin, it would have been worthy of the tree of life.
