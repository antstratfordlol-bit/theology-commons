the faithful departed is testified both by the very custom of the Church, which has obtained from antiquity, and by many and varied prayers of doctors, both Latin and Greek, recited at different times and places and handed down in writing; but that souls are liberated by such subsidies from certain purging torments and a temporary fire, which might have this power, we have nowhere found written explicitly, neither in the prayers and chants which are performed for them, nor in the books of the doctors; but we have received that those who are detained in hell, already condemned to eternal torments, which they suffer either in reality and experience or fear in certain expectation, are indeed relieved and experience some small consolation, but are not entirely liberated from punishment, nor held by any hope that they will ever attain this liberation. [This is shown by] what is narrated about the great Macarius, the Egyptian ascetic, who, having questioned a skull found in the desert, was taught this by divine power; and also what Basil the Great writes word for word in his prayers customarily recited at Pentecost: "You who, in this most solemn and saving festival of all, have deigned to receive suppliant propitiations and intercessions for those who are detained in hell, and offer us great hopes that there will be for the detained a relaxation from those troubles by which they are afflicted, and refreshment from You." We assert the prayers and liturgies made in their name, with the concurrence of divine goodness and clemency, which of such faults either immediately pardons and dismisses some—namely, whatever were admitted through human frailty, as that great Dionysius says in the Consideration of the mystery concerning those who have piously fallen asleep—or after some time, perhaps, having applied equal scales, either releases and remits them entirely, or relieves those guilty of them until that final judgment. But for the removal of these, we see no necessity for another punishment or purgation by fire; for fear purges some, while the torments of conscience purge others, which gnaws the soul more sharply than any fire; others, however, [are purged by] the very loss of divine glory and the obscurity of the future, whether they will ever attain it.
