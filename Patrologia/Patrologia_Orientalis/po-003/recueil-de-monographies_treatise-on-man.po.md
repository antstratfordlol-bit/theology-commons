2° Its powers and their classification. - The soul has two powers: reason and life. The vital power has two operations that also rise to the rank of power: anger and desire. Desire is between two other operations: moderation and excess. The malice of desire is excess and its goodness is moderation. Anger is also between fear and valor. The operation that is done with the vigor of anger is good, but that which weakens before passions and which fears death is bad.

Reasoning is placed between desire and anger; knowledge between intelligence and reasoning; intelligence between calm and agitation; thought between obedience and disobedience. Each of them is the help and the defender of its companion which is united to it by the operation of the act, which (operation) brings into evidence the powers hidden in the soul. 3° Role of the powers of the soul. - Reason, reasoning, knowledge, intelligence, and thought are found naturally in the soul, like life, desire, and anger. * These properties of the soul reveal themselves and are grasped by our senses with (those) of the body. Knowledge, by its operation on the soul, prevails over reasoning, intelligence, and thought—just as science prevails over wisdom and prudence—for it is close to divine things and it elevates the soul to the knowledge of hidden things. Intelligence regulates reasoning and serves (to direct) knowledge toward a work of perfection, of wisdom, and of prudence.
