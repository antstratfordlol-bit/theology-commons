### LXXVII HOMILY OF SEVERUS ARCHBISHOP OF ANTIOCH

Everyone, so to speak, approves of the reading of the sacred Gospels that we have instituted appropriately, on the night of Sunday, concerning the resurrection of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ. Some acknowledge, it is true, that they derive profit from it, but estimating that they hear it too frequently in a way, they declare that they are troubled by it, the evangelists not saying the same things about the same facts, but things that contradict each other and that lead the listener rather toward disbelief.

For whom must one believe? Matthew who writes that the resurrection took place ὀψὲ τῶν σαββάτων, John who recounted that the same fact occurred in the morning when darkness still reigned, or Luke and Mark who called the same moment, one the first dawn, the other the rising of the sun. To resolve the problem submitted to us and the other questions that arise from the examination of the text of Scripture, we must, despite our weakness, be encouraged by the God who is resurrected and present an explanation that is clear. For he who has spread the seed of the reading and who has made it grow in the ears of all, it is just that he also give an account of the questions it raises. I will try to do so, and to deduce the solution of the questions from the very words of those who raised them. The sacred writers of the Gospels did not say that the Lord is resurrected either ὀψὲ σαββάτων, or when the greater part of the night had passed, or even at dawn, or when the sun had already cast its rays.

It follows that if the resurrection took place this divine night, by the admission and agreement (of all the evangelists) none indicated the hour, which is unknown to everyone, except to the God who is resurrected and to the Father - who alone knows the Son as he himself is known by the Son - and to the Spirit who probes everything, even the depths of God.

But the angel who first opened his mouth to announce the resurrection, unveiling the power, entirely worthy of God, of Him who was resurrected, cried out: He is not here, but he is risen. Declaring that he had been resurrected by the Father, spared, as I have said, the weakness of the listeners, but has the same meaning and not another. For how does the Father act? By his own power, obviously. Now, who is the power of the Father? No one other than Christ. Christ is, indeed, the power of God and the wisdom of God. Christ therefore resurrected himself, even if he is said to have been resurrected by the Father. It is thus that the evangelists say the first (day) τῶν σαββάτων, while it would be necessary to say the first day of the "week".
