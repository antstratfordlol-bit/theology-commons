1. The divinely inspired and holy Fathers, Athanasius and Basil, Gregory and John, Cyril and Proclus, and whoever else held the same views as they did, strove with great zeal to transmit in writing the ineffable kindness toward us and the exceptional indulgence of the Word of God, which He showed when He was clothed in our flesh. And almost all of them began where the salvation of the world, the offspring, was born, as they were moved by the solemnity that is celebrated on that day. If any of them wished to trace the discourse further back, namely from the entry of Gabriel to the Virgin, he connected the discourse to that day; but it is manifest that none of them delivered a sermon on the great day of the Annunciation, where it is altogether fitting that this be done, concerning which festival we shall speak, God helping us.

There was in the days of Herod the king a certain priest named Zacharias, of the course of Abijah. That 'of the course of Abijah' signifies that Zacharias, after the time of Abijah was fulfilled, had taken up the sacred office of ministry. Whence it appears that according to that time the priests alternated their turns, just as the law commands that the high priest enter into the holy place once a year. That time is the time of the Feast of Tabernacles. Now the Feast of Tabernacles is celebrated by the Jews in the month of October, as Gabriel himself testifies through the words he spoke to the Virgin. For when he had seen her trembling at his words, and wished to persuade her of the power of Him who had sent him, he said: 'And this is the sixth month with her who is called barren.'

These things, however, have been said by me so that you may perceive with your ears what is fitting, and call to memory the ineffable beneficence of God. But to come now to the subject at hand, the present day is great and excellent, nor can any discourse show the humanity that occurred on it. Today, the counsel predetermined before the ages concerning the salvation of the human race is fulfilled. Today, the Word, lacking a beginning, is shown as an infant enclosed in a virginal womb, together with the Father. Today, He who resides inseparably in the paternal bosom is comprehended in the womb of the Virgin. Today, that heavenly One becomes also earthly, not transmuting His divinity, but remaining what He was and becoming what He was not. Today, He who once had formed Adam from the clay is clothed in His own creation.

When, however, I recall with myself such indulgence, my mind and reason are seized with dizziness, and my tongue is tied, nor do I find anything that might befit such a benefit. For what can human reason express about such a wonderful salvation of our race, unless perhaps something true, which is nevertheless below the mystery?
