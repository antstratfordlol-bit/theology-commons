And Isaiah prophesied that He would choose the good, before He knew or preferred any evil, announcing a prophecy that was weaker, as is evident, than the sublimity of the mystery, although it was great; in reality, the very possibility of choosing evil was lacking to Him, nor was there anything that He did not know at that time. And the body indeed progressed in age, but heavenly grace was present to Him immediately from the cradle; and although He was perfect immediately according to the soul, yet with the body progressing according to age, He seemed to the eyes of those looking on to have a growing virtue. For there was a time when you were bestowing a better lot on this fatherland and every nation with God, while they were diligently clinging to His laws. After they began to wander, and because of this to be punished by temptations from heavenly justice, you were delivering again those taking refuge in you from the impending evils, and adding to prayers a change for the better, immediately proved by deeds and many promises for the future. When, however, with perversity increased in us, afflictions with much vehemence deservedly supervened, as extreme things are accustomed to be—although they themselves would not have been the last, if we had immediately willed to repent well, and to investigate and remove the causes of temptations; we, however, on the contrary, still added, if any evil had remained previously unattempted—we drank the cup of the last wrath, and with sterile cries, we still irritated, let alone appeased.

Therefore if He permitted us living then to be killed, because we were living so perversely and in all ways were saddening the author of our life, if He abandoned immediately even those who were following us, what hope of reviving is there henceforth, when we do not even feel that we have been mortified? Or rather, what of the latest things is it not necessary to expect from this just series of inflicted evils, if anything of the latest has been left, in the judgment of the prudent and those judging rightly? Of these, therefore, we will no longer be troublesome to you nor to God, content with His sentence; but of those things of which we are conscious concerning ourselves and others, we retain no good hope regarding these, unless insofar as it is permitted, taught by a sufficient and rare example never to lose hope in God, who died for sinners; we ask only that you lead us out of the present life more quickly, because we are weary, conformed by charity alone to common calamities, supplying the improvidence of others, because we have seen the whole deformity of the race unworthy of the gifts of God and yours, but worthy only of our sins long since known, because of which, deprived of all good things, alas, we have been permitted even to come to the end and live. Lead us out, however, in the paternal religion and virtue, with God and you conscious, although this should be hidden from all men. This we ask from God through you after the first petition recently made, because to Him is due glory and adoration from all that are, unto the ages. Amen.
