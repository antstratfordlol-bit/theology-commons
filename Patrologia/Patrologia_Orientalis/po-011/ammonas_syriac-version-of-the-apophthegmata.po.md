# Syriac Version of the Apophthegmata

**Author**: Ammonas  
**Series**: PO Tome 11  
**Columns**: pp. 409–424  

[p. 409] {#po.11.p409}
ܐܢܐ ܫܐܠ ܠܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ ܘܐܡܼܪ ܠܗܿ, ܐܡܪ ܠܝ ܦܬܓܡܐ ܕܐܚܝܐ ܒܗ. ܗܼܘ ܕܝܢ ܐܡܼܪ ܠܗ. ܙܠ ܥܒܼܕ ܚܘܫܒܟ. ܐܝܟ ܥܳܒܺܕܝ ܒܝܫ̈ܬܐ ܗܠܝܢ ܕܐܝܬ ܒܒܝܬ ܐܣܝܪ̈ܐ. ܕܡܫܐܠܝܢ ܠܗܠܝܢ ܕܥܐܠܝܢ ܠܘܬܗܘܢ ܘܐܡ̇ܪܝܢ. ܕܐܝܟܐ ܗܘ ܗܓܡܘܢܐ ܘܠܐܡܬܝ ܐܬ݂ܐ ܠܗܪܟܐ. ܘܡܢ ܣܘܟܝܐ ܕܕܚܠܬܐ ܪܥܠܝܢ. ܗܟܢܐ ܐܦ ܝܚܝܕܝܐ. ܚܝܒ ܕܗܘܐ ܚܳܐܪ ܒܟܠܙܒܢ. ܘܡܟܣ ܢܦܫܗ ܘܐܡܿܪ. ܘܝ ܠܝ ܕܐܝܟܢܐ ܥܬܝܕ ܐܢܐ
1. A brother questioned Abba Ammoun and said to him: "Tell me a word that will make me live," and he said to him: "Go (and) reason like the criminals who are in prison: they question those who come near them and they say: Where is the governor and when is he coming here? and they tremble because of the fear of waiting. It is thus that the solitary must always consider, reprimand himself and say: Woe to me! How shall I be able to present myself before the tribunal of Christ, and what answer shall I be able to make to Him? If you meditate always in this way, you can be saved." 2. See the variants, note 9.
ܕܐܩܘܡ ܩܕܡ ܒܝܡ ܕܡܫܝܚܐ. ܘܐܝܿܟܢܐ ܐܦܢܐ ܠܗ ܦܬܓܡܐ. ܐܢ ܗܟܢܐ ܗܼܘܝܬ ܪܢܼܐ ܒܟܠ ܙܒܢ. ܡܫܟܚ ܐܢܬ ܕܬܚܝܐ܀ ܐܡܿܪ ܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ. ܕܐܪܒܥܣܪ̈ܐ ܫ̈ܢܝܢ ܥܒܶܕܬ ܒܐܣܩܝܛܝ. ܟܕ ܡܬܟܫܦ ܐܢܐ ܠܐܠܗܐ ܒܠܠܝܐ
3. Abba Ammoun said: "I spent fourteen years in Scetis praying to God day and night to grant me to overcome anger."
ܘܒܐܝܡܡܐ. ܕܢܬܠ ܠܝ ܕܐܙܟܐ ܠܪܘܓܙܐ܀ ܐܚܶܐ ܐܡܪܝܢ. ܡܛܠ ܡܢܐ ܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ ܥܕܡܐ ܠܐܪܒܥܣܪ̈ܐ ܫ̈ܢܝܢ ܠܐ ܙܟܝܗܝ ܠܚܫܐ ܕܪܘܓ̇ܙܐ. ܟܕ ܛܒ ܐܡܼܪ ܠܢ. ܕܠܐ ܫܠܐ ܒܐܝܡܡܐ ܘܒܠܠܝܐ ܡܢ ܕܡܬܟܫܦ
The brothers said: "Why did Abba Ammoun go up to fourteen years without overcoming the passion of anger, since he told us that he did not cease day and night to pray to God in pain and tears that He might grant him to overcome this passion?"


[p. 411] {#po.11.p411}
ܗܘܐ ܠܐܠܗܐ ܒܢܝܫܐ ܘܒܕܡ̈ܥܐ ܕܢܬܠ ܠܗ ܥܠܘܗܝ ܙܟܘܬܐ܀ ܣܒܐ ܐܡܿܪ. ܡܛܠ ܕܟܒܪ ܥܠܒ ܗܘܐ ܒܗ ܚܫܐ ܗ̇ܘ ܝܬܝܪܐ. ܐܝܟ ܕܡܢ ܡܘܙܓܐ ܕܦܓܪܗ. ܐܠܐ ܗܕܐ ܗܝ ܚܬܝܬܬܐ. ܕܐܝܟ ܕܥܡ ܓܢܒܪܐ ܘܚܝܠܬܢܐ ܡܩܪܒܝܢ ܗܘܘ ܥܡܗ ܢܝܫ̈ܐ ܘܫ̈ܐܕܐ. ܗܟܢܐ ܓܝܪ ܡܩܪܒܝܢ ܫܺܐܕܐ ܥܡ ܐܒܗ̈ܬܐ. ܚܝܠܬܢܐܝܬ ܘܬܩܝܦܐܝܬ. ܟܠܢܫ ܐܝܟ ܡܫܘܢܬܗ.
The old man said: "It is because this excessive passion already dragged him along because of his constitution; but it is certain that the passions and the demons fought against him like a giant and a hero. It is thus that the demons fight against the Fathers, with strength and ardor, each according to his measure; and as much as (the Fathers) shine by patience, that is to say by endurance, so much the struggle is prolonged with them."
ܘܐܝܟ ܕܢܬܢܨܚܘܢ ܒܡܓܪܬ ܪܘܚܐ. ܐܘܟܝܬ ܒܡܣܝܒܪܢܘܬܐ. ܗܟܢܐ ܡܬܡܬܚ ܗܘܐ ܥܡܗܘܢ ܩܪܒܐ܀ ܡܫܬܥܐ ܗܘܐ ܐܢܫ ܡܢ ܐܒܗ̈ܬܐ ܕܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܣܒܐ ܚܕ ܒܩ̈ܠܝܬܐ ܘܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܠܗ ܥܡܠܐ ܣܓܝܐܐ ܘܠܒܝܫ ܗܘܐ ܣܪܝܓܬܐ. ܘܐܙܠ ܠܘܬ ܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ ܘܟܕ ܚܙܝܗܝ ܕܠܒܝܫ ܣܪܝܓܬܐ ܐܡܲܪ ܠܗ ܗܕܐ ܠܐ ܡܘܬܪܐ ܠܟ ܡܕܡ.
4. One of the Fathers recounted that there was an old man at the Cells and he had great asceticism and he was dressed in a mat. He went near Abba Ammoun, and the latter, seeing that he was dressed in a mat, * said to him: "That is of no use to you." And (the other) questioned him and said: "Three thoughts torment me:
ܘܫܐܠܗ ܘܐܡܼܪ. ܬܠܬܐ ܚܘܫܒܺܝܢ ܫܢܩ̈ܝܢ ܠܝ ܐܘ ܕܐܙܠ ܠܡܕܒܪܐ. ܐܘ ܕܐܦܘܩ ܒܐܟܣܢܝܐ. ܐܘ ܕܐܚܒܘܫ ܢܦܫܝ ܒܩܠܝܬܐ. ܘܠܐ ܐܩܒܠ ܠܐܢܫ. ܘܗ̇ܘܝܬ ܐܟܠ ܐܢܐ ܚܕܐ ܠܬܪ̈ܝܢ ܝܘܡ̈ܬܐ. ܘܐܡܼܪ ܠܗ ܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ. ܘܠܐ ܚܕܐ ܡܢ ܗܠܝܢ ܡܫܟܚ ܐܢܬ ܕܬܥܒܕ. ܐܠܐ ܙܠ ܬܒ ܒܠܚܘܕ ܒܩܠܝܬܟ. ܘܗܼܘܝܬ ܐܟܠ ܩܠܝܠ ܟܠܝܘܡ. ܘܗܼܘܐ ܐܝܬ ܒܠܒܟ ܒܟܠ ܙܒܢ ܡܠܬܗ ܕܗܘ ܡܟܣܐ. ܕܐܠܗܐ ܢܚܘܢܝܢܝ ܠܚܛܝܐ. ܘܗܟܢ ܡܫܟܚ ܐܢܬ ܕܬܚܝܐ܀
either to go to the desert, or to leave for a foreign country, or to shut myself in a cell, to see no one and to eat once every two days." Abba Ammoun said to him: "You must do none of that; but go, remain only in your cell, eat a little each day, and always have in your heart the word of the publican: God, have mercy on me, a sinner, and thus you can live."


[p. 412] {#po.11.p412}
ܗܘܐ ܒܙܒܢ ܐܘܠܨܢܐ ܠܐܚ̈ܢܐ ܒܕܘܟܬܐ ܕܥܡܪܝܢ ܗܘܘ ܒܗ̇ ܘܨܒܝܢ ܗܘܘ ܕܢܫܒܩܘܢܗ̇. ܘܐܬܘ ܠܘܬ ܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ: ܘܗܐ ܗܘ ܪܕܐ ܗܘܐ ܒܐܠܦܐ. ܘܢܙܐ ܐܢܘܢ ܕܐܙܠܝܢ ܥܠ ܣܦܬܗ ܕܢܗܪܐ: ܘܐܡܪ ܠܐܠܦܪܐ ܕܢܩܪܒܝܘܗܝ ܠܐܪܥܐ. ܘܩܪܐ ܐܢܘܢ ܠܗܠܝܢ ܐܚ̈ܢܐ ܘܐܡܪ ܠܗܘܢ: ܐܢܐ ܗܘ ܐܡܘܢ ܕܨܒܝܢ ܐܢܬܘܢ ܕܬܐܙܠܘܢ ܠܘܬܗ. ܘܐܦܝܣ ܐܢܘܢ ܕܢܗܦܟܘܢ ܠܕܘܟܬܗܘܢ: ܘܒܝܐ ܐܢܘܢ ܘܐܡܪ ܠܗܘܢ ܕܢܚܡܣܢܘܢ. ܠܝܬ
5. A trial came to the brothers in the place where they lived; and they wanted to abandon it. And they went near Abba Ammoun, and behold, he himself was sailing in a ship, and he saw them going on the bank of the river and he told the boatman to land him on shore. And he called these brothers and said to them: "I am Ammoun, to whom you desire to go." And he persuaded them to return to their place, and he consoled them and told them to take courage; for there was no prejudice to the soul in this matter, but it was a human tribulation.
ܗܘܐ ܓܝܪ ܒܗ̇ ܒܨܒܘܬܐ ܚܘܣܪܢܐ ܕܢܦܫܐ: ܐܠܐ ܫܚܩܐ ܗܘܐ ܐܢܫܝܐ܀ ܐܬܐ ܒܙܒܢ ܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ ܕܢܥܒܪ ܢܗܪܐ. ܘܐܫܟܚܢ ܕܡܬܩܢܝܢ ܠܗ̇ ܠܣܦܝܢܬܐ ܕܡܥܒܪܐ ܓܒܪ̈ܐ: ܘܝܬܒ ܠܗ. ܘܗܐ ܣܦܝܢܬܐ ܐܚܪܬܐ ܐܬܬ ܕܡܥܒܪܐ ܗܘܬ ܐܢܫ̈ܐ. ܘܩܪ̈ܐܘܗܝ ܘܐܡܪܝܢ ܠܗ. ܬܐ ܐܦ ܐܢܬ ܐܒܐ: ܥܒܪ ܥܡܢ. ܘܥܢܐ ܘܐܡܪ ܠܗܘܢ. ܕܐܠܐ ܐܢ ܥܒܪܬ ܒܣܦܝܢܬܐ ܕܕܡܣܝܘܢ ܠܐ ܥܒܪ ܐܢܐ. ܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܕܝܢ ܥܠܘܗܝ ܓܘܪܙܐ ܕܛܪ̈ܦܐ. ܘܝܬܒ ܗܘܐ ܘܓܕܠ ܣܝܪܐ: ܥܕܡܐ ܕܐܬܬܩܢܬ ܣܦܝܢܬܐ ܗ̇ܝ. ܘܗܝܕܝܢ ܥܒܪ. ܘܐܪܡܝܘ ܠܗ ܐܚ̈ܐ ܬܝܒܘܬܐ ܟܕ ܐܡܪܝܢ. ܠܡܢܐ ܥܒܕܬ ܗܕܐ. ܐܡܪ ܠܗܘܢ
6. Abba Ammoun came one day to cross the river, and he found that they were preparing the boat that carries the luggage (sic L; B bears: that carries men), and he sat in it. And behold, another boat came that was carrying certain people (sic L; B bears: that was carrying women), and they called him and said to him: "Come, you too, Abba, cross with us." And he answered and said to them: "If it is not to cross in the public boat, I will not cross." Now he had with him a bundle of (palm) leaves, and he stood weaving the reed until the boat was ready, and then he crossed. And the brothers paid him reverence, saying: "Why did you do that?" The old man said to them: "So that I do not always walk with a hurried and troubled spirit." It is therefore a teaching to follow the way of God with calm.

ܣܒܐ. ܥܠ ܕܠܐ ܒܟܠ ܙܒܢ ܐܗܠܟ: ܟܕ ܡܣܪܗܒ ܘܫܓܝܫ ܚܘܫܒܝ. ܗܕܐ ܓܝܪ ܬܢܢܘܝܬܐ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇: ܕܒܡܛܟܣܘܬܐ ܢܪܕܐ ܒܐܘܪܚܬܗ ܕܐܠܗܐ܀ ܐܙܠ ܗܘܐ ܒܙܒܢ ܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ ܠܘܬ ܐܒܐ ܐܢܛܘܢܝܣ. ܘܛܥܐ ܒܐܘܪܚܐ. ܘܝܬܒ ܩܠܝܠ ܘܕܡܟ. ܘܩܡ ܡܢ ܫܢܬܗ:
7. Abba Ammoun went one day near Abba Anthony and he took the wrong road, and he sat down a little and slept. And he rose from his sleep and he prayed


[p. 414] {#po.11.p414}
ܘܨܠܝ ܠܐܠܗܐ ܘܐܡܪ. ܒܥܐ ܐܢܐ ܡܢܟ ܡܪܝܐ ܐܠܗܐ: ܠܐ ܬܘܒܕ ܓܒܝܠܬܟ. ܘܬܠܝܐ ܥܝܢܘ̈ܗܝ ܘܢܙܐ: ܒܕܡܘܬ ܐܝܕܐ ܕܒܪ ܐܢܫܐ ܬܠܝܐ ܗܘܬ ܠܥܠ ܡܢܗ ܒܫܡܝܐ ܘܡܚܘܝܐ ܗܘܬ ܠܗ ܐܘܪܚܐ: ܥܕܡܐ ܕܐܬܐ ܘܩܡ ܠܥܠ
to God and said: "I pray you, Lord God, do not lose your creature." And he lifted his eyes and he saw, like a man's hand that was suspended above him in the sky and was showing him the way, until he arrived and stood above the cave of Abba Anthony.
ܡܢ ܡܥܪܬܗ ܕܐܒܐ ܐܢܛܘܢܝܣ. ܘܟܕ ܥܠ ܠܘܬܗ ܘܚܙܝܗܝ: ܐܬܢܒܝ ܠܗ ܐܒܐ ܐܢܛܘܢܝܣ ܘܐܡܪ. ܕܐܝܬ ܠܟ ܕܬܐܪܒ ܒܕܚܠܬ ܐܠܗܐ. ܘܐܦܩܗ ܠܒܪ ܡܢ ܡܥܪܬܐ: ܘܚܘܝܗ ܟܐܦܐ ܘܐܡܪ. ܨܥܪ ܠܟܐܦܐ ܗܕܐ ܘܡܚܝܗ̇. ܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܥܒܕ ܗܟܘܬ: ܘܐܡܪ ܠܗ. ܐܒܐ ܐܢܛܘܢܝܣ. ܗܟܘܬ ܐܦ ܐܢܬ ܐܝܬ ܠܟ ܕܬܐܬܐ ܠܡܫܘܚܢܬܐ ܗܕܐ: ܘܬܛܥܢ ܝܘܩܪܐ ܘܨܥܪܐ ܣܓܝܐܐ. ܘܗܘܐ ܗܟܢܐ. ܘܐܝܪܒ ܐܒܐ ܐܡܘܢ ܘܡܢ ܣܓܝܐܘܬܐ ܕܛܝܒܘܬܗ ܠܐ ܐܝܕܥ ܒܝܫܬܐ. ܘܒܗ̇ ܒܡܝܬܪܘܬܗ: ܡܢ ܕܗܘܐ ܐܦܣܩܦܐ ܩܪܒܘ ܠܗ ܒܬܘܠܬܐ ܚܕܐ ܕܒܛܢܬ ܘܐܡܪܝܢ ܠܗ: ܦܠܢ ܥܒܕ ܣܘܥܪܢܐ ܗܢܐ. ܢܩܝܒܠܘܢ ܡܪܕܘܬܐ. ܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܚܬܡܗ̇ ܠܟܪܣܗ̇. ܘܦܩܕ ܕܢܬܠܘܢ ܠܗ̇
8. When he had entered and seen him, Abba Anthony prophesied to him and said: "You will grow in the fear of God." And he made him go outside the cave, and he showed him a stone and said: "Insult this stone and strike it." * And he did it. And Abba Anthony said to him: "You too, you will arrive at this degree; and you will endure a considerable burden and reproach." And it was so. And Abba Ammoun progressed and, because of his goodness, he did not know evil. In his state of perfection, when he was bishop, they brought him a virgin who was pregnant and they said to him: "It is such a one who did this; let them receive a penance." But he signed her womb, and he ordered to give her six

Those who were near him said to him: "What are you doing there? impose a penance on them." And the old man did not presume to judge anyone, for he was full of limitless mercy and goodness toward all men.

24. A brother asked Abba Ammonius and said: "How does it behoove a man to act, when he wants to begin any business whatsoever: whether to go, or to come, or to pass from one place to another, so that his business may take place according to the will of God and that it may be freed from the deception of the demons?" The old man said to him: "A man must first consider in his mind and see what is the cause of what he wants to do and from where it originates, whether it originates from God or from Satan, or from man himself; he will do the first and he will turn away from the two following, otherwise he will be mocked in the end by the demons. Then he will pray and he will ask God to do what is of God: he will set to work and after that he will boast in God. 25. The same said again: "Bear with everyone just as God also bears with you."
