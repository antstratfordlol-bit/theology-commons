BY THE SAME HNANA All things that are done in the holy Church by the children of faith are for the good of the whole world. Certainly, for lack of research, few are the people who know these things, but their ignorance does not deprive them of them; for, just as the birth of Christ is for the advantage of the whole world, although there are very few who remember it; just as his baptism is for the expiation of the sins of all men, although only Christians have kept the memory of it; * just as his fast, his passion, his resurrection, his ascension, and the gift of grace are a good for the whole universe, although their memory is present to few people, so the praises (of God), the thanksgivings, the prayers, and the rogations that are done in the Church of the faithful are for the good of the whole world. Saint Paul divides into four types all the reasonable requests that we make to God, and calls them: requests, prayers, supplications, and thanksgivings. Here the Apostle has omitted "praises of God," not because he did not know that we must before all things sing the praises of God, but (he omitted it) firstly because he knew that it is natural for all men to be moved by the greatness of the works of God to celebrate his praises, and secondly because the praises of God are strictly annexed to these types of prayer; we believe indeed that he can answer our requests; now there is no one who enjoys the goods * of God and who can neglect his praises, unless he has a penchant for atheism.

Saint Paul, after having shown in how many ways prayers and requests are made, then tells us what to ask for and for whom to pray: For kings, he says, and for all those who are constituted in dignity. By this, he makes us understand that when friendship and peace reign between kings and great dignitaries, all men taste their sweet fruits, and thus, we too, thanks to the maintenance of this peace, will be able to easily follow the path of the fear of God and of purity. Such a prayer, he adds, is pleasing to God: for he wants all men to live and to arrive at the knowledge of the truth. But God does not need the prayer of men. Why then would our prayer be pleasing to him? It is in relation to us that our prayer is pleasing to him, because it is suitable and useful for us.
