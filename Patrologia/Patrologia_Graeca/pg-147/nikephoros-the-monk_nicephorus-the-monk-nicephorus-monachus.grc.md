### Notitia ex Philocalia

Νικηφόρος ὁ ὁσιώτατος Πατὴρ ἡμῶν, ὁ ἐν μὲν τῷ ἁγιωνύμῳ ὄρει τοῦ Ἄθω τῶν τῶν ἀσκητικῶν ἀγώνων ἀνύσας δίαυλον, μικρῷ δὲ πρὸ τοῦ τεσσαρακοστοῦ τριακοστοῦ καὶ χιλιοστοῦ ἔτους ἀκμάσας, καθηγητὴς καὶ μυσταγωγὸς τῶν ὑψηλῶν τῆς ἀσκητικῆς φιλοσοφίας μαθημάτων ἐγένετο Γρηγορίου τοῦ Θεσσαλονίκης, καθάπερ αὐτὸς που περὶ αὐτοῦ μαρτυρεῖ· διὰ τῆς ἀπεριμερίμνου δὲ ἡσυχίας ἑαυτῷ μόνῳ συγλάζων, καὶ δι᾿ ἑαυτοῦ ἤδη ἡνωμένου, τῷ ὑπερκοσμίῳ καὶ ἀκροτάτῳ τῶν ἐφετῶν ἑνωθεὶς ἀπορρήτως, μακαρίως ἐντεῦθεν τὸν ἐνυπόστατον ἐν καρδίᾳ τῆς χάριτο; ἔπαθε φωτισμόν. Τοῦ τοίνυν τῆς θεώσεως ἀμιμήτου μιμήματος, καὶ θεοποιοῦ δώρου αὐτός τε πρῶτον ἐκεῖθεν πλουσίως ἀναπλησθείς, τούτου καὶ ἡμῖν ὁ Μακάριος, διὰ τοῦ παρόντος αὐτοῦ Πονήματος, εἴ γε βουλοίμεθα τῶν ἴσων ἐκείνῳ ἀξιωθῆναι γερῶν, πατρικῶς καὶ ἀφθόνως μετέδωκεν, ἐφ᾿ ᾧ τὰς ἐν ταῖς τῶν ἁγίων Πατέρων βιογραφίαις περὶ νήψεως, προσοχῆς τε καὶ προσευχῆς ὀπῃδήποτε διαγορευούσας χρήσεις συνερανισάμενος, προσθεὶς δ᾿ οἷκοθεν, καὶ τὴν, ὡς οὐκ ἄν τις ᾠήθη καλλίω, φυσικὴν, καὶ ἐπιστημονικὴν ἀληθῶς μέθοδον τῆς δι᾿ εἰσπνοῆς ῥίνας ἔνδον καρδίας ἐπισυναγωγῆς τοῦ νοὸς, καὶ τῆς κατ᾿ αὐτὴν τοῦ Κυρίου Ἰησοῦ ἐπικλήσεως, στάθμην οἷον ἀκριβεστάτην ἱερᾶς νήψεως, καὶ κλίμακα καθαρᾶς καὶ ἀρεμβάστου προσευχῆς, καὶ τῶν ἐξ αὐτῆς πηγαζομένων ἀγαθῶν, τοῖς σωθῆναι ἐθελουσιν ὑπεστήριξε, πρῶτος αὐτὸς, οἷά τις νέος Βεσελεὴλ, κατασκευάσας τῇ ἀρχιτεκτονίᾳ τοῦ πνεύματος.
Ἀναβαίνετε λοιπὸν, ἀναβαίνετε, ὅσοι ἐν ὑμῖν αὐτοῖς κατοικῆσαι τὸν Χριστὸν δι᾿ ἐφέσεως ἔχετε, καὶ εἰς τὴν τοῦ ἁγίου Πνεύματος εἰκόνα μεταμορφωθῆναι ἀπὸ δόξης εἰς δόξαν ἐθέλετε, καὶ ἑπομένως θεουργηθῆναι, καὶ τῆς σωζομένων φαεινῆς κληρουχίας ἀξιωθῆναι.
ANNO DOMINI MCCCXL.
NICEPHORUS MONACHUS
NOTITIA
(Φιλοκαλία τῶν ἱερῶν Νηπτικῶν, Venetiis, 1782, fol., p. 869.)
Venerabilis Pater noster Nicephorus, qui in A sancto monte Atho asceticorum certaminum stadium confecit, paulo ante annum millesimum trecentesimum et quadragesimum floruit. Altissimis asceticæ philosophiæ institutis Gregorium Thessalonicensem imbuit, ut ipse alicubi testatur. Tranquillam et a sollicitudinibus liberam vitam apud ipsum solum peregit, ipsi fuit conjunctus; summis et supernaturalibus præsidum mandatis fuit ineffabiliter eruditus, et gratiæ illuminationem beatificam recepit. Cujus divinæ sanctitatis exemplar factus, cœleste donum, quo prius digaus habitus fuerat, nobiscum generosa voluit per præsentem tractatum communicare, ut ejusdem mercedis participes fieri mereremur. In sanctorum Patrum Vitis exempla collegit de sobrietate, de attentione, de oratione et de variis adjumentis; e proprio fonte naturalem et scientificam adjecit methodum, qua pulchriorem nemo excogitatur sit: De operatione spiritus in corde et de invocatione nominis Domini Jesu; regulam tradidit exartissimam de sancto jejunio et scalam puræ et perfectæ orationis; demum bona subsequuntur, quæ inde profluunt salvari volentibus. In his autem seipsum, veluti novum Beseleel, exercuit, conducente et operante Spiritu sancto.
Ascendite igitur, ascendite qui in votis habetis ut Christus vobis inhabitet; qui desideratis ut C ad imaginem Spiritus sancti reformemini et de gloria in gloriam assurgatis; qui concupiscitis ut illiciamini divini et beatam sortem sanctorum collocatis.
NICEPHORUS THE MONK
NOTICE
(Philocalia of the Sacred Neptic Fathers, Venice, 1782, fol., p. 869)
Our venerable Father Nicephorus, who completed the stadium of ascetic contests on the Holy Mountain of Athos, flourished a little before the year 1340. He instructed Gregory of Thessalonica in the highest teachings of ascetic philosophy, as he himself testifies in some place. He spent a tranquil life, free from anxieties, dwelling in solitude; he was united to Him; he was ineffably instructed by the highest and supernatural commandments, and received the beatific illumination of grace. Having become an exemplar of this divine holiness, he wished generously to share with us, through the present treatise, the heavenly gift of which he had first been deemed worthy, so that we might merit to become partakers of the same reward. In the Lives of the holy Fathers, he collected examples concerning sobriety, attention, prayer, and various aids; and from his own source he added a natural and truly scientific method, than which no one could devise a more beautiful one: On the operation of the Spirit in the heart and the invocation of the name of the Lord Jesus; he handed down a most exact rule concerning holy fasting and a ladder of pure and perfect prayer; finally, the good things follow which flow from this for those who desire to be saved. In these matters, he exercised himself like a new Bezalel, with the Holy Spirit leading and working.
Nicephorus, our most venerable Father, who completed the course of ascetic struggles on the holy mountain of Athos, and who flourished a little before the year 1340, became the guide and mystagogue of the high teachings of ascetic philosophy for Gregory of Thessalonica, as he himself testifies somewhere regarding this. By means of an untroubled stillness, he lived for God alone, and, already united to Him through himself, he was united ineffably to the supracosmic and supreme object of desire; from this he received in his heart the light of grace in its fullness. Having been enriched thus from the source by this inimitable imitation of theosis and this deifying gift, the blessed one shares it with us, his children, through this present work of his, if we desire to be deemed worthy of the same rewards as he; he shares it fatherly and abundantly. For he gathered together the uses concerning watchfulness, attention, and prayer that are set forth everywhere in the biographies of the holy Fathers, and adding thereto from his own heart, he also added the—as no one would have thought possible—more beautiful, natural, and truly scientific method of the gathering of the mind into the heart by means of nasal inhalation, and of the invocation of the Lord Jesus according to it; he supported it with a most accurate standard of sacred watchfulness, and a ladder of pure and undistracted prayer, and the goods that spring from it, for those who wish to be saved, being the first himself, like a new Bezalel, to construct it with the architecture of the Spirit. Ascend, therefore, ascend, you who have it in your desires that Christ should dwell within you, and who wish to be transformed into the image of the Holy Spirit from glory to glory, and consequently to be deified, and to be deemed worthy of the radiant inheritance of the saved.
