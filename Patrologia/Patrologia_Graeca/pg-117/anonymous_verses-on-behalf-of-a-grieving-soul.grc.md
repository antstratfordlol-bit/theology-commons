ANONYMI VERSUS EX PARTE ANIMÆ DOLENTIS
Telis sauciatus sum cupiditatum, telis animæ exitiosis; ut medicus, sana; ut misericors, manum præbe.
Terram primam habui matrem, iterum me condet terra: quæ est temporalium fruitio? quæ vitæ voluptas?
Hic mecum lamentamini, mecum hic deflete, vos quibus compatiens est animus, quibus benigna mens est.
Amovi animam meam a salutaribus, nocentibus vero admovi; o crudelis error! vitam duco, sed non quietam; malæ meæ actiones vel ante finem suadent vivere in timore et angustia.
Peccavi tibi qui judex super humanam naturam sedebis: quid fiam? quo fugiam? qur tibi abscondar?  Ingemisco et sæve exagitor mortis cogitationibus, non reformidans finem, sed quæ post finem sequentur.
Misericorditer aspice, Salvator, in animam miseram quæ in multis peccavit, quæ iram tuam excitavit.
Lucis decus paratum est illis qui hic recte degunt, tenebræ his qui prævaricantur; fuge, anima mea, has tenebras. Malorum remissionem dona mihi, Christe, actionum, qui bonitatis es abyssus, et fons benignitatis.
Post finem inutilis gemitus omnis, omnis lamentatio; nunc efficax est gemitus, efficax et luctus.
Leges Christi contempsi, legem cupiditatum  diligens: quis de me tandem non lamentabitur? quis de me non plorabit?
Hospites non aspexi benigne, pauperibus non sum inclinatus, egenis domum clausi: proh! crudelem mentem!
Totus es, Salvator, suavitas, mansuetudo inexhausta: immuta in suavitatem animæ meæ acerbitatem.
Mari jactor, Christe, peccatorum; da mihi, ut Petro, manum; salva me sicut gubernator.
Negligenter disperdidi meam omnem vitam. Heu mihi! quomodo effugiam supplicia intoleranda?
Viscera tua ad iram coegi, viscera paterna tua; sed benigne reprehende, neque me in ira castiga.  Quænam est bonitas tua, Christe, quæ patientiæ copia, quod caduca adamantem non uris, sed sustines?
Cupiditatum sylvam congessi, ignis materiam
cognoscere poetæ nomen; nam politicos nunquam legi (et heu! multos legi) tam bonos, tam elegantes, tam facili vena profluentes, nisi forsan quos parili distichorum artificio composuit Symeon Metaphrastes, ediditque Allatius Diatr. de Symeon. p. 132.

man most deserving of note in our memory, and most learned and skilled to the highest degree in the Greek and Latin languages, is Ludolphus Neocorus, or Kusterus, who, having left his ancestral faith in Paris and embraced the Roman faith, died on the 12th of October, A.D. 1716. After his *Diatribe on Suidas*, which was published at Cambridge in 1701, in which he recounts the faults and defects of the edition of Portus, and relates with what resources and what apparatus he himself undertook a new one, correcting and illustrating various passages of Suidas by way of a specimen, he brought the work itself to completion five years later, and allowed his new Greek-Latin Suidas with notes to issue from the Cambridge press in the year 1705, in three folio volumes. In the Preface to this edition, while he discusses accurately the author whom he undertook to illustrate, his age, the virtues and faults of his Lexicon, and the previous editions, I did not wish to do what had already been done, but to present here to you the complete Dissertation of that most famous man—my friend while he lived—together with my own observations.
The Lexicon of Suidas, as it does not belong to our project, we omit; but so that you may bear this less painfully, diligent Reader, we present to you: 1st, *Notitia* in which the age of Suidas and the value and dignity of his book are discussed; 2nd, Three very extensive indices, arranged according to the order of the alphabet, which represent the whole of Suidas, albeit concisely. The first displays the writers about whom Suidas treats; the second, those whom he cites; the third, the writers investigated by learned men in Suidas, where he attributes passages to unnamed authors.
