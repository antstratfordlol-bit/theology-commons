This is John, whom Christ loved, and who was a virgin and an evangelist; he remained in Jerusalem in his own house—the mother of the Churches—to which the apostles fled for fear of the Jews. There the Passover was prepared; there the first mystery was delivered to the disciples; there after the resurrection the Lord appeared to them; there the Holy Spirit descended upon the disciples; there he showed Thomas the marks of the nails; there the apostles ordained as the first bishop the son of Joseph, the brother of the Lord—not the son of Zebedee, the brother of John. For Zebedee is called a ship-owner in his own right, and he was the first among the prominent men who were in Galilee.
After the death (of Zebedee), John sold his paternal inheritance in Galilee and purchased a dwelling in Jerusalem; whence, as is said of him, he was known to the high priest.
He received the most holy Theotokos into his house until her resurrection, and afterwards, having preached the Word, he himself was also taken up.
James, however, having become the first bishop of Jerusalem, is called the brother of the Lord according to the flesh, but the son of Joseph the carpenter by his carnal wife. For Joseph had four sons—James, Simon, Judas, and Joseph—and two daughters, Esther and Martha, by his wife Salome, who was the daughter of Haggeus, the brother of Zacharias, the father of John, the son of Barachias, the son of Abia the priest.
And Joseph was a widower at that time, so that John the Baptist and Salome were children of brothers-german. For Haggeus, the son of Barachias, was the paternal uncle of John. Likewise also Zacharias, the brother of Haggeus, was the paternal uncle of Salome, the wife of Joseph the carpenter.
Furthermore, the holy Lady, our Theotokos, had three sisters from Bethlehem, daughters of Mathan the priest and his wife Maria, during the reign of Cleopatra and Sapor the Persian, before the reign of Herod the son of Antipater. The name of the first was Maria; of the second, Sobe; of the third, Anna. The first, therefore, married in Bethlehem and gave birth to Salome the midwife. The second likewise in Bethlehem gave birth to Elisabeth; and the third, in the land of Galilee, gave birth to Mary, the Mother of God. Hence, Salome the midwife, Elisabeth, and the holy Theotokos were the daughters of three sisters; and from this, John the Baptist and our Lord were cousins.
Christ, however, is called the son of Joseph, both because he nurtured him together with his own sons, and because he was of the lineage of Haggeus, the brother of Zacharias the priest, the son of Barachias.
II.
We find, however, in the divine Scripture, that in the sixteenth year of her life the most holy Lady Theotokos gave birth to our Savior and Lord Jesus Christ. And she was baptized by Peter and John the apostles, while Christ himself baptized them. The Theotokos lived for eleven years after the saving passion; thus the sum of the years of her whole life was sixty-one. And the holy John the Theologian also lived until Domitian the Roman emperor, so that the entire time of his life was one hundred and ten years.
III.
But the most holy patriarch of Jerusalem, the great Sophronius, in an encomium celebrating John the Evangelist, teaches the Church of God that the father of the Theologian was Zebedee, and his mother was Salome, who herself was the daughter of Joseph. Joseph had four sons and three daughters: as sons, James, Simon, Jude, and Joseph; and as daughters, Esther, Martha, and Salome, who was the wife of Zebedee and mother of John. Hence, the Savior was John’s uncle, being the brother of Salome, who was the daughter of Joseph.
IV.
The castle called Panormum was built by a certain Greek named Panormus; it is double-walled and composed of iron and brass. In the northern gate of the same castle there stood a rugged tower and a two-headed female statue; where also a notable event occurred. For when that castle was burning in a fierce fire, and the whole city together with its walls was leveled to the ground, that tower of the gate, in which the statue had been placed, remained standing. Moreover, as the fire repeatedly drew closer to the place, the flame would recoil from the statue by five cubits, as if repelled by something. Chosroes, the tyrant of the Persians, took it away, and it is worshipped in Persia to this day, as the superintendent of the vivarium, having been captured there and escaped by flight, declared in the Chronicle of Hippolytus, which is published for the third time. Seek out that extraordinary account.
The place called Smyrnium, near the quadruple portico, has beneath the earth, toward the north, a subterranean grotto of ten cubits; within it are nine statues not far from the holy temple of Saint Theodore. These statues are: three of Constantine the Great and his wife Fausta, and of Hilarion the praepositus, and of the boy...
fourth, the son of Constantine, whose head, as Herodotus and Hippolytus the chroniclers relate, was severed by his father; the father, affected by the grief of this murder, for forty days, they say, followed him in mourning, neither washing his body nor refreshing himself with rest in his bed. Moreover, he made a statue of pure silver, gilding it with much gold—one head of pure gold, I think—with these words inscribed upon the forehead: "My son, struck by injury." With this intent and with the mind of those doing penance, he would bow down, and falling prostrate in a religious manner, he begged for the forgiveness of his sins from God. The remaining seven (read: five) statues are those of Severus, Harmatus, Xeuxippus, Vigilantius—who built the house of Vigilantius—and Eleutherius, who built the palace in the senate. All these were punished by the sword, and their statues were erected by the one who had injured them, so that he might beg for forgiveness from the one who had committed the offense. This custom passed to their children, and from them to others, up to the time of Valens the Arian. The Arians, therefore, not being able to bear the defeat they had received at the hand of Constantine, buried the statues in the earth in the aforementioned four-sided portico, near the temple of Saint Theodore.

Hippolytus the Theban, famous in the XIth century (a), is the author of a Chronicle from Christ's birth to his own times. He cites Eusebius, Eustathius (of Antioch), and Georgius (Syncellus) as chronologers. He also cites Gregory of Nicomedeia, Basil the Great, and the Nicene Fathers, and Symeon Metaphrastes (b), whom he calls κύριον Συμεῶνα ("Lord Symeon"), as if he were still living at that time. Hippolytus the Theban is praised by Mich. Glycas, p. 227, by Codinus, and by others. Nicephorus also has material from the same Chronicle, which, mistakenly, in 11, 3 of his Hist., he attributes to Hippolytus of Portus. The fragments of this Chronicle, after Sambucus (c), Canisius (d), and Lambecius (e), were published more fully from   Vatican Greek manuscripts. He lived in the world after the assumption of human nature by Christ. FRAGMENT I.
From the Chronological Commentary of Hippolytus Thebanus regarding the lineage of our Lord Jesus Christ.
Our Lord Jesus Christ was therefore born in the year 5500.

FRAGMENTA.
Under the Emperor Augustus, He was born in a cave on the 25th day of December.
From Adam to Noah, 2242 years.
And from Noah to Abraham, 1170 years.
And from Abraham to Moses, 444 years.
And from Moses to David, 599 years.
And from David to Christ, 1045 years, making in total 5500 years.
From the incarnation of Christ to the arrival of the Magi, 2 years.
And from the flight into Egypt to the death of Herod, the son of Antipater, 3 years.
They dwelt in Egypt at Heliopolis near Memphis for 3 years.
And from their return to their own country in Galilee to the death of Archelaus, 19 years.
With Archelaus, Augustus also dies in his 88th year, the 56th of his reign.
From the death of Archelaus to the baptism of Christ, 3 years, making in total 30 years.
From the baptism of Christ to the stoning of Zacharias, 1 year.
And from the death of the latter to the death of John, his son, which occurred in prison under the younger Herod, the son of Archelaus, 1 year.
And from the death of John to the saving passion, 6 months.
For John the Baptist lived 33 years; thirty indeed in the desert, and he baptized for three years in the Jordan, preaching repentance. And in this way our Lord Jesus Christ lived for thirty years in his youth.
After his baptism, he chose disciples to preach his kingdom, for 3 years, until the cross.
Whence the Fathers in the Nicene synod ordained that presbyters should not be ordained before thirty years of age, saying that it is sufficient for the disciple to be as the master, and for the servant as his lord.
From the saving passion and the ascension to the stoning of Stephen, 7 years, and from that time until the light which appeared to Paul, 6 months.
And from the baptism and preaching of Paul until the assumption of our most immaculate Lady, there are 3 years, making in total 44 years.
For the all-pure Theotokos lived among men in the temple for 14 years, and in the house of Joseph for 4 months,   and she received the annunciation, and conceiving, she bore our Lord Jesus Christ, making in total 15 years.
And from the incarnation of Christ, she lived 33 years.
Up to the assumption of Christ, in the house of John, 11 years.
And after the Lord's assumption, with his disciples, she lived in the house of John the Evangelist for 11 years.


[1031] {#pg.117.1031}
So that all her years were 59.
This is John, whom Christ loved, and who was a virgin and an evangelist; he remained in Jerusalem in his own house—the mother of the Churches—to which the apostles fled for fear of the Jews. There the Passover was prepared; there the first mystery was delivered to the disciples; there after the resurrection the Lord appeared to them; there the Holy Spirit descended upon the disciples; there he showed Thomas the marks of the nails; there the apostles ordained as the first bishop the son of Joseph, the brother of the Lord—not the son of Zebedee, the brother of John. For Zebedee is called a ship-owner in his own right, and he was the first among the prominent men who were in Galilee.
After the death (of Zebedee), John sold his paternal inheritance in Galilee and purchased a dwelling in Jerusalem; whence, as is said of him, he was known to the high priest.
He received the most holy Theotokos into his house until her resurrection, and afterwards, having preached the Word, he himself was also taken up.
James, however, having become the first bishop of Jerusalem, is called the brother of the Lord according to the flesh, but the son of Joseph the carpenter by his carnal wife. For Joseph had four sons—James, Simon, Judas, and Joseph—and two daughters, Esther and Martha, by his wife Salome, who was the daughter of Haggeus, the brother of Zacharias, the father of John, the son of Barachias, the son of Abia the priest.
And Joseph was a widower at that time, so that John the Baptist and Salome were children of brothers-german. For Haggeus, the son of Barachias, was the paternal uncle of John. Likewise also Zacharias, the brother of Haggeus, was the paternal uncle of Salome, the wife of Joseph the carpenter.
Furthermore, the holy Lady, our Theotokos, had three sisters from Bethlehem, daughters of Mathan the priest and his wife Maria, during the reign of Cleopatra and Sapor the Persian, before the reign of Herod the son of Antipater. The name of the first was Maria; of the second, Sobe; of the third, Anna. The first, therefore, married in Bethlehem and gave birth to Salome the midwife. The second likewise in Bethlehem gave birth to Elisabeth; and the third, in the land of Galilee, gave birth to Mary, the Mother of God. Hence, Salome the midwife, Elisabeth, and the holy Theotokos were the daughters of three sisters; and from this, John the Baptist and our Lord were cousins.
Christ, however, is called the son of Joseph, both because he nurtured him together with his own sons, and because he was of the lineage of Haggeus, the brother of Zacharias the priest, the son of Barachias.


[1033] {#pg.117.1033}
II.
We find, however, in the divine Scripture, that in the sixteenth year of her life the most holy Lady Theotokos gave birth to our Savior and Lord Jesus Christ. And she was baptized by Peter and John the apostles, while Christ himself baptized them. The Theotokos lived for eleven years after the saving passion; thus the sum of the years of her whole life was sixty-one. And the holy John the Theologian also lived until Domitian the Roman emperor, so that the entire time of his life was one hundred and ten years.
III.
But the most holy patriarch of Jerusalem, the great Sophronius, in an encomium celebrating John the Evangelist, teaches the Church of God that the father of the Theologian was Zebedee, and his mother was Salome, who herself was the daughter of Joseph. Joseph had four sons and three daughters: as sons, James, Simon, Jude, and Joseph; and as daughters, Esther, Martha, and Salome, who was the wife of Zebedee and mother of John. Hence, the Savior was John’s uncle, being the brother of Salome, who was the daughter of Joseph.
IV.
The castle called Panormum was built by a certain Greek named Panormus; it is double-walled and composed of iron and brass. In the northern gate of the same castle there stood a rugged tower and a two-headed female statue; where also a notable event occurred. For when that castle was burning in a fierce fire, and the whole city together with its walls was leveled to the ground, that tower of the gate, in which the statue had been placed, remained standing. Moreover, as the fire repeatedly drew closer to the place, the flame would recoil from the statue by five cubits, as if repelled by something. Chosroes, the tyrant of the Persians, took it away, and it is worshipped in Persia to this day, as the superintendent of the vivarium, having been captured there and escaped by flight, declared in the Chronicle of Hippolytus, which is published for the third time. Seek out that extraordinary account.
The place called Smyrnium, near the   quadruple portico, has beneath the earth, toward the north, a subterranean grotto of ten cubits; within it are nine statues not far from the holy temple of Saint Theodore. These statues are: three of Constantine the Great and his wife Fausta, and of Hilarion the praepositus, and of the boy...


[1035] {#pg.117.1035}
A fourth, the son of Constantine, whose head, as Herodotus and Hippolytus the chroniclers relate, was severed by his father; the father, affected by the grief of this murder, for forty days, they say, followed him in mourning, neither washing his body nor refreshing himself with rest in his bed. Moreover, he made a statue of pure silver, gilding it with much gold—one head of pure gold, I think—with these words inscribed upon the forehead: "My son, struck by injury." With this intent and with the mind of those doing penance, he would bow down, and falling prostrate in a religious manner, he begged for the forgiveness of his sins from God. The remaining seven (read: five) statues are those of Severus, Harmatus, Xeuxippus, Vigilantius—who built the house of Vigilantius—and Eleutherius, who built the palace in the senate. All these were punished by the sword, and their statues were erected by the one who had injured them, so that he might beg for forgiveness from the one who had committed the offense. This custom passed to their children, and from them to others, up to the time of Valens the Arian. The Arians, therefore, not being able to bear the defeat they had received at the hand of Constantine, buried the statues in the earth in the aforementioned four-sided portico, near the temple of Saint Theodore. From the Chronological Commentary of Hippolytus the Theban.
1. From the incarnation of Christ until the coming of the Magi, there are two years. And from the flight into Egypt until the death of Herod, son of Antipater, there are three years and five days. Joseph and Mary lived in Egypt, in Heliopolis, which is near Memphis, with Jesus for three years and twenty days. After their return, that they might live in Galilee, until the death of Archelaus, there are [19] years and 47 days. Along with Archelaus, Augustus also dies. He lived, indeed, 88 years and reigned 56 years. From the death of Archelaus, moreover, until the baptism of Christ, which he received from John in the Jordan, there are 16 years and 30 days. From the baptism until the lapidation of Zacharias, there is one year. [For in the meantime, while he was performing the sacred rites according to custom, they pelted him with stones and, hurling them against his head, they slew him; and dragging his body away, they hung it in the valley of Josaphat.] From the lapidation of Zacharias, moreover, until the death of his son John, which was carried out in prison under Herod the Younger, the son of Archelaus, there is one year. From the death of John until the salvific passion, there are 6 months. For John the Baptist lived thirty-six years. For thirty years he was occupied in the desert, while in the other six he administered baptism in the Jordan, preaching repentance. Thus the Lord Jesus Christ was in the thirtieth year of his age. After


[1037] {#pg.117.1037}
A baptism was received, he took and chose for himself disciples, proclaiming with them his kingdom until his saving passion, for three years. For this reason, the canons of the three hundred and eighteen divine Fathers of the Nicene Synod order and decree that no one be chosen into the order of presbyters before the age of thirty. For it is sufficient, they say, for the disciple to be as his master, and for the servant to be as his lord.
II. From the saving passion, and his ascension,   until the stoning of the holy Stephen, the first martyr, until the light which was seen by Saul and appeared to him, there are six months. And from the light that appeared to Saul, and from his baptism and preaching, until the assumption of the holy Virgin Mary, there are three years and forty-four days. The most holy Theotokos and Virgin lived on earth fifty-nine years. And indeed in this manner: in the temple fourteen years, in the house of Joseph four months, and immediately she was greeted and received the herald of the divine birth from the archangel Gabriel; and when she had conceived our Lord Jesus Christ, she gave birth to him on the day of December... the fifteenth year.   But she lived while our Lord Jesus Christ was living here in human nature for thirty-three years. After the Lord's assumption and ascension, she lived with his disciples in the house of John the Evangelist for eleven years, days... the total years of the life of the Virgin and Mother are fifty-nine. This is that same John whom the Lord loved, who was a virgin, who also wrote the Gospel, who remained in Jerusalem at the place called holy Sion, the mother of all churches; this was his dwelling. There, therefore, the apostles fled because of their fear of the Jews. There, with the doors closed, the Lord appeared to his disciples after he had risen from the dead; and he gave them the Holy Spirit, and peace. There, on the eighth day, the Lord confirmed Thomas, who was doubting, showing him the wound in his side and the shape of the nails.   There, the paschal lamb was prepared for our Lord and Savior Christ to eat with his disciples. And from there, having sung hymns, they went out to the Mount of Olives, to a place called Gethsemane. There, the Lord first celebrated the mystery of the Father with his disciples. And he initiated them into the mystical worship, and he shaved their heads, and blessed them, and ordained them, and delivered to them the mystical worship of his commandments and of his great works; and he displayed all the vision and glory of his divine form, and the spectacle of the blessed ones by whom he is beheld, and all the glory, and order, and brilliance of his Church; and he deemed them worthy of the divine communion of his body and blood; and that faithful Nicodemus before his passion, and

after his resurrection, he remained faithful. There they accomplished the first of all the mysteries of Christ, the mystery of the apostles. There the Lord designated his first bishop, and the apostles did so; namely, James, the brother of our Lord. For I do not mean the son of Zebedee, but the brother of our Lord. For James, the disciple of the Lord, was indeed the son of Zebedee, and the brother of John the Evangelist. Zebedee, however, was their father: he is called *idioklēros*, that is to say, having a ship of his own. Among the illustrious men of Galilee, John held the first place. After the death of his father Zebedee, he sold and dispersed the property he had inherited from Zebedee, and bought himself another in Jerusalem.
III. Thus, therefore, he received the holy Theotokos into his home, preaching the word of the truth of Christ in Ephesus in Asia, and he eventually fell asleep in the Lord there, pursuing his name and great deeds with due praises.

And showing him the mark of the nails, he had him eat the Passover with the disciples. From there, having sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives to the place called Gethsemane. Chronicle of the Incarnation of our Lord Jesus Christ, of the great God and our Savior.
Our Lord was born of the Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary in the third year of the one hundred and ninety-fourth Olympiad. That was the forty-second year of the emperor Augustus, and the thirty-second year of the reign of Herod the son of Antipater. Augustus reigned for fifty-six years, and Herod for thirty-seven.
Therefore, Herod died when our Lord was six years old in the flesh. After Herod, his son Archelaus reigned for nine years, and he was sent into exile in Gaul, and Antipas, who was also the son of Herod the son of Antipater, reigned. When Augustus died and Archelaus had been sent into exile, Christ was   fifteen years old from his incarnation. Tiberius succeeded Augustus, and Antipas, also a son of Herod, succeeded Archelaus. Therefore, they are found to have reigned at the same time: Tiberius over the Romans, and Antipas, the son of Herod, over the Jews, as has been said. Thus, the first year of the reign of Tiberius and Herod is found to be the sixteenth year of Christ from his incarnation. These things being so, in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius, the Lord was thirty years old from his incarnation when he came to the baptism of John and began his divine preaching, as the most divine Luke says. In the nineteenth year of the reign of Tiberius, the divine...

It is certain, therefore, that this Herod killed the Forerunner, and being in Jerusalem at the time of the divine passion, sat in judgment on the Lord; and after the life-giving resurrection, he reigned for another five years and was sent into exile with Herodias to Vienne. After him reigned Agrippa, who is also called Herod, son of Aristobulus, of Mariamne, son of the first Herod. This Agrippa, who is also called Herod, killed James the son of Zebedee. He too reigned for seven years, and after him reigned the second Agrippa, under whom Paul was tried in Caesarea by Festus in the 26th year.   And after these things Titus took Jerusalem by storm; and forty years are counted from the passion of the Savior until the capture of Jerusalem. Some write that three hundred myriads died there; the siege lasted for two years, in such a way that they ate their own children because of the severe hunger.

EDITOR'S ADMONITION
Georgides, an author of uncertain age, whom one might perhaps conjecture to have written in the 10th or 11th century, can serve as an example of that genre of writing which flourished in the Middle Ages, both in the East and in the West, in the culling of the writings of the Fathers. Furthermore, because the fragments extracted are inscribed with their author's name, they might perhaps serve at some time to identify those authors whose writings lie anonymous in manuscript codices. Cardinal Angelo Mai produced several fragments of Georgides’ book as a specimen; we are publishing the entire book after. Boissonade.
