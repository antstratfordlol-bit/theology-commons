DRAMA ON THE FALL OF THE FIRST PARENT
The serpent, having crept to our ancestors 
And seduced them by blandishments long ago,
Stupified by that famous disaster,
And calling to memory the ancient curse
Which the nature of mortals received from them,
Since the enemy appeared superior in battle,
Conjecture your misfortune in them,
Having suffered a similar lot.
For when the dragon, who had fallen in a wretched ruin,
Saw the nature of spiritual beings,
And the lower creation brought into existence 
With unspeakable speed, by word alone and by will,
The august, heavenly seat, namely,
Cherubim, Principalities, Seraphim, and Thrones,
Archangels, and the ranks of the formidable Angels,
The earth, heaven, and the entire arrangement of the stars,
Water and fire, air together with the aether,
A luminary passing through the sky, illuminating the day,
And another gracefully brightening the night,
And all the swimming and winged creatures,
And the whole world appearing through the Word;
And seeing man, who by the hand and will alone
Received a more divine existence,
And was shown as the master of all things subject to him,
Differing from all else by his reason,
And held in honor here before all others,
Because he was shown to have been made in the image of God,
He moves war against him out of envy,
Until he drove him out from the seat of Eden, 
In which he had been placed to work and to guard,
And to rejoice in the fruits yielded from thence.
Eden was the miracle of all the places of the East,
Which, as if by a four-fold river, the water irrigated,
Displaying the fertility of all fruits,
Spreading their lovely beauty everywhere,
And most delightful to the eyes of those eating them.
When the deceiver, trembling, had approached it,
Having seen her such as she had come forth from the hand of God,
And from there delighting the eyes with her beauty,
He approaches Eve, the nature more sluggish
And more clever for enticing the man,
Whom God, having pulled a small fragment 
From his side, had formed as a helper and companion of life,
And had given her to him most wisely,
A reward for his sleep and a solace for his labors.
To whom, also setting forth the meaning of the commandments,
He granted the use of all the trees, while only one...
he commanded them not to touch at all; lest, if they should eat, they would pay a terrible penalty of death, but if they should not violate the law concerning it, they might live without sorrow in a blessed series of ages.
