NARRATION OF JOHN, THE MOST REVEREND MONK OF JERUSALEM
I, a humble man and the least of all, desire to demonstrate before this holy and sacred synod of yours, with all truth, how and when and whence this most evil and God-hated heresy of the Christians—or rather, to speak more truly, of the accusers of Christians and the iconoclasts—took its beginning. Intending to use brevity of speech, I have judged it proper to commit the account to paper and writing, so that nothing of the truth should escape me.
When the tyrant, or advisor, of the godless Arabs had died (this man was named Seleiman), Umar succeeded him. Upon assuming authority, he immediately summoned the destroyer Masalman, a most hostile foe, and turned him back from this region. By the grace of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ and of His Mother, the holy Theotokos, he returned to Syria having accomplished nothing of what he had hoped, with great shame and the destruction and death of his own Saracen army. After Umar died, he was succeeded by Yazid, a man who was light-headed, unstable in mind, and not of sound intellect.
Now, there was in Tiberias a leader of the lawless Jews, a sorcerer and instrument of soul-destroying demons, named Sarantapechus, a bitter enemy of the Church of God. Having learned of the instability of the ruler Yazid, this most wicked Jew approached him and attempted to prophesy and predict certain things to him. Having thus crept up to the tyrant and gained his favor, and having made the tyrant’s ears receptive, he said: "I wish, O chief advisor, moved by the benevolence I feel for you, to teach you a way, easy and simple, that will please you, by which length of life will be added to you, and you will remain in possession of your authority for thirty years, provided that you fulfill my words."
And that foolish tyrant, his mind captivated by a longing for longevity (for he was a licentious and dissolute man), replied that whatever he ordered, he would readily do; and if he attained the result, he would reward him with the highest honors. The sorcerous Jew said to him: "Order immediately, with no delay or postponement, that throughout all the regions and places of your dominion..."
Col. 519] A ...and at once, without any delay or postponement, to write an encyclical letter throughout all your jurisdiction, that every iconographic painting, whether on panels or by mosaics on walls, or in sacred vessels and altar coverings, and whatever else of this sort is found, should be erased and utterly destroyed in all the churches of the Christians; and not only so, but also whatever in the marketplaces of the cities exists for the sake of order and beauty in any likeness whatsoever. And moved by a satanic wickedness, the false prophet added that any likeness that was visible should also be displayed, contriving to manifest his hatred toward us.
3. The wretched tyrant, yielding to this with lightness and giving credence to it, sent and removed the holy icons and the remaining figures from every province under him. And in this manner, he stripped the churches of God of their ornamentation through the sorcerer Hebrew, indiscriminately, throughout all his cities and places, before the evil could reach these shores. And since the most religious Christian men refused to destroy the holy icons with their own hands, those who were sent for this purpose—the Amira—pressed into service the God-hated Hebrews and the wretched Arabs; and thus they burned the sacred icons, and the houses of the churches they in places plastered over, and in others scraped away.
4. Having heard these things, the false bishop of Nacolia and those with him imitated the lawless Jews and the impious Arabs, and insulted the churches of God.
5. I judge it worthy to bring to your holy ears also what kind of end that wretched man and sorcerer Hebrew met. For when Ezidus, the chief counselor who did this, had lived for no more than two and a half years after the perpetration of this act, he died and departed into the eternal fire. And the icons were restored to their ancient foundation and honor; but his son, Ulidus by name, indignant that the sorcerer had, as it were, slain his own father, ordered him to be put to death by a most shameful execution, judging him to have received the wages worthy of his false prophecy.
NOTICE [NOTICE]
To the Narrative of John Cameniata on the Sack of Thessalonica
The destruction of the famous city of Thessalonica, under the most illustrious leader and pirate Leo of Tripoli, while Leo son of Basil was emperor, was a sight that not one of the annalists of his age allowed to pass without recording it in clear memory. Yet, as is the nature of annals, they touched upon it only briefly, so that beyond the slaughter of the city, the great massacre of the citizens, and their exportation into slavery, they recorded nothing in particular. John Cameniata alone, being present at the events, and noble among the spoils along with his father, uncle, mother, and brothers, recorded everything accurately and brought it before our eyes as if it were present. He described so vividly the spirits of the citizens, the concern of the emperor, the weakness of the forces against the enemy, and the city not sufficiently prepared to sustain the sudden—or nearly sudden—assault of a most powerful enemy—an enemy against whom no forces equal in strength appeared anywhere in the empire. Indeed, the royal city itself feared greatly and dreaded its own destruction, attributing it to the divine Numine and counsel that Leo had not dared to attempt the siege through the straits of the sea. Cameniata reveals that his father was the exarch of all Greece and Achaia, of a dignity nearly episcopal; and that he earned a ransomed life (though not liberty) for all manner of people.
