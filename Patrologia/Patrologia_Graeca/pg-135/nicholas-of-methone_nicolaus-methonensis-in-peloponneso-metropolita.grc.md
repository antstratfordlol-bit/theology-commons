Nicolaus, Methonensis in Peloponneso episcopus et metropolita, multum Mich. Et alterum quod incipit: Ἐχρῆν μὲν μηδ᾿ ἀπολογεῖσθαι τοῖς οὕτω καινοφώνοις καὶ καινοπίστοις. Nec non tertium De axymis evolvisse se testatur Allatius, lib. II, c. 10, De consensu, § 2, pag. 627, cujus initium: Τὸν μὲν πρῶτον λόγον ἐποιησάμην, ὦ Λατινόφιλε, πρὸς σέ. Aliud scriptum De processione Spiritus sancti idem memorat quod incipit: Βασιλεῦ οὐράνιε, Παράκλητε ἀγαθέ, sed illud juniori cuidam Νικολάῳ λάμψαντι νέῳ Μεθώνης tribuendum suspicatur, qui scripserit post Nicephorum Blemmydam. In bibl. Bavarica asservati sunt præter librum De processione Spiritus sancti Græco-Latinum, liber De sancta Eucharistia in pane fermentato conficienda: — liber De jejunio Latinorum in Sabbato, et de sacerdotum conjugio, et quod in Quadragesima non sit perfecta Missa celebranda. In eadem bibl. atque in aliis obvia hujus Nicolai Discussio theologicæ institutionis Procli, Platonici philosophi. Ex scripto De processione Spiritus
exsistentiam sanctissimi Spiritus designantem, A Deiferi Patres et intelligunt et accipiunt, quemadmodum et processionem ipsam, et productionem. NICOLAUS, BISHOP AND METROPOLITAN OF METHONI IN THE PELOPONNESE
NOTICE
(FABRIC. Biblioth. Gr. vol. VI, p. 294.)
Nicolaus, bishop and metropolitan of Methoni in the Peloponnese, much later than Michael Cerularius and to be referred to the twelfth century, and not to be confused with Nicolaus Cabasilas (a), wrote many things against the Latins, the greatest part of which is still unpublished: the first book *On the Primacy of the Pope* is cited by Nic. Comnenus, p. 366, in the preface to the *Mystagogia*; the second book, *Against the Latins*, p. 356. Allatius, in his *De consensu*, lib. II, c. 10, § 2, p. 627, testifies that he also examined his *Syllogisms on the Procession of the Holy Spirit*, the opening of which is: "It has been acknowledged that the whole Godhead is a monad and the one." And another which begins: "It was not necessary to apologize to those so novel-voiced and novel-believed." Also a third, *On Unleavened Bread*, whose opening is: "I have made my speech, O Latin-lover, to you." The same author mentions another writing *On the Procession of the Holy Spirit* which begins: "O heavenly King, good Comforter," but he suspects that this latter should be attributed to a certain younger Nicolaus, who wrote after Nicephorus Blemmydas. Preserved in the Bavarian library, besides the book *On the Procession of the Holy Spirit* (Greek-Latin), are the book *On the Holy Eucharist to be Consecrated in Leavened Bread*; the book *On the Fasting of the Latins on Saturday*, and *On the Marriage of Priests*, and *That a Complete Mass is Not to be Celebrated in Lent*. In the same library, and in others, one finds this Nicolaus's *Discussion of the Theological Institution of Proclus, the Platonic Philosopher*.
