I.
NOVELLA DE ELECTIONIBUS PONTIFICUM [1]. (A. 1187).
Scriptum imperatoria manu subsignatum, quod irritas facit electiones, quæ fiunt per absentiam pontificis alicujus in urbe commorantis; atque statuit in posterum non aliter fieri electionem debere, nisi omnes in urbe degentes pontifices arcessiti fuerint, et vel præsentes adfuerint, vel sententias suas exhibuerint. Act. mensis Septembris die x feria quarta, indictione 5.
Qui non arguit peccatum fratris sui, ait Scriptura, similis est ei qui venenum relinquit admorso ab animali venenoso. Quorsum autem hoc a majestate mea in orationis ipso vestibulo prolatum sit, ubi progressa fuerit oratio, clarissimo indicabit. Quippe cum sanctissimus Cyzici metropolitanus majestati nostræ nudius-tertius suggesserit, nonnunquam aliqua præter sacros divinosque canones, et religiosas leges in synodicis confratrum suorum conventibus fieri, atque obsecraverit, ut eorum ratio habeatur apud tribunal majestatis meæ, juncto sanctissimo domino meo et universali patriarcha, cum gubernatoris ecclesiæ locum sortita sit mea majestas ab eo qui eam unxit, et imperio præfecit; habeatque licentiam corrigendi ea quæ præter ecclesiasticos canones admittuntur: noluit majestas mea rejicere preces metropolitani supplices, nec indiscussas relinquere. Quamobrem arcessito hodie sanctissimo domino meo et universali patriarcha, utque etiam cæteris sanctissimis patriarchis, videlicet Antiocheno et Hierosolymitano, prætereaque commorantibus in hac magna urbe pontificibus; requisivit ab indicato metropolitano Cyzici, qui et ipse præsens erat, ut audiente hoc religioso cœtu diceret atque proponeret, quæ vellet. Is autem denuo nihil dissimilans, ea ipsa proposuit, de quibus etiam nudiustertius ad majestatem meam retulerat, nimirum divinos sacrosque canones in nonnullis negligi ac violari. And in support of their claim they produced a record of the patriarchal notary, Stephen Narsites, made on the 23rd of August of the 4th indiction, recently passed, which when read aloud ran word for word as follows: "On the 23rd day of the month of August, the 7th day of the week, in the 4th indiction, in the presence of the reverend *episkopianoi* the masters Constantine Chiromaches, Theodore Phialites, and Michael Chamules, we, being deputed by our most holy lord and ecumenical patriarch, and with the knowledge also of the most honorable chartophylax of the most holy Great Church of God, the master Eustathius Chandrenus, to summon the most holy metropolitan of Cyzicus, lord John, so that on the morrow he might come up to the divine and holy synod, and be present at the forthcoming votes for the widowed most holy churches, or else set forth his opinion; and to signify to him that if he did not appear, such votes would proceed even in his absence, because he had been warned about these matters in various ways also by the reverend *episkopianos*, the master Constantine Chiromaches, and had been unwilling to fulfill his notifications; we arrived this day at the cells of the said most holy metropolitan, situated near the venerable monastery of Studium, and finding him lying there on a bed, as one who was not in good health, we told him how we had been deputed by the patriarchal and divine dignity, exhorting him regarding the matters indicated above. For if by chance he were unwilling either to be present on the morrow at the forthcoming (as it was said) votes for the widowed most holy churches, or to set forth his opinion regarding them, these would proceed even in his absence. He, however, replied to us thus: that also at other times votes for churches had been carried out by his fellow-ministers without his presence and opinion. Wherefore let the same be done by them also for the churches now widowed; for he cannot come up to the divine and holy synod and be present at the votes, because he is severely ill. Having been much entreated by us to at least set forth his opinion, he was unwilling to do even this. For the declaration of these things, we have set forth the present record, which, having been transcribed and confirmed in the customary manner, we have delivered in the month and indiction written above. The humble deacon of the most holy Great Church of God and patriarchal notary, Stephen Narsites." 2. The adversaries of the bishop of Cyzicus, therefore, making use of the words of such a record, said that the bishop of Cyzicus had been summoned to all the votes; and that they for their part had done what was their duty, but that he had been unwilling to appear; adding that the *episkopianoi*
had been sent by order of the most religious chartulary, according to custom, to the cells where the metropolitan of Cyzicus was staying, where the patriarchal notary was also present for the second election, as has been recounted; and that they had, according to custom, called him to the votes which were to be conducted by them. These men, having first gone to my most holy lord and ecumenical patriarch, and then to those others, said that they had not found him in his own cells, but had learned that he was staying in a certain suburb of the city. But the metropolitan of Cyzicus resisted even these things more stoutly against his fellow-ministers, adding to the argument of the metropolitan of Iconium that such a summons for an election had never been made to a metropolitan by his brother bishop—even if one were to grant that this could happen—but by those who are customarily sent for the calling of the vote, with the day of the election being formally set, and the messengers being sent by the chartulary; and so, by those summoned, either appearing in person or becoming part of the vote through signatures of agreement or disagreement, the expressed will of their written opinion would necessarily become clear, as well as who seemed to be present through letters and who was absent. Since, therefore, he says, by the account of the metropolitan of Iconium, I was in the great city on the day before the solemn day of the election, and likewise on the days following the election, how can any credence ever be given to those who say that I was not in the great city at the time of the election? And he requested that the voting record be brought forth and examined, to see if the things proposed by his brethren were found recorded therein. And indeed, he says, if I alone were absent from the voting, perhaps what was being put forward would have some basis; but since other metropolitans were also absent from the voting for the churches that were being balloted, and they themselves were not called, it is clearly evident that those who oppose the truth are contradicting it. With regard to the very words of the notice, however, he again set forth the things mentioned above: namely, that he himself, not wishing to conceal the unbridled scandal which was consuming his soul, revealed the cause in the notice of summons, requesting the correction of those things which had moved him to indignation. But the authors of the election, having set aside his defense, had performed the election as they pleased and on their own authority. Following this, the first-seated bishop of Caesarea, who was present, also cried out, saying that he himself, also, from the time he had been ordained bishop, had never been summoned to attend a vote, even though three churches—Dyrrhachium, Miletus, and Christianopolis—had been voted upon. Indeed, not even today was notice given to him by anyone regarding the assembly of the metropolitans at my majesty; but, moved by his own accord, he had gone in with the other high priests to the tribunal of my majesty. The bishop of Leucas also spoke in agreement with the speech of the bishop of Caesarea, complaining that no consideration had been given to him in the votes for the three aforementioned churches. Therefore, he, not having attended, and not
having joined his own votes to the authors of those elections. But the bishop of Caria also entered and said that, having joined the bishops who had met to conduct the elections for the widowed most holy churches, since he did not see some of the bishops residing in the imperial city present, he asked if their opinions were being held. He was told, however, that the bishop of Cyzicus, having been notified by Narsites to be present for the upcoming votes, had said that such elections had taken place in his absence at other times, and it was nothing new for this to be done again. Not discerning that this statement had a good implication, he said that it was in no way possible for those elections to proceed this day, especially because other brothers were also absent on a journey: namely, the prelate of the first see, the bishop of Chalcedon, and the bishop of Leucas. Some of the metropolitans also followed his opinion—the bishop of Methymna, the bishop of Nazianzus, the bishop of Paronaxia, and others. But, he says, those who thought otherwise prevailed, and the elections proceeded. Those who were present—the bishop of Methymna, the bishop of Nazianzus, and the bishop of Paronaxia—accepted this speech of the bishop of Caria as true. Thus, the sides in this matter were as it were joining battle and opposing one another, and a unanimous vote was reached by those who had voted on them. But

My Imperial Majesty, having attended more carefully to the things proposed by the parties, and not wishing for ecclesiastical laws, apostolic and synodical traditions, and their holy canons to be violated—since I also discovered that the opponents of the bishop of Cyzicus, his confreres and fellow ministers, had alleged nothing that had any force, but that rather the elections of the aforementioned churches—Claudiopolis, Maroneia, Dyrrhachium, Miletus, and Christianopolis—had proceeded against what was enacted in the aforementioned holy canons and against the ancient custom received by the Great Church in elections—insisting upon the traces of those very canons, has decided and pronounced (the most holy patriarchs also declaring the same judgment together with my Majesty) that the ordination and the elections of those thus elected, as has been said, possess no force, but are to be considered as if they had not taken place, and the ordination of the one who was ordained for Maroneia is to be judged as null, just as the nineteenth canon of the Council of Antioch, inserted above, dictates, stating clearly in these words: "But if the matter is carried out otherwise than as here defined, the ordination shall have no force;" but that other elections must be held for the said churches, in which the elections and ordinations proceeded against the canons, as has been described.
