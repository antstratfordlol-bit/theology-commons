53 Filius sapiens lætificat patrem, et pater prolis amans in filio prudente delectatur. Dominus quippe est qui mentem indit quando loquendum est, et aurem ad audiendum impertit. Penes ipsum sapientiæ 54 thesaurus reconditur, et ab illo omne donum perfectum emanat. Reges in throno collocat, universique dominatum ipsis elargitur. Nunc ergo aurem præbe, fili, et hac imbutus doctrina, sapiens apud prudentes eris, et apud sapientes prudens ceuseberis. Benedicent tibi populi, et beatum prædicabunt multæ gentes. Disce quæ præ omnibus scitu opus sunt, et prudenter imperii gubernacula accipe. De præsentibus tecum cogita, futura edisce, ut una cum recto consilio experientiam colligas, et præclare res magnas aggredi valebis. Primum quidem tradam quæ gens in qua re prodesse Romanis possit, in qua obesse : item qualiter et quomodo singulæ se habeant, et a qua gente possint et bello impeti et subjugari. Amen. NOTES OF ANSELM BANDURI It is pleasing here to offer some remarks on the aforementioned anonymous history regarding the conversion of the Russians to the faith of Christ. And first, that this anonymous writer says that the Russians adhered to the rite of the Greeks, for the reason that the legates sent to Rome and Constantinople to contemplate both rites preferred the Greek rite to the Roman; almost the same things are narrated by historians concerning the conversion of Wlodomir, duke of the Russians, who received the faith of Christ under the emperors Basil and Constantine, who gave him their sister Anna in marriage; for he also adhered to the Greek rite, because the legates, whom he had sent to Constantinople for that purpose—men who were military and still semi-barbarian—had vehemently praised them, but reported that the rites of the Latins, or other messengers of the same stamp, were not performed with sufficient religious care, and that the temples were poorly adorned; unless this was later invented by the proponents of the Greek schism, since it is established that Wlodomir and most of his sons were primarily zealous for the Catholic faith, two of whom, David and Romanus, or Boris and Gleb, are counted in the catalog of saints even by the Russians, who are united to the Roman Church. But what he says about Cyril is that he is that bishop and apostle of the Slavs, the brother of Saint Methodius, bishop of Olomouc in Moravia, and also an apostle of the Slavs; whose memory is celebrated on the tenth day of March. Saint Cyril was born in Thessalonica and was formerly called Constantine, who on account of his marvelous talent was called the Philosopher; but afterwards, with the consent of the Pope, he was called Cyril, as is clear from the life of the same saints Cyril and Methodius from the Blauduran manuscript, published in the *Acta Sanctorum* on the day of March; which says thus: "In the time of the emperor Michael, there was a man noble by birth, born in the city of Thessalonica, by the name of Constantine, who on account of his marvelous talent was called the Philosopher." He, fifty days before his death, by the permission of the Supreme Pontiff, took for himself the name Cyril, etc. The same is held in another life of the same published in the same place: and also in the history of the kingdom of the Slavs by the priest of Dioclea. But it is incorrectly stated in the Breviary, which the Bohemians, Moravians, and Poles use today, that the father of Cyril and Methodius was Constantine, surnamed the Philosopher; for this was indeed the name of Cyril himself, as is evident from the things said above, and from the assertion of Anastasius, an eyewitness, in a letter to Charles the Bald, King of the Franks, written in the year [blank], in which he announces that the body of the holy Pope Clement was brought to Rome by Constantine the Philosopher; yet it is established that this was done by Cyril, the brother of Methodius; whose father was Leo the Patrician, as writes the Priest of Dioclea, page [blank] of the edition of Joannes Lucius: but the things which Lucius there notes concerning Constantine the Philosopher, Cyril, and Methodius, are entirely futile. [Notes section] ...says that Constantine the Philosopher was the inventor of the Slavonic letters: "The Slavonic letters," he says, "discovered by a certain philosopher Constantine, by which due praises to God resound, we rightly praise." Assuredly this our anonymous writer, the Dioclean, the Acts of Saint Cyril, and the remaining writers attribute this—following Cyril, who was also named Constantine, as we have shown above—to him; and indeed there is a perpetual tradition among our people that one of the two Slavonic alphabets, called Kiuriliza, is the very one of Cyril, named from the Slav name Kiurul, which in Latin sounds "Cyril": while the other, called Bukuiza, belongs to the divine Jerome. But what I myself think concerning the alphabet of S. Jerome and the version of the Holy Scriptures made by him, as they say, into the Slavonic language, I will willingly confess. In the works of the divine Jerome I have nowhere found even a trace of the Slavonic language: which, if he had known it, he would undoubtedly have used in explaining the Scriptures, and frequently when dealing with Hebrew names. Where, however, the divine Jerome calls it his "native speech" or "the language of our people," no learned man is ignorant that it is to be understood of the Latin language, which at that time was in use throughout the whole Roman world.
