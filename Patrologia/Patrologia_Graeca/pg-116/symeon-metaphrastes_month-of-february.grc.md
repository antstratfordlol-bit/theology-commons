MONTH OF FEBRUARY
THE CONTEST OF THE HOLY AND GLORIOUS HIEROMARTYR BLASIUS, BISHOP OF SEBASTEA
I. At the time when the cult of idols prevailed and many men worshipped created images and things, then also the faith of all the saints flourished, along with which flourished the faith of the holy and glorious hieromartyr Blasius. For this holy and venerable man, Blasius, having lived his whole life in a mild manner of conduct—as it is written in Job—was innocent, blameless, truthful, pious, and religious, abstaining from every evil deed. Wherefore, because of his pure and irreproachable life, the faithful who were in the city of Sebastea in Cappadocia chose him as bishop. But Saint Blasius, fearing the impiety and lawlessness of those who then held power, departed to the mountain called Argeus and dwelt in a cave. And the wild beasts would gather to him and be blessed by him; and if it chanced that one of them fell into his meditation, they would wait at the cave as if endowed with reason, and they would not depart from the saint unless he laid his hand upon them, blessing them. II. In those days, the governor Agricolaus ordered all kinds of animals to be hunted down for the wild-beast combat and destruction of the holy martyrs who were then suffering, and the hunters, having gone out into the mountains, came even to this mountain called
Argaeus, where the holy and venerable man and bishop, Blasius, was hiding. And when the hunters approached the cave and saw at its entrance a multitude of wild animals standing by and leaping about, they said to one another, "What could this mean?" And having come closer, they found inside the cave the holy Blasius sitting as if in a bridal chamber, distributing what was needed to each one through blessings and healings, and offering his prayers to God. And immediately, having turned back, they reported to the governor Agricolaus what they had seen. The governor ordered more soldiers to accompany them, so that as many Christians as they might find hiding there, they should bring to him. And the sent soldiers, having gone into the mountain and entered inside the cave, found only the holy Blasius praying and glorifying the Lord, and they said to him, "Come out quickly; the governor calls you." Seeing them, the holy Blasius rejoiced greatly and said, "Come, children, let us go together; for the Lord has remembered me today. For in this night He appeared to me a third time, saying: 'Arise, offer me sacrifices according to the custom of your priesthood.' Now therefore, my children, you have come well; may my Lord Jesus Christ be with you."
III.
As they were coming, Gentiles on the road were turning to the knowledge of God through his gentleness and teaching. For indeed, through his unceasing prayers and supplications, the Lord performed healings; and when the infirm were brought to him, he would lay his hands upon them and send them all away whole. Nor did he heal only men, but also beasts and cattle. Thus, it happened that if anyone were to swallow a bone or anything of the sort, they would suddenly bring the sufferer to him, and through his holy prayer, he would immediately restore him to health. Whence it happened that a certain woman, who had an only son, while he was eating a portion of fish, swallowed a bone and remained speechless. The mother of the child, therefore, having heard about the holy man, brought him and cast him at his feet, half-dead, crying out with wailings and saying to the holy man, "Have mercy on my son, servant of our Savior Jesus Christ, for he is my only one!" And she recounted to the holy man what had suddenly happened to her child. The holy man of God and great priest, Blasius, having laid his hand upon the throat of the child, prayed thus, lifting his eye to heaven and saying to God: "You who have become a prompt healer and savior to those who call upon you in truth, hear my supplication and, by your invisible power, remove the bone lodged in this child, healing him; and if from now on, whether in men or in infants or in cattle, any such thing should ever happen, and anyone there should remember my name, saying, 'O God, by the intercession of your servant Blasius, hasten your help,' do you immediately perform the healing upon him to the glory and honor of your name." And when he had said these things, he immediately restored the boy to health as he was before, and returned him to his mother, while his mouth never ceased praising and glorifying God. The martyr of God was celebrated by the speech of men
not only in Sebastea, but also in Nicopolis; for the martyr was also learned in the science of medicine.
IV. As the holy man was traveling along the road, it happened that a pig belonging to a certain poor woman, who possessed nothing else, was snatched away by a wolf. The woman, being a believer, approached the holy martyr against the beast. The holy priest and martyr of Christ, Blasius, smiling, said to her, “Do not be sad, woman, nor weep; for your pig shall be restored to you alive and unharmed.” And the holy man continued on his journey; and while he was hastening on his way, the wolf, having kept the pig unharmed, returned it to the widowed woman. When the holy Blasius had entered the metropolis of Sebastea, the governor Agricolaus ordered him to be cast into prison. And on the following day, the magistrate, sitting upon the tribunal, ordered the holy Blasius to be brought forward. When he stood before him, the governor, seeing him, began first to use blandishments toward him, and greeted him first, saying, “Hail, most beloved Blasius, friend of the gods and ours.” But holy Blasius said to him, “Hail to you also, excellent governor; but do not call them gods, but rather unclean and wicked demons, which shall be delivered to eternal and unending fire along with those who worship them.” The governor, enraged at these words, ordered him to be beaten with rough cudgels. Being beaten for many hours, the martyr of Christ, Blasius, said to the governor, “O man devoid of sense and deceiver of souls, do you think to draw me away from the confession of my God by your tortures? But you cannot. For I have Jesus Christ, who strengthens me. Do what you will for the rest.”
V. But when the most impious governor could not move him from the faith in Christ, he ordered him to be led back into prison. When that pious old woman and widow learned of the endurance and patience of the holy hieromartyr Blasius and his constancy of faith, she slaughtered the pig she had received back from the beast by the command of the holy martyr; and she cooked its head and feet, and put them in a basket with seeds of the earth and some other things from her poverty along with fruits. Having also lit wax candles, she brought them to the holy martyr in prison, and fell at his feet, begging and entreating him to partake of them. The holy hieromartyr of Christ, Blasius, having given thanks and partaken of them, blessed the woman and then instructed her, saying, “Woman, in this manner perform my memory, and every good thing from my God shall not depart from your house; furthermore, if anyone, imitating you, performs my memory in this way, he shall have the gift and blessing of my God unceasingly all the days of his life.” And that honorable and blessed old woman, having received the command from the holy martyr Blasius, praising God, went away joyfully to her house; and in this blessed widow was fulfilled the Scripture which says, “It shall be said everywhere, she did it, for his memory” (Matt. xxvi, 13).
THE CONTEST OF ST. BLASIUS AND HIS COMPANIONS
VI. The tyrant, having sat down a second time in the tribunal, ordered the martyr of God to be brought forward. When he was brought, the governor said to him, "Blasius, do you sacrifice to the gods, or do you wish to perish miserably?" Saint Blasius said, "Let the gods who did not make the heaven and the earth perish. The death which you threaten me with, O tyrant, will be to me the procurer of eternal life." The governor, seeing the immutability of his faith, ordered him to be hung upon a stake and lacerated severely. While the martyr of God, Blasius, was being lacerated for a long time, he said to the governor, "O most impious and lawless man, do you think to frighten me with blows? I have Jesus Christ who strengthens me, and I do not fear your visible torments, looking instead toward the expected goods which are promised to those who seek Him." The jailers, having seen these women, seized and led them to the governor, saying to him that "These also are Christians." Having seen them, the governor himself said to them, "Be persuaded and sacrifice to the gods, that you may be judged worthy of both glory and great honors from me; otherwise, you are about to die bitterly." Those venerable and holy women, answering, said to him, "If you wish, O governor, that we should sacrifice to your gods, let us go together to the edge of the lake, and do you put your gods into a sack and seal them with lead, while we, having washed our faces in the lake, will approach and sacrifice to them." Having seen these things, the governor changed his expression, and becoming enraged, he roared like a lion, and clapping his hands, he was very angry at the soldiers and said, "O most wicked and terrible servants, why did you not hold the gods so they would not be thrown into the depths of the lake?" The soldiers, answering, said to the governor, "O most powerful governor, these women spoke a lie to you and sank your gods. We, not knowing this, did not hold them." But those venerable and holy women said, "The true God does not suffer a lie, but deaf wood and stones and gold and silver do, and all who trust in them." Then the governor, becoming enraged, ordered a very intense furnace to be ignited, and lead to be melted, and iron combs to be brought, and seven bronze combs to be heated, and a bright linen shroud to be spread in another place; and he said to the holy women, "Choose one of the two: either to worship and sacrifice to the gods, and save your souls, walking on the linen, or, if you do not wish, to experience the torments which are prepared for you." Amen.

MONTH OF FEBRUARY
THE CONTEST OF THE HOLY AND GLORIOUS HIEROMARTYR BLASIUS, BISHOP OF SEBASTEA
I. At the time when the cult of idols prevailed and many men worshipped created images and things, then also the faith of all the saints flourished, along with which flourished the faith of the holy and glorious hieromartyr Blasius. For this holy and venerable man, Blasius, having lived his whole life in a mild manner of conduct—as it is written in Job—was innocent, blameless, truthful, pious, and religious, abstaining from every evil deed. Wherefore, because of his pure and irreproachable life, the faithful who were in the city of Sebastea in Cappadocia chose him as bishop. But Saint Blasius, fearing the impiety and lawlessness of those who then held power, departed to the mountain called Argeus and dwelt in a cave. And the wild beasts would gather to him and be blessed by him; and if it chanced that one of them fell into his meditation, they would wait at the cave as if endowed with reason, and they would not depart from the saint unless he laid his hand upon them, blessing them.   II. In those days, the governor Agricolaus ordered all kinds of animals to be hunted down for the wild-beast combat and destruction of the holy martyrs who were then suffering, and the hunters, having gone out into the mountains, came even to this mountain called


[819] {#pg.116.819}
Argaeus, where the holy and venerable man and bishop, Blasius, was hiding. And when the hunters approached the cave and saw at its entrance a multitude of wild animals standing by and leaping about, they said to one another, "What could this mean?" And having come closer, they found inside the cave the holy Blasius sitting as if in a bridal chamber, distributing what was needed to each one through blessings and healings, and offering his prayers to God. And immediately, having turned back, they reported to the governor Agricolaus what they had seen. The governor ordered more soldiers to accompany them, so that as many Christians as they might find hiding there, they should bring to him. And the sent soldiers, having gone into the mountain and entered inside the cave, found only the holy Blasius praying and glorifying the Lord, and they said to him, "Come out quickly; the governor calls you."   Seeing them, the holy Blasius rejoiced greatly and said, "Come, children, let us go together; for the Lord has remembered me today. For in this night He appeared to me a third time, saying: 'Arise, offer me sacrifices according to the custom of your priesthood.' Now therefore, my children, you have come well; may my Lord Jesus Christ be with you."
III. As they were coming, Gentiles on the road were turning to the knowledge of God through his gentleness and teaching. For indeed, through his unceasing prayers and supplications, the Lord performed healings; and when the infirm were brought to him, he would lay his hands upon them and send them all away whole. Nor did he heal only men, but also beasts and cattle. Thus, it happened that if anyone were to swallow a bone or anything of the sort, they would suddenly bring the sufferer to him, and through his holy prayer, he would immediately restore him to health.   Whence it happened that a certain woman, who had an only son, while he was eating a portion of fish, swallowed a bone and remained speechless. The mother of the child, therefore, having heard about the holy man, brought him and cast him at his feet, half-dead, crying out with wailings and saying to the holy man, "Have mercy on my son, servant of our Savior Jesus Christ, for he is my only one!" And she recounted to the holy man what had suddenly happened to her child. The holy man of God and great priest, Blasius, having laid his hand upon the throat of the child, prayed thus, lifting his eye to heaven and saying to God: "You who have become a prompt healer and savior to those who call upon you in truth, hear my supplication and, by your invisible power, remove the bone lodged in this child, healing him; and if from now on, whether in men or in infants or in cattle, any such thing should ever happen, and anyone there should remember my name, saying, 'O God, by the intercession of your servant Blasius, hasten your help,' do you immediately perform the healing upon him to the glory and honor of your name."   And when he had said these things, he immediately restored the boy to health as he was before, and returned him to his mother, while his mouth never ceased praising and glorifying God. The martyr of God was celebrated by the speech of men


[821] {#pg.116.821}
not only in Sebastea, but also in Nicopolis; for the martyr was also learned in the science of medicine.   IV. As the holy man was traveling along the road, it happened that a pig belonging to a certain poor woman, who possessed nothing else, was snatched away by a wolf. The woman, being a believer, approached the holy martyr against the beast. The holy priest and martyr of Christ, Blasius, smiling, said to her, “Do not be sad, woman, nor weep; for your pig shall be restored to you alive and unharmed.” And the holy man continued on his journey; and while he was hastening on his way, the wolf, having kept the pig unharmed, returned it to the widowed woman. When the holy Blasius had entered the metropolis of Sebastea, the governor Agricolaus ordered him to be cast into prison. And on the following day, the magistrate, sitting upon the tribunal, ordered the holy Blasius to be brought forward.   When he stood before him, the governor, seeing him, began first to use blandishments toward him, and greeted him first, saying, “Hail, most beloved Blasius, friend of the gods and ours.” But holy Blasius said to him, “Hail to you also, excellent governor; but do not call them gods, but rather unclean and wicked demons, which shall be delivered to eternal and unending fire along with those who worship them.” The governor, enraged at these words, ordered him to be beaten with rough cudgels. Being beaten for many hours, the martyr of Christ, Blasius, said to the governor, “O man devoid of sense and deceiver of souls, do you think to draw me away from the confession of my God by your tortures? But you cannot. For I have Jesus Christ, who strengthens me. Do what you will for the rest.”
V. But when the most impious governor could not move him from the faith in Christ, he ordered him to be led back into prison.   When that pious old woman and widow learned of the endurance and patience of the holy hieromartyr Blasius and his constancy of faith, she slaughtered the pig she had received back from the beast by the command of the holy martyr; and she cooked its head and feet, and put them in a basket with seeds of the earth and some other things from her poverty along with fruits. Having also lit wax candles, she brought them to the holy martyr in prison, and fell at his feet, begging and entreating him to partake of them. The holy hieromartyr of Christ, Blasius, having given thanks and partaken of them, blessed the woman and then instructed her, saying, “Woman, in this manner perform my memory, and every good thing from my God shall not depart from your house; furthermore, if anyone, imitating you, performs my memory in this way, he shall have the gift and blessing of my God unceasingly all the days of his life.” And that honorable and blessed old woman, having received the command from the holy martyr Blasius, praising God, went away joyfully to her house; and in this blessed widow was fulfilled the Scripture which says, “It shall be said everywhere, she did it, for his memory” (Matt. xxvi, 13).
