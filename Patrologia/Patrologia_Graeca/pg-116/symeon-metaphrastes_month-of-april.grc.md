(Acta SS. Bolland ad diem 2 Aprilis, Græce in cod. Paris. 1481.)
1. Furebat in Christianos Saporius rex Persarum, et Magis, qui erant pars Persarum, jussit ut eos persequerentur. Qui etiam acrius invadentes, eos quos persequebantur, cogebant colere ignem et solem. Qua de causa beatus quoque Acepsimas ab eis comprehenditur, qui ortus quidem erat ex patria Naesson (quæ est quidem sita in finibus Persarum; est autem episcopali ornata dignitate), ætate senex, ut qui esset octoginta annos natus : senex prudentia, mitis et moderatus, ab ira alienus, abstinenter vitam agens, egenos impertiens suis facultatibus, intentus orationibus, forma elegans, anima elegantior, et externis motibus iis qui illos cernehant, significans internam constitutionem, et factis et verbis pietatem enuntians, et vitæ et rectorum dogmatum magister effectus Christianis. Quo factum est, ut totam adversariorum aciem tanquam adversus fortissimum antagonistam in se evocaverit. Circa quem quidem, etiam antequam comprehenderetur, fit tale quid prophetice :

Propterea tanquam flos agri exsiccaberis, et vita miserabiliter eversa, traderis igni, qui non potest exstingui: ut quem nunc adoras, illic re ipsa punitus sentias, non esse ignem Deum, sed ipsum quoque alium nosse opificem, qui est etiam Deus omnium, quæ cadunt sub aspectum.
V. His majore ira incenso mago, graviores catenæ sanctum circumligarunt et eum tunc interior accepit custodia. Die autem sequenti comprehensus etiam fuit maxime venerandus presbyter Joseph, ortus ex pago, qui vocatur Bethlabuca, quod  exponitur, recte scribentis; ipse quoque senex et jam intra terminos Davidicos, ut qui esset septuaginta annos natus. Senectuti autem decori erat quædam gratia, et species venerandos canos reddebat lætiores, cujus anima quidem zelum spirabat, et dogma Christianorum mirandum in modum defendebat. Summe vero is deditus erat temperantiæ, et continebatur cor ejus metu divino. Præterea autem comprehenditur etiam in primis colendus diaconus Aithalas. Eum pagus aluit Bethnoadara, vir et spiritu fervens, et qui Christi charitate valde accendebatur. Erant autem ei mores propensi ad arguendum, et pleni dicendi libertate instar magni Eliæ, ut qui vitæ, ut opinor, sequerentur libertatem et animæ puritatem. Florebat vero in eo quædam quoque gravitas, quæ ei conciliabat ab omnibus venerationem. Cum autem ambo producti fuissent ante judicem, is torvo oculo eos intuens: O genus, inquit, perditissimum! cur in simpliciores homines irrepentes, et vestris doctrinis eorum oculos præstringentes, deducitis ad falsam Christianorum religionem? Beatus autem dixit Joseph: Nos nec uti præstigiis, nec fallere didicimus. Verum autem et stabile dogma defendentes, eos qui erant circa religionem deducimus ad veritatem, docentes adorare unum Deum, qui est cum aliis rebus Dominus solis et ignis. Dixit vero magnus: Quodnam autem dogma est veritati consentaneum? idne, quod rex totius terræ ipse et ejus proceres defendunt, an quod viles et abjecti homines, sicut vos, qui decepti estis, prædicant?

Εὐτελεῖς μὲν ἡμεῖς, ὁ τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἱερεὺς ἀπεκρίνατο, τοῦτο τῆς ἐντολῆς Χριστοῦ κελευούσης· ἐπεὶ οὐκ ἐν πλούτῳ καὶ ὑπερηφανίᾳ εὐδόκησεν ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν ὁδὸν ἀνθρώπου κατευθύνεσθαι· διὰ τοῦτο καὶ πολλάκις γοῦν ἔχοντες ἑαυτοὺς ταπεινοῦμεν, τὰ ὄντα πένησι προτέμενοι, πλοῦτον ἀντιλαβεῖν αἰώνιον ἐκδεχόμενοι καὶ δόξαν τὴν οὐδέποτε διαπίπτουσαν. Ὁ μάγος, Ὑμεῖς, εἶπεν, ὄκνῳ δουλούμενοι ἀργίαν τε περὶ πλείονος ἔργου ποιούμενοι περιέρχεσθε τὰς οἰκίας προσαιτεῖται τινες ἄθλιοι καὶ χερσὶ προσέχοντες ἄλλοτρίαις. Ἡμεῖς μὲν γὰρ ἰδίοις χερσὶ πονούμενοι, πορισμοῦ δικαίου κοινωνοῦμεν τοῖς δεομένοις· ὑμεῖς δὲ πλεονεξίᾳ προσκείμενοι οὐ μόνον οὐδ᾿ ἐτιοῦν ἐργάζεσθε, ἀλλὰ προσαφαιρεῖσθε καὶ τὰ ἀλλότρια. Ὅτι δὲ ἡμᾶς πλούτου ἔφης ἐρᾶν, τί τὸ προσὸν ἐκείνῳ κατανοήσαντες ἀγαθὸν, προσδεδέσθαι ἂν αὐτῷ εἴχομεν; Οὐχὶ δραπέτης ἐστὶν καὶ κομιδῇ ἄπιστος; Οὐχὶ τούτῳ μὲν πρόσεστι νῦν, αὔριον δὲ πρὸς ἄλλον μεταφοιτᾷ τὸν τοῦ δεσπότην ὥσπερ ἐξαρνησάμενος; Οὐ πόνων οὗτος καὶ πολέμων δημιουργὸς ἄντικρυς; Οὐχὶ ληστῶν καὶ κλεπτῶν ἐφάλκεται ὀφθαλμούς; Εἰ δὲ τὴν ἐκ τούτου φῇς ἢ δόξαν ἢ μέγα τι τὴν δόξαν λογίζῃ, τί τῶν ὀνείρων αὕτη διαφέρει καὶ τῶν σκιῶν; Ὀλίγον εὔφρανεν, εἶθ᾿ ἑσπέρας καταλαθούσης, ἀπέστη καὶ πρὸς τὸ μὴ ὂν ᾤχετο. Εἰ δὲ καὶ μικρόν τι τῷ παρόντι βίῳ συμπαραμένοι, ἀλλ᾿ ὁ θάνατος ἤδη καταλαβὼν καὶ αὐτὴν ἔχει συναπιοῦσαν· πικραὶ δὲ κολάσεις τὸν ἐνταῦθα ταῖς ἡδοναῖς ἐφέντα διὰ παντὸς ἕξουσιν.

VI. Viles quidem et abjecti homines sumus nos, respondit Christi sacerdos, hoc jubente Christi praecepto, quoniam non in divitiis et superbia visum est Deo nostro viam hominis dirigere. Et ideo nos saepe nostra sponte nos humiles reddimus, facultates nostras prodigentes pauperibus, pro iis nos accepturos aeternas divitias. Nam si in congregandis divitiis studium poneremus, tantum ex nostris manibus commodi percepissemus, et tantas opes collegissemus, quantas nec tibi quidem licet, qui eas colligis ex pauperibus, et metis alienos labores. Nos enim propriis manibus laborantes, juste quaesitis pauperes impertimus. Vos autem soli avaritiae dediti et plura habendi cupiditati, non solum nihil ipsi operamini, sed praeterea etiam aufertis aliena. Quoniam vero nos quoque dicis amare divitias: quodnam in eis bonum inesse animadvertentes, nos ad eas parandas defigeremus? Non sunt ex fugitivae et valde infideles? Non hodie quidem huic adsunt, cras vero migrant ad alium, priori domino quodammodo abnegato? Non magnam in eum, qui possidet, concitant invidiam? Non ex labores et bella aperte architectantur? Non latronum et furum attrahunt oculos? Quod si dicis ex iis aliquain oriri voluptatem, aut gloriam, magnum quid esse reputes? quidnam ea differet a somniis et umbris? Parva affect voluptate; deinde cum adventarit vespera, exstincta est et redacta in nihilum. Quod si etiam in praesenti vita parum manserit, mors tamen jam adveniens, eam quoque habet simul abeuntem; acerba autem supplicia eum, qui se hic dedidit voluptatibus, tenebunt perpetuo.
VII. Haec cum sic dicerentur, interpellans magus: Has, inquit, prolixas mittens nugas, maximo deo soli et igni cultum. ut par est, exhibe. Ad haec dixit Christi adorator: Ne erres, neque putes te hoc unquam tuis esse contemplaturum oculis: me, inquam, relicto effectore solis et ignis, illius opera adorare, et adeo repugnare meis verbis et doctrinae. Crudelissimus autem magus, postquam audivit haec verba, ira accensus, jubet statim eum extensum verberari virgis mali punicae, cujus rami erant spinosi. Cum vero sacras illas carnes lania rent filii interitus: Ago tibi gratias, dicebat athleta, in caelum extendens oculos, quod dignatus sis me meo sanguine tinctum, peccati sordes pure abluere. VIII. Ad venerandum autem aspiciens Aithalam  judex, qui erat animo implacabilis: Tu vero quid dicis, inquit? Implebisne jussum regis, et adorabis maximum solem, et gustabis sanguinem, et cum muliere habebis consuetudinem, et liberaberis ab iis quæ tibi impendent, malis: an eris etiam, sicut alii, contumax et inobediens? Christi autem martyr Aithalas, vere semper florens et cœlestis planta pietatis: Tuæ, inquit, immundæ et caninæ appetitionis est hoc munus, sanguinem gustare, et pro Deo, quæ Dei sunt, colere. Absit autem, ut ego adeo cæcutiam corporis et animæ oculis, ut hunc videns solem, ad eum usque consistam, et Deum omnino putem id quod apparet, et non ulterius progredior sincerio-re oculo cogitationis, et  Deum quidem id, quod movet: rem vero creatam reputem id, quod movetur. Si autem mihi sic judicanti et plagas et mortem minaris, non ideo mihi persuadebis, ut discam tuum dogma, et negem veram religionem. Propter hæc iratus impius: Sed nullus, inquit, sanæ mentis mortem vitæ prætulerit, idque propter stultam et minime consideratam constantiam. Quamobrem nec tibi credendum est dicenti te paratum esse mori pro tuo dogmate et religione. Respondit Sanctus: Vestri, qui a vana spe pendent, et nullam æternæ vitæ habent exspectationem, postquam hinc excesserint, jure existimari potuerunt esse et vitæ cupidi, et amantes corporis. Quorum autem spe vera et  firma alitur anima, quod et ea sit immortalis, et sit rursus cum suo corpore coitura post mortis dissolutionem, et æternæ vitæ futura particeps, iis præsens vita res est parva et contemnenda, et mors minime terribilis. V.

He was, moreover, devoted to temperance, and his heart was kept by the fear of God. VI. We are indeed common and abject men, the priest of Christ replied, for Christ’s commandment so orders it, since it did not seem good to our God that the way of man should be directed in riches and pride. And therefore we often humble ourselves of our own accord, bestowing what we possess upon the poor, expecting in exchange to receive eternal riches and glory that never fails. For we, working with our own hands, share the fruits of our just labors with those in need; but you, bent on greed, not only do nothing yourselves, but even steal the property of others. But as for your saying that we love wealth, what good have you perceived in it for which we would be so bound to it? Is it not a fugitive and utterly untrustworthy thing? Does it not stay with one man today, only to migrate tomorrow to another, having, as it were, renounced its former master? Is it not the manifest creator of toils and wars? Is it not what attracts the eyes of robbers and thieves? But if you say that pleasure or glory arises from it, and you consider this glory to be a great thing, how does it differ from dreams and shadows? It delights for a little while, then, when evening comes, it is extinguished and departs into nothingness. And even if it should remain a little while in the present life, death, already drawing near, holds it as well, only for it to depart along with the deceased; but bitter punishments will hold forever the man who has given himself over to pleasures here.”
VII. When these things had been said, the magus interrupted and said, “Cease these tedious trifles, and render the worship that is due to the greatest god, the sun and the most shining fire.” To this the worshiper of Christ said, “Do not err, nor think that you will ever see this with your own eyes: that I, having abandoned the Creator of the sun and the fire, would worship His works, and so contradict my own words and teaching.” But the most cruel magus, when he heard these words, incensed with anger, immediately ordered him to be stretched out and beaten with rods of pomegranate, whose branches were thorny. And as the sons of perdition tore at those sacred reaches of flesh, “I give thanks to Thee,” said the athlete, lifting his eyes to heaven, “that Thou hast deigned to have me baptized in my own blood, to wash away the filth of sin purely.” The executioners, filled with even greater anger at what he had said, beat him with such blows that his voice was cut off. Then, binding him with two chains, they shut him in the prison, giving to Acepsimas a beautiful, albeit involuntary, consolation. For he happened to have been imprisoned there before.
VIII. Looking toward the most venerable Aeithalas, the judge, who was implacable in his spirit, said: "What do you say? Will you fulfill the king's command, and worship the greatest sun, and taste blood, and have relations with a woman, and be freed from the evils that threaten you; or will you also, like the others, be stubborn and disobedient?" But the martyr of Christ, Aeithalas, that truly ever-flourishing plant of piety and heavenly work, said: "This is the task of your own unclean and dog-like appetite, to taste blood and to worship the things of God instead of God. But may it never happen to me to be so wretchedly blinded in the eyes of both body and soul that, while seeing this sun, I stop at it and think that the visible thing is altogether God, without proceeding further with a clearer eye of the mind, and considering that which moves, God, and that which is moved, a creature. If, to me as I judge in this way, you threaten lashes and death, you will not thereby persuade me to learn your dogma and abandon the true religion." Enraged at these words, the impious one said: "But no man of sound mind would prefer death to life, and that for the sake of a stupid and thoroughly unconsidered stubbornness. Therefore, you are not to be believed when you say that you are prepared to die for your dogma and religion." The holy man replied: "Those of your sort, who hang upon empty hopes and have no expectation of an unending life after this departure, might reasonably be thought to be lovers of life and lovers of the body. But for those whom true and firm hopes nourish in the soul, that it is itself immortal and is destined to reunite with its own body after the dissolution of death and to share in eternal life, for them this present life is a small and despicable thing, and death is not at all terrible. For true is our God and Master, who says: 'Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul.'" (Lc. XII, 4).
IX. But that adamant athlete, whose spirit was broken by nothing, just as if he felt no pain from such a great torment, looking with a noble and lofty mind toward the judge, said: "As it seems, the bodies of men that are torn apart provide you some pleasure, in the manner of dogs or ravens, desiring to be filled by them. Know, therefore, clearly, that I hold these torments brought by you of no account. Either devise, then, more novel ones, or know that I count these as nothing." And the Magus, immediately turning to the executioners, said: "Why do you show mercy and spare the impious one with the scourges? For truly you see...
how, while being deemed worthy of leniency, he uses great freedom in his insults against us." But they, as if awakened by a goad by his words, attacked with such vigor that, from the weight of those who had climbed upon him, and the intensity of those pulling, and the cruelty of those scourging, the joints of his body were completely loosened, his bones were broken, and his flesh was shredded. Having therefore carried him, since he could not use his feet, they threw him like a burden into the prison, where also the pair of saints was confined.
X. Five days passed, and they brought them forth from the prison to a place called Paradise, where there also happened to be a temple of the fire worshipped by them, in which the wicked one sat enthroned and said to the saints: "Tell me quickly, do you still persist in the same foolish opinion, or have you changed your counsel to something better and salutary?" The saints answered immediately as if with one mouth: "Let it be known to you, judge, that the opinion we have had from the beginning, in this we still continue, and until the end we shall have the same; not by threats, not by tortures, not by gifts, nor by anything else at all are we changed. For we worship one God, and we have recognized Him alone to be the Lord of all things that are." Hearing this, the Magus devised another new torture, and ordered them to be girded with ropes through their armpits, and their loins, and their shins, and upon each rope a wooden bar to be inserted, and to be turned around, and tightly twisted by the ropes; and when this was done, it was a grievous sight, and one worthy of tears even to gentle eyes. For the wooden bars were turned and the ropes were twisted, and consequently the flesh was compressed, and the crushing was very severe; for even to those standing far away, the sound was heard as if from the bones. And that which was said to them during the torture was: "Do not oppose the will of the King." Then that which was spoken in return by the saints: "Those who yield to the will of your king oppose the will of God." And it was indeed the third hour when the torture began, and it was prolonged until the sixth hour while the athletes suffered in this way.
XI. Being frustrated, therefore, the judge ordered them to be taken back again to the prison. Wherefore they were carried off, not by their own feet, but carried by others because they were utterly deadened. And it was commanded to the guards that if anyone were caught sharing food or drink or clothing with them, he would be subjected to the ultimate punishment. Into this prison, therefore, the soldiers of Christ were cast, enduring every affliction and every vexation for His sake, and they remained imprisoned for three years, finding no relief from hunger nor consolation for the evils in the prison, unless some of those who were confined with them, taking pity on them, secretly offered them a little bit of food. For the guards who sat by the prison pitied them for their calamities and their old age,
unable to do anything for their relief because of their fear of the judge, they only sympathized with them and offered the tear of compassion.
XII. Then, after that bitter and long-lasting affliction, when Sapor was in that place (it was called Bithmada), he who had been entrusted with punishing them brought them out of the prison. Had you seen them, you would have said they were like shadows; so greatly had they wasted away from the long hardship. He then placed them before the tribunal of Ardasabor; this man was the prince of all the magi who were in Persia. And he straightway said to them, "What then, are you Christians?" The saints replied, "Yes, we are Christians, and we worship the only living God, and serve Him alone." The tyrant said, "You see how the torments brought upon you have caused you great affliction, and even more, I think, have the long-standing hardship and imprisonment. For this has transformed your features and caused such terrible wasting away. Wherefore I would advise you to obey me, as I suggest what is advantageous for you, and to spare yourselves, and worship the radiant sun. If not, it remains for you to be subjected to the most grievous death."
XIII. The holy Acepsimas replied: "I am not unaware that you have the utmost zeal for your ancestral dogma, and that this is your intention, if it were at all possible, to cause everyone, whether by force or by persuasion, to be drawn to the same religion. But let it never enter your mind regarding us, that you will persuade us, whether by flatteries, or threats, or by other inflictions of evils, to depart from the dogma and confession that has been once for all confirmed among us. For we are ready to suffer everything rather than depart from the faith in Christ." The tyrant said, "I do not ignore that for you Christians, death is considered to be a rest, and that all your zeal is toward this, so that you may enjoy those things for which you have conceived hope; but it shall not be permitted to you to attain that which you desire so quickly. For I shall first waste away your flesh little by little, and thus impose a bitter end to your life." The saint replied: "You have already heard it from us before, and now learn regarding this with certainty, that we take absolutely no account of either your torments or your threats. For He who provided a helping hand in the previous evils, He will provide aid now as well, and will prepare us to bear without pain those things which are about to be brought upon us. And if you wish to make a trial of our fortitude in the depth of old age, nothing stands in the way; for you will know that you are laboring in vain and attempting the impossible."
XIV. Filled with anger at these words, the judge orders whips made of raw hide to be brought and placed before their eyes. Then, addressing the saints, he said, "I swear by the great god and the glorious fortune of Sapor, if you do not obey his command, I shall consume your flesh with these, without feeling any pity. And if it should come to pass that you die all the more quickly,
then I shall still punish you even when dead, and having cut you limb from limb, I shall cast you to the birds and dogs. The holy Acepsimas said: Since you have sworn, not by God, but by a created thing and even by the fortune of a man, I fear that you will not care about the oaths, nor will the things sworn be kept inviolable. But if you have these things securely and firmly, behold our bodies for you; use them according to your judgment, without showing us, as you say, any pity or forbearance. After he had spoken thus, the wicked judge ordered him, with his hands stretched out, to be beaten severely on his back and chest. And the torturers reached such a degree of cruelty that they did not relent at all, until the flesh was torn from his bones and flowed down to the ground together with his blood. And the herald cried out: "Obey the king’s will, and live." But the priest of God, as long as he had strength, shouted in return: "I shall fulfill the will of my God, being strengthened by him; as for your king, I do not receive it even with the tips of my ears." And when his voice was being cut off, and his strength was already failing little by little, and death was all but present, lifting his eyes on high, and confirming by a nod that he kept the confession into God unchangeable, in that very endurance of terrible things he committed that invincible soul to God, the judge of the contest.
XV. Furthermore, the bitter torturers, breathing fury, vented their rage even upon the now dead body after the fashion of crows. Then, having pushed it headlong and cast it out into the square as something profane, the wretches posted guards so that he might not be deemed worthy of burial by any of those who love Christ, since those who were the most eager worshipers of demons were striving to dispel the strongest fear of the demons. But three days later, the guards being occupied with the arrival of the daughter of the king of the Armenians—I mean the one who was given as a hostage of peace to the one then reigning over Persia—the treasure, which is truly never spent, was stolen by the Christians.
XVI. Thus, then, when the blessed Acepsimas had received the end of his life on the tenth of October, in the manner that has been told, the most holy Joseph was brought in after him. The unholy judge, turning his attention to him, said: "You have seen, to what a bitter end your companion departed from life. You, therefore, if you do not obey my words, once you have been subjected to similar tortures, will undergo the same death as he." But the divine priest of God said: "What I said before to him who held the office of judge before you, the same I will now say to you: that I shall never prefer a created thing to the Creator, nor shall I worship a creature instead of the One who established it, as long as I am in my right mind." With these words, the governor, seized with great rage, ordered that he too be stretched out in the same way as the former one and scourged with ox-hide thongs, and that the herald cry out that he would be safe if he obeyed the king's will. But he cried out: "There is one God, and besides him there is no other, in whom we also live and move and are, to whom also the lo-
loyal service we offer, caring little for the king's commands." Since he was beaten for many hours and was now almost dead, believing him to have expired, they lifted him up and threw him into the forum. Then, having learned that a tiny remnant of breath still remained in him, they shut him up in prison.
XVII. After him, a third man was introduced, the wise Aithalas, to whom the judge said: "With both life and death set before your eyes, as well as glory and shame, do not choose the worse, leaving behind the better, nor tread the same path as those who went before, who by their own folly received the end they deserved. But if you follow my advice, you shall be deemed worthy of the greatest honors and you shall obtain such rewards from the king. If not, having become the cause of innumerable punishments for yourself, you shall then be violently deprived of the light that is most desirable to all." But the divine Aithalas said: "I should be ashamed before heaven and earth if, while my friends, who were burdened with old age and advanced in years, fought most bravely, I myself were seen to be a lover of life and afraid of your tortures, I who am of a more vigorous age. No, by the death of my Christ, I will not be a lover of this present life, nor will I betray my piety for those goods that are seen, nor for the sake of harsh things." Because of these words, great fury seized the governor, and lashes were brought against the holy man. He, however, called him a filthy dog and base, and said: "Just as your mind, as you said, is rotten and weak, so also are torments merely bugbears to miserable and base souls, but not to noble men whose minds are inflamed with the desire for Christ."
XVIII. Stupefied by these things, the mad judge said to those sitting with him: "What is this? How is it that these who are called Christians despise the present life and so intensely thirst for death?" They replied: "Because, attending to the doctrines handed down by the fathers, they believe that there is another world, far better and more wonderful than the present, for the sake of which they also despise the present life." And while these things were being said, both through the length of time and the most violent application of lashes by those torturing him, the structures of the athlete's body were being dissolved, and his flesh, as it was being lacerated, flowed to the ground together with his blood. When the judge had ordered him to be let go for a little while, as if he felt pity for him, he said: "But if you would wish to obey the royal command, there are physicians among us who will quickly heal your wounds." To whom the blessed one immediately replied: "Even if you could heal my wounds with a single word, O you who are wounded in mind and soul, not even then would I pay heed to you regarding things you so foolishly advise." "I said it as a test," the judge said; "for even if you were willing to obey the king's command, this would be of no profit to you, since your wounds are already procuring death for you. Therefore, I myself will ensure that you become an example to all Christians, so that they may not use such insults against the rulers without fear. But if even in other matters, the most holy
martyr said, you have often spoken what is foreign and false, now, even if you did not wish it, you have spoken the truth, and that which will entirely come to pass before long. For you will in truth establish me as a good example of fortitude and greatness of soul for those who come after, who, looking to our patience as to some archetype, will easily endure the torments of you and those like you.
XIX. The judge was seized with astonishment at what had been said, and having summoned one of those who had great power with him (who was called Adesche, and had originated from the city that is called Arbel), he said: "Take these men, and lead them to your city to be stoned by the hands of Christians; for I wish your ingenuity to accomplish this for me, for which reason I did not wish to inflict death upon them by the sword." Immediately, therefore, having loaded them onto pack animals as if they were some inanimate objects and having bound them fast, he set out for his country; since they could not sit on the animals, suffering the utter paralysis of their limbs, they were cast upon the ground in the places where they had to stop, like dead bodies. And indeed, when they had been brought to the city of Arbel, a dark and sunless prison received them, in which, as putrefaction of their flesh arose from their wounds, much sanies flowed forth, and the saints were afflicted with the most bitter pains, especially because it was not permitted to any of the Christians to enter to them and vouchsafe them even the smallest care. But a certain woman who was pious and fearing the Lord, dwelling in the borders of that same city, arrived at the prison at an unseasonable hour of the night, and with sufficient money having appeased the guards, she succeeded in taking the saints away secretly and bringing them to her own house, which was not far off, being carried in the hands of her own servants. Then, having set them down—they were not even able to speak—she wiped away the sanies from them, and with certain mild medicines she consoled their pains, bound them with clean linens, anointed them with precious ointments, and in all ways showed how pious she was and Christ-loving, kissing the members of the martyrs, weeping over them, and anointing herself with the sanies that dripped from them.
XX. When the blessed Joseph had at last and with difficulty come to himself, he said: "Your compassion and generosity toward us, O most holy woman, is acceptable both to God and to us who struggle for His sake; but to weep for us so immoderately is far from the true hope of Christians and our faith." To him, that most venerable of women replied: "My soul is filled with joy, considering how Christ has endued you with such fortitude, that you have endured the bitterness of such great pains; though I would have been even more glad if I had seen you perfected by martyrdom. But to weep is peculiar to human nature, which is moved by compassion for one who is of its own kind. But you must not be grieved," the divine Joseph replied, "nor is it fitting to weep for our sake, knowing fully that those who for Christ's sake
endure afflictions are procuring for themselves eternal joy and the kingdom of heaven." As the day waned, they were again secretly returned to prison.
XXI. When six months had passed, the magistrate who had received the holy men to be stoned was removed from office, and another, more savage than he, succeeded him, by name Zeroth, to whom the king had also given orders that the captured Christians be put to death by being pelted with stones by the hands of Christians. When he arrived at the city and had entered the fire temple to worship the fire, the fire-worshippers said to him, "There are some here who have for a long time now been Christians, locked away in safe custody, who, as we learned earlier, were subjected to most grievous punishments and torments, yet could not be persuaded to deny their own religion." Upon hearing this, he immediately ordered the holy men to be brought before the tribunal, and says to them, "Since King Sapor has overthrown well-fortified and powerful cities, and has brought under servitude very many nations that were difficult to subdue, how is it that you, who are indeed under his power and enjoy the fruits of his land, despise his commands so greatly, just as if you were defectors from him?" To this, the admirable Joseph said, "If we had instigated any tyranny or rebellion against your king, it would have been necessary for him entirely to invade us with armed forces and an assembled army, or at least to send against us someone who was strong of hand, experienced in affairs, and most capable of leading an army. But we are so far from attempting such things that he has not sent another against us, but you, who are more accustomed to fighting with women living in chambers than with men. For we have given ourselves up like sheep for the slaughter, as we have learned to be subject to all authorities."
XXII. The governor said, "It seems you are provoking my anger in order to have me take your life more quickly with these insults against us. But you will not attain what you desire. For I shall stretch you out over a long time and delay, and I shall consume you little by little with many tortures." He therefore orders him to be bound by the feet with ropes and to be hung head downwards, and to be struck with dried ox-sinews. Thus, then, the veins, having been opened by the violence of the tortures, provided a flow of blood as if from a spring. The crowd that stood around, accusing the judge of inhumanity, was moved to pity and tears, marvelling at the endurance of the holy old man. Some of the great men who were standing around, approaching the martyr in secret, said, "Enter secretly with us into the temple of our god, if you are ashamed to be seen by the many, and by sacrificing, be freed from these punishments." But he, with a clear voice, said, "Depart from me, depart, you who work iniquity, for the Lord has heard my prayer." Then, when three hours had already passed, he orders him to be released from his bonds and brought before him, and says, "What, are you persuaded by the king's command, and do you choose life over death, or not?" The holy man answered:
Absit, may it never happen to me to desire such a life as would see me offering adoration to the sun rather than to its Creator. "What then," he said, "is death to be preferred by you to life?" The holy man said: Yes; for this death becomes to me the procurer of eternal life and establishes for us the cause of many good things, which things are neither to be seen by eyes, nor heard by ears, nor even grasped by the mind. To these words, that mocker and scoffer, as if ridiculing the martyr’s apology, said: "You owe us, therefore, great thanks, if we have procured for you so many good things. And surely you will arrange for us also to become partakers of them, since we have been your abettors toward the acquisition of such blessedness." The holy man said: Do not deal ironically with us, O judge. For we have been taught by the divine commandment to love even our enemies and to do good to those who hate us; but yet, as long as we are alive in the present life, we also pray that you, departing from error, may repent and learn piety, so that there may be a portion and lot for you also in the future among the ineffable good things of God; but in the age to come it is not given to us to remove punishment or to grant good things; for this lies with God alone, who has also the authority to judge the living and the dead.
XXIII. The governor said: Setting aside such contentions for the present, and these good things of yours that you dream of, obey the king’s command. For I will subject you to such great tortures that through you other Christians may also be persuaded not by any means to exchange the things they hold in their hands for those that are in hope. The holy man said: For this is my desire, as you yourself have also known: to be sent with speed to the eternal mansions. Even if you bring upon me ten thousand other tortures, even if another comes after you, I shall be found perfected in the love of Christ; for I have God strengthening me, for whom I have chosen to endure these things. And I will not, as you say, be an exhortation to Christians to deny piety through your torments, but rather an incitement to the true worship of God. For they, seeing me in such advanced age making light of your tortures, will have a beautiful example of courage, and they will not be lacking in imitation. Having heard these things, the governor, stunned by the fearless constancy of the blessed elder, commands him to be carried by some—since he could not walk—and locked up in prison again.
XXIV. Then looking toward the blessed Aithalas, he says: And you, are you possessed by the same madness, and do you refuse the adoration of the greatest luminary? The herald of the truth answers: The Lord Jesus Christ lives, the Son of the living God, in whom I have hoped from my youth, as I have an unchangeable mind; and no one will persuade me to accord the honor of God to his creatures. When that arrogant and barbaric man heard these things, he commands the holy man to be stripped of his footwear, to be hung upon a tree, and to be scourged with ox-hide thongs. And he with a loud
voice proclaimed himself a Christian. But the ungodly man had grown weary of scourging, rather than the holy man expressing anything relaxed or unworthy of his endurance for Christ. He commands, therefore, that he be brought down from the tree, and that some Manichaean, who had been caught in some improprieties, be brought into the midst, and, "Look," he says to the martyr, "at him openly abjuring his own religion." Having therefore given leave for the Manichaean to be tormented, he forced him to deny it. He held out at first, and seemed to contend against the blows. But when the scourgers pressed more violently, and the blows struck him forcefully, overcome by the pains, he immediately becomes an apostate, and cried out, "Anathema to Manes and his dogmas!" And they then bring him an ant, commanding him to kill it. He, having taken it, killed it immediately. Upon which the holy man, overflowing with joy and as it were smiling at what had occurred, said, "The worshipper of Manes is in misery, and he has been caught having committed murder, as one who has killed his own god. But I consider myself blessed, according to Paul, because being openly strengthened by the power of Christ, who said, 'I have overcome the world,' I preserve my resolve unconquered."
XXV. Filled with anger at this, the mad judge commands the holy man to be beaten with thorny rods of a Persian apple tree. And the blows so worked upon him that he became almost mute and did not even perceive the blows that were being inflicted; for which reason, as if he were already dead, they dragged him and cast him out. But one of the magi, when he happened to see that body stripped bare, and having been moved by compassion for his nature, covered it with a rag. But those of that same religion, having seen what had occurred, accused him to the governor, and he was immediately punished with bitter lashes, and receives this as a reward for his compassion from those who were utterly uncompassionate. However, they deliver the blessed Aithalas, who was still breathing, into custody. When the judge learned that a certain governor of the highest rank, Saborius by name, had arrived at his village (this was called Marcellaria, from the name), he entrusts to him the examination of the martyrs. He, being no less impious and savage than his predecessors, looking upon the holy men, said, "Reverencing your gray hair, and pitying your affliction, I counsel you to worship the sun and taste the blood of the sacrifices, that having been saved from a bitter death, you may gain the life which is sweet to all." The holy men as if with one mouth said to him, "It is the custom for flesh-eating dogs to taste blood, not humans. But it is fitting for you rather to be satiated with such things, for you possess the madness of a dog, and bark and rage against Him who made you."
XXVI. When he ordered the holy men to be scourged, those who stood by, moved by compassion for them, said, "By tasting a decoction instead of the blood, according to the secret intent, be saved, and be rid of the punishments that threaten you." But they answered, "God forbid that we should, either by taste or in any other way, tarnish the purity of our faith and this long-lived old age
A. That crafty and altogether wicked ruler said, "But if pure meat were brought to you, would you not even taste that?" The saints said, "What can be clean from an unclean source, and what could ever be unpolluted from your defiled hands? Therefore, abandon these devices of yours and be eager to bring a speedy verdict against us; for you labor in vain, beating the air and ignorantly striking him who cannot be tamed." Having therefore consulted and decreed common with those seated in counsel that the saints should be stoned by the hands of Christians, they gather a multitude of Christians, among whom they also seized the admirable Isdandul. And having again carried the blessed Joseph to the judgment hall, they present him. The saint gestures to the governor to come closer to him, as if he were about to hear something secret. When he had immediately come up and approached, the martyr, filling his mouth with saliva, spat upon that detestable face, saying, "Are you not ashamed, you most shameless one, to be treating this common nature so savagely and hostilely, and to be dragging a body already affected by death back to examination?" He, having been thoroughly reproached by those who were sitting with him for this excessive cruelty, and having been mocked to a degree beyond words, returned to his own seat, affected with shame.
XXVII. When they had moved the saint a little further off and bound his hands behind his back, they dug a pit for him and, having lowered him into it, buried him up to the waist. Then they surrounded the Christians who had been arrested and, striking them, commanded them to pelt the illustrious man with stones. Among them they also urged the holy Isdandul to do the same, but she said, "Never in all time has such a thing been heard of, that a woman should be forced to stretch out her hand against holy men, as you are doing now; you who abstain from contending against your enemies, but lift up your weapons against us and fill with blood and slaughter the motherland that lives in peace." They fastened a spike to a long reed and commanded her to prick the saint with it. But she said, "God forbid that I should do such a thing; for I would gladly drive this into my own heart sooner than touch the holy body of the saint with it even in the slightest." Thus, showing a manly steadfastness, she appeared more powerful than the parricides had imagined; and they covered the saint with such a flurry of stones that only his head was visible, the rest of his body being buried in the pile of stones. When one of the impious leaders saw that it was still moving, he ordered one of the executioners to take a stone, as large as his hand could hold, and cast it upon it from above. When this was done and his head was crushed by the weight of the stone, the blessed one commends his precious soul to Christ. His venerable body was then guarded by sentries as if it were some treasure. When three days had passed and the fourth had already arrived, a great and terrible earthquake took place; then, as lightning appeared and great claps of thunder lowered down...
fire from heaven—this being sent down, it reduced the guards to ashes, while the heap of stones it scattered like dust, making manifest to all the honor God awards to the martyr. But the body became invisible, whether God had willed to transfer it in ways known only to Himself, or whether human hands had taken it away.
XXVIII. When they had led the venerable Aithalas to the village called Patrias, they brought it about that he too should be overwhelmed with stones by the hands of Christians. Some monks who lived in that place came by night and, without the guards knowing, took away his body, committing a truly praiseworthy theft, a deed worthy of the light and not of darkness. Then, after performing the customary rites for him, they laid him in a notable spot. In the very place where it happened that the saint was slain, God wrought a wonder worthy both of His own power and of the glory of the martyr. A myrtle plant sprang up there, which became a deliverance from every kind of illness. When for five years miracles were thus wrought through this plant, those who held to the false religion of the Gentiles, being smitten with envy in their souls and unable to bear seeing these wonders as a refutation of their own religion, cut down the plant by its very roots and cast it into the fire. But many of those whose souls had been purified saw for a long time lights around that spot, and divine powers ascending and descending, and sending forth glory to Him who glorifies His saints. The martyr of Christ, Aithalas, was perfected in the month of June, having displayed the same steadfastness and endurance even unto death as those who were perfected before him, by the aid and grace of Christ our true God, to whom belongs all glory, honor, and adoration, together with His D unoriginate Father and the life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
