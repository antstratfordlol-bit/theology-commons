Argumentum et causa reprehensionis erat Herodianum in Barda exemplum et incestus. Eum contumacem, neque resipiscentem ab ecclesia rejeci. Deinde imperator ipse modo pro illo deprecabatur, modo atrociter minabatur, nisi avunculum in gratiam reciperem. Postquam vero impulsus a Barda statuit matrem cum sororibus a palatio submovere, præcepit mihi ut illas attonderem, quas ego invitas tondendas non existimavi. Has ergo ob causas a throno deturbatus sum, et pulsatus atque eliminatus, et in custodia vinculisque, cum iis qui mecum fuere senseruntque, eadem passis, detentus ante judicum vestrorum adventum. Cum autem ad vos litteras, quibus episcopos statum rerum omnium docebamus, per Laurentium presbyterum et duos Stephanos dedissemus (quorum alter subdiaconus, alter laicus), illos etiam sacramento adegimus, ut eas ipsi manu suæ sanctitatis porrigerent, nihil egere.
Nos vero ut jussi sumus ad tribunal Caiphano non absimile comparere, ad sanctitatis vestræ judicium magno clamore provocavimus. Sed non erat qui nos audiret. Deinde rursum citati rogavimus: Quomodo nos, o Patres, jubetis comparere? ut monachos et damnatos, an ut judicandos? Qui renuntiarunt ad concilium, imo latrocinium, perreximus: cumque jam prope abessemus, submisit tyrannus in publicam viam, qui sacris nos vestibus exuerent. Cumque jam ad templum protrusi essemus, tres prædicti judicum viatores, Laurentius et duo Stephani ad nos mittuntur, qui ad me: Quid tu, inquiunt, tot ob crimina damnatus exauctoratusque, sacram tamen stolam ausus es induere? Ergo me per vim a meis avulsum solum ad imperatorem sistunt, qui me contumeliis extemplo onerat. Respondente porro me, convicia paulo mitiora esse tormentis, paululum repressit furorem, jussitque in ligneo scamno me sedere. Interim cum verba ultro citroque fierent, rogavi ut Rhodoaldum et Zachariam legatos tuos liceret mihi salutare, factaque potestate eos consalutavi, atque interrogavi, qui adessent. Legati, inquiunt, sumus Nicolai papæ Romani, causæ tuæ cognoscendæ gratia missi. Quærenti rursum, an a sanctitate tua litteras ad me haberent, responderunt, nullas se habere; eo quod, aiebant, non ad patriarcham, sed ad eum qui in provinciali concilio seu damnatus esset, veniremus, parati omnia ex canonum decretis definire. Ergo, inquam, prius adulterum removete: quod si facere non potestis, ne judices quidem estis. Qui manu imperatorem indicantes: Ita imperat, inquiunt, ille. Deinde palatini ad me conversi hortantur, exiguntque nunc consulendo, nunc minitando, abdicationem: sed frustra. In the meantime, as words were exchanged   back and forth, I asked if it might be permitted for me to greet your legates, Rhodoaldus and Zacharias; and having received permission, I greeted them and inquired for what purpose they were present. "We are legates," they said, "of Pope Nicholas of Rome, sent for the sake of investigating your case." When I asked again if they had a letter for me from your Holiness, they replied that they had none, because (they said) they were not coming to a patriarch, but to someone who had been condemned in a provincial council, and they were prepared to define all things according to the canons. "Therefore," said I, "first remove the adulterer; but if you cannot do this, then you are not even judges." They, pointing with their hand to the ruler, replied, "So he wills it." Thereupon the men around the emperor turned to me, urging and demanding my abdication, sometimes by counseling, sometimes by threatening; but they did not persuade me. Therefore, they turned to the metropolitans, and by other slanders and accusations they harassed them, asking why those who had not previously resisted my deposition were now again [calling] me patriarch...


[859] {#pg.105.859}
To whom the metropolitans replied, saying:   Since two evils were set before us, namely, the emperor's wrath and the uprising of the populace, we chose the lesser one at that time; but you, who are about the emperor, restore the throne to the patriarch and do not concern yourselves with us. But in these matters the imperial party returned again to entreaty, asking me to resign clearly, and so let the adulterer securely possess the Church. As I was not persuaded, that day passed by. On these terms the assembly was dissolved, and they continued doing the same for many days. But when they failed to achieve what they were aiming at, they turned to another kind of machination, and having sent the aforementioned ministers of the judges, they summoned me to the council. But I said, that I would not go there either; for I do not recognize you as judges doing anything according to the prescription of the Church, since you have not even cast out the usurper from the midst, but instead you even eat with him, and you accepted his gifts even while standing at a distance. For at the city of Raedestus they met you—garments, and chasubles, and pectorals. Such judges as you I do not accept, but convey me to the Pope, and from thence I shall gladly accept the judgment. This also all those who were with me cried out with a loud voice. Then we asked them to read the aforementioned letters. For in them were contained the canonical proofs of Pope Innocent on behalf of the holy Chrysostom, that John ought not to enter the court before he was restored to his proper throne. And the fourth canon of the Council of Sardica: If any bishop has been deposed, and says that he has a defense, no other [person] is to be appointed in his place until the bishop of the Roman Church makes a decision concerning him. And many other such things were inscribed in the letters, which we beseech you, most reverend Father and bishop, and most loving of brothers, to seek out and read. But since they still pressed us nonetheless to come to them, we thus testified: As is apparent, my Fathers, you have neither read the canons nor do you know the ecclesiastical decree.   For the rule is such: That let a bishop who is called by a synod be summoned by two bishops, and let this be done twice and thrice. But you summon me by two, one a deacon and the other a layman. And we indeed made these excuses. But they produced witnesses who said they were ready to confirm under oath that I was not created and consecrated rightfully and by law. To this I again replied: Who are these, I say? And who will believe them? And what canon decrees that the emperor should produce witnesses? Therefore, if I am not an archbishop, neither are you emperor, nor are these bishops, nor indeed the adulterer; for all you are the product   of my unworthy hands and prayers. If the adulterer were a member of the Church, I would perhaps willingly have yielded; but now, how shall I establish a stranger as shepherd of the sheep of Christ? Many things stand against this man. One is that he is of those condemned and cut off by anathema from the Church. And not only have they suffered this from us   and from other patriarchs as well, but even from your own authority, since with them by


[861] {#pg.105.861}
the wretched Zacharias he was forbidden to perform sacred rites, or to take communion, or to perform ordinations; yet they have done all things contrary to this before they were absolved from the censure. A Another point is that he was a man of the world, belonging to the secular order, and before he was a sheep, he acted as a shepherd. Furthermore, he was ordained by one who had been excommunicated and deposed. But also, when he persuaded the metropolitans to side with him, he gave those who requested it a signed document concerning me, and, having sworn an oath, he wrote that he would do nothing contrary to my dictates as if he were receiving a father. And not even forty days had passed since his inauguration when he publicly deposed me, though I was absent, and condemned me with anathema. To the Archbishop of Cyzicus, moreover, at the mandate of Photius, his fingers were broken, and the copy of the signed document—which he believed had been stolen from him—was taken, and he himself was cast down from his throne. Others, meanwhile, he oppressed, and others he won over with gifts so that they might destroy the signed document which they had written concerning me. To these things the tyrant replied nothing, but he brought forward witnesses suborned to swear an oath against me, as though I had not been regularly and holily promoted and consecrated.   Here I called out to the bishops present in a loud voice: "Admit, Fathers, the one request of your lowly old man, and come to us." Therefore, the judges and the tyrants took me aside from the rest, and once they had separated me from the others, they begged me again to renounce my right and to yield. As I refused, they began to make threats. Then those executioners left the bishops with me and withdrew. The bishops, however, began to treat with me again (concerning the document of renunciation, of course), to whom I replied that it was necessary for them to understand the state of my case, and only then to demand from me what they were requesting; and that they could learn of my situation from no other source than from me alone; for no one else from such a great multitude of hegumens, priests, monks, and laymen dared to speak freely.   For they had seen the Metropolitan of Ancyra, because he had spoken more freely for the truth in the council, struck by that nefarious parricide himself with the scabbard of his sword upon his head, so that it drenched his vestment in blood, and they had seen what other savage deeds he had dared against others. When I had recounted these things, they appointed a meeting for the following day. And being separated, we went home. But after the tenth day, they seized me and dragged me to the Caiaphan tribunal, and brought forward witnesses who would say that I had not been legitimately created. What need I say here of my being afflicted with so many blows? For seven days they forced me to remain without food, without sleep, and without rest. I have explained these things in brief.   Do you, my most holy lord, show toward me the bowels of your mercy, and as the great Apostle said, say also yourself: "Who is weak, and I am not weak?" Look upon your predecessors, the patriarchs—I mean Fabian, Julius, Innocent, and Leo—and simply all who struggled manfully for the faith and for the truth, and emulate them, and arise as a champion for us who have suffered such indignities.
