Decebat enim ut quæ a se divinæ haberet potentiæ exempla, atque ad matris, qua suscepta esset, sterilitatem respiceret, nec cujus nuntius illi afferebatur, virginitati fidem abnueret. Verum videamus, qui ejus secundum carnem fuerint parentes, quantaque fulti virtute, et quomodo speciem Jacob, quam pere ᵃ.
Quis igitur nescit Joachim et Annam, qui divinæ infantis parentes exsistant, par illud venerabile ac religiosum? Quis ita tardus, quis in divinis Litteris sic peregrinus, ut non teneat, ter beatos illos omnis pietatis ac justitiæ, per eam, qua vixerunt ætatem, summum verticem adeptos esse? Atque id manifestum, nedum ex fructu, quem non magis precum instantia, quam natura fœta germinarunt, verum etiam ex illorum historia, quæ inculpatos eis mores, vitamque Deo placitam ac sanctam, ascribit; cum genus in Davidem ac Judam referrent, morumque ingenuitate atque indolis probitate, nec non mandatorum Dei custodia, omnem Israelem nobilitarent.
Hi nimirum, cum virtutis censu (uti dictum est) divites essent, quin et terrenis bonis affluerent; nempe gregibus, armentisque, ac reliqua omni substantia fulti essent, uno hoc solum a perfecta felicitate se abesse existimabant, quod nempe absque liberis consenuerant: erantque omnia doloris plena, ac mæstitia cuncta oppleverat, quod haud secus ac vetustæ Saræ, menstrua Annæ fieri desierant⁷; nec Joachimo erat successor, nec cernebatur qui familiæ scintilla foret. Accedebat et irritans vicinorum exprobratio, ac quantum nec dici potest ægræ mentis doloris sensum succendebat. Ac quidem Joachim duplicia dona Domino offerebat; cum his forte Deum propitiaret, ac orbitatis probrum a se depelli posse cogitaret. Nihilominus tamen sic quoque in iisdem infecunditatis probris manebat, donec tandem vir justus extremum jam animi anxins et humiliatus, carnalibus despectis hostiis, et quibus imperfecti imperfecte obsequi se et indulgere existimant, iisque ut quæ non admodum placeant, spretis, sacrificium Deo spiritum contribulatum et contritum ac humiliatum offerendum operæ pretium duxit⁸, creditque per continentiam et orationem apprehensurum se, cujus æger animi desiderio erat.

They traced their lineage back to David and Judah, and by the nobility of their ways and the excellence of their character, and by the keeping of the commandments of God, they ennobled the whole of Israel. These people, while being rich, as has been said, in virtue, and abounding also in earthly goods—having flocks and herds and all other possessions in abundance—suspected that they were failing of perfect blessedness in this one thing alone: for they were growing old in life childless; and all things were full of grief, and all things were filled with despondency for them, because for Anna, like the ancient Sarah, the cycles of women had ceased, and for him there was no successor, and no spark of the family was to be seen. And in addition to these things, the reproach from neighbors smoldering—it is impossible to say how much it greatly fueled their despondency. Joachim indeed offered double gifts to the Lord, perhaps wishing to appease Him with these, and thinking to flee the reproach of childlessness; but he remained nonetheless in the same reproaches of unfruitfulness, until at last the righteous man, being at a loss and humbled, abandoned the carnal sacrifices, and those which the imperfect think they offer imperfectly, and regarding them as not at all desirable, he despised them. He had resolved to offer as a sacrifice to God a contrite spirit and a contrite and humbled heart; and he believed that through temperance and prayer he would grasp what he desired. For it was necessary for him who was to give birth to the most holy treasure of the Most Holy Spirit first to be filled with the divine operations of the Spirit Himself, and to be warmed toward the conception by a holy and spiritual zeal. How then does he do this? And they truly were in the midst of these things; but what of the all-merciful Lord, who does all things and transforms them for the better, who accomplishes the petitions and thoughts of His saints superabundantly? Did He perhaps disregard the supplication of the righteous? Or did He attend to the cry, yet defer the gift to a later time? Or did He grant it immediately, but delay the kind of gift they asked for? By no means; rather, He responds immediately through the voice of a holy angel, [announcing] the conception and birth of the Theopais (the Child of God). He announces that she will be renowned unto the ends of the earth. O the power of faith! O the magnificent gift of the Most High! They, having despaired of nature, ask for that which transcends nature; He truly grants a grace that corrects the deficiency in them. And they, indeed, ask simply for a child, so that the reproach of childlessness might be removed, but He grants them His own Mother: one who heals not only the affliction of the parents, but who fills the whole human race with spiritual and true joy. This one, conceived and carried in the barren womb more by the word of God’s promise than by the law of nature, issues forth today from her maternal chambers like the full moon. Today it is clearly possible to see, according to the prophetic inspiration, truth springing up from the earth and righteousness looking down from heaven. For the Lord, demonstrating His righteousness from above to those who asked in a worthy manner, gave benignity, declaring the word of the divine promise; and Anna, having accepted this by faith, produces the promised truth as if from the earth—that is, from her own womb—and presents her to all men as an animate and living fruit. Today she who is to be the Mother of God according to the flesh, but His daughter according to the spirit of holiness, undergoes a birth according to nature, and the daughter of the great King is known as the daughter of man; and she who is to stand at the right hand of the King of all in the heavens is just now born upon the earth, is delivered by midwives, is washed in water, is wrapped in swaddling clothes, and is deemed worthy of the care due to infants. And she who is expected to cradle in her virgin arms the One who contains the heaven and God is encompassed by aged arms, and she now flees from aged and maternal breasts, she who in a supernatural manner shall soon nurse the One who provides food for every living creature.
