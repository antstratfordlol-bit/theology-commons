ANNO DOMINI DCCCXC.
NICETAS DAVID
PAPHLAGO
NOTITIA HISTORICA ET LITTERARIA.
(Leo ALLATIUS, Diatriba de Nicetis, ap. Mai. Biblioth. nov., VI, part. II, p. 5.)
Inter varia cognomina et dignitatum titulos unum Nicetam latitantem, sed propter eadem incommode in plures variosque atque immaniter divisum, si fieri potest, suis partibus integrum in medium adducamus. Fuit is Byzantinus et Paphlago dictus. Joannes Scylitza in proœmio suæ historiæ : Ὁ γὰρ Δαφνοπάτης Θεόδωρος, Νικήτας ὁ Παφλαγὼν, Ἰωσὴφ Γενέσιος, καὶ Μανουὴλ οἱ Βυζάντιοι, Νικηφόρος διάκονος Φρὺξ, καὶ τὰ ἑξῆς. Namque Theodorus Daphnopates, Nicetas Paphlago, Josephus Genesius, et Manuel Byzantini, Nicephorus diaconus Phryx, etc. Ex Scylitza sua hausit Cedrenus. Consentit hisce Nicephorus Callistus Hist. lib. xiv, 28. Sed Paphlago, cognomene fuerit, an episcopatus titulus, quis extricet ? Plane in multis manuscriptis legitur Νικήτας Παφλαγονίας, Nicetas Paphlagoniæ, ut in codice Vaticano 1246, et ante Vitam Ignatii patriarchæ. Et potuit quoque esse ex Paphlagonia, sed Byzantina civitate donatus: neque id probatione eget: sunt enim omnes omnium scriptorum chartæ de alienis in cives proprios ascitis à civitatibus plenæ. Et episcopatus Paphlagoniæ nullus apparet. Fuit is quoque binominis: namque et Davidis appellatione insignitur. Hinc sæpius Νικήτα τοῦ καὶ Δαυΐδ, Nicetæ qui et David; et absolute a nonnullis Nicetas David appellatur. Et videant qui David hoc cognomentum fuisse tradunt, quo se fulciant fundamento. In aliquibus codicibus adnectitur loco Davidis et Paphlagonis, Δαδύβρων, Dadybrorum, qui erat episcopatus sub Gangrensi Paphlagoniæ. Hinc arguo non male Dadybrensem episcopum, dictum etiam Paphlagonem, qui nempe possideret in Paphlagonia episcopatum. Huic additur quoque nomen philosophi, Νικήτα τοῦ καὶ Δαυΐδ τοῦ φιλοσόφου. Nicetæ, qui et David, philosophi, ut clare videtur in codicibus manuscriptis Vaticanis 344, 365 et 679. Et in aliis, nullo alio addito, Νικήτα τοῦ φιλοσόφου, Nicetæ philosophi, non quod tantum institutum monachorum professus, sed quod inter alia potissimum sibi philosophiæ munus ascriberet, quales plerique eo ævo fuere, et primus inter eos ὑπάτου τῶν φιλοσόφων titulo cohonestabatur. Quare hic fuerit Nicetas, qui et alio nomine David vocabatur, Byzantinus', dignitate philosophus, Dadybrorum in Paphlagonia sub Gangrensi episcopus : et omnia, quæ sub Nicetæ nomine diversis hisce titulis ad varios referuntur, huic soli vindicantur (a).
Floruit circa Leonis et Constantini tempora, ut colligitur ex proœmio Vitæ sancti Ignatii patriarchæ, quam ille neque indocte neque eleganter concinnavit : Εἰ δὲ καὶ πρὸς τοῖς ἐσχάτοις, καὶ τοῖς καθ' ἡμᾶς τούτοις ἐγγίζοντες τύχοιεν καιροῖς, πολλῷ μᾶλλον ἀξιομνημονευτότατοι ἂν εἶεν ἁπάντων, καὶ ἀξιέπαινετοι, ὡς πολὺ πλέον οἰκοδομεῖν τὴν ἐγγύτητι δυνάμενοι τὴν Ἐκκλησίαν · τὸ γὰρ ἀρχαῖον καὶ πάμπολυ κατὰ χρόνον διεστὼς, εἰ καὶ πιστεύεται τῇ ἀξιοπιστίᾳ τῆς Γραφῆς, ἀλλὰ καὶ θᾶττον ἂν τοῖς πολλοῖς πρὸς τὴν μίμησιν ἀπογινώσκοιτο, λογιζομένοις ὡς κρεῖττον, ἢ κατὰ τὴν παροῦσαν, τῶν πρεσβυτέρων ἐκείνων δυναμένων κατορθοῦν γενεάν · ὅσα δ' ἂν τοῖς καθ' ἡμᾶς κατορθοῦσθαι πιστεύοιτο καιροῖς, μᾶλλον ἂν οἶμαι ταῦτα παρακινίζειν τοὺς εὐσεβεστέρους, καὶ πρὸς τὸν ἴσον τῆς ἀρετῆς διερεθίζειν ζῆλον· διὰ ταῦτά μοι τοῦτον ἐνστήσασθαι νῦν τῷ λόγῳ τὸν ἀγῶνα · καί μοι σφοδρὸς πόθος κατακάρδιος ἀληθινοῖς διηγήμασι καὶ πάσης προλήψεως ἀπηλλαγμένος ψεύδους, τὸν μέγαν ἱεράρχην, καὶ ποιμένα τῆς βασιλίδος Ἰγνάτιον ὑπό γε θεῷ πνεύματι τῆς ἀληθείας ὑφηγητῇ, καὶ συλλήπτορι τοῖς ἀγνοοῦσι, ὁποῖός τις ἦν, ἐκ τῆς κατ' αὐτὸν πολιτείας

IN THE YEAR OF OUR LORD 890. NICETAS DAVID PAPHLAGO HISTORICAL AND LITERARY NOTICE (Leo ALLATIUS, *Diatriba de Nicetis*, in Mai, *Biblioth. nov.*, VI, part II, p. 5.)
Among the various surnames and titles of dignity, one Nicetas lies hidden, but because these are inconveniently and monstrously divided into many varied persons, let us, if possible, bring him forward entire in his own parts. He was called a Byzantine and a Paphlagonian. Joannes Scylitza says in the preface to his history: "For Theodore Daphnopates, Nicetas the Paphlagonian, Josephus Genesius, and Manuel are Byzantines; Nicephorus the deacon is a Phrygian, etc." For Theodore Daphnopates, Nicetas Paphlago, Josephus Genesius, and Manuel are Byzantines, Nicephorus the Deacon is a Phrygian, etc. Cedrenus drew his own account from Scylitza. Nicephorus Callistus, *Hist. eccl.* lib. XIV, 28, agrees with these. But whether Paphlago was a surname, or a title of bishopric, who can disentangle it? In many manuscripts it is clearly read *Nicetas Paphlagoniae*, "Nicetas of Paphlagonia," as in the Vatican codex 1246, and before the *Life of Ignatius the Patriarch*. And he could also have been from Paphlagonia, but gifted with the rights of the city of Byzantium: nor does that require proof, for the charters of all authors of every kind are full of men from foreign parts adopted as citizens by their own cities. And no bishopric of Paphlagonia appears. He was also known by two names, for he is also distinguished by the appellation of David. Hence, we very often find *Nicetæ qui et David*, "Nicetas who is also David"; and he is called simply Nicetas David by some. And let those who claim that David was his surname look to the foundation upon which they rest. In some codices, in place of David and Paphlago, *Dadybrorum* is added; which was a bishopric in Paphlagonia under Gangra. Hence I argue, not incorrectly, that he was the bishop of Dadybra, also called the Paphlagonian, inasmuch as he held a bishopric in Paphlagonia. To this is also added the name of "philosopher," *Nicetæ, qui et David, philosophi*, "Nicetas, who is also David, the philosopher," as is clearly seen in the Vatican manuscripts 344, 365, and 679. And in others, with nothing else added, *Nicetæ philosophi*, "Nicetas the philosopher"—not that he had only professed the monastic rule, but that among other things he especially attributed to himself the task of philosophy, as most men of that age did, and he was the first among them to be honored with the title of "Master of the Philosophers." Wherefore this man was Nicetas, who was also called by the other name, David, a Byzantine, a philosopher by rank, bishop of Dadybra in Paphlagonia under Gangra: and all things which, under the name of Nicetas, are referred to various people by these diverse titles, are claimed for him alone (a).
He flourished about the times of Leo and Constantine, as is gathered from the preface to the *Life of Saint Ignatius the Patriarch*, which he composed neither unlearnedly nor inelegantly: "If even approaching the final times and these present days of ours, they would be much more noteworthy than all others, and deserving of praise, since they would do much more to build up the Church in their nearness; for that which is ancient and separated by a great span of time, even if it is believed on the trustworthiness of Scripture, yet it might be rejected by the many for the purpose of imitation, as they contemplate that it is better to consider what our elders could accomplish in their own generation; if those things which were accomplished in our days were to be believed, I think they would much more incite the more pious, and stir up a zeal for virtue that is equal. For these reasons I have now undertaken this struggle in my discourse; and a burning desire possesses my heart to set forth, with true narratives and completely free from any prejudice of falsehood, the great hierarch and shepherd of the Queen of Cities, Ignatius, through the Spirit of God, the teacher of truth, and a helper to those who are ignorant of what kind of man he was, from the conduct of his life..." (a) Thus far we had published the notice concerning this Nicetas from Allatius in *Script. vet.* t. IX, pref. p. xix; whom however it will be shown below to be different from Nicetas the bishop of Serræ. A. M.
