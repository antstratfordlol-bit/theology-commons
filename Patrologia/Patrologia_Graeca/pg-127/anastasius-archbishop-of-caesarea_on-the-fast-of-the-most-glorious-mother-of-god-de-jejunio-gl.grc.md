# On the Fast of the Most Glorious Mother of God (De Jejunio Gloriosissimae Deiparae)

**Author**: Anastasius, Archbishop of Caesarea  
**Series**: PG 127  
**Columns**: 517–532  

### De Jejunio Gloriosissimae Deiparae


[517] {#pg.127.517}
ANNO DOMINI MXCV.
ANASTASIUS
CÆSARIENSIS IN PALÆSTINA ARCHIEPISCOPUS
NOTITIA
(Oudin, De script. eccles, II, 850.)
Anastasius, Cæsareæ Palæstinæ archiepiscopus, vixit circa annum 1090. Scripsit tractatum De jejunio sanctissimæ Deiparæ, quodque illud servandum sit ut legitimum, ex ms. codice Regiæ Galliarum Bibliothecæ eductum, quem Græce et Latine produxit Joannes Baptista Cotelerius, Veterum Monumentorum Ecclesiæ Græcæ vol. III, pag. 432, ex Niconis Pandecte. Anastasius in hoc opusculo De jejunio sanctissimæ Deiparæ, pag. 458, ex sancti Niconis a se allegati auctoritate, inquit hinc jejunio duplex nomen impositum, appellarique passim Quadragesimam vel Jejunium sanctorum apostolorum, nec non gloriosæ Deiparæ: nominibus binis, priori quidem, quia maxima pars hujus Quadragesimæ protendebatur ab octava die festi Pentecostes usque ad festum Sanctorum Apostolorum, ex indulgentia Patrum modicis jejunii induciis datis, ab hoc festo ad Kalendas Augusti; posteriori autem, quod omnis tandem hæc Quadragesima in honorem Deiparæ fieret, atque ad festum Dormitionis illius terminaretur. Hic ergo claruit et scripsit post S. Niconem Armenum, qui ab anno sæculi decimi sexagesimo primo clarus, anno demum 998 die 26 Novembris debitum humanæ naturæ solvit. Atque hinc, aliquot post sancti Niconis mortem decadibus annorum exstitisse, nec anno 1090, hunc Anastasium Cæsareæ archiepiscopum anteriorem esse concludimus, cujus opinor solus mentionem facit, qui ejus Tractatum edidit Græce et Latine Joannes Baptista Cotelerius tomo III operis laudati.

ANASTASII EPISCOPI CÆSAREÆ PALESTINÆ DE JEJUNIO GLORIOSISSIMÆ DEIPARÆ, Quodque servandum sit ut legitimum (COTELEBIUS, Monumenta Ecclesiæ Græcæ, tom. III, p. 452 sqq.)
Quoniam tua erga me postulatio, frater spiritualis, erat de jejunio gloriosissimæ Dei Genitricis, ut ea de re scripto acciperes in benedictionem et munus; ego vero licet indignus, attamen ut omnes orthodoxi, ego quoque inutilis servus Matris Dei, quemadmodum accepi, observare connicor; tum maxime quia in loco ubi habitamus, sanctæ meæ Dei Genitricis monasterii templum est ac vocatur. Oportet ergo illud jejunium diligentius custodire, non solum nos, verum etiam Christianos omnes, quemadmodum a divinis Scripturis testimonia habemus. Quod enim caret testimonio, firmitate destituitur, et quod propria voluntate suscipitur, noxium est; quod autem ex Scripturis et legibus habet testimonia, firmum simul atque utile exsistit. Cæterum de legitimo hoc jejunio dictum est in opere quod Synodicum appellatur. Continet autem ille liber tres duntaxat synodos; quarum duæ de fide disserunt, tertia vero de nuptiarum differentia et dissidio, unde et Unionis nuncupatur. Aliud porro est hujusmodi synodicum ab eo quod dicitur Nomocanon; quod scilicet hoc quidem omnes simul sanctas synodos et divinos canones, legesque

Καὶ πάλιν εἰς λόγον Ἰωάννου (2) μητροπολίτου Νικαίας γραφέντα στηλιτευτικῶς πρὸς τὸν καθολικὸν τῆς μεγάλης Ἀρμενίας (3) κατὰ τῆς τοῦ Ἀρτζιβουρίου νηστείας, ἀνατρέπων ταύτην ὡς παράνομον· ἐν οἷς φησὶν, ὅτι Μήτε τῶν ἁγίων ἀποστόλων γραψάντων τὴν τοιαύτην νηστείαν, μήτε τῆς ἐν Νικαίᾳ συνόδου, μήτε ἄλλου τινὸς τῶν Πατέρων, παρεκτὸς τετράδος καὶ παρασκευῆς καὶ τῆς μεγάλης Τεσσαρακοστῆς (5), καὶ τῆς ἑβδομάδος τοῦ Πάθους τοῦ Κυρίου, καὶ τῆς τῶν Χριστουγέννων νηστείας, καὶ τῶν ἁγίων ἀποστόλων, καὶ τῆς ὑπερενδόξου Θεοτόκου, τίς ὁ διδάξας ὑμᾶς νηστείαν νηστεύειν ἐθνῶν; Ὁρᾷς πῶς καὶ ὧδε ῥητῶς καὶ ἠκριβωμένως συναριθμεῖ τὴν τῆς ὑπερενδόξου Θεοτόκου νηστείαν, ταῖς λοιπαῖς τῶν ὀρθοδόξων νηστείαις; Ἐγὼ δὲ καὶ ἄλλαις περὶ ταύτης ἐντυχον μαρτυρίαις· ἀλλ᾿ ἀρκεῖν ἡγημαι ταύτας [al. ταύτας], ὡς γέγραπται, ὅτι, Ἐπὶ στόματος δύο ἢ τριῶν μαρτύρων πᾶν ῥῆμα σταθήσεται.
Ὅτι δὲ μετὰ τὰς προγραφείσας μαρτυρίας πολλοὶ ταύτην κρατοῦσι καὶ ἐκ παραδόσεως ἁγίων καὶ ἀρχαίων Πατέρων καὶ εὐσεβῶν, δῆλον ἡμῖν ἐκ τε τῶν εἰρημένων, καὶ πάλιν ἑτέρου τινὸς τῶν Πατέρων ἰσχυρῶς ἀνατρέποντος τὰς τῶν ἐναντίων ψευδεῖς καὶ ἐπικινδύνους νηστείας. Ὅμως οὖν λέγομεν καὶ ἡμεῖς ὧδε, ὅτι ἐνταῦθα τὸ ὄνομα τῆς Θεομήτορος ἐν τῇ ταύτης νηστείᾳ ὁμολογούμενον, οὐ συνεσκιασμένως, ἀλλὰ τρανῶς καὶ φανερῶς, τῆς ὑπερενδόξου Θεοτόκου λέγεται. Οὐδὲ γάρ εἰς ὄνομά τινος τῶν παρ᾿ Ἕλλησι μυθευομένων παρ᾿ αὐτῶν καὶ τιμωμένων λέγεται ἡ τοιαύτη νηστεία, οὔτε πάλιν τῶν τινῶν αἱρέσεων, ὥσπερ ἡ τοῦ Ἀρτζιβουρίου, καὶ ἄλλων τοιούτων ψευδῶν. Ἀλλ᾿ οὐδὲ ψιλῶς τῆς ἁγίας Μαρίας λέγεται, καθὼς οἱ τῶν αἱρετικῶν παῖδες φρονοῦσι καὶ λέγουσιν· οὔτε ἄλλως καὶ ἄλλως καθ᾿ οἱονδήποτε ἐπιτήδευμα καὶ συνεσκιασμένως, καθάπερ οἱ τὰ τοιαῦτα ποιοῦσι τὴν ἀπάτην καὶ ἀπώλειαν τῶν ἁπλουστέρων· ἀλλὰ φανερῶς, ὡς εἴρηται, εἰς

Illic igitur in synodo Unionis, postquam dixit ac pronuntiavit de pœna irroganda trigamis, modo et tempore excommunicationis eorum, subjungit hæc verba; Tribus solum temporibus fruentur participatione divinorum mysteriorum: semel, in salutari Christi Deique nostri Resurrectione; secundo, in Natali Christi; tertio, in Obdormitione gloriosissima Dei Genitricis: quia, inquit, in his jejunium præcedit, et emolumentum ex eo proveniens. Ecce hic velut magis necessarium præfert jejunio Sanctorum Apostolorum. Et adhuc in Invectiva quam Joannes metropolita Nicææ scripsit ad Catholicum magnæ Armeniæ contra jejunium Artziburii, quod tanquam illegitimum evertit; ubi ait: Cum nec sancti apostoli hoc jejunium scripto consignarint, neque Nicæna synodus, neque alius quispiam ex Patribus, sed tantummodo tetradem et parasceven, sive quartam et sextam feriam, et magnam Quadragesimam, et hebdomadem Passtonis Dominicæ, atque jejunium Natalis Christi, nechon jejunium Sanctorum Apostolorum, itemque gloriosissimæ Deiparæ, quis docuit vos jejunare ac peragere gentium jejunium? Vides quomodo etiam hic ad verbum et accurate reliquis recte de fide scutientium jejuniis connumeret jejunium gloriosæ admodum Deiparæ? Ego vero alia quoque de eodem nactus sum testimonia; sed hæc arbitror sufficere, sicut scriptum est: In ore duorum aut trium testimonium stabit omne verbum¹.
Quod autem post superius scripta testimonia, multi hoc jejunium ex traditione sanctorum et veterum Patrum piorumque teneant, nobis liquet tum ex memoratis, tum præterea ex alio quodam Patre, qui adversariorum falsa periculosaque jejunia fortiter evertit. Attamen nos quoque hoc loco dicimus, nomen Matris Dei hic in ejus jejunio professum, non obumbrate et obscure, sed clare ac aperte nominari gloriosæ supra modum Virginis. Neque enim jejunium hoc profertur nomine alicujus ex iis qui a Græcis seu gentilibus fabulose excogitati sunt atque honorati, nec etiam ex iis quorum dam hæresum, sicut jejunium Artziburii, aliorumque pariter mendacium; sed nec simpliciter sanctæ Mariæ dicitur, uti sentiunt aiuntque hæreticorum filii seu hæretici; neque aliter juxta ullum artificium aut tenebricose, quemadmodum ii similia faciunt ad deceptionem exitiumque simpliciorum; at manifesto, ut dictum est, in nomine Deiparæ effertur

ac peragitur hujusmodi jejunium; quo nempe modo reliqua nomen Filii Deique ejusdem Virginis gerunt; cum Ecclesia sibi proponat, Christianos prius purificari, ad occursum festorum Domini ejusque Matris, ex afflictione ad gaudium, et spiritualem lætitiam transeuntes, pie considerantes ea quæ in ejusmodi solemnitatibus a Salvatore Christo propter salutem ex dispensatione simul ac benignitate peracta sunt. Est enim festum celebrare, ut illi qui in theologia præcelli (Gregorio) et nobis ac veritati placet, cognoscere vim mysterii. Porro qui non ita affecti sunt, non advertunt se Judaice festa peragere. Quod si quis quæsierit, ubinam et quando, et a quo synodus Unionis celebrata sit, audiat a nobis, non in angulo, inquam, neque in civitate una e multis, neque a vili quodam, sed in regia urbe, hoc est Constantinopoli, et a religioso patriarcha, et episcopis, cæteroque Ecclesiæ cœtu, ac per pios orthodoxosque imperatores, Romanum senem et Constantinum, perque reliquos religiosos, sapientes et cognitione præditos viros. Ii quippe omnes in Spiritu sancto congregati, Ecclesiam ex causa diversarum nuptiarum scissam, Dei gratia unierunt, quo factum est ut convenienter appellationem Unionis acceperit. Sancta ergo hæc synodus, post finem totius constitutionis, velut confirmans et obsignans ea quæ a superioribus synodis fuerant statuta, hujusmodi verba subjungit : Canon primus : Universa quæ præter ecclesiasticam traditionem, et doctrinam ac dispositionem sanctorum celebrumque Patrum innovata gestaque sunt, vel in posterum gerentur, anathema. Canon quartus : Contemptui habentibus sacros divinosque canones beatorum Patrum nostrorum, qui et sanctam Ecclesiam fulciunt, totamque rempublicam Christianam ornant, et ad divinam pietatem dirigunt, anathema. Atque ita quidem scripta testimonia constituunt, legitimum esse immaculatæ prorsus Deiparæ jejunium. Non minori vero multum apud me loco ea sunt quæ ex traditione per vivam vocem accepi. Namque patriarcha Antiochiæ totiusque Orientis, beatæ memoriæ domnus Petrus, ipse hæc observabat tradebatque cæteris. Simili quoque modo domnus Theodosius; prætereaque quæ nunc patriarchatum tenet sanctissimus domnus Joannes, hoc omnibus tradidit jejunium, scriptis usus testimoniis. Atque illi omnes, coryphæi seu apostolorum principis sedem obtinentes, non a se exorsi illud servaverunt, sed cunctos prædecessores suos secuti : quibus nos etiam viles obsequendo, initium Augusti custodimus, sive postquam sanctorum Maccabæorum solemnitatem peregerimus. ὄνομα τῆς Θεοτόκου λέγεται καὶ τελεῖται ἡ τοιαύτη νηστεία· ὥσπερ οὖν καὶ αἱ λοιπαί, εἰς ὄνομα τοῦ Υἱοῦ καὶ Θεοῦ αὐτῆς ὀνομάζονται· σκοπὴν ἐχούσης τῆς Ἐκκλησίας τοῦ προαγνίζεσθαι τοὺς Χριστιανούς, εἰς τὴν ἀπαντὴν τῶν ἑορτῶν τοῦ Κυρίου καὶ τῆς Μητρὸς αὐτοῦ· ἐκ θλίψεως εἰς χαρὰν μεταβαίνοντας, καὶ εὐφροσύνην πνευματικήν· εὐσεβῶς ἀναθεωροῦντας τὰ ἐν ταῖς τοιαύταις ἑορταῖς, ἐπὶ σωτηρίᾳ ἡμῶν παρὰ τοῦ Σωτῆρος Χριστοῦ τελούμενα οἰκονομικῶς ἅμα καὶ φιλαγθρώπως. Ἑορτάσαι γάρ ἐστιν, ὡς τῷ τῆς θεολογίας προὔχοντι, ἡμῖν τε καὶ τῇ ἀληθείᾳ δοκεῖ, τὸ γνῶναι τοῦ μυστηρίου τὴν δύναμιν. Οἱ δὲ μὴ οὕτω διακείμενοι, λελήθασιν ἑαυτοὺς Ἰουδαϊκῶς ἑορτάζοντες. Εἰ δέ τις ζητοίη, ποῦ καὶ πότε καὶ παρὰ τίνος ἡ τῆς ἑνώσεως γέγονε σύνοδος, ἀκουέτω παρ᾿ ἡμῶν, ὅτι οὐκ ἐν γωνίᾳ φημί, οὔτε εἰς τὴν τυχοῦσαν πόλιν καὶ παρὰ τοῦ τυχόντος, ἀλλ᾿ ἐν τῇ βασιλευούσῃ τῶν πόλεων, τῇ Κωνσταντίνου φημί, καὶ παρὰ εὐσεβοῦς πατριάρχου καὶ ἐπισκόπων, καὶ τοῦ λοιποῦ πληρώματος τῆς Ἐκκλησίας, καὶ παρὰ εὐσεβῶν καὶ ὀρθοδόξων βασιλέων, Ῥωμανοῦ τε τοῦ γέροντος, καὶ Κωνσταντίνου, καὶ τῶν λοιπῶν εὐσεβῶν σαφῶν τε καὶ γνωστικῶν ἀνδρῶν. Οὗτοι γὰρ ἅπαντες ἐν ἁγίῳ Πνεύματι συναχθέντες, τὴν Ἐκκλησίαν ἐσχισμένην (4) οὖσαν ἐξ αἰτίας τῶν διαφόρων γάμων, τῇ τοῦ Θεοῦ χάριτι· ἥνωσαν· ἐξ οὗπερ καὶ τὴν τῆς ἑνώσεως προσωνυμίαν εἴληφε προσηκόντως. Ἡ τοιαύτη τοίνυν ἁγία σύνοδος, μετὰ τὸ τέλος πάσης διατάξεως, ἐπικυροῦσα ὥσπερ καὶ σφραγίζουσα τὰ διὰ τῶν πρώην συνόδων διαταχθέντα, ἐπάγει λέγουσα· Κανὼν πρῶτος· Ἅπαντα τὰ παρὰ τὴν ἐκκλησιαστικὴν παράδοσιν καὶ τὴν διδασκαλίαν καὶ ὑποτύπωσιν τῶν ἁγίων καὶ ἀοιδίμων Πατέρων καινοτομηθέντα καὶ πραχθέντα, ἢ μετὰ τοῦτο πραχθησόμενα, ἀνάθεμα. Κανὼν τέταρτος· Τοῖς ἐν καταφρονήσει τιθεμένοις τοὺς ἱεροὺς οὓς καὶ τὴν ἁγίαν Ἐκκλησίαν ὑπερείδουσι, καὶ πρὸς θείαν ὁδηγοῦσιν εὐλάβειαν, ἀνάθεμα. Ἄλλ᾿ οὕτω μὲν αἱ γραφικαὶ μαρτυρίαι συνιστῶσιν, ἔνθεσμον εἶναι τὴν τῆς πανάχραντου Θεοτόκου νηστείαν. Εἰσὶ δέ μοι καὶ κατὰ παράδοσιν διὰ ζώσης φωνῆς, οὐχ ἧττον τούτων καταπολύ. Ὁ γὰρ πατριάρχης Ἀντιοχείας καὶ πάσης Ἀνατολῆς ὁ ἐν μακαρίᾳ τῇ λήξει κῦρις Πέτρος, αὐτὸς ταῦτα ἐφύλαττε, καὶ τοῖς λοιποῖς παρεδίδου· ὁμοίως καὶ ὁ κῦρις Θεοδόσιος· πρὸς δὲ καὶ ὁ νῦν πατριάρχης κῦρις Ἰωάννης καὶ ἁγιώτατος, πᾶσι παρέδωκε ταύτην, ἐγγράφως ἔχων τὰς μαρτυρίας. Καὶ οὗτοι δὲ πάντες τὸν τοῦ κορυφαίου θρόνου ἐπέχοντες, οὐκ ἀφ᾿ ἑαυτῶν ὁρμώμενοι, ταύτην ἐφύλαττον, ἀλλὰ τοῖς πρὸ αὐτῶν πᾶσιν ἑπόμενοι· οἷς καὶ ἡμεῖς οἱ εὐτελεῖς ἐπακολουθοῦντες, ἀπαρχὴν Αὐγούστου φυλάττομεν, ἤγουν μετὰ τὸ τελέσαι τὴν τῶν ἁγίων Μακκαβαίων ἑορτήν. ἡ παρασκευή, προλαμβάνοντες εἰς τρίτην καὶ εἰς τὴν πέμπτην ἢ εἰς τὴν προλαβοῦσαν Κυριακήν, ἀποτυρούσωσαν, καὶ τοῦ λοιποῦ τὴν νηστείαν κρατείτωσαν· καὶ μὴ ἡ τῶν Μακκαβαίων νηστείας οὔσης ἡμέρα, πρόφασις γένοιτο ἡ ἑορτὴ αὐτῶν, ἐν τῷ καταλεύειν τὴν νηστείαν. Καὶ μακάριοι καὶ εὐλογημένοι, οἱ τῆς εὐλογημένης Θεοτόκου τὴν Τεσσαρακοστὴν φυλάττοντες ἐν τῇ κοιμήσει αὐτῆς. Τῷ δὲ Θεῷ ἡμῶν δόξα εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας. Ἀμήν.

Since your request to me, spiritual brother, was regarding the fast of the glorious Mother of God, so that you might receive in writing a blessing and gift concerning this matter; and since I, though unworthy, am like all the orthodox, an unprofitable servant of the Mother of God, I endeavor to keep it just as I have received it; and especially because in the place where we dwell, there is a temple, and it is named for my holy Mother of God. Therefore, it is necessary to keep this fast more diligently, not only for us, but also for all Christians, just as we have the testimonies from the divine Scriptures. For that which lacks testimony is found to be uncertain, and that which is undertaken by one's own will is harmful; but that which has the testimonies of the Scriptures and the laws is found to be both firm and beneficial.
Regarding this authorized fast, it is spoken of in the work called the *Synodicon*. Such a book contains only three synods; and two of these treat of the faith, while the third is about the difference and disagreement in marriages, from which it is called the *Union*. Such a *Synodicon* is one thing, and what is called the *Nomocanon* is another; for the latter contains all the holy synods together, and the divine canons, and the civil laws, whereas the former...
includes only three synods. There, therefore, in the Synod of Union, after stating and proclaiming the penalty to be imposed upon those who marry three times, and the mode and time of their excommunication, he adds these words: "They shall enjoy the participation of the divine mysteries only at three times: once, at the saving Resurrection of Christ our God; secondly, at the Nativity of Christ; and thirdly, at the Dormition of the most glorious Mother of God," because, says he, a fast precedes these, and the benefit resulting from it. See how here he prefers this fast as more necessary than the fast of the holy Apostles. And again, in the Invective which John, Metropolitan of Nicaea, wrote to the Catholicos of Greater Armenia against the fast of Artziburium, which he refutes as illegitimate, where he says: "Since neither the holy apostles consigned this fast to writing, nor the Nicene synod, nor any other of the Fathers, except the fourth and sixth days of the week, and the great Quadragesima, and the week of the Lord’s Passion, and the fast of the Nativity of Christ, and of the holy Apostles, and of the most glorious Theotokos, who taught you to fast and observe the fast of the nations?" Do you see how here too he explicitly and accurately enumerates the fast of the most glorious Theotokos with the other fasts of the orthodox? I have indeed encountered other testimonies regarding this, but I think these to be sufficient, as it is written: "In the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall stand."
That many, however, keep this fast after the aforementioned testimonies, and according to the tradition of the holy and ancient, pious Fathers, is clear to us both from what has been said, and again from another of the Fathers who strongly refutes the false and dangerous fasts of the adversaries. We ourselves also say here, that the name of the Mother of God is confessed in this fast of hers, not obscurely, but clearly and openly, as the name of the most glorious Virgin beyond measure. For this fast is not called by the name of any of those who are fabulously invented and honored by the Greeks or Gentiles, nor again by that of any heresies, such as the fast of Artziburium and other such falsehoods. But it is not called simply "of holy Mary," as the children of the heretics think and say, nor in any other way by any artifice or obscurely, as those who do such things for the deception and destruction of the simpler ones do; but manifestly, as it has been said, it is celebrated in the name of the Theotokos.
Just as the other fasts are also named in the name of her Son and God, the Church has the goal of having Christians purified in anticipation of the feasts of the Lord and of his Mother, passing from affliction to joy and spiritual gladness, piously contemplating those things which were accomplished in such solemnities by the Savior Christ for our salvation, both by dispensation and by his kindness. For to celebrate a feast, as he who excels in theology (Gregory) says, which pleases us and the truth, is to know the power of the mystery. But those who are not so disposed do not notice that they are celebrating feasts in a Jewish manner. And if anyone asks where, when, and by whom the synod of Union was celebrated, let him hear from us that it was not in a corner, I say, nor in just any city, nor by just anyone, but in the royal city—the one of Constantine, I say—and by a pious patriarch and bishops, and the rest of the body of the Church, and by pious and orthodox emperors, Romanus the elder and Constantine, and the rest of the pious men, both wise and endowed with knowledge. For all these, gathered in the Holy Spirit, through the grace of God united the Church, which had been torn asunder because of diverse marriages, from which it has appropriately received the name of Union. This holy synod, therefore, after the end of all the regulations, as if confirming and sealing those things which had been established by the previous synods, adds words of this kind: Canon first: Everything that is innovated and done contrary to the ecclesiastical tradition and the teaching and disposition of the holy and celebrated Fathers, or that shall be done hereafter, let it be anathema. Canon fourth: To those who hold in contempt the holy and divine canons of our blessed Fathers, which both support the holy Church and adorn the whole Christian commonwealth, and guide men toward divine piety, let them be anathema.
And so indeed the written testimonies establish that the fast of the utterly immaculate Theotokos is legitimate. But not of less weight with me are those things which I have received from tradition by the living voice. For the patriarch of Antioch and of the whole East of blessed memory, Lord Peter, was himself keeping these things and handing them down to the others; likewise also Lord Theodosius; and moreover, the one who now holds the patriarchate, the most holy Lord John, has handed this fast down to everyone, using written testimonies. And all these men, holding the seat of the prince of the apostles, not setting out from themselves, kept it, but followed all their predecessors; whom we also, though base, in following, keep the beginning of August, that is, after we have performed the solemnity of the holy Maccabees. But if indeed a fasting day occurs, that is, whenever we thus hear of the Church being divided into parts.
And blessed and fortunate are they who observe the Quadragesima of the blessed Theotokos for her Dormition. To our God be glory unto the ages. Amen.
Otherwise, on the same fast.
The fast which precedes the Dormition of the most holy Theotokos has been handed down by the holy Fathers and divine patriarchs, and all the cities and regions of the Orthodox keep it with honor, especially the great and prosperous city of Constantinople, and the Great Church. But later, in the days of Lord Leo the Philosopher and Emperor, the feast of the Transfiguration having been invented, some who were lovers of pleasure took the occasion of those days—the days of the fast, I mean—to eat meat on that same day, as if they were supposedly honoring the feast of the Savior. This the most wise emperor prohibited by established law; whence the entire fifteen-day fast is observed by all and kept in purity; and those who do so are protected more and more by the protectress of the whole world, the most holy Theotokos.
OF SAINT NICON.
And let these things be said concerning the fast of the most holy Theotokos. But we also see another fast taking place, concerning which we have been unable to find anything handed down in writing; nevertheless, it is observed deeply and everywhere. I speak of the Exaltation of the precious Cross. And just as it was said above concerning the great saints, that some of the faithful prepare themselves for their feasts through purification, so also they say of this feast that some of the faithful purify themselves beforehand, on account of the salutation and veneration of the precious Cross, and again on the very day, on account of the same salutation, and because on that day everything concerning the Cross is chanted and read—both the Apostle and the Gospel, and in a word, the whole office of the Cross is performed; and that it is celebrated for one day in the catholic Churches, and the fast is reasonable. Concerning the fast of the second day of the week, this is decreed both in the Nomocanon of the Faster and in the Typikon of Jerusalem; in the same way as the discourse previously demonstrated the fast of the most glorious Dormition of the Theotokos, it is reported in the Synodikon in the penance for those involved in third marriages. These, then, are the universal fasts of Christians, from the divine Scriptures: Monday, Wednesday, and Friday throughout the year, unless a reasonable cause prevents it, and the great Quadragesima, and the week of the Passion of the Lord, and the Quadragesima of the Nativity of Christ, or...
the week of the Passion of the Lord, and the Quadragesima of the Nativity of Christ, and those of the holy apostles, and that of the most glorious Theotokos. But that of the precious Cross we have received not from the scriptures, but from common tradition, as was written above. And this is the precise account concerning the fasts.

The Typika, furthermore, the Studite and the Jerusalemite, celebrate two days, namely the feast itself and the day following. The fast of the precious Cross, therefore, was not delivered by any writings, but is held by common tradition. And let the matter regarding the fasts have its end here.

(Leo Allatius, *De Nicetis*, ap. Mai, *Bibl. nov. Patr.*, tom. VI.)
Nicetas Seron is mistakenly divided into two by Possevinus in his *Apparatus*, namely into Nicetas, bishop of Heraclea, and Nicetas Seron. But he is most poorly treated by the Gesnerian Epitomators as Nicetas Heradius, Nicetas Serronius, Nicetas of Serres [τοῦ Σερρῶν], and Nicetas, metropolitan of Heraclea. For he is one and the same author who composed all that is ascribed to the aforementioned. In my diatribe on Psellus, when I was debating against heretics regarding the age of Michael Psellus, in order to refute them, I brought forward many things from manuscripts about Nicetas David and Nicetas Seron, which even they had to concede, and subsequently I concluded that Nicetas Seron and Nicetas David were distinct individuals; and I held the opinion that he flourished in the age of Theophylact, archbishop of Bulgaria, to whom the ninth letter of Theophylact among those published by Meursius is addressed. Regarding the diversity of the Nicetas, I have with me the most learned Vossius in his new edition on Greek writers: yet it is the opinion of Leo Allatius, in his diatribe on Psellus, that these Nicetas are distinct persons, and I subscribe rather to him in this part. He was first a deacon and master or doctor of the Great Church, then metropolitan of Serres, then translated to Heraclea. He was a man most learned and pious. He wrote many and various things. But his primary and not inglorious efforts were in explaining the Bible according to the mind of the holy Fathers.

NICETAS SERRONIUS: NOTICE
(Leo Allatius, *De Nicetis*, ap. Mai, *Bibl. nov. Patr.*, tom. VI.)
Nicetas Seron is mistakenly divided into two by Possevinus in his *Apparatus*, namely into Nicetas, bishop of Heraclea, and Nicetas Seron. But he is most poorly treated by the Gesnerian Epitomators as Nicetas Heradius, Nicetas Serronius, Nicetas of Serres [τοῦ Σερρῶν], and Nicetas, metropolitan of Heraclea. For he is one and the same author who composed all that is ascribed to the aforementioned. In my diatribe on Psellus, when I was debating against heretics regarding the age of Michael Psellus, in order to refute them, I brought forward many things from manuscripts about Nicetas David and Nicetas Seron, which even they had to concede, and subsequently I concluded that Nicetas Seron and Nicetas David were distinct individuals; and I held the opinion that he flourished in the age of Theophylact, archbishop of Bulgaria, to whom the ninth letter of Theophylact among those published by Meursius is addressed. Regarding the diversity of the Nicetas, I have with me the most learned Vossius in his new edition on Greek writers: yet it is the opinion of Leo Allatius, in his diatribe on Psellus, that these Nicetas are distinct persons, and I subscribe rather to him in this part. He was first a deacon and master or doctor of the Great Church, then metropolitan of Serres, then translated to Heraclea. He was a man most learned and pious. He wrote many and various things. But his primary and not inglorious efforts were in explaining the Bible according to the mind of the holy Fathers.
What has reached our hands are: his grammatical canons chanted according to musical mode. Grammatical rules which are harmonized to musical chant, as well as troparia. Nicetas Serron male a Possevino in duos discerpitur in apparatu, in Nicetam nimirum Heracleæ episcopum, et Nicetam Serron. Sed pessime ab Epitomatoribus gesuerianis in Nicetam Heradium, Nicetam Serronium, Nicetam τοῦ Σερρῶν, et Nicetam metropolitanum Heracleæ. Namque unus et idem est auctor, qui omnia prædictis ascripta composuit. In diatriba mea de Psellis, cum de ætate Michaelis Pselli adversus hæreticos disceptarem, ut eos refellerem, multa de Niceta Davide, et Niceta Serrone, quæ et illi concedere debebant, ex codicibus manuscriptis attuli, et postmodum conclusi Nicetam Serronem, et Nicetam Davidem alium atque alium fuisse; in eamque ivi sententiam, Theophylacti Bulgariæ archiepiscopi ætate floruisse, ad quem nona Theophylacti epistola inter impressas a Meursio legitur.
