PROLOGUS HUJUS OPERIS.
Antiochus ille, qui mundo penitus renuntiave, et in magni illius Sabæ monasterio non exiguo tempore in secessu vitam duxit; dum sibi ipsi vacaret et attenderet, adeptus rerum salutarium notitiam secundum illud præceptum, Vacate et scitote, collectis Spiritus sancti divitiis, neque segnis ad eas egentibus largiter effundendas, cum centenarium fructum retulisset et talentum multiplicasset, ut tanquam servus fidelis, prudens et bonus a clemente Domino benedictionem acciperet: hic, inquam, Antiochus quo tempore efferus Persarum animus impetusque eorum sanguinarius totam Orientalem plagam Romano sceptro parentem miserabili exitu ad suum traxit imperium, hinc opportunam nactus occasionem, sacrum quemdam librum ex divinis eloquiis adornavit, ut illo eodem in libro manifestissime declarat, eidemque hujusmodi titulum indidit, illumque in medium protulit ad maximam præsentium sui temporis et futurorum utilitatem. Post hæc autem alius quidam monachus, verbo quidem imperitus, sed non scientia secundum Paulum divinum præconem, qui a teneris in lege divina meditatus fuerat die ac nocte, ut docet psalmista David, oliva fructifera effectus plantatus in domo Dei in monte cui nomen Maurus, multo tempore signis apud indigenas et advenas celebratus, quo tempore Constantinus cognomento Ducas imperii gubernacula administrabat, arrepta et ipse occasione invasionis impiorum Turcarum; secum reputans eloquia divinitus tradita secundum magnum et sublimem illum Dionysium esse nostræ hierarchiæ sub substantiam et facultates, veramque perniciem et animæ mortem nec esurie nec siti induci, sed potius penuria audiendi verbi Dei: nec posse tali temporum conditione sacros libros magno numero transportari, quandoquidem grandi instante metu ex regione in regio-
easily inflammable over the foundation of Christ. For this reason, we have need here for the voluntary burning that comes through tribulation. For just as gold is purified in a furnace, so too does the soul receive its own splendor through the cauterizing fire of tribulation. Let us be on guard, brethren, lest by contaminating the first and second garment, in which we were clothed through baptism and repentance, we be cast out from that pure and radiant bridal chamber, and our lot be cast with the hypocritical demons. For are not the wicked authors hypocrites, who, being darkness, pretend to be light? Let us place our hope in Him who has willed to grant us purity through the burning of trials which we endure either voluntarily or by necessity. Our hope shall not be put off, and that which we expect shall be brought to its conclusion. And then shall appear the blessedness of those who have been proven by fire through patience, of which may we all be made partakers in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be glory and power and adoration, with the Father who is without beginning, and the all-holy and good and life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.

PROLOGUS OF THE PRESENT WORK.
Antiochus, that man who renounced the world in its entirety and lived for no small time in retirement in the monastery of the great Sabas in Jerusalem; while he occupied himself with and attended to himself, and had attained the knowledge of saving things according to that commandment, "Be still and know," having thus amassed the riches of the Spirit, and nor being sluggish in pouring them out liberally to those in need, having brought forth fruit hundredfold and multiplied the talent, so that as a faithful, prudent, and good servant he might receive the blessing from the benevolent Lord: this Antiochus, when at that time the beastly mind of the Persians and their bloodthirsty impulse dragged the whole Eastern region, which was subject to the Roman scepter, into their own empire with a miserable outcome; finding therein a providential occasion, he composed a sacred book from the divine utterances, as he himself in that book most clearly declares, and gave it such a title, and brought it into public view for the greatest benefit of those who from then until now have encountered it. Thereafter, another monk, unlearned in speech but not in knowledge according to Paul the divine herald, and who from his tender years had meditated day and night in the divine law, as the Psalmist David teaches, having become a fruit-bearing olive tree planted in the house of God, on the mountain which is named Maurus, honored for a long time among the natives and the strangers, at the time when Constantine surnamed Ducas was administering the helm of the empire, having himself seized the occasion of the invasion of the impious Turks; and considering with himself that the divinely-transmitted oracles, according to the great and sublime Dionysius, are as it were the substance and faculties of our hierarchy, and that true destruction and the death of the soul are brought about not by hunger nor by thirst, but rather by the poverty of hearing the word of God; and that in such a condition of the times, sacred books could not be transported in great numbers, since great fear was impending from region to region.
