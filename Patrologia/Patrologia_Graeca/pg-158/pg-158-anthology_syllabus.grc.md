For if we were to see, or perhaps would seem to see, those who are believed to be towers of strength, and are believed by others, miserably ruined, we would be astonished. I speak the truth, for I know many religious people from various orders who today preach Christ with great fervor, but tomorrow would shamefully deny Him. Therefore, I think it is more useful to distrust everyone and fear all. But let us return to the beginning. We, who live in these parts, know and experience the truth of all that has been said, and even greater and worse things than we describe, so what do you think our hearts are like? I confess, such great sorrow, such mental anguish, such bitterness has befallen us, that for a long time now, neither food nor sleep has brought us anything but sadness. Even the sight of the sun, the green regions, the sea, and the land, evokes lamentation rather than solace, and since we have been in these parts, we have not had a single hour of peace from the things we continually hear and see. For we are in Constantinople, in a most prominent place, in the Chapel of Constantine, near his palace, which was consecrated by Saints Jerome, Augustine, and Nicholas, along with the other 318 Fathers of the Council of Chalcedon, from where, ascending to a high point, we can easily see the entire city, which was once the most excellent in the world, but now lies in such great calamity, that it is more fitting to weep than to marvel, for it appears to have been a paradise of delights, both in its location and its buildings, and other advantages, with so many churches and monasteries, now collapsed in the most astounding ruin, both inside and out, so much so that you would think you had stumbled upon a forest of monasteries, in which two hundred, three hundred, and even a thousand monks resided, singing praises to the Most High in the most sweet melody, so that the very air resonated with an angelic harmony in the middle of the night. But now, alas, thorns and thistles climb over the walls, and owls and buzzards resonate there. If someone were to see the destroyed cities, once marvelous, such as Chalcedon and Nicaea, which are almost adjacent to this city, and many others, of which not even a sign remains, I do not think they could restrain themselves from tears, for all these things have been destroyed by the hands of infidels. We often speak of these things, we lament them, and we spend a significant part of our time mourning, preferring to die rather than see the final evils, which increase daily for our people.
