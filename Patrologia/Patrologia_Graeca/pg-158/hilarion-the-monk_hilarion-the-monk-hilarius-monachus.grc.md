Ἐν τῷ μὲν διαλέγεσθαί με περὶ τοῦ ἄρτου μυστικοῦ τῶν Ἑλλήνων, καὶ ἀζύμου τῶν Λατίνων, ἐδείχθη μοι λόγος, ἤτοι ἐξήγησις τινὸς Νικήτου, Πατρῆς ἐν τοῖς Ἕλλησι σοφωτάτου, ἐν ᾧ μὲν πολλὰ περὶ τῶν ὀξύμων ἐν τῷ δείπνῳ τοῦ Χριστοῦ, ταῦτα εἰς τοὺς Λατίνους ἀφήγηται· Οὐκ ἦν Ἄζυμα κατὰ τὴν Πέμπτην ἐκείνην, τρισκαιδεκαταίας οὔσης τῆς σελήνης. Κατὰ γὰρ τὴν πεντεκαιδεκάτην αὐτῆς νενομισμένον ἦν τὰ Ἄζυμα γίνεσθαι. Καὶ διὰ τοῦτό φησιν ὁ θεῖος Λουκᾶς, καὶ οἱ λοιποὶ τῶν ἀποστόλων εὐαγγελισταί· Λαβὼν ἄρτον, καὶ εὐχαριστήσας, ἔκλασεν ἄρτον, λιτὸν καὶ οὐκ ἄζυμον· οὐ γὰρ ἦν. Ταῦτα ὁ Νικήτας. Καὶ ἄλλαχοῦ· Εἰ δὲ Χριστὸς ἐτύθη καὶ ἐσταυρώθη τῇ τεσσαρεσκαιδεκάτῃ τῆς σελήνης, κατὰ τὴν ἡμέραν τῆς Παρασκευῆς, τῷ δὲ Σαββάτῳ ἦν  ἑορτὴ ἀζύμων, πότε τὰ ἄζυμα ἔφαγε, καὶ πότε ταῦτα τοῖς ἀποστόλοις ποιεῖν ἐν τῇ Καινῇ Διαθήκῃ παρέδωκεν; καὶ ἑτέρα. Ἐγὼ δ’ ἂν εἴποιμι θαυμάζειν με ταῦτα τοῦ Νικήτου, ἀνδρὸς πεπαιδευμένου. Δύο γὰρ λέγει σαφῶς ἐναντία τοῖς Εὐαγγελίοις, καὶ τοῖς τοῦ Νόμου ῥήμασι, περὶ τῆς δηλοῦτι τοῦ ἀμνοῦ βρώσεως, καὶ καιροῦ Πασχικοῦ, ὅτι τῷ Σαββάτῳ ἦν, οὐ τῇ Παρασκευῇ. Ἔξεστι δὲ ῥᾳδίως ἀποφαίνειν ταῦτα ὅλα εἶναι ψευστᾶ, καὶ οὐδὲν τάληθές. Καὶ τὸ πρῶτον μὲν λέγειν, τὸν Χριστὸν ἐσθίοντα τὸν ἀμνὸν οὐ φαγεῖν τὰ ἄζυμα, ἀντιλογίαν ἔχει. Cum de mystico Græcorum pane et Latinorum azymo dissererem, oratio mihi quædam, sive expositio Nicetæ cujusdam, Patris inter Græcos doctissimi, ostensa est, in qua ille pleraque de azymis in Cœna Domini, sed hæc adversus Latinos, excurrit: Feria illa quinta non erant Azyma, cum tertia et decima luna mensuraretur : namque in decima quinta lege præscriptum erat, Azyma fieri. Proptereaque divinus Lucas, et reliqui inter apostolos evangelistæ dicunt : « Accipiens panem, et benedicens, fregit, » panem utique communem, et non azymum; nondum enim erat. Hæc Nicetas. Et alio in loco : Si vero Christus sacrificatus est, et crucifixus quarta et decima luna, die Parasceves; Sabbato vero erat dies festus Azymorum, quando azyma comedit, et quando ea apostolis facere, in Novo tradidit Testamento? et reliqua. Ipse vero dixero, me ista summiopere admirari, a Niceta, viro equidem crudito et docto, prolata. Duo namque adversus Evangelia legisque verba aperte enuntiat, de agni scilicet esu, et paschali tempore, quod esset Sabbato, et non Parasceve. Quæ omnia facili negotio falsa, et nihil veri in se continere demonstrare possumus. Et primum quidem dicere Christum edentem agnum, azyma non comedisse, contradictione involvitur. Nunquam enim in lege Deus de agno paschali, absque eo quod azymorum mentionem induceret, præcepit. Namque ad Moysen et Aaron ita effatur : Tollat unusquisque ovem per familias et domos suas, ovem perfectam, masculam, anniculam, ex agnis et hædis, immolabitque eam universa multitudo, et edent carnes nocte illa assas igni, et azymos panes cum lactucis agrestibus 1. Hæc sunt verba legis divinæ ad legum latores. Si itaque Christus agnum ex præscripto legis comedit, quomodo dicetur azyma non comedisse? Et in Novo Testamento Matthæus evangelista: Prima autem die Azymorum accesserunt discipuli ad Jesum, dicentes illi: Ubi vis paremus tibi comedere Poscha? Et Marcus: Primo die Azymorum, quando Pascha immolabant, dicunt ei discipuli usus: Quo vis eamus, et paremus tibi, ut manduces Pascha? Et Lucas: Erat autem dies Azymorum, qua immolandum erat Pascha, et misit Petrum et Joannem, dicens: Euntes parate nobis Pascha, ut manducemus. Quæ Theophylactus archiepiscopus Bulgariæ, Græcus interpres hunc in modum exponit: Nominat evangelista primum Azymorum, tanquam eum qui Parascevem præcederet, et qua ad vesperam edebant azyma. Videte ergo, Græci dilectissimi, apertissime colligi tum ex veteri lege, tum ex Novo Testamento, Christum, cum ederet agnum, comedisse quoque azyma. His addere possumus et Judæorum consuetudinem, qui ad vesperam quartæ et decimæ lunæ azyma comedebant. Quod si Latinos ex panis appellatione redarguitis, Christus scilicet panem accipiens, et benedicens, fregit, dico omne fermentatum panem esse. Et cum quispiam panem pronuntiat, utrumque notari et azymum et fermentatum. Et quanam ratione quidam stolide atque insipienter asseverant, azymum non esse panem, ex novo quodam panis etymo argumenta sumentes? ἄρτον panem nempe dici, quod in se contineat ἄρσιν elationem fermenti, azymum vero, quod hanc elationem non habet, panem dici non posse. Nugacia sunt hæc etyma. Nos vero non absque ratione, et ut philosophus addecet, rem puram enuntiamus: Panem genus esse, et in species suas azymum et fermentatum, quemadmodum animal in hominem et equum, subdividi, et æquivoce genus de suis speciebus prædicari. Et veluti homo et equus nomen et definitionem animalis admittunt; nomen, quod utraque animalia, definitionem, quod utraque sunt essentia animata et sensibilis: non alia ratione azymum et fermentatum panis. Namque idem nomen participant, quod utrumque panis est, et definitionem, quod, essentia frumenti panisque utrumque dicitur esse. Ad hæc, quis recte fermentum panis perfectionem nuncupabit, quod eumdem panem corrumpit? Namque Paulus inquit, Nosse scitis, fratres, modicum fermentum totam massam corrumpere? Ex Mosaica porro lege, in qua præscriptum est, Decimo et quarto die primi mensis ad vesperam Pascha Domino, et decimo quinto die mensis, hic erit dies festus Azymorum Domino. ARGYROPULUS: For it is not from the Father alone, so that the Son might not be thought unlike him, not having the same perfections as the Father. And for this reason, not even consubstantial, which in no way differs from the heresy of Arius, which alienates the Son from the divine nature of the Father.
AGALLIANUS: But, my good man, Arius did not alienate only the Son from the divine nature, but also the Holy Spirit. Macedonius is no less of a heretic than Arius, being diseased with this same opinion regarding the Spirit alone. If, therefore, we are Arians, and [if we are] granting to the Son the perfections of the Father—which you perhaps believe to be natural—then you certainly have no argument left with which you will escape the madness of Macedonius. But for us who provide reasonable accounts regarding this, confirmed by the saints, it is easy to dissolve the accusation. You, however, entrenched in such a position, and thinking that things are as you propose the arguments, will never be able to escape Macedonianism. For he himself declared earlier that if powers are different, then those things themselves would be different in essence. If, therefore, you really think it necessary for the powers and perfections of the Father to belong to the Son, it is absolutely necessary that they also belong to the Spirit. Then the Son will be generative and procreative. Similarly, the Spirit will also be generative and procreative, even if neither is begetting and proceeding in vain. But if the Son acts according to the one, and the Spirit according to neither, and the Father according to both, and if their energies are different, then they themselves would be different in their essences, and much more so as [they are] of the powers, given that the energies exist: consider to what this matter will lead. So that you have appeared not only as a Macedonian, but also as an Arian himself, and no argument will deliver you from this disaster.
We, however, say that to beget and to proceed are hypostatic perfections of the paternal person, as not co-existing with the essence of the Father, but, insofar as it is in the Father, we hear from the theologians that this is his property: the Source, and the God-begetter, and the Fruit-bearing, in which are [the acts of] begetting and proceeding; and for this reason, it is necessary that neither of these—or at least, begetting and proceeding—should be shared with another of the persons from him, but belong to him alone. For all things befitting God exist in the whole Godhead, concerning which Gregory is in agreement, calling it common to Father, Son, and Holy Spirit that they are unbegun and... Come then, let us see, he says, the things of the Father, so that we may recognize the image if it is his. Is the Father eternal? Immortal, King, Powerful, Light, Almighty, God, Lord, Creator, and Maker. These must be in the image, so that he who has truly seen the Son has seen the Father. If, therefore, the saints had known that it was without danger to believe the Son was a cause or a co-cause with the Father, and that from this his equality and consubstantiality with the Father was acquired, in what other struggles would they have used this? Or from where else in the discourses against Nestorius would they have attempted it, earlier and more nobly, than from this? But nowhere among such discourses, though there are many, is such a thing found, as if it had been overlooked because it was weak, or as if it were a matter of secondary importance. For if it were true, it would be the most powerful and important of all.
But one will find countless other things in the writings of the saints that establish the consubstantiality in the super-essential Trinity from common attributes befitting God, but never from the hypostatic perfections and properties; for they are not of the nature to be shared. Rather, this has seemed to the saints a source of heresies and blasphemy, from which all retreated as from fire. If, therefore, the Father is the sole source of the super-essential Godhead, and it is his property to be the source and cause of the Godhead contemplated in the Son and the Spirit, and he is proclaimed as the sole cause, and from him is the Spirit and from no other—for we do not say the Spirit is from the Son—there is no reasonable cause why anyone should not be considered an Arian, saying that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone. For how [could one be an Arian], if what is said is most pious and in agreement with the theology of the teachers? But he who fears so much and turns away from the unlikeness of essence, has fallen into such an abyss by the very things you have devised.
ARGYROPULUS: For this reason, the teachers of the Church and the Seventh Ecumenical Council declared that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father through the Son, not as if the Son were a cause, nor simply a source and producer of the manifestation of the Spirit, but as a co-cause and source, and from a source.
