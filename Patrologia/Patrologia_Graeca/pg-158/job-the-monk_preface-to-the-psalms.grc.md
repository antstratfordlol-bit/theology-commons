JOB THE SINNER’S PREFACE TO HIS COMMENTARY ON THE PSALMS.
Our purpose in venturing to unfold the sacred utterances of David, as though stirring up some aromatic fragrance of incense and delighting soul, mind, and every sense, is not to suggest that the divine teachers and theologians of the Church of old, in expounding these utterances along with the other Scriptures, examined and contemplated them inadequately or with any negligence, and so handed them down to us who came after them. Certainly not: such a thing is far removed from their wisdom and grace alike. For they have said and recorded everything fully and perfectly, and in a manner that could not be bettered; and whatever was lacking has been supplied by them through the power and grace of the Holy Spirit, in accordance with this promise of the Lord. Rather, having chosen, next to the canonical Scriptures, to breathe and speak these utterances above all, to turn them over in my mind, to dwell upon them throughout my life, and to have nothing else
at the threshold now, at such a time and in such a condition, save by offering these words alone as a prayer. May the same be granted equally to all who bear the name of Christ. Henceforth relying on the utterances of the blessed Fathers and teachers, which, after the first and true light, “which enlightens every person coming into the world,” and his words and commandments of eternal life, suffice to enlighten anyone whatsoever, I too, emboldened by their prayers, have launched the frail little boat of my understanding upon the open sea of their spiritual knowledge, to sail safely alongside their sound, richly laden merchantmen and cargo ships and accompany them on their course. Someone might say that some will perhaps find our work rather more elegant, since its manner is also somewhat novel. I have placed my trust in the same wise helmsman and Master, God the Word, and in the same fellow workers, equipment, and instruments—the champions of the word and guardians of our souls—and in the same assistance of favorable winds, the help and grace of the all-holy Spirit. By his power and their supplications I have been brought into the harbor of the divine will, in words as well as in deeds. Hence perhaps no one would readily condemn us if we have ventured so far in boldness and have not ourselves, along with the many excellent men who came after them, rested content with the lengthy, varied, and beneficial discourses of the divine Fathers.
