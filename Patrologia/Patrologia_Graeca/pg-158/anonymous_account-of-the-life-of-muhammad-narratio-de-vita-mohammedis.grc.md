Ἀνδρισθέντος δὲ τούτου ἐρασθεῖσα αὐτοῦ ἡ γυνή, συνεγένετο αὐτῇ πορνικῶς· φωραθεῖσα δὲ καὶ τὸ ὄνειδος ἐκφυγεῖν ἐθέλουσα τῶν πολλῶν φανερῶς τοῦτον ἄνδρα πεποίηται· δοῦσα δὲ αὐτῷ μέρος τι τῶν αὐτῆς ὑπαρχόντων εἰς ἐμπορίαν ἐξέπεμψε· ἡνίκα δὲ ἔλλα. ὅτι σεληνιαζόμενος συνεχῶς οὐκ ὀλίγον αὐτῇ ὄνειδος ἐνεποίει, ὅθεν παρήγγειλεν αὐτῷ ζητεῖν καὶ ἐρωτᾶν, ὅπου ἂν περιγένοιτο, εἰ ἄρα δυνηθείη εὑρεῖν τινα θεραπευτὴν ἰατρὸν τοῦ ἐλευθερῶσαι αὐτὸν τοῦ πάθους. Τοιγαροῦν καθήμενος; ὁ ῥηθεὶς μοναχὸς ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ καὶ ἰδὼν τὸν Μωάμεδ πορευόμενον, ἐπυνθάνετο αὐτοῦ ἐκ ποίας ἔρχοττο πόλεως· μετὰ πολλὰ δὲ αὐτὸς τοῦτον ἐρωτήσας, ὅτου χάριν προσμένει παρὰ τὴν ὁδόν· ἀλλ᾿ ἀλλ᾿ ἕτερο; θατέρου πυνθανόμενος, ὡς ἤκουσε μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων ὁ Μωάμεδ, ὅτι μετέρχεται καὶ ἰατρικὴν ἐπιστήμην, περιχαρὴς ὥσπερ γενόμενος καὶ τὸν αὐτοῦ σεληνιασμὸν ἀπαγγείλα, ὡς ἤκουσεν ὅτι δύναται τοῦ τοιούτου πάθους αὐτὸν ὁ μοναχὸς ἐλευθερῶσαι, δοὺς αὐτῷ μέρος τι δαπάνης· ἀφῆκεν εἰπών, Ἐν τῷ ἐπαναστρέφειν με ἐξ Ἱερουσαλὴμ ἀναβιβάσω σε μετ᾿ ἐμαυτοῦ ἐν τῷ ἐμῷ οἴκῳ ἐς μεσοποταμίαν ἕνεκεν ἐμοῦ τε καὶ ἄλλων ἰατρείας πολλῶν, ἐπεὶ ἡ χώρα ἀπορεῖ κομιέθ ἰατροῦ, εἰς τὸ συλλέξαι χρήματα καὶ ἀνειμένην καὶ ἀνώδυνον διαβιδάζειν ζωήν. Εἰς Ἱερουσαλὴμ τοίνυν κατελθὼν καὶ ὁμιλήσας τισὶ τῶν αἱρετικῶν, καὶ μάλιστα Ἰουδαίοις καὶ Νεστοριανοῖς, ὡς λίαν ὢν φιλόλογος, ἀλλὰ δὴ καὶ Ἰουδαίοις σοφοῖς

Ἐν δὲ τόπῳ γενόμενος, ἔνθα τὴν ῥηθεῖσαν μοναχὸν εὗρεν οἰκοῦντα παρὰ τὴν ἐδὸν, ἀνελόμενος αὐτὸν κατὰ τὸ πρὸς αὐτὸν σύνθημα καὶ ἐπὶ τῆς καμήλου τοῦτον ἀναβιβάσας ἥνυον τὴν ὁδοιπορίαν· κατὰ τὴν ὁδὸν ἀναδιδάξαντες αὐτὸν ὁ μὲν μοναχὸς τελείως τὴν Ἀρειανισμῶν, οἱ δὲ Ἑβραῖοι τὴν περιτομὴν ὡς σύνθημα ποιησάμενοι μετ᾿ αὐτοῦ, ἐλέσθαι τὴν ἑαυτῶν θρησκείαν τὴν Μωάμεδ, μόνον εἰ ὑπ᾿ αὐτῶν ἰατρευθείη. Ἐν τῇ ἰδίᾳ τοίνυν τούτου πατρίδι τῇ Μεσοποταμίᾳ ἀνασωθέντες λαβὼν τὸν μοναχὸν καὶ τοὺς Ἰουδαίους ἐξένισεν αὐτοὺς παρὰ τὸν ἴδιον οἶκον, δοὺς τῷ μοναχῷ ἰδίως οἶκον σμικρότατον, ἐγγὺς τοῦ αὐτοῦ οἴκου εἰ; τὸ καταμένειν. Πολλῶν δὲ φιλωνεικιῶν γεγονυιῶν μετ᾿ ἀλλήλων, τοῦ τε μοναχοῦ μετὰ τῶν Ἰουδαίων καὶ αὖ ἐκείνων πρὸς αὐτὸν δισχυριζομένων καὶ ἀμφοτέρων, ὡς ἄρα ὁ Μωάμεδ ἐδιδάχθη τὴν σφετέραν θρησκείαν, ὁ μοναχὸς ἤθελε τοῦ Μωάμεδ χωρισθῆναι. Ὁ δὲ παρικλήθη προσμεῖναι καὶ ὃ ἂν αὐτὸς εἴπῃ πρᾶξαι, ὑμάτιον ὁ μοναχὸς ποιησάμενος περὶ τοῦ Μωάμεδ, καὶ εὑρὼν ἐν αὐτῷ ὡς μέγας γενήσεται Μωάμεδ καὶ αὐξήσει, αἱ αἵρεσιν τινα καὶ θρησκείαν ἐπαναλάδηται, προσκαλεσάμενος τὸν Μωάμεδ, ἔφη πρὸς αὐτόν· Εἰ ἀκούσεις μου τῆς συμβουλῆς, ὅσον οὔπω μέγας γενήσῃ. Τοῦ δὲ ὑπακούειν συνθεμένου ἔφη αὐτῷ· Ὅτε πίπτων εἰς τὴν γῆν σεληνιάζῃ, ἀνιστάμενος λέγε, ὅτι τὸν ἄγγελον Γαβριὴλ ὁρῶν πρὸς ἐμὲ ἐρχόμενον καὶ ὁμιλοῦντα, μὴ δυνάμενος ἀντοφθαλμῆσαι τῇ λαμπρότητι τοῦ προσώπου αὐτοῦ πίπτω, ὡς ὁρᾶτε, ἐς γῆν.— Καὶ ὁ μοναχὸς, ἰδὼν τοῦ, ἐγχωρίους τελείως βαρβάρους καὶ δεισιδαίμονας· καὶ ἑτοίμους δέξασθαι, ἃς πρὸς αὐτοὺς ἐρεῖν φλυαρίας ἐσκαιωρήσατο, ἔλεγε τοῖς παροῦσι καὶ ὁρῶσι τὸν δαιμονιζόμενον· Θεωρεῖτε ἃ γίνεται, βλέπων τὸν Θεὸν καὶ τοὺς ἀγγέλους ὁ Μωάμεδ ἔνθα.ς γίνεται. Οἱ δὲ προρρηθέντες· Ἰουδαῖοι δυσχεράναντες τῷ Μωάμεδ καὶ μὴ θέλοντες αὐτὸν ἰατρεύειν ὡς μὴ ἐξακολουθήσαντα τῇ αὐτῶν θρησκείᾳ μᾶλλον ἢ ταῖς ὑποθήκαις τοῦ μοναχοῦ, θέλων καὶ αὐτοὺς ἱκανοποιῆσαι, παρεδέξατο ἐκ τῶν Ἰουδαϊκῶν ἠθῶν τὴν τε περιτομὴν καὶ τὸ ὑπομόναζειν μόνον τοὺς προφήτας· τὰ δὲ γεγραμμένα ὑπὸ τε Μωσέως καὶ τῶν λοιπῶν προφητῶν, ὡς τῷ μοναχῷ ἔδοξε, πέπραχε περὶ τούτων. Τούτων οὕτως γενομένων καὶ τῶν ἐγχωρίων πιστευσάντων τῷ νέῳ νόμῳ τοῦ Μωάμεδ, ἀποροῦντες ἀρχῆς ἐξ ὑποθήκης τοῦ μοναχοῦ, καὶ ἄρχοντα τοῦτον ἑαυτοῖς ἐποιήσαντο, ὡς εἶναι ὁμοῦ καὶ νομοθέτην καὶ προφήτην καὶ βασιλέα· τότε καὶ στρατιωτικὸν σύστημα ποιησάμενος ἐλήζετο τὰς πέριξ κωμοπόλεις. Ἀκουσθεὶς δὲ τῷ βασιλεῖ Ἡρακλείῳ ἀπέστειλε στρατὸν πρὸς αὐτὸν, καὶ παραγενόμενοι οἱ Ῥωμαῖοι ἐκεῖσε ἄρδην τοὺς ὑπουργοὺς Μωάμεδ ἀποκτείνουσιν· αὐτὸς δὲ φυγὼν μετ᾿ ὀλίγων τινῶν στέλλει πρὸς βασιλέα πρεσβείαν καλῶν ἑαυτὸν δοῦλον τοῦ βασιλέως, καὶ ἀξιῶν, ἵνα ἐάσῃ τοῦτον ἀναπαύεσθαι ἐν ἑνὶ μέρει τῆς Αἰθιοπίας, διδόναι τῷ βασιλεῖ φόρον· ὁ δὲ πεισθεὶς εἴασε τοῦτον. Μετὰ δὲ τοῦτον οἱ διάδοχοι αὐτοῦ καὶ τὸ ὑπόλοιπον τελείως ἀπώλεσαν καὶ διέφθειραν καὶ ἠφάνισαν. Τοῦτο μὲν ἐν συνόψει εἴρηται περὶ τούτου ἅπαν τὸ ἀληθές. Τέλος.
The thrice-cursed and most godless Muhammad, who among the Hagarenes subject to him is called Machumet and is honored by them as a great prophet and even as one from God, appeared in the days of the Emperor Heraclius, having been born in a certain part of Ethiopia to Ethiopian parents. He grew up and reached this point, progressing for the worse in truth, in the following manner. A certain monk named Gerasimus, being an extreme Arian and Pneumatomachian, who they say originated from Isauria, was raised in the city of Constantine and in the city of the Romans. Though much pressured by the pious to renounce Arianism and the other heresies by which he was held, he was not persuaded. Therefore, so that he might not be a scandal of perdition to many, being otherwise learned, he was exiled by the orthodox to the regions of Coele-Syria. Living there in a certain cave by the royal road in order to beg for his livelihood, since he was hated, he occupied himself mostly with astronomy and the effective method. Seeing those passing by, he saw one day, among other travelers, a certain young man, the aforementioned Machumet, driving a few camels and descending from Mesopotamia for trade in Jerusalem. For this aforementioned Muhammad, having been orphaned and wandering about while still very young from door to door and being fed by leftovers, was seen by a certain very wealthy widow named Fatuma, who took him into her house, raised him, and sent him out day by day to tend her own camels.
When he had grown up, the woman fell in love with him and had illicit relations with him. Having been discovered, and wishing to escape the reproach of the multitude, she openly made him her husband. She gave him a portion of her possessions and sent him out for trade. She was otherwise distressed because, being constantly afflicted with epilepsy, he caused her no small reproach. Whence she instructed him to seek and inquire, wherever he might arrive, if he could find some physician to heal him and free him from the affliction. Therefore, the aforementioned monk, sitting on the road and seeing Muhammad traveling, asked him from what city he came. After much questioning of each other as to why he was waiting by the road—but simply one asking the other—when Muhammad heard, along with the others, that he also practiced medical science, he became overjoyed. Having reported his own epilepsy and having heard that the monk could free him from such an affliction, he gave him a portion of his expenses and left, saying, "Upon my return from Jerusalem, I will take you with me to my house in Mesopotamia for the sake of my own healing and that of many others, since the country is completely lacking in physicians, so that you may collect money and lead a relaxed and painless life." Having descended into Jerusalem and having conversed with some of the heretics, and especially with Jews and Nestorians, as he was very fond of discourse, but also with wise Jews...
...having encountered, and having been taught by the Jews [the doctrine of] the monarchy, and having devoured all her wealth, the monk, defending himself to her, said: Do not be distressed, for Mahomet is a great prophet. Hearing these things and believing them, as a woman will, she exhorted and preached to the other women that Mahomet is a great prophet; and the error, passing from the women to the men, who were barbarians and very ignorant, was believed to be the aforementioned prophet of God. And when the monk saw his design prospering, he thought it necessary to give them both a faith and a law, for the establishment, supposedly, of Arianism and the other heresies for which he was an exile; and sitting down, he wrote a book, the so-called Koran, which is the law of God, having sown into it all his own impiety, teaching that God has neither Word nor Spirit, and that Christ is not God, but a great prophet, and he wrote down many other absurdities; and having given it to his own disciple Mahomet, he proclaimed to those senseless people that this book had descended to Mahomet from heaven from the angel Gabriel while he was sleeping; and they believed it to be so, as the monk had secured it regarding the new law. And the aforementioned Jews, being distressed with Mahomet and not wishing him to be a prophet, as he had not followed their religion but rather the suggestions of the monk, and wishing to satisfy them as well, he accepted from the Jewish customs both circumcision and the mere naming of the prophets; but as for the things written by Moses and the other prophets, he acted according to what seemed best to the monk. These things having happened, and the locals having believed in the new law of Mahomet, they were at a loss for a leader, and by the suggestion of the monk, they made him their ruler, so that he was at once lawgiver, prophet, and king; then, having also created a military system, he plundered the surrounding villages. When this was heard by the Emperor Heraclius, he sent an army against him, and the Romans, having arrived there, utterly killed the ministers of Mahomet; but he himself, having fled with a few others, sent an embassy to the Emperor, calling himself a servant of the Emperor, and asking that he be allowed to rest in one part of Ethiopia, promising to pay tribute to the Emperor; and he, being persuaded, allowed it. [He took] from the Pneumatomachi the doctrine of the spirit-fighters, from the Hemerobaptists the daily washing for the supposed remission of their own sins, wrongly interpreting the voice of Isaiah, "wash yourselves and become clean," and from the Nestorians and Arians that Christ is not the true God, but a great prophet who came for the salvation of the world; having collected these and other seeds of the impious from there, being young and still a fire-worshipper, upon his return he encountered two Jews who were practicing medicine, who were fellow travelers with him to Mesopotamia, and having been overjoyed, as he was about to be healed by them, as they asserted, he finished the journey with them. And having arrived at the place where he found the aforementioned monk living by the road, he took him up according to the agreement with him, and having mounted him upon his camel, they finished the journey; along the way, they instructed him, the monk fully in Arianism, and the Hebrews in circumcision, having made it a sign with him, that Mahomet should choose their religion, only if he were healed by them. Therefore, having arrived safely in his own homeland, Mesopotamia, he took the monk and the Jews and hosted them at his own house, giving the monk a very small house privately near his own for him to stay in. And many quarrels having occurred between them, the monk with the Jews and theirs against him, and both asserting that Mahomet had been taught their religion, the monk wished to be separated from Mahomet. And when he was entreated to remain and to do whatever he might say, the monk made a horoscope regarding Mahomet, and finding in it that Mahomet would become great and would increase if he should take up some heresy and religion, he called Mahomet to him and said to him: If you will listen to my advice, you will soon become great. And he having agreed to obey, he said to him: When you fall to the ground and have a seizure, upon rising, say that you saw the angel Gabriel coming to you and speaking, and that being unable to look upon the brightness of his face, you fall, as you see, to the ground. And the monk, having seen the locals were completely barbarian and superstitious and ready to accept the nonsense he had devised, said to those present and watching the one possessed: Observe what is happening, seeing God and the angels, Mahomet becomes inspired. And when he would rise, and according to the suggestion of the monk would say that he falls because he sees an angel, the monk called him a prophet, deceiving those who heard. And when the wife of Mahomet was distressed, as Mahomet found an opportunity, he plundered Syria; then, from little or nothing, having been restored to much because of our sins, he destroyed the greater part of the Roman Empire. And after him, his successors completely destroyed and ruined and annihilated the remainder. This has been said in summary regarding all the truth about him. The End.
