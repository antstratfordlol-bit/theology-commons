### Die 5


[999] {#pg.115.999}
11. Hæc cum audisset martyr, ad cœlum quidem  tollit oculos, quod illine proficiscitur, evocans auxilium; signo autem Christi munita (5); Es moribus quidem, ait, varius, o consularis, vaferque et callidus; sed ad nullam utilitatem aperte tibi redibit hæc perversitas; neque enim me capies adulans, nec me minans perterrefacies, nec consulens me poteris in aliam traducere sententiam, nec mollire id, quod est in me promptum propter Christum patiendi studium. Confido enim in solo et vero Deo, et in eo omnem vitam meam reposui. Si mihi ergo credas, miserere potius tui erroris. Consule tibi ea, quæ sunt utiliora, nempe surda non colere simulacra; de quibus alicubi dixit noster propheta in spiritu : « Dii, qui cœlum et terram « non fecerunt, pereant (6); » et : « Dii gentium  « dæmonia, Dominus autem cœlos fecit ». Et rursus : « Simulacra gentium argentum et aurum, « opera manuum hominum. Similes eis fiant, qui « colunt ea » (7).
Ill. Postquam hæc audivit judex, ira rursus repletus, jussit quidem ejus capillos radi in superficie (8). Illud autem factum est, et maximum consecutum est miraculum. Caput enim rursus fuit comatum. Ex quo judex majore ira motus, jubet prunas ardentes imponi ejus capiti, deinde etiam acetum ei infundi, dolores sic præbens acriores. Propter quæ generosa virgo, Domine Jesu Christe, dixit, eorum, qui in te fiduciam posuerunt, certum et firmum auxilium, quo tuos sanctos tres pueros in medio flammæ ardentis incombustos conservasti (9), tu quoque nunc mihi adsis, et ad ea, quæ propter te suscipiuntur, confirma supplicia, ne quando de me liceat dicere inimicis veritatis : « Ubi est Deus eorum? » Post preces autem relaxata fuit martyr a doloribus, et Deum laudabat ei agens gratias. Quæ quidem statim ægre ferens consularis, ferreos candentes obeliscos (10) jubet infigi uberibus martyris. Hoc ergo gravi ei illato tormento, carnes quidem ferreis illis obeliscis consumebantur; cordis autem ejus amor in Christum magis accendebatur; deinde etiam jubet lampades ignis ejus admoveri lateribus. Amen. Hæc illa sic oravit, et dissoluta sunt vincula, et solus lapis fuit in profundo demersus. Illa autem (o miraculum!) firmo ac stabili pede stabat super dorsum maris, et per aquas, tanquam per terram solidam ingrediens, proficiscebatur ad littus (13). Interim vero cum vidisset manentem consularem et exspectantem, quisnam futurus esset rei exitus, dicit: Salve, consularis. Ne nunc quidem intellexisti virtutem Christi, qui in me operatur? Hanc dicendi libertatem cum non ferret vir exsecrandus, eo autem, quod in eam acciderat, miraculo esset obstupefactus, a se discessit, eumque vox et sermo defecit. V. Hinc animo perplexus, et plane ignarus, quid ageret, præses non dubitavit alia excogitare supplicia. Jubet ergo manuum et pedum ungues ei evelli. Et illi quidem protinus fuerunt amputati. Tormentum autem ab illa reputabatur, perinde ac si alius pateretur (17). Ipse autem rursus prioribus alia adjiciens, ei quoque jubet dentes extrahi: sed quomodo præses in gravibus inveniendis, ita nec illa in gravibus ferendis suppliciis fuit defatigata. Itaque purum ejus corpus probro afficere cogitavit sceleratus. Et ad ministros conversus, Eam, inquit, accipite et in excelsum et medium aliquem locum ducite et præcones euntes per universam regionem finitimam, ad probrum ei afferendum convocent chorum impudicorum. Postquam autem probro et contumelia satis fuerit affecia, eam ad me reducite, ut ea, quæ desunt, ei adjiciam. Martyr autem Christi ei respondit: Est mihi Christus, qui omnia sola voluntate transmutat, et solo nutu, qui etiam meam animam hodie suscipiet immaculatam, tuis, o profane, dispersis et dissipatis consiliis. Hæc cum dixiset, manus simul, mentem et oculos in cœlum tollens, oravit Deum, quem desiderabat. Postquam autem oravit, cum pace apud eum deposuit animam sacrificans purum corpus et spiritum ei, propter quem omnia pro nihilo reputabat, melius judicans mori, quam peccare etiam præter sententiam (18).
VI. Qui autem, etiam post ejus mortem, apud se immortalem volebat alere insaniam, consularis quid machinatur? Jubet ejus corpus cum multa arena in saccum injici et projici in profundum maris, non magis illam deinceps crucians, quam Christianos, et volens eos privare divitiis et gloria reliquiarum (19). Atque sic quidem ad affectum est deductus consularis jussus; et in undas projectum fuit corpus martyris. Sed ne tunc quidem tui oblitus est, o Charitine, Deus, qui vicissim glorificat eos, qui ipsum glorificant; nam tunc quoque, ut solet, ostensus est faciens magnalia; et tres quidem
cumulus inter aculeos ferreos hic expressus non sit. Hoc autem rotæ supplicium sæpenumero non esse sine igne illatum, ex Apuleio et Euripide docet Pitiscus in suo Lexico verbo Rota.

IV. But since she was immutable in her confession both in heart and on her lips, the judge, who was just as unyielding in his wickedness as she was constant in her torments, orders a stone to be tied to her neck, and thus for her to be cast into the depths of the sea. This having been done, "I give You thanks, O Lord," the martyr cried out, "that it has seemed good to You that I should pass through the sea water for the sake of Your blessed name, so that I may be found pure on the day of the resurrection; but show Your miracles in me, as You always have and do even now, through which Your great name may be glorified more and more for ever and ever. Amen."
She prayed thus, and her bonds were loosed, and the stone alone sank into the deep. But she (O the miracle!) stood with an unshaken foot upon the surface of the sea, and treading upon the waters as if on solid ground, she went toward the shore. Meanwhile, seeing the proconsul waiting and appearing to expect to see what the end of the matter would be, whatever it might come to, she says: "Greetings, proconsul. Have you not yet understood the power of Christ who works in me? Or do you still persist in being blinded and in fighting against the truth? If, therefore, abandoning the darkness, you should run to the true light, you will procure salvation for yourself; but if you wish to persist still in your former ways, you will be the cause of many good things for me, but for your own soul you will be the agent of destruction."
The accursed man, not bearing this outspokenness, and being struck with amazement at the miracle concerning her, was beside himself and was seized with speechlessness. But when, at length, he had with difficulty recovered, he still did not wish to understand. But he says: "The things that have taken place persuade me to think the power of the Galilean is great, but I will endeavor to prove that these things were rather brought about by sorcery." But again, the help from on high displayed its accustomed power, and an angel descending quenched the flame of the coals, and indeed relaxed the strength of the executioners, so that they could not turn the wheel at all.
V. The governor, therefore, being perplexed here, did not, however, lack the ability to devise other tortures. He orders, then, the nails of her hands and feet to be torn out. And they were immediately cut away; but to her, it was as if another were suffering the torture. And he himself, adding other things to the previous ones, commanded her teeth to be knocked out as well; but just as she did not tire of bearing the dreadful things, so he himself did not cease from finding further dreadful things. The wretch then devised a plan to insult her pure body, and turning to his ministers, "Take her," he said, "and bring her to one of the high and most central places, and let heralds going throughout the entire neighboring region summon a choir of the profligate to offer her abuse. After she has been sufficiently insulted and disgraced, bring her back to me, so that I may add the things that remain." But the martyr of Christ answered him: "Christ is my [Lord], who transforms all things by his will alone and a nod of his mind; and he shall receive my soul immaculate today, having scattered and dispersed your counsels, O profane one." Having said these things, lifting her hands at once, and her mind and eyes toward heaven, she prayed to the God whom she desired. And when she had finished praying, she entrusted her spirit to him in peace, sacrificing both body and spirit in purity to him for whom she counted all things as nothing, judging it better to die than to sin even against her will.
VI. But what does the proconsul, wishing to nurture his immortal madness even after her death, devise? He commands her body to be cast into a sack together with much sand, and to be thrown into the deep of the sea, not so much tormenting her as thereafter afflicting Christians, and wishing to deprive them of the wealth and glory of her relics. And thus, then, the order of the proconsul was carried out, and the body of the martyr was thrown into the waves. (Acta SS. Bolland., for the 7th day of October.)

And again: 'The idols of the gentiles are silver and gold, the works of men’s hands. Let those who worship them become like them.'"
III. Since the judge heard these things, once again filled with anger, he commanded that the hair of her head be shaved off to the skin; and this was done, and a very great miracle followed it, for her head was again covered with hair. The judge, moved by greater anger, ordered hot coals to be placed upon her head, and then for vinegar to be poured onto it, thus providing for even sharper pains. Upon which the noble maiden said: "Lord Jesus Christ, the sure help of those who have placed their trust in Thee, who preserved Thy three holy children in the midst of the burning flame unharmed, do Thou also stand by me now and confirm me against the punishments being inflicted for Thee, lest it ever be possible for the enemies of the truth to say regarding me: 'Where is their God?'" And after this prayer, the martyr was released from her pains, and she was thus glorifying God with words of thanksgiving. At these words the proconsul again grew angry and ordered iron obelisks, heated in the fire, to be driven into the breasts of the martyr. So, when this cruel torture was applied to her, the flesh was being consumed by those iron obelisks, but the flame of her heart’s love for Christ was kindled all the more.
