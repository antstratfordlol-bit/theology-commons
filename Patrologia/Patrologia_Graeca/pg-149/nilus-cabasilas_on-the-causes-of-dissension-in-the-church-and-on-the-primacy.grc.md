LIBER PRIMUS, sive Oratio, demonstrans non aliam dissidii Ecclesiarum Latinarum et Græcanicarum causam esse, quam quod Papa ejus quod controversum est cognitionem ac judicium ad œcumenicam synodum deferre detrectet, sed ipse solus controversiæ magister ac judex sedere velit, reliquos vero discipulorum instar dicto audientes habere. Quod quidem ab apostolorum et Patrum legibus actionibusque est alienum.
Equidem existimo oportere me cum Latinis verba facturum, ejuscemodi orationis initium invenire, quod probabilem rationem in se habeat. Hæc vero est, causam intueri propter quam hoc Ecclesiarum dissidium et ortum sit primum, et in tantum efferbuerit, ut ne sperandus quidem sit finis, nisi Deus auxiliatricem manum porrexerit. Videmus sane et medicos, et alios qui operibus suis finem aliquem propositum habent, ante de curationis genere decernere, quam causarum ratio manifesta fuerit. Eiusdem generis fuerunt. Neque haec igitur dissidii est causa, multoque minus ipse Scripturae sermo, quod tanquam concisus nihil aperte de eo quod controvertitur, proponit. Scripturam enim incusare tantumdem fuerit ac Deum ipsum incusare. At Deus extra omnem culpam est. Penes quem vero sit culpa, quivis mente praeditus facile dixerit. Atqui homines qui ex utraque natione, Italica scilicet et Graecanica, confluunt, docti pariter ac probi, non patiuntur ut hominum idoneorum penuriam incusemus, eique distractionem hanc inputemus. Neque vero illud in causa est, quod Latini affirmant, nos nobis primatum arrogare velle, et in secundis post Romanorum principatum nolle consistere, simulque idcirco pacem detrectare. Neque enim unquam cum Romana Ecclesia de primatu contendimus, neque de secundo loco nunc agitur. Sed neque vetus consuetudo, neque Patrum decreta nos latent, quibus Romana Ecclesia antiquissima omnium Ecclesiarum declaratur. Alioqui neque aequum fuerit, ut id quod inficiamur, nobis objiciatur, certaque hujus rei demonstratio hoc praetextu occultetur. Quenam igitur tandem est hujus dissidii causa? quod scilicet quaestio controversa communi oecumenicae synodi decreto non sit confirmata; quod ejus solutio explicatioque non fiat ex veteri Patrum in ejuscemodi negotiis consuetudine, sed quod Romani quidem magistrorum in hac quaestione partes sibi sumunt, alios vero instar discipulorum dicto audientes habere velint. BOOK ONE,
or a discourse demonstrating that there is no other cause for the schism of the Latin and Greek Churches than this: that the Pope refuses to refer the cognition and judgment of the matter in controversy to an ecumenical council, but wishes to sit alone as the master and judge of the controversy, and to have the rest listen like disciples. Which, indeed, is foreign to the laws and practices of the Apostles and the Fathers.
I believe that for me, about to converse with the Latins, it is necessary to discover such an opening for the discourse as may contain within itself a reasonable basis. This is to contemplate the cause for which this schism of the Churches first arose and has flourished to such a degree that there is not even cause to hope for an end, unless God should extend a helping hand. B We see indeed that doctors, and others who have some end set before them in their works, do not decide upon the mode of treatment before the nature of the causes has become clear. For once the cause is removed, one may indeed hope well for peace; but while it remains, all labor is undertaken in vain. Therefore, the cause of this schism, as I surely suppose, is not the loftiness of the dogma, surpassing human comprehension. For all other matters, too, just as many as have caused trouble for the Church at various times, are like this. Neither, then, is this the cause of the schism, nor by much… [index: entries preserved]
less is the speech of Scripture itself [the cause], as if it were concise and not clearly declaring concerning that which is in dispute. For to accuse Scripture is the same as to accuse God. But God is without fault. Yet anyone who has intelligence would say that it is a matter of those who choose [to be at fault]. Moreover, men—those who come from both nations, the Italian and the Greek—both wise and good, do not allow us to blame a shortage of men, and to attribute the causes of the schism to this. But neither is that the case which the Latins claim, that we wish to arrogate the primacy to ourselves, nor that we refuse to be shown to be second after the authority of the Romans, and at the same time, because of this, jump away from peace. For we have never disputed with the Roman Church about the primacy, nor is the point of discussion now about the second place. But neither have we been so ignorant of the ancient custom, or the decrees of the Fathers, in which the Church of Rome is proclaimed the oldest of all Churches. Besides, it is not even fair that what we deny should be leveled against us, and that the certain demonstration of this matter should be hidden under this pretext. What, then, is truly the cause of this dissension? It is that the matter in question is not confirmed by a common ecumenical synod, and that the resolution does not proceed according to the ancient custom of the Fathers in such matters; but that the Romans wish themselves to sit as masters of the matter in question, and to hold others in the rank of disciples, obeying their word. But the decrees of the Fathers do not have it so.
