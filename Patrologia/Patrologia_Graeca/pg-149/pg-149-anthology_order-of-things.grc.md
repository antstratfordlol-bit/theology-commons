ORDER OF THE CONTENTS WHICH ARE CONTAINED IN THIS VOLUME
For if, leaving aside the tongue and running past those or those athletes who receive [it], you should be willing to fix your mind upon the discourse itself and to gaze upon the torch of the soul within, the small things will not appear small to you, but rather things lacking nothing at all, the debts being paid to you by us; since we know that God does not seek hymns according to their worth, but the fire of the heart, from which they proceed. For this reason, all things that fill creation, although unequally, yet in accordance with the capacity of their nature, are stirred to hymns of the Creator; and those of the animals that are vocal hymn Him with proportional voice, but the sea, and the earth, and stones, and snows also hymn Him, sending up a silent and unspeakable hymn, and God receives all things kindly. If, indeed, a crown of olive was woven for the Olympic victors, and not one of silver or gold, it is clear from this, too, that one should look not at the nature of what is given, but at the intention of the one awarding it. Do not, therefore, look at the crown being forged and braided for you from the logical vessels—if it be small and least worthy of your greatness—but look instead at the soul that is weaving it, with how much and what kind of eagerness it heaps up the matter of praises, and what kind of fuel it casts for such a fire; and you might see, as through a mirror, in some of these small syllables, treasures of pure faith and precious things of burning desire. But may the King of kings and almighty Lord transmit the royal power to you for long ages with great cheerfulness, so that it may be a cause of joy for your subjects also for long cycles of years.
