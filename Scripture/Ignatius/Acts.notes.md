# Acts — Notes

## Doctrinal notes

### Acts 1:1 {#nt.Acts.1.1}

*the first book*: i.e., St. Luke’s Gospel.

### Acts 1:14 {#nt.Acts.1.14}

*brethren*: See note on Mt 12:46.

### Acts 1:22 {#nt.Acts.1.22}

An apostle must be a witness to Christ’s resurrection.

### Acts 2:14 {#nt.Acts.2.14}

Peter assumes the leadership in public. In this discourse we have the earliest form of the apostolic preaching.

### Acts 3:1 {#nt.Acts.3.1}

In the early days, the first Christians observed the prescriptions of the Jewish law.

### Acts 4:2 {#nt.Acts.4.2}

The Sadducees did not believe in the resurrection of the dead.

### Acts 4:32 {#nt.Acts.4.32}

*everything in common*: They freely shared what was theirs individually; cf. Acts 5:4.

### Acts 5:11 {#nt.Acts.5.11}

*Church*: i.e., the Christian and Messianic community; a term borrowed from the Old Testament.

### Acts 5:20 {#nt.Acts.5.20}

*Life*: cf. Acts 9:2, “the Way.” These terms recall the words of Jesus, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life” (Jn 14:6).

### Acts 5:34 {#nt.Acts.5.34}

*Gamaliel*: Teacher of St. Paul; cf. Acts 22:3.

### Acts 6:1 {#nt.Acts.6.1}

*Hellenists*: Greek-speaking Jews of the Dispersion, who had their own synagogues in Jerusalem and read the Scriptures in Greek.

### Acts 8:20 {#nt.Acts.8.20}

Hence the word “simony,” meaning “buying and selling spiritual powers and privileges.”

### Acts 9:5 {#nt.Acts.9.5}

Jesus identifies himself with his followers.

### Acts 9:13 {#nt.Acts.9.13}

*saints*: i.e., Christians, made holy by baptism.

### Acts 10:16 {#nt.Acts.10.16}

The vision was to prepare Peter for his reception of Cornelius the Gentile and his household into the Church; cf. also Acts 15.

### Acts 12:1 {#nt.Acts.12.1}

The second wave of persecution: cf. Acts 8:1.

### Acts 13:16–41 {#nt.Acts.13.16}

This first recorded sermon of Paul is similar to that of Peter in Acts 2:14–36.

### Acts 16:10 {#nt.Acts.16.10}

This is the first of the passages in Acts in which the story is told in the first person plural, indicating that Luke, the author, was there. The manuscript Codex Bezae, however, has a “we” passage in 11:28.

### Acts 16:13 {#nt.Acts.16.13}

Being a Roman colony, Philippi had no synagogue within its walls.

### Acts 19:35 {#nt.Acts.19.35}

*the sacred stone* or statue of the goddess which, according to legend, came down from heaven. Possibly a meteorite.

### Acts 20:7 {#nt.Acts.20.7}

Celebration of the Eucharist on the Lord’s day, i.e., Saturday evening, according to the Jewish way of reckoning a day from sunset to sunset.

### Acts 20:34 {#nt.Acts.20.34}

Paul insisted on working for his living, though recognizing the apostle’s right to support by the faithful; cf. 1 Cor 9:4–7.

### Acts 21:4 {#nt.Acts.21.4}

*told Paul not to go*: This was not a command. The Holy Spirit enlightened them about what lay before Paul and they naturally wished to spare him; cf. verse 11.

### Acts 22:20 {#nt.Acts.22.20}

*your witness*: Greek, “martyr.” Witnessing by one’s death (i.e., martyrdom) is the supreme example.
