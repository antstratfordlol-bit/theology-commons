# Titus

#### Salutation

## Titus 1 {#Titus.1}

[1]{#Titus.1.1} Paul, a servant[^Titus-1-1a] of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, to further the faith of God’s elect and their knowledge of the truth which accords with godliness, [2]{#Titus.1.2} in hope of eternal life which God, who never lies, promised ages ago [3]{#Titus.1.3} and at the proper time manifested in his word through the preaching with which I have been entrusted by command of God our Savior;

[4]{#Titus.1.4} To Titus, my true child in a common faith:

Grace and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.

#### Titus in Crete

[5]{#Titus.1.5} This is why I left you in Crete, that you might amend what was defective, and appoint elders in every town as I directed you, [6]{#Titus.1.6} if any man is blameless, the husband of one wife, and his children are believers and not open to the charge of debauchery and not being insubordinate. [7]{#Titus.1.7} For a bishop, as God’s steward, must be blameless; he must not be arrogant or quick-tempered or a drunkard or violent or greedy for gain, [8]{#Titus.1.8} but hospitable, a lover of goodness, master of himself, upright, holy, and self-controlled; [9]{#Titus.1.9} he must hold firm to the sure word as taught, so that he may be able to give instruction in sound doctrine and also to confute those who contradict it. [10]{#Titus.1.10} For there are many insubordinate men, empty talkers and deceivers, especially the circumcision party; [11]{#Titus.1.11} they must be silenced, since they are upsetting whole families by teaching for base gain what they have no right to teach. [12]{#Titus.1.12} One of themselves, a prophet of their own, said, “Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons.” [13]{#Titus.1.13} This testimony is true. Therefore rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith, [14]{#Titus.1.14} instead of giving heed to Jewish myths or to commands of men who reject the truth. [15]{#Titus.1.15} To the pure all things are pure, but to the corrupt and unbelieving nothing is pure; their very minds and consciences are corrupted. [16]{#Titus.1.16} They profess to know God, but they deny him by their deeds; they are detestable, disobedient, unfit for any good deed.

#### Teach Sound Doctrine

## Titus 2 {#Titus.2}

[1]{#Titus.2.1} But as for you, teach what befits sound doctrine. [2]{#Titus.2.2} Bid the older men be temperate, serious, sensible, sound in faith, in love, and in steadfastness. [3]{#Titus.2.3} Bid the older women likewise to be reverent in behavior, not to be slanderers or slaves to drink; they are to teach what is good, [4]{#Titus.2.4} and so train the young women to love their husbands and children, [5]{#Titus.2.5} to be sensible, chaste, domestic, kind, and submissive to their husbands, that the word of God may not be discredited. [6]{#Titus.2.6} Likewise urge the younger men to control themselves. [7]{#Titus.2.7} Show yourself in all respects a model of good deeds, and in your teaching show integrity, gravity, [8]{#Titus.2.8} and sound speech that cannot be censured, so that an opponent may be put to shame, having nothing evil to say of us. [9]{#Titus.2.9} Bid slaves to be submissive to their masters and to give satisfaction in every respect; they are not to talk back, [10]{#Titus.2.10} nor to pilfer, but to show entire and true fidelity, so that in everything they may adorn the doctrine of God our Savior.

[11]{#Titus.2.11} For the grace of God has appeared for the salvation of all men, [12]{#Titus.2.12} training us to renounce irreligion and worldly passions, and to live sober, upright, and godly lives in this world, [13]{#Titus.2.13} awaiting our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior[^Titus-2-13c] Jesus Christ, [14]{#Titus.2.14} who gave himself for us to redeem us from all iniquity and to purify for himself a people of his own who are zealous for good deeds.

[15]{#Titus.2.15} Declare these things; exhort and reprove with all authority. Let no one disregard you.

#### Maintain Good Deeds

## Titus 3 {#Titus.3}

[1]{#Titus.3.1} Remind them to be submissive to rulers and authorities, to be obedient, to be ready for any honest work, [2]{#Titus.3.2} to speak evil of no one, to avoid quarreling, to be gentle, and to show perfect courtesy toward all men. [3]{#Titus.3.3} For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, slaves to various passions and pleasures, passing our days in malice and envy, hated by men and hating one another; [4]{#Titus.3.4} but when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, [5]{#Titus.3.5} he saved us, not because of deeds done by us in righteousness, but in virtue of his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal in the Holy Spirit, [6]{#Titus.3.6} which he poured out upon us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior, [7]{#Titus.3.7} so that we might be justified by his grace and become heirs in hope of eternal life.
[8]{#Titus.3.8} The saying is sure.

I desire you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed in God may be careful to apply themselves to good deeds;[^Titus-3-8d] these are excellent and profitable to men. [9]{#Titus.3.9} But avoid stupid controversies, genealogies, dissensions, and quarrels over the law, for they are unprofitable and futile. [10]{#Titus.3.10} As for a man who is factious, after admonishing him once or twice, have nothing more to do with him, [11]{#Titus.3.11} knowing that such a person is perverted and sinful; he is self-condemned.

#### Final Messages and Benediction

[12]{#Titus.3.12} When I send Ar'temas or Tych'icus to you, do your best to come to me at Nicop'olis, for I have decided to spend the winter there. [13]{#Titus.3.13} Do your best to speed Ze'nas the lawyer and Apol'los on their way; see that they lack nothing. [14]{#Titus.3.14} And let our people learn to apply themselves to good deeds,[^Titus-3-8d] so as to help cases of urgent need, and not to be unfruitful.

[15]{#Titus.3.15} All who are with me send greetings to you. Greet those who love us in the faith.

Grace be with you all.
