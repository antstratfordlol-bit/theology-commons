# 1 Timothy — Notes

## Doctrinal notes

### 1 Tim 1:2 {#nt.1Tim.1.2}

Timothy, son of a Greek father and a Jewish mother, was already a Christian when Paul met him (Acts 16:1). A close association ensued.

### 1 Tim 1:4 {#nt.1Tim.1.4}

*myths and endless genealogies*: A reference to the Jewish legends and spurious pedigrees added by false Judaizers to the Biblical narratives; cf. the Book of Jubilees.

### 1 Tim 1:20 {#nt.1Tim.1.20}

*delivered to Satan*: A form of excommunication; see note on 1 Cor 5:5.

### 1 Tim 2:6 {#nt.1Tim.2.6}

*ransom for all*: This is why Paul wants prayers for all (verse 1).

### 1 Tim 3:1 {#nt.1Tim.3.1}

*bishop*: At this time an office probably not distinct from that of priest.

### 1 Tim 3:11 {#nt.1Tim.3.11}

*women*: i.e., deaconesses.

### 1 Tim 4:3 {#nt.1Tim.4.3}

*forbid marriage*: As some Gnostics did.

### 1 Tim 4:3 {#nt.1Tim.4.3b}

*abstinence from foods*: As practiced by Judaizers.

### 1 Tim 5:3 {#nt.1Tim.5.3}

*real widows*: i.e., with no one to help and support them.

### 1 Tim 5:12 {#nt.1Tim.5.12}

Paul had no objection to widows marrying again; cf. 1 Cor 7:8–9. But the widows here had clearly made some sort of vow or promise to serve the Church in singleness. Paul recommended that younger widows should marry again (verse 14).
