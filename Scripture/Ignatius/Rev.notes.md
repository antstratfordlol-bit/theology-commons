# Revelation — Notes

## Doctrinal notes

### Rev 1:4–8 {#nt.Rev.1.4}

Describes the glorious coming and reign of the Messiah.

### Rev 1:13 {#nt.Rev.1.13}

*Son of man* refers to Dan 7:13. The Messiah is described in symbolic terms.

### Rev 2:10 {#nt.Rev.2.10}

*ten days*: Not literally. It means the persecution will be short.

### Rev 2:20–21 {#nt.Rev.2.20}

*immorality* here seems to mean idolatry rather than sexual excess.

### Rev 2:24 {#nt.Rev.2.24}

*deep things of Satan*: The doctrines of the Nicolaitans. *They* called them the “deep things of God.”

### Rev 2:28 {#nt.Rev.2.28}

*morning star*: Probably Christ himself.

### Rev 3:12 {#nt.Rev.3.12}

*new name*: cf. Is 62:2. Perhaps it was “the Word,” or perhaps it is not to be revealed till the last day.

### Rev 4:3 {#nt.Rev.4.3}

John describes God in symbolic terms.

### Rev 4:4 {#nt.Rev.4.4}

*elders*: They perform a priestly and royal task, since they praise God and share in the government of the world.

### Rev 4:6 {#nt.Rev.4.6}

*four living creatures*: cf. Ezek 1:4–25: the four angels who preside over the government of the world. But in Christian tradition these symbols are used for the four evangelists.

### Rev 4:8 {#nt.Rev.4.8}

*Holy, holy, holy*: Quoted in the *Sanctus* at Mass.

### Rev 5:1 {#nt.Rev.5.1}

*a scroll*: This contained God’s designs, kept secret till now; being written on both sides, nothing could be added.

### Rev 5:6 {#nt.Rev.5.6}

The seven horns and seven eyes symbolize Christ’s full power and knowledge.

### Rev 6:1 {#nt.Rev.6.1}

Begins the account of the destruction of the Roman Empire (chapters 6–9).

### Rev 6:5 {#nt.Rev.6.5}

*balance*: Symbol of famine. The balance was to measure rations.

### Rev 7:4 {#nt.Rev.7.4}

*a hundred and forty-four thousand*: A symbolic number, i.e., twelve (the sacred number) squared and multiplied by 1,000 to denote a multitude. It is the Church, the spiritual Israel, that is meant.

### Rev 7:14 {#nt.Rev.7.14}

*the great tribulation*: The Neronian persecution?

### Rev 8:5 {#nt.Rev.8.5}

Coals from the altar of burnt offering were brought to the altar of incense.

### Rev 9:1 {#nt.Rev.9.1}

*star*: A fallen angel.

### Rev 9:14 {#nt.Rev.9.14}

*Euphrates*: The region of the Parthians.

### Rev 9:21 {#nt.Rev.9.21}

*immorality*: See note on 2:20–21.

### Rev 10:7 {#nt.Rev.10.7}

*mystery of God*: i.e., the establishment of the kingdom of God following on the destruction of Israel’s enemies.

### Rev 10:9 {#nt.Rev.10.9}

*bitter . . . sweet*: The scroll related both the sufferings and the victories of Christ’s Church.

### Rev 11:1–19 {#nt.Rev.11.1}

The Jerusalem here described stands for the Church, which is to be persecuted by the Romans.

### Rev 11:2 {#nt.Rev.11.2}

The three and a half years’ persecution of the Jews by Antiochus Epiphanes, 168–165 b.c., had become the standard time of a persecution. Three and a half years equals 42 months equals 1,260 days (verse 3).

### Rev 11:3 {#nt.Rev.11.3}

*two witnesses*: As they have yet to die, possibly they are Elijah and Enoch.

### Rev 11:8 {#nt.Rev.11.8}

*the great city*: i.e., Rome.

### Rev 12:1–6 {#nt.Rev.12.1}

The *child* brought forth is the Messiah; the *dragon* is the devil; the *woman* who gave birth to the Messiah is Israel, and then becomes the Christian Church, which continually gives birth to the faithful.

### Rev 12:14 {#nt.Rev.12.14}

*a time, and times, and half a time*: This is the three and a half years of 11:2.

### Rev 12:17 {#nt.Rev.12.17}

Mary, the mother of the Messiah, must also be included in the meaning.

### Rev 13:1 {#nt.Rev.13.1}

*a beast*: This symbolizes the material forces of evil, arrayed against the Church.

### Rev 13:11 {#nt.Rev.13.11}

*another beast*: i.e., the false prophets.

### Rev 13:18 {#nt.Rev.13.18}

*six hundred and sixty-six*: The letters of Nero’s name plus the title of Caesar, given their numerical meaning in Hebrew and added together, make 666.

### Rev 14:4 {#nt.Rev.14.4}

Although tradition tends to take this literally, the context and Old Testament metaphor suggest that it means they have kept free from idolatry.

### Rev 14:8 {#nt.Rev.14.8}

*Babylon*: i.e., Rome.

### Rev 15:3–4 {#nt.Rev.15.3}

The song of Moses in Ex 15:1–18 celebrated victory over Pharaoh. This is seen as foreshadowing the triumph of the Lamb.

### Rev 16:14 {#nt.Rev.16.14}

*the great day*: On which all the Gentile armies shall be gathered to give battle.

### Rev 16:16 {#nt.Rev.16.16}

*Armageddon*: i.e., Megiddo, where Josiah was defeated by the king of Egypt, cf. 2 Kings 23:29.

### Rev 17:1 {#nt.Rev.17.1}

*great harlot*: i.e., Rome.

### Rev 17:2 {#nt.Rev.17.2}

*fornication*: i.e., idolatry.

### Rev 18:11–20 {#nt.Rev.18.11}

The description abruptly assumes the language of Ezekiel’s prophecy of the destruction of Tyre, another city notorious for its sins (Ezek 27:1—28:19).

### Rev 19:7 {#nt.Rev.19.7}

*marriage of the Lamb*: i.e., final establishment of the kingdom of God. The spouse is the Church.

### Rev 20:3 {#nt.Rev.20.3}

The destruction of the dragon must coincide in time with that of the beast (19:20), so that the first resurrection with the reign of the martyrs refers to the revival and expansion of the Church after the years of persecution.

### Rev 21:1 {#nt.Rev.21.1}

Creation will be renewed one day, freed from corruption and illumined by God’s glory.

### Rev 21:8 {#nt.Rev.21.8}

*second death*: i.e., eternal damnation.
