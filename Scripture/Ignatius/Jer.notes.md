# Jeremiah — Notes

## Doctrinal notes

### Jer 1:1 {#nt.Jer.1.1}

Jeremiah, sometimes called a prophet of doom, lived at the most tragic period of Israel’s history, during which Jerusalem was destroyed by Nebuchadrezzar and the people were carried off into captivity. He was of a priestly family and he makes it clear in his book that he obeyed God’s call to prophesy most reluctantly. It was his task, under the circumstances, to preach repentance and prophesy destruction if repentance was not forthcoming. But his words fell on deaf ears. In so far as the people heard at all, it was only to resent what he said and make him suffer for it. As the prophet was of a specially sensitive and affectionate nature, his sufferings were all the more acute.

### 31:31–34 {#nt.Jer.1.1b}

He did not always prophesy doom. Thus in 31:31–34, he foretells the new covenant in terms that remind us of passages in the prophet Isaiah. His words may not have had much effect during his lifetime but after his death his influence was considerable, as was the case with other prophets also; cf. Mt 23:29–30. Not all the prophecies in the book are from Jeremiah himself but some have been inserted later. An important feature of the book is the quantity of biographical material, which tells us a great deal about the prophet.

### Jer 1:6 {#nt.Jer.1.6}

Typically, Jeremiah is reluctant to accept an office for which he feels himself ill suited, and which, he foresees, can bring nothing but suffering and disappointment.

### Jer 3:16 {#nt.Jer.3.16}

The ark must have been destroyed at the same time as the temple in 586 B.C. In the Messianic times the presence of the Lord will not be restricted to the ark of the covenant; cf. Rev 21:22.

### Jer 7:4 {#nt.Jer.7.4}

Trust in the temple’s presence without true service of God and observance of his commands is vain, just as earlier a similar trust in the presence of the ark was fruitless without moral observance; 1 Sam 4:3.

### Jer 8:7 {#nt.Jer.8.7}

cf. Is 1:3.

### Jer 9:23–24 {#nt.Jer.9.24}

The basis of true religion.

### Jer 13:1–11 {#nt.Jer.13.1}

Note here and elsewhere the use of symbolic action throughout Jeremiah’s ministry.

### Jer 30:8 {#nt.Jer.30.8}

The new covenant and the restoration.

### Jer 31:15 {#nt.Jer.31.15}

Quoted by Matthew and applied to the Holy Innocents (Mt 2:18).

### Jer 31:31–34 {#nt.Jer.31.31}

The new covenant; cf. Mt 26:28.

### Jer 33:15 {#nt.Jer.33.15}

cf. Is 11:1: “a shoot from the stump of Jesse.”

### Jer 35:1 {#nt.Jer.35.1}

This chapter is our chief source of information about the little-known sect of Rechabites; cf.2 Kings 10:15–16, 23. They obeyed what they felt to be a call to serve God in the wilderness and desert places. The ancient nomad life during the Exodus was always looked back to as the time of the greatest fidelity to God.

### Jer 52:1 {#nt.Jer.52.1}

Historical supplement which recapitulates and enlarges on 2 Kings 24:18—25:30. Cf.Is 36–39, which was added in the same way.
