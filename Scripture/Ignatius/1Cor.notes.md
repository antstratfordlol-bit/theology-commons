# 1 Corinthians — Notes

## Doctrinal notes

### 1 Cor 1:2 {#nt.1Cor.1.2}

*saints*: A word commonly used for Christians in Paul’s letters and in Acts.

### 1 Cor 1:12 {#nt.1Cor.1.12}

*Cephas*: i.e., Peter. It does not follow from this that he had even been to Corinth, but it does indicate his authority there.

### 1 Cor 2:1–2 {#nt.1Cor.2.2}

Paul’s failure at Athens convinced him that lofty words and worldly wisdom were less effective than Jesus crucified.

### 1 Cor 3:13 {#nt.1Cor.3.13}

*the Day*: i.e., the day of the Lord, God’s searching judgment.

### 1 Cor 3:16 {#nt.1Cor.3.16}

*God’s temple*: The dignity of the Christians.

### 1 Cor 5:1 {#nt.1Cor.5.1}

*father’s wife*: Evidently his stepmother.

### 1 Cor 5:5 {#nt.1Cor.5.5}

*to Satan*: Not only excommunicated, but in some sense given over to suffering, for his own good.

### 1 Cor 5:9–10 {#nt.1Cor.5.9}

*immoral*: Literally, “fornicators.”

### 1 Cor 5:11 {#nt.1Cor.5.11}

*guilty of immorality*: Literally, “a fornicator.”

### 1 Cor 6:1 {#nt.1Cor.6.1}

*the unrighteous*: i.e., civil courts in which the judges were, of course, pagan.

### 1 Cor 6:9 {#nt.1Cor.6.9}

*the immoral*: literally, “fornicators.”

### 1 Cor 6:9 {#nt.1Cor.6.9b}

*homosexuals*: Greek has “effeminate nor sodomites.” The apostle condemns, not the inherent tendencies of such, but the indulgence of them.

### 1 Cor 6:12 {#nt.1Cor.6.12}

This saying is possibly an exaggeration of the freedom from the Mosaic law that Christians enjoyed. The saying has been applied to sinful practices, as is clear from the following verses.

### 1 Cor 6:13 {#nt.1Cor.6.13}

18, *immorality*: i.e., sexual immorality.

### 1 Cor 7:2 {#nt.1Cor.7.2}

Note Paul’s insistence on equality of man and woman in certain aspects of Christian marriage, and his recognition that the unmarried state is also a gift from God.

### 1 Cor 8:1–13 {#nt.1Cor.8.1}

Animals sacrificed to pagan gods were often sold as meat in the market. Could Christians buy such meat? Paul allows it so long as scandal is avoided.

### 1 Cor 9:3 {#nt.1Cor.9.3}

Paul set great store by the fact that he has earned his living and waived his right to support by the faithful. He used this as an authentication of his apostolate.

### 1 Cor 9:5 {#nt.1Cor.9.5}

*wife*: Greek, a “woman,” a “sister.” This could mean either a woman who is a Christian or a wife who is a Christian. There were pious women who ministered to the apostles (Lk 8:3). As many of the apostles must have been married, they may have been ministered to by their wives, though it is possible they had left their wives in answer to the Lord’s command to leave all (Lk 18:28–29).

### 1 Cor 9:5 {#nt.1Cor.9.5b}

*brethren*: See note on Mt 12:46.

### 1 Cor 10:20 {#nt.1Cor.10.20}

Paul appears to forbid partaking in sacrificial meals. In verse 27 he says they may eat meat offered to idols if it is at an ordinary meal, unless it would cause scandal to anyone present.

### 1 Cor 11:20 {#nt.1Cor.11.20}

There was apparently a common meal before the Eucharist at which food and drink were to be shared. Paul condemns the abuses that had crept in.

### 1 Cor 12:1 {#nt.1Cor.12.1}

The spiritual gifts here referred to were common in the first age of the Church and helped to establish it on a firm basis.

### 1 Cor 12:31 {#nt.1Cor.12.31}

Love, however, is far superior to these gifts.

### 1 Cor 15:13 {#nt.1Cor.15.13}

Again, the resurrection of the dead is linked with Christ’s resurrection; cf. Rom 8:11.

### 1 Cor 15:29 {#nt.1Cor.15.29}

Apparently a custom of vicarious baptism for those who had died without it. Paul mentions it without approving it.

### 1 Cor 16:1 {#nt.1Cor.16.1}

The collection to be made everywhere for the poor Christians in Jerusalem.
