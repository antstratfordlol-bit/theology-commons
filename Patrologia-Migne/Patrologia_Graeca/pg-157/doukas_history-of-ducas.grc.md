# History of Ducas

**Author**: Doukas  
**Series**: PG 157  
**Columns**: 739–740  

[739] {#pg.157.739}
Constantinus filius Irenes, ab ea excaecatus, et Maria ejus prima uxor, abneptis S. Philareti Eleamonis, 208 duaeque ipsorum filiae, Irene et Euphrosyne, quae fuit uxor Michaelis Balbi, et Anna filia Theophili imperatoris.
Sciendum est, in monasterio gynaecei Gastriorum, ad dextrum latus ecclesiae versus orientem, sepultum esse corpus S. Theodorae, uxoris Theophili imperatoris, tresque filias ipsius, Theclam, Anastasiam et Pulcheriam.


[740] {#pg.157.740}
ἡ ἀπόκειται Κωνσταντῖνος ὁ υἱὸς Εἰρήνης (50) ὁ τυφλωθεὶς παρ᾿ αὐτῆς, καὶ Μαρία ἡ πρώτη αὐτοῦ γυνή, ἡ γεγονυῖα ἐγγόνη τοῦ ἁγίου Φιλαρέτου (51) τοῦ Ἐλεήμονος, καὶ αἱ δύο αὐτῶν θυγατέρες, Εἰρήνη καὶ Εὐφροσύνη (52), ἡ γυνὴ Μιχαὴλ βασιλέως τοῦ Τρανλοῦ, καὶ Ἄννα ἡ θυγάτηρ Θεοφίλου (53) τοῦ βασιλέως.
Ἰστέον ὅτι ἐν τῇ γυναικείᾳ μονῇ τῶν Γαστρίων (54), εἰς τὸ πρὸς ἀνατολὰς δεξιὸν μέρος τῆς ἐκκλησίας, ἐτάφη τὸ σῶμα τῆς ἁγίας Θεοδώρας τῆς γυναικὸς Θεοφίλου τοῦ βασιλέως, καὶ αἱ τρεῖς αὐτῆς θυγατέρες, Θέκλα, Ἀναστασία καὶ Πουλχερία.
Anselmi Bandurii notae.
Constantine, son of Irene, blinded by her, and Maria his first wife, great-granddaughter of Saint Philaret the Merciful, and their two daughters, Irene and Euphrosyne, who was the wife of Emperor Michael the Stammerer, and Anna, daughter of Emperor Theophilus. It should be noted that in the women's monastery of the Gastria, on the right side of the church towards the east, the body of Saint Theodora, empress of Theophilus, and her three daughters, Thecla, Anastasia, and Pulcheria, are buried.
Anselmi Bandurii notes.
CENTURY XV.
DOUKAS. NOTICE
(Hankius De rer. Byzant. Script. Græc., p. 630.)
Our Ducas, whose first name is unknown to us, had as his paternal grandfather Michael Ducas, a learned man from the imperial lineage of the Ducae, who, when the adversaries were pursuing in war John Cantacuzenus (after Emperor Andronicus Palaeologus the younger had been taken from the living in the year of Christ 1341) the guardian of Emperor John Palaeologus, and most of his other friends were slaughtered in Constantinople, fled to Asia, and finally found a safe seat in Europe among the Ephesians. He indicates this in the *Byzantine History*, chapter 5, in the translation of Ismael Bullialdus: "Among those indeed (over two hundred friends of John Cantacuzenus were cast into prison and, except for six, given to death) who were snatched from such great danger, was my paternal grandfather, Michael Ducas. They furthermore, having put on the mantles or cloaks of the monks of that monastery (in the subterranean cave of which they had hidden), fled from Constantinople, and having been ferried across to the opposite continent, Asia, they scattered, some to the eastern regions, others to Bithynia, and others elsewhere; and my grandfather sought Asia, and visited Jesæum (whom Bajazet, the sultan of the Turks, had appointed prince of Ionia)
