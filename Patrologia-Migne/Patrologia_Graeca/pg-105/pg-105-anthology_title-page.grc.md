365 ORATIO XVII. - IN LAUDEM S. ANASTASIÆ. 366
As for those things which were above nature, they had remained in her because of Him who is above nature; so too did the things according to nature remain visible through Him. Being then bloodless from the flow of blood, she asked for a little water to rinse her mouth. A certain man offered it, one who was a Christian in religion, but who until that time was thought to be of the sect of the heathens: the athlete’s name was Cyrillus. When Probus saw him, and recognized that he was disposed toward the champion not only by the compassion of nature but also by the identity of faith, he ordered him to be led away to death. And since the shame was poured out brilliantly upon the tempter, but the victory of the athlete—who was finishing the course of her martyrdom and nearing the crowns—appeared more brilliant, she was condemned to be taken out of the city and beheaded.   For the adversary could no longer bear to be trampled underfoot and mocked by a delicate girl. It was fitting that, like that Egyptian, this one too, being hardened, and having campaigned in this last effort against this daughter of Israel, should be completely frustrated in his attempt, just as the undefiled dove, with the severing of her head from her body, had flown away and been directed toward the dwellings above; while he himself was suffocated in those red floods of blood of hers, and paid this just penalty for his most unjust aggression.
In this manner did Anastasia cast the heavy adversary, the soul-destroying serpent, who had indeed attacked her fiercely, but had been repelled by her more fiercely, into an impassable chaos and Tartarean crevasses, having ground him down with her beautiful feet like the mud of the streets.   And she, committing herself to the divine angels, with archangels making the way, and all the supercelestial powers going before and welcoming her, being magnified and applauded, is offered with glory to the King and Bridegroom, Christ. She is crowned upon her head with an amaranthine wreath; she receives from His right hand rewards corresponding to her toils, or rather, far better and higher ones.
The all-beautiful limbs of the undefiled one, more precious than any gold, more valuable than any precious stone—the one most cruel of all beasts had given them as food to vultures and beasts; but due to the providence of Him who keeps all the bones of the righteous  , they remained untouched and unviolated by these, and were delivered to holy Sophia for burial. And in what manner? For it is not just to keep silent about this either.   For the good mother was anxious, as was natural, for her most excellent daughter according to the Spirit. She was anxious, lest by suffering something ignoble during the tortures, she might belie her promises. She was prostrate, then, on the floor at home, lying there with loud lamentations, with one final blow remaining to complete the ten, namely, the severing of her neck.
