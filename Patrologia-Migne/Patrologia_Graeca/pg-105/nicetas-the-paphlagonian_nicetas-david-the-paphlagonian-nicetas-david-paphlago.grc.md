ANNO DOMINI DCCCXC. # NICETAS DAVID PAPHLAGO ## NOTITIA HISTORICA ET LITTERARIA.
(Leo ALLATIUS, Diatriba de Nicetis, ap. Mai. Biblioth. nov., VI, part. II, p. 5.) Inter varia cognomina et dignitatum titulos unum Nicetam latitantem, sed propter eadem incommode in plures variosque atque immaniter divisum, si fieri potest, suis partibus integrum in medium adduca- mus. Fuit is Byzantinus et Paphlago dictus. Joannes Scylitza in proœmio suæ historiæ: Ὁ γὰρ Δαφνοπάτης θεόδωρος, Νικήτας ὁ Παραλαγῶν, Ἰωσὴφ Γενέσιος, καὶ Μανουήλ οἱ Βυζάντεοι, Νικηφόρος διά- κονος Φρῦξ, καὶ τὰ ἐξῆς. Namque Theodorus Daphnopates, Nicetas Paphlago, Josephus Genesius, et Manuel Byzantini, Nicephorus diaconus Phryx, etc. Ex Scylitza sua hausit Cedrenus. Consentit hisce Nice- phorus Callistus Hist. eccl. lib. XIV, 28. Sed Paphlago, cognomenne fuerit, an episcopatus titulus, quis extricit? Plane in multis manuscriptis legitur Νικήτας Παραλαγούς, Nicetas Paphlagoniæ, ut in codice Vaticano 1246, et ante Vitam Ignatii patriarchæ. Et potuit quoque esse ex Paphlagonia, sed By- zantina civitate donatus: neque id probatione egei: sunt enim omnes omnium scriptorum chartæ de alienis in cives proprios ascitis a civitatibus plena. Et episcopatus Paphlagoniæ nullus apparet. Fuit is quoque binominis: namque et Davidis appellatione insignitur. Hinc sæpius Νικήτα τοῦ καὶ Δαυτῶ, Nicetæ qui et David; et absolute a nonnullis Nicetas David appellatur. Et videant qui David hoc cogno- mentum fuisse tradunt, quo se fulciant fundamento. In aliquibus codicibus adnectitur loco Davidis et Paphlagonis, Δαδόβρων, Dadybrorum, qui erat episcopatus sub Gangrensi Paphlagoniæ. Hinc arguo non male Dadybrensem episcopum, dictum etiam Paphlonem, qui nempe possideret in Paphlagonia epi- scopatum. Huic additur quoque nomen philosophi, Νικήτα τοῦ καὶ Δαυτῶ τοῦ φιλοσόφου. Nicetæ, qui et David, philosophi, ut clare videtur in codicibus manuscriptis Vaticanis 344, 365 et 679. Et in aliis, nullo alio addito, Νικήτα τοῦ φιλοσόφου, Nicetæ philosophi, non quod tantum institutum monachorum professus, sed quod inter alia potissimum sibi philosophiæ munus ascriberet, quales plerique eo ævo fuere, et primus inter eos ὑπάτου τῶν φιλοσόφων titulo cohonestabatur. Quare hic fuerit Nicetas, qui et alio nomine David vocabatur, Byzantinus', dignitate philosophus, Dadybrorum in Paphlagonia sub Gangrensi episcopus: et omnia, quæ sub Nicetæ nomine diversis hisce titulis ad varios referuntur, huic soli vindicantur (a). Floruit circa Leonis et Constantini tempora, ut colligitur ex proœmio Vitæ sancti Ignatii patriarchæ, quam ille neque indocte neque ineleganter concinnavit: Εἰ δὲ καὶ πρὸς τοῖς ἐσχάτοις, καὶ τοῖς καθ' ἡμᾶς τούτοις ἐγγίζοντες τύχοιεν καὶροῖς, πολλῷ μᾶλλον ἀξιομνημονεύτοτατοι ἀν εἶναι ἀπάντων, καὶ ἀξι- εταίνετοι, ὡς πολὺ πλέον οἶκοδομεῖν ἦ ἐγγύτητι δυνάμεοι τὴν Ἐκκλησίαν· τὸ γὰρ ἀρχαῖον καὶ πάμπολον κατὰ χρόνον διετῶς, εἰ καὶ πιστεύεται τῇ ἀξιοπιστίᾳ τῆς Γραφῆς, ἀλλὰ καὶ θάπτον ἀν τοῖς πολλοῖς πρὸς τὴν μίμησιν ἀπογινώσκοιτο, λογιζομένοις ὡς κρεῖττον, ἦ κατὰ τὴν παροῦσαν, τῶν πρεσβυτέρων ἐκείνων δυναμένων κανατοθοῦν γενεάν· ἦσα δ' ἀν τοῖς καθ' ἡμᾶς καταρθοῦσθαι πιστεύοιτο καὶροῖς, μᾶλλον ἀν οἶμαι ταῦτα παρακνήσειν τοὺς εὐσεβεστέρους, καὶ πρὸς τὸν ἰσον τῆς ἀρετῆς διερεθίζειν ζῆλον· διὰ ταῦτά μοι τοῦτον ἐνστήσασθαι νῦν ἐσφ λόγῳ τὸν ἀγῶνα· καί μοι σφαδρὸς πόθος κατακάρδιος ἀληθινοῖς διηγήμασι καὶ πάσης προλήψεως ἀπηλλαγμένοις ψεύδους, τὸν μέγαν ἱεράρχην, καὶ ποιμένα τῆς βασιλίδος Ἰγνάτιον ὑπάρχει θεῷ θεῷ πνεύματι τῆς ἀληθείας ὑφηγητὴν, καὶ συλλήπτορι τοῖς ἀγνοοῦσι, ὄποῖς τις ἦν, ἐκ τῆς κατ' αὐτὸν πολιτείας (a) Hactenus notitiam de hoc Niceta nos ex Allatio edideramus in Script. vet. t. IN THE YEAR OF OUR LORD 890. NICETAS DAVID PAPHLAGO. HISTORICAL AND LITERARY NOTICE. (Leo Allatius, *Diatriba de Nicetis*, ap. Mai. *Biblioth. nov.*, VI, part. II, p. 3.) Among the various surnames and titles of dignity, one Nicetas lies hidden, but because of the same name he is inconveniently divided into many varied and monstrously separated persons; if possible, I shall bring him forth, integrated in his own parts, before the public. He was a Byzantine, and called a Paphlagonian. Joannes Scylitza in the proemium of his history: "For the Daphnopates Theodore, Nicetas the Paphlagonian, Joseph Genesios, and Manuel the Byzantines, Nicephorus the deacon the Phrygian, and so forth." For Theodore Daphnopates, Nicetas Paphlago, Joseph Genesios, and Manuel are Byzantines; Nicephorus the deacon is a Phrygian, etc. Cedrenus drew his material from Scylitza. Nicephorus Callistus (Hist. eccl. lib. XIV, 28) agrees with these. But whether "Paphlago" was a surname or a title of a bishopric, who can disentangle? Clearly in many manuscripts one reads "Nicetas of Paphlagonia," as in Vatican codex 1246, and before the Life of Ignatios the patriarch. And he could also have been from Paphlagonia, but endowed with status in the city of Byzantium: nor does this require proof, for the papers of all writers are full of those of other lands who were adopted as true citizens by cities. And no bishopric of Paphlagonia appears. He was also a man of two names: for he is distinguished by the appellation of David. Hence, quite often, "Nicetas who is also David"; and by some he is called absolutely Nicetas David. And let those who hand down that David was his surname look to the foundation upon which they rest. In some codices, in place of David and Paphlago, "Dadybron" is attached, which was a bishopric in the Paphlagonia of the Gangra region. Hence I argue that he was not unsuitably [called] the Dadybrenian bishop, also called the Paphlagonian, namely because he possessed a bishopric in Paphlagonia. To this is also added the name of Philosopher: "Nicetas who is also David the Philosopher," as is clearly seen in the Vatican manuscript codices 344, 365, and 679. And in others, with nothing else added, "Nicetas the Philosopher," not because he professed only the institution of the monks, but because among other things he especially attributed to himself the task of philosophy—as were most men of that age, and the foremost among them was honored with the title *hypatos ton philosophon* (Chief of the Philosophers). Wherefore this was the Nicetas who was called by the other name David; a Byzantine, a philosopher by rank, bishop of Dadybra in Paphlagonia under [the metropolis of] Gangra; and all the things which, under the name of Nicetas, are referred to various people by these diverse titles, are to be vindicated for this one man. He flourished around the times of Leo and Constantine, as is gathered from the proemium of the Life of Saint Ignatios the patriarch, which he composed neither unlearnedly nor inelegantly: “And if they should happen to be among the most recent and those times adjacent to our own, they would be all the more worthy of record and praise, as they are capable of building up the Church the more by their proximity. For the ancient and very distant in time, even if it is believed on the trustworthiness of Scripture, yet it would be sooner dismissed by the many for imitation, considering that it is better than the present generation, as those elders were able to succeed. But whatever is believed to have been achieved in our own times, I think would much more goad those who are more devout, and incite them to an equal zeal for virtue. For these reasons I undertake now this contest in my discourse; and a fierce longing is in my heart to describe, in true accounts and ones devoid of all pretense of falsehood, what manner of man the great hierarch and shepherd of the Queen of Cities, Ignatios, was—under God the Spirit of truth as guide and helper for those who do not know—from the manner of life which he lived.”
If, however, it happens that they approach nearer to our own recent age, then they are indeed much more to be commemorated and lauded above all others, as those who, by virtue of the proximity of their times, can be of much greater benefit to the Church. For that which is very ancient and removed by long intervals of time, even if it deserves faith on account of the authority of the writer, nevertheless causes most people to despair of the effort of imitation, as those who think that it was much easier to live virtuously in those centuries of our ancestors than in our own. But those things which are believed to have been done rightly within our own memory, I think, fire the minds of the pious much more sharply to emulate the honor of virtue. Therefore, a huge desire of mind impelled me to pursue this cause, or struggle, with my speech, and with God the Spirit of truth as guide and helper, to show to those who do not know what kind of man Ignatios was, what his quality, and how great this prelate and pastor of the royal city was in the manner of his life. I speak of that Ignatios who, while posterior in age and flourishing before this generation of ours, none the less conceded nothing to those previous venerable pontiffs either in faith or in confession. What *genea*, or age, is, before which Ignatios is said to have lived, is taught by all the most learned grammarians. Wherefore it will be a matter of doubt to no one that this Nicetas flourished under the aforementioned emperors. Verum alterius consilii memoriam recolens, et regii ac paterni præcepti, « Fili mi, cave ne multos libros conscriberis, » sedulo admonentis, et causam proferentis, quod nullus sit finis aut terminus compositionem infinitæ numinis sapientiæ, et laborem asserentis animæ et corporis immoderatam rei meditationem esse; utilitatem vero, quæ inde consequitur, et totius divinitus inspiratæ Scripturæ facultatem in Dei timorem, et præcepti observantiam, veluti in unum caput referentes, usurpare licere. Quapropter monitum hoc dicendi finem faciam. Has interpretationes maxima ex parte Billius in suam Nazianzeni editionem Latine parum cautus sub Cyri nomine transfudit. Et in epistola ad cardinalem Sirletum: Reliqua etiam opuscula, quæ Cyrus explanavit, versibus item reddidi. Sed cyrus (id est dominus) ante nomina positum quid sit, fusius in aliis diximus.
