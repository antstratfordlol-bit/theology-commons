# Callistus Kataphygiotes (Callistus Cataphugiota)

**Author**: Callistus Cataphygiota  
**Series**: PG 147  
**Columns**: 833–834  

### Notitia ex Philocalia


[833] {#pg.147.833}
ΚΑΛΛΙΣΤΟΣ ΚΑΤΑΦΥΓΙΩΤΑ
ΝΟΤΙΤΙΑ
(Ἐν Φιλοκαλίᾳ τῶν ἱερῶν Νηπτικῶν, p. 1115)
Κάλλιστος ὁ ὁσιώτατος Πατὴρ ἡμῶν ὁ καὶ Καταφυγιώτης ἐπικαλούμενος (ἴσως τῆς ἐπικλήσεως ταύτης ἔτυχεν ἀπὸ Ἐκκλησίας τινός, τῆς Θεοτόκου, Καταφυγῆς οὕτωσι ἐπικεκλημένης) τίς τε ἦν, καὶ τίνα ἔσχε πατρίδα, καὶ ποῦ τὸν ἀναχωρητικὸν διήνυσε βίον, οὐδὲν τούτων παρὰ τῶν ἱστοριῶν μεμαθήκαμεν. Ὅσον δὲ ἀπὸ τῶν παρόντων αὐτοῦ κεφαλαίων τεκμηριώσασθαι, ἀνὴρ ἦν τῷ ὄντι περὶ τὴν ἔξω καὶ τὴν ἔσω παιδείαν φιλοσοφώτατος, καὶ μάλιστα πρὸς θεωριῶν ὕψος, καὶ βάθος, μῆκός τε καὶ πλάτος νοητόν, εἴπερ τις ἄλλος, διαβατικώτατος. Ἐπὶ τοσοῦτον γὰρ ὁ μακάριος πρὸς τὸ ὑπερκόσμιον τῆς Τριαδικῆς ὑπερουσιότητος ἀπόκρυφον Ἐν ἀνετείνετο συνεργείᾳ τῆς χάριτος, καὶ οὕτως ἐπὶ τὴν τοῦ Θεοῦ ἐποψίαν, καὶ ἄμεσον ἕνωσιν, καὶ νοερὰν σιγὴν, καὶ ἀπεράγνωστον ἀγνωσίαν πάντων ἀπερἁπλῶς ἀπολελυμένος ἀνηνέχθη δι᾿ ὑπερβολὴν καθαρότητος, ὡς ἄγγελον ἄλλον ἀληθῶς, καὶ Θεὸν κατὰ χάριν ἐπὶ γῆς φαίνεσθαι. Τινὲς μὲν οὖν αὐτὸν ἔφησαν Κάλλιστον εἶναι τὸν Ξανθόπουλον τὸν τῆς Κωνσταντίνου πατριαρχεύσαντα, τὸν τὰ ἑκατόν, φημί, ἕτερα κεφάλαια συγγράψαμενον, δυσὶ τεκμηρίοις χρώμενοι. Ἐκεῖνα μὲν γὰρ, φασὶ, ταπολλὰ περὶ πρακτικῆς διειλήφασι, ταῦτα δὲ περὶ θεωρίας μόνης καὶ βίου θεωρητικοῦ. Πράξεως δὲ καὶ θεωρίας συνημμένων ἀλλήλαις οὐσῶν, ἔδει κατὰ τὸ εἰκὸς ἵνα γε εἶναι καὶ τὸν περὶ τούτων διερμηνεύοντα. Πρὸς δὲ, ὅτι καὶ πολλὰ τῶν ἐκείνοις, ταῦτα καὶ τοῖς παροῦσιν ἐμφέρεται, ὅσα γε εἰς ἐπιβολὴν νοὸς, καὶ παραδοχὴν ἥκει, καὶ θείαν ἕνωσιν, καὶ καρδιακὴν ἐνέργειαν καὶ φωτισμόν. Οἱ δὲ, ἕτερον ἐκεῖνον τοῦτον φασὶ διὰ τὸ παρηλλαγμένον ὕφος τῆς ἑκατέρων φράσεως. Ἡμεῖς δὲ μᾶλλον τοῖς προτέροις συναινέσαι δεῖν ἔγνωμεν, μηδὲν τὴν τοῦ ὕφους δεδωκότες ἐναλλαγήν. Δυνατὸν γάρ ἐστι καὶ μάλ᾿ εὔπορον τοῖς σοφοῖς καταλλήλως ταῖς ὑποθέσεσιν ἁρμόζειν καὶ τὰ τῆς φράσεως, καὶ ταπεινῶς μὲν τὰ ταπεινὰ, ὑψηλῶς δὲ καὶ μεγαληγόρως ἐκφράζειν τὰ ὑψηλά. Τοῦτο δὲ ἀληθῶς ἀνιαρὸν, ὅτι εἰς ἑκατοντάδα που, ὡς ἐξ εἰκασμοῦ, τῶν κεφαλαίων πληθουνομένων, καὶ τῶν ὅσα δὴ πώποτε περὶ θεωρητικοῦ βίου, διειλήφασι ὑψηλοτάτων δὲ ὄντων, ὡς ἐμὲ εἰδέναι, καὶ τελευτάτων τοῖς τε νοήμασι, καὶ τῷ μεγαληγόρῳ τῆς φράσεως, καὶ τῇ ἄλλῃ καλλιπείᾳ, ναὶ μὴν καὶ ταῖς τῶν συλλογισμῶν ἀνάγκαις, μόνα τὰ παρόντα κατὰ τὸ εἰς ἡμετέρας χεῖρας ἐλθὸν πρωτότυπον διεσώζοντο.


[834] {#pg.147.834}
Callistus, sanctissimus Pater noster, Cataphugiotes etiam cognominatus (hoc forte cognomen adeptus est ex quadam ecclesia Deiparae, quae ex ipsa Cataphyga cognominata erat). Ubi anchoreticam vitam egerit, ex historicis non didicimus. Quantum autem ex ipsis ejus capitibus licet conjicere, vir erat reipais in profanis sacrisque litteris exercitatissimus, ac praesertim contemplationis altitudine, profunditudine, longitudine ac latitudine intellectus, si quis alius, eximius. Adeo autem beatus ille vir ad supernaturale Trinitatis supersubstantialis absconditum unum, gratiae efficacia, tendebat, et adeo ad Dei contemplationem et immediatam unionem et spirituale silentium et superignorantem ignorantiam, omnibus omnino solutus puritatis excellentia ferebatur, ut angelus vere et deus secundum gratiam in terra visus sit. Nonnulli quidem eum dixere esse Callistum Xanthopulum, Constantinopolitanum patriarcham, eum dico qui centum alia capita conscripsit, duobus testimoniis fulti. Illa enim, ut aiunt, ad activam vitam pertinent, haec vero ad solam contemplationem et vitam contemplativam. Cum autem actio et contemplatio inter se uniantur, oportebat, ut congruum sit, eumdem de utraque disseruisse. Adde etiam quod multa ex illis, eadem et in prasentia capita translata sunt, quae ad mentis impetum et receptionem spectant, et ad divinam unionem et cordis operationem ac illuminationem. At vero non eumdem esse dicunt ob utriusque styli textus comparationem. Nos vero prioribus assentiendum esse censemus, textus mutationis nulla rationem habentes. Fieri enim potest facillime ut sapientes materie ipsi adaptent, et humilia humiliter, excelsa excelso et sublimiter eloquantur. Dolendum vero est quod ex centum, ut conjicere licet, capitibus, quae quaelibet de vita contemplativa tractantia superant, quantum scire possint, quaeque tum cogitationibus, tum styli sublimitate et aliis dotibus perfecta sunt, vel etiam syllogismorum ratione, sola praesentia in hoc originali in nostras manus transmisso supersunt.
CALLISTUS CATAPHUGIOTA · NOTICE
Callistus, our most holy Father, also surnamed Cataphugiota (he perhaps obtained this surname from a certain church of the Theotokos, which was called Cataphyge), is a man whose origin, birthplace, and where he lived his anchoritic life, we have not learned from histories. However, insofar as one can conjecture from his present chapters, he was a man truly most philosophical in both secular and sacred letters, and especially, if anyone ever was, most capable of traversing the height and depth, length and breadth of intelligible contemplations. For the blessed man was so drawn by the efficacy of grace toward the hidden unity of the supernatural, superessential Trinity, and was so led to the contemplation of God, to immediate union, to spiritual silence, and to the super-ignorant ignorance—being entirely released from all things by the excellence of his purity—that he truly appeared on earth as another angel and, by grace, a god. A Some have indeed said that this Callistus is the Xanthopulus who was patriarch of Constantinople, I mean the one who wrote the other hundred chapters, supporting this with two proofs. For they say that those chapters pertain to the active life, while these pertain only to contemplation and the contemplative life. Since action and contemplation are united with one another, it was fitting, as is reasonable, that the same person should have discoursed on both. Add also that many things from those are translated and contained in these present chapters, things which pertain to the movement and reception of the mind, and to divine union, and to the activity and illumination of the heart. Others, however, say that he is not the same person, because of the different style of the text in each. We, for our part, have judged it best to agree with the former, having no regard for the change in style. For it is very possible and quite easy for wise men to adapt their expression to the subject matter itself, and to speak of humble things humbly, and of high things in a high and lofty manner. But it is truly a matter for lamentation that, of the hundred chapters—as one might guess—which (as far as I can know) surpass all others treating of the contemplative life, and which are perfect both in their thoughts, in the sublimity of their style, and in other ornaments, and indeed in the necessity of their syllogisms, only the present ones have been preserved and transmitted into our hands in this original manuscript.
