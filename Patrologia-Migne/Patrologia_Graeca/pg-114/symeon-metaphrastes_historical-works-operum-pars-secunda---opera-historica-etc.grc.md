Another Alphabet
Consider, O my miserable and most wretched soul, the most dreadful judgment seat of the great trial. For you are about to stand naked and exposed, giving an account for what we have done in life, whether good things or bad, unjust or just. If you have been merciful while living, you shall receive mercy; or if you have given out of necessity, you shall likewise be saved. God is no respecter of persons; for he assigns equal rewards. If you have covered someone naked in this life, and have pitied the needy, and visited the sick, and filled the hungry, and satisfied the thirsty, and prepared a dwelling for the homeless and wanderers, the Judge will repay you with interest and grace. Likewise, he will make you a stranger to all kinds of goodness if you have not at all pitied any of the needy. If you have not filled the poor man, the hungry and the thirsty. If you have not clothed those who are destitute out of your wealth. If, having the power to save someone, you have not saved him from necessity. But you, O soul, being attentive and reflecting on these things, open your bowels of compassion to those in need. Distribute things that perish, so that you may gain things that remain. Lend pure love and faith to God, O soul, and love your neighbor as yourself, so that we may be deemed worthy of the kingdom above.
III. To my own soul
Soul, groan and with shedding of tears, hasten to erase the scroll of your sins; but do not wish to write new debts again, and provoke him. Christ, I know, is merciful; but if by chance the common end of life should seize you while so polluted and mired in filth, woe is me! What will you do? How will you drink the bitter cup which the wrath of the Lord mixes?
