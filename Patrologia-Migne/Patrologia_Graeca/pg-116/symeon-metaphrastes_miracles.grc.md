The blessed Marianus, having awakened from sleep and narrated to those nearby what he had seen in his dream, said, lamenting: "And where shall I find you now, or your house, O my lord and most genuine Demetrius, when you are dwelling in the imperial city, and I am laid low in Thessalonica? Woe to me, wretched man! How great was the joy with which the dream gladdened me, and with how great a grief has the reality overwhelmed me!" Then one of those standing by, moved by God, answered: "I, sir, will point out to you your patron and genuine friend, and after God, your lord Demetrius, who is both now in the imperial city and dwelling here, and stands there before the King of all, and yet does not abandon this place."
15. This is impossible for those who bear flesh and blood, but for the all-holy and preeminent martyr of Christ, Demetrius, it is very easy. For by his immaterial soul and unclouded faith, he inhabits the royal city above, Jerusalem, and does not abandon Thessalonica, which nurtured him. He has, therefore, houses in both cities; and he urged you, my lord, to come to his house to obtain health. Let us consider, therefore, if it is possible for you, bearing a body, to arrive at his house which is in the Jerusalem above; for he promised to deliver you from your disease [in this way]. But indeed, it is not natural for us now, with an earthly tent, to reach those things. It remains, therefore, that the one who spoke to you in your sleep referred to his venerable house which is in this God-protected metropolis of the Thessalonians.
16. "Why then do we delay, sir, and not go, and harvest the visitation from him?" And having said this, the servant raised his lord from the bed, and having carried him with the hands of other fellow servants, having improvised a seat for him with sides joined together on each side (for he could not easily be carried on a horse), they brought him into the all-venerable temple of the all-glorious martyr Demetrius. And having entered, and seen the decorum of the house, and the spiritual fragrance therein, nourishing the souls of the faithful like the paradise of God, he said to the servants carrying him: "Cast me down upon the floor." They were unwilling, because he was motionless in every part of his body; nevertheless, they obeyed him against their will as he commanded more strictly. And having been cast onto the marble, and having raised only his eyes—for he could not move his head, as his tendons were completely dissolved—he prayed thus:
17. "Holy and all-holy victor in the games, and genuine servant of Christ, you who gave your soul for Him, and gained the souls of many, and offered them to Him; you who saw your body wounded by spears for His sake, and being glorified, and snatching the bodies of many from the teeth of the lion even until now,
and returning them full of faith, and bringing those who are venerated to God; but also for me, and for the grace given by Him, pour forth your healings upon me, the new paralytic, who have taken refuge in the pool of your compassion.
18. For even if I have been in infirmity for thirty-eight years, yet I have exceeded even those thirty and eight years in pains. But why? Assuredly because I have even surpassed him in sins. As, therefore, you have imitated Christ, your God, in gentleness and goodness and the exercise of doctrine, emulate Him also in mercy and humanity; and, emulating Him, pour forth upon me your mercy and your compassionate healings. For if my sins do not stand in the way of your will, He who crowned you has granted you the power and the capability to do so. Therefore, just as he, as soon as God said: «Arise and take up your bed and walk,» found the power to respond to the word, so also you, like one of God’s household and clothed with His grace from the time you entered the struggle for Him and were crowned with victory, may you desire that this also come to pass in me. And I shall proclaim your power, proven by its effect. I shall arise, and like the lame deer, I shall leap; and I shall take up not only my bed, as did that ancient paralytic, but also, as gifts, I shall offer to your house the actions of gratitude that come from grace.
19. For thus, through you, O most philanthropic martyr of the Lord, glory and honor and adoration will be given to Him, and to the Father, and to the Holy Spirit, by those who see me now and by those who shall remember me hereafter, by men and by the supernal powers, unto the ages of ages. Amen. And when he had said these things with tears, while all were listening, he instructed his servants to lift him up and place him immediately upon the pallet prepared there for that purpose. And having been placed there, immediately, from the exhaustion of his body and the sorrow of his soul, he was seized by sleep; and behold, he saw again that same most glorious man, exceeding in every virtue, Demetrios, appearing to him in his sleep, saying: "Because you have obeyed correctly, and because you have correctly pondered the things spoken to you by me, you have come correctly; and therefore, what has been promised to you by God, I shall fulfill. But to restore your body to health is not, indeed, a great thing for me, but that your soul, after the healing of the body, should remain in the good-inclined state in which it is now—this is difficult."
20. For souls, when they are in some way pressed by adversities and by the sequence of evils and pains, according to what is written, {are ruled by tribulation}
to confess to Him, and to submit themselves to prayers and supplications, according to what is sung in the Psalms: "When He slew them, then they sought Him, and turned and sought God early." But when they have attained relief and prosperity, they become puffed up and arrogant, and speak injustice not only against men, but even against God. Again, it is written: "And Jacob ate and was filled, and the beloved kicked; he was fattened, and he abandoned God who made him, and departed from God his Savior." If, therefore, you render me secure by a certain and sincere promise, that, being healed in body, you will preserve intact the health of your soul which you now have (I mean humility and love toward God and your neighbor), the help of Christ through me is prepared for you. When he had said that "You know your genuine Marianus, never rejoicing in pride," that one adorned with the cultivation of all virtues, namely Demetrius, replied:
21. "You have no evil, most beloved, do not lose heart," and stroking his head with his right hand and raising his neck, and embracing his shoulders and chest, and touching his whole body with his hands, HE spoke in this manner: "Christ our God heals you, who raises up those who are bowed down." And immediately the prefect, with the voice as if still sounding to him, was awakened, and weeping sweetly with joy, wished to relate to his servants the things that had been seen by him. And indeed, what had happened up to the admonition and exhortation, and the promise made by him with tears, he narrated, with only his tongue moving while the rest of his body remained immobile; but when he began to say that the man had said to him, "Christ our God heals you, who raises up those who are bowed down," before the word was even completed, he rose up of his own accord and sat upon the low bed on which he was lying. Not understanding what had happened—for he was as if beside himself with joy—he asked to dress and go out. And they, being more astonished at the miracle than he himself, and unable to answer anything, having with difficulty come to themselves, and having run to those dwelling nearby, because the praetorium of the prefects was far off, they borrowed a tunic, a belt, a cloak, and footwear. For they happened to have none of these with them, in that they did not even hope that their master, in such a state, would ever be in need of such things.
