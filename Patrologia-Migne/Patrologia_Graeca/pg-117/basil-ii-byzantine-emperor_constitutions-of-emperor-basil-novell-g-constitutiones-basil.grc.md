« Capita Constitutionis hujus ex Harmenopulo ». — Præscriptionem annorum XI. tollit, statuens, ne annorum XL præscriptio potentioribus inopes injuste atque avare opprimentibus quidquam prosit: neve pauperes ab iisdem potentioribus læsi per annorum XL præscriptionem submoveantur. Ne terminorum quoque descriptiones robur habeant, licet aureis bullis contineantur, siquidem injuste ac fraudulenter factæ sint. 1. « De annorum XL præscriptione ». — Quoniam majestas mea comperit, ex quo cœpit imperare, motasque tam ab opulentis, quam pauperibus causas instituit cognoscere, locupletiores fraudandorum aliorum cupidos prætextum habere probabilem, nimirum annorum quadraginta præscriptionem; et in hoc incumbere, sive muneribus, sive viribus suis intervenientibus, ut hoc annorum quadraginta spatium transeant, et ab eo tempore pleno dominio possideant illa quibus improbe pauperes fraudarunt: hanc ipsam legem tulit, partim ut ea, quæ hactenus accidere, corrigat; partim ut potentes, qui jam vivunt, reprimat; et victuros deinceps, coerceat: scituros, se nullum hinc commodum percepturos, sed futurum ut res alienæ non tantum ipsis, sed et ipsorum hæredibus adimantur. II. The Golden Bull of Emperors Basil and Constantine granting liberties to the Venetian merchants. (A. 992.)
In the name of the Father, etc. Basil and Constantine, Emperors of the Romans, to all to whom this our pious signet shall be shown, the faithful in God.
Not only because of the petitions which are under our hand, providing for the most merciful and praiseworthy to be obeyed, but also because strangers and those pertaining to the public are to be heard with providence and piety; for indeed, since piety is sufficient to show mercy, and we signify that long-standing goodness is granted by imperial piety.
For although the Doge of the Venetians, and those who are under him, and with the intercession of all the people, made a petition to our empire, that each one of their ships, which comes from their province, or rather from another province and city, coming with business, should donate nothing else but two solidi; for they said that they were formerly donating as much as 30 solidi, and therefore many *commerciarii* were demanding it, and it was found that more than 30 solidi were given by each ship to our God-crowned empire. For one thing, that the Christian people, having come to be under our faith and to God with us, and another for the promise which they made of old, that not only should they preserve the city of those to be found, according to ancient customs, with prompt willingness and tireless services, and perhaps journeying through our empire into Lombardy, to direct its loading, and to work with their ships, and to find no occasion or memory of doing so in this service.
Therefore, we grant their petition and command by this our chrysobull, that each one of their ships, which comes from Venice and from other parts with their business; to donate nothing else to the public for the loading coming from Abydos but two solidi, and when they depart for their province, they shall arrive at everything [with] each one donating seventeen solidi for the ship.
And if they are able to bring themselves, the Venetians [Logothetae] from the house, who will be at that time; and he is to be given by him [the] *commerciarius*, by seeking those same *commerciarii*, when they wish to return to their province.
And they are not to have license to hold them, through greed, for more than three days, and if it should happen, when there is necessity or occasion for it, that he owes a way and he is [obliged] by the same Venetians because of the occasion of their defense, otherwise because they have business to carry in their ships to Constantinople, namely
Amalfitians, Jews, Lombards, from the city of Bari and others, but they have brought forward only their own business. And if they do this, not only could they not defend them, but they have lost their own just cause. Furthermore, we order that only through the logothete who shall be of the *diornon* at that time are these vessels of the Venetians to be examined, and the Venetians themselves are to be searched, weighed, and judged according to the ancient custom; and for those whose trial perhaps increases between them or with others, let them be searched and judged only by that same logothete and by no other judge whatsoever. Therefore we command everyone. The clerks who are under him, and the notaries of the sea-coast, the luminarchs, the epologerni—and they are also called silocalami—the commercial agents of Avidus, or other men, who are reduced even to the least service for the imperial service, and indeed for the public service as well—have no authority at any time, for any occasion, whether the Venetians themselves or that vessel attempt to touch, or search, or inquire with any pretext whatsoever, or say to them anything for which it will be committed to our Empire to be under the hand of the logothete of the *diornon* alone, and by him alone let them be weighed. Observing all the above-mentioned matters regarding them, and working with other services with their own vessels for the loading of our enemy who perhaps wishes to direct himself against our empire in Lombardy. Considering all this and expecting that whoever has dared to do the contrary to what we have commanded and written, or to violate and oppose it, the indignation and wrath of our empire shall come upon him, with the sole demonstration of our own chrysobull seal being sufficient; which was done in the month of March, the fifth indiction; in which our pious and God-ordained empire has confirmed it. Amen.

IV.
New constitution of Basil Porphyrogenitus published in the month of January, in the ninth indiction, in the year 6504.
Chapters of this Constitution from Harmenopulos.
— He removes the prescription of XL years, decreeing that the prescription of XL years shall not profit the powerful who unjustly and greedily oppress the needy; nor shall the poor, having been injured by these same powerful men, be removed by the XL years' prescription. Nor shall descriptions of boundaries have strength, even if they are contained by golden bulls, provided they were made unjustly and fraudulently. He also wills that the Fiscus be excluded by no interval of time, but that it may seek its right from the Augustus Caesar, and deservedly recover it.
1. « Of the prescription of XL years ». — Since my majesty, from the time it began to rule, has undertaken to examine the cases moved by both the wealthy and the poor, it has found that the powerful who desire to overreach have a reasonable pretext, namely the forty-year prescription; and they apply themselves to this, either through bribes or through their own intervening strength, so that they may pass this space of forty years and from that time possess by full right those things of which they have wickedly defrauded the poor. He has brought forth this very law, partly to correct those things which have happened until now, and partly to restrain the powerful who are now living, and to coerce those who will live hereafter: knowing that they will receive no profit from this, but that the result will be that those alien things will be taken away not only from them, but also from their heirs.
