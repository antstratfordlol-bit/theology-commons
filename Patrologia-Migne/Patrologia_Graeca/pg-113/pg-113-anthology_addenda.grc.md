In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Romanus, faithful emperor and autocrat of the Romans. He who provides all good things, the artificer of visible and invisible creation, formed man from the beginning as immortal. For He proposed this to Himself, that man, being composite, might be found in the likeness of the simpler angels and give glory to his benefactor: not that the Creator has need of glory borrowed from the creature, for He is in need of nothing; but that the creature, rendering the due glory to the Creator, might be filled with just glory and become like the angels. But when the first created being, who until then rejoiced in simplicity, fell from the heavenly sphere, he could not bear to see man adorned with the same simplicity, and having devised contrary causes and whispered to him that he would become God through the tree of knowledge, he thus deceived him and cast him down to the earth. And having fallen, he lay in a miserable state and one worthy of no small grief, in that he had no strength thereafter to raise himself up. For he needed three means to recover his former dignity: humility against the former pride, obedience against the former disobedience, and finally, to be prepared to die for his race, against which he had previously acted, having been deceived. He, however, not only to perform these things, but also from sin...
not only doing these things, but he was not even able to depart from sin. For which reason God, who is three-personed, having created him at the beginning, later remembered him again, and the only-begotten Son of God himself, descending from the indescribable bosoms of the Father, becomes man for the sake of men. To whom it is an inescapable duty for every pious person to render worshipful adoration and to repay him with all kinds of gifts according to one’s ability, with mouth, soul, and heart: with the mouth, indeed, today and both publicly and privately to hymn, bless, and glorify his long-suffering and care for the human race; with the soul, to worship him unceasingly as God; and with the heart, to offer to him voluntarily the best and most precious of the things one possesses, as the sole cause and provider of all good things. For this reason, the wise lawgiver of ancient Israel also said: "In your mouth and in your heart and in your hands," and again, as if from the person of God, "You shall not appear before me empty," showing that it is necessary for God to be glorified through these things, since he alone is the true examiner of them. Therefore, if this is inescapable for all for the sake of piety, it is much more so for those who have reigned through him, and this for two reasons: first, as they owe manifold thanks to the Lord of all, inasmuch as they are honored by him more abundantly than others; and second, as they are to be standards of virtue and patterns of piety for others. To the same [God] it is also necessary that his immaculate mother and virgin, the Theotokos Mary, be honored, as she is the provider of salvation for all. But indeed, after her, the life-giving and most venerable wood of the Cross must be honored and worshipped; for concerning this the prophet-king David also says: "Exalt the Lord your God and worship at the footstool of his feet, for it is holy."

For these reasons, therefore, my pious imperial majesty, caring for its subjects and providing for all things pertaining to it—since it is a law for every Greek and barbarian to struggle even unto blood for those of the same race, and the law of God is again more divine and more brilliant than this, commanding each one to give himself for another—my imperial majesty has seen fit to renew the walls of the venerable monastery of the renowned empress Pulcheria the virgin, which is most worthy of the reverence shown to Athos. For I found it so demolished and utterly dissolved that the walls appeared as if they were in the waters. Such also was the venerable monastery of my holy and God-beloved athletes, the Forty, which was formerly called that of the Chimarros, but now is called Xeropotamou, so that not even a likeness of a wall was visible, due to the violent hand of the Ishmaelites. Having used as a guide and co-worker in this my imperial majesty the most holy Paul, the prince of philosophers, surnamed Xeropotamenos, by the assistance and grace of God I built a temple both very large and very beautiful, and I bestowed upon it gold-woven garments and many gold-embroidered vestments and sacred veils and holy patens and chalices of gold.

But since the senate and all the people have justly petitioned that they not be deprived of the pledge of heavenly grace, my imperial power has decreed that every three years two imperial secretaries and one centurion shall be sent by the Roman imperial authority, and two clerics by the synod of the ecumenical patriarch, and that the wood shall be carried publicly with a military escort and the pomp of the leading senators on the first day of the month of August, and be brought in with selected monks of our venerable monastery of Xeropotamou for its adoration and exaltation throughout the world; then it shall be returned and carried back to the said monastery with fitting pomp and reverence. For this reason, my imperial power orders an annual stipend to be given to the Xeropotamou Fathers for our spiritual salvation, to provide the necessities for the aforementioned monks living there in virtue, by which they may sustain their lives. For if my imperial power has shown mercy through chrysobulls to those monks who dwell on Olympus, Kyminas, and Latros, it will much more usefully provide for its own petitioners for their perpetual protection. 3. Hence the present chrysobull edict grants and affirms to the said monastery, by which it wills and decrees that upon its publication, the monks of this monastery of the most holy Paul of Xeropotamou, by the privilege of that life-giving and precious wood, shall receive from the treasury of the Roman emperor one thousand eight hundred gold coins of my currency each year, on account of the aforementioned hospices and hospitals, and that they shall celebrate solemnly with zeal and nocturnal vigil three annual festivals: namely, the entry of the Mother of God into the holy temple; for that mountain which is called holy was given to her as an inheritance from above; the exaltation of the precious wood of the most holy cross throughout the world, because it was granted by our pious imperial power through this largesse; and the martyrdom of the holy and God-beloved lambs, the forty martyrs, because all the venerable relics of these saints were placed in this monastery by a chrysobull from the first who possessed it, the illustrious Empress Pulcheria the virgin, at the time when the great holy forty martyrs of Christ appeared to that blessed empress in a dream. In addition to these, three holy buildings situated within the royal city have been assigned and consecrated as inalienable possessions: namely, that holy temple of the most holy Mother of God in the Neorion, built from the foundations by our imperial power, and my paternal palace contiguous to it with the structures around and in the entire circuit, that is, the Myrelaion, and the conspicuous temple of the forty saints in the Constantinian quarter with the pertinent hospices and structures, so that they may be possessed and held by the venerable monastery of my imperial power as long as the sun moves over the earth.
