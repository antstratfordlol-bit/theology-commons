And since the most religious Christian men refused to destroy the holy icons with their own hands, those who were sent for this purpose—the Amira—pressed into service the God-hated Hebrews and the wretched Arabs; and thus they burned the sacred icons, and the houses of the churches they in places plastered over, and in others scraped away.

And the icons were restored to their ancient foundation and honor; but his son, Ulidus by name, indignant that the sorcerer had, as it were, slain his own father, ordered him to be put to death by a most shameful execution, judging him to have received the wages worthy of his false prophecy.
NOTICE [NOTICE]
To the Narrative of John Cameniata on the Sack of Thessalonica
The destruction of the famous city of Thessalonica, under the most illustrious leader and pirate Leo of Tripoli, while Leo son of Basil was emperor, was a sight that not one of the annalists of his age allowed to pass without recording it in clear memory. Yet, as is the nature of annals, they touched upon it only briefly, so that beyond the slaughter of the city, the great massacre of the citizens, and their exportation into slavery, they recorded nothing in particular. John Cameniata alone, being present at the events, and noble among the spoils along with his father, uncle, mother, and brothers, recorded everything accurately and brought it before our eyes as if it were present. He described so vividly the spirits of the citizens, the concern of the emperor, the weakness of the forces against the enemy, and the city not sufficiently prepared to sustain the sudden—or nearly sudden—assault of a most powerful enemy—an enemy against whom no forces equal in strength appeared anywhere in the empire. Indeed, the royal city itself feared greatly and dreaded its own destruction, attributing it to the divine Numine and counsel that Leo had not dared to attempt the siege through the straits of the sea. Cameniata reveals that his father was the exarch of all Greece and Achaia, of a dignity nearly episcopal; and that he earned a ransomed life (though not liberty) for all manner of people.
