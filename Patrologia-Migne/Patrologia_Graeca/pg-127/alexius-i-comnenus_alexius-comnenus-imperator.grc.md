II.
De sacris vasibus et quod ea nullo deinceps tempore profanum in usum converti debeant.
Qui corpore male affecti sunt, medicaque curatione egent, si modo se morbo laborare senserint, multo facilius a medicis curantur. Qui vero morbi quo laborant, gravitatem minime persentiscunt, imo nec se ægrotare sciunt, ii procul dubio non parum negotii et sibi et medicis facessere solent. Hoc ipsum quidem certe in animi quoque morbis perspicuum est. Atque ex nobis, cum peccant alii quamlibet in profundum malorum gurgitem prolapsi, in utramvis securi dormimus aurem, scelerumque gravitati succumbentes, vix ac ne vix quidem nos peccare sentimus, quia nimirum malus ille habitus jam in naturam transiit. Quibus autem salutiferi sui luminis scintillam aliquam Deus indidit, quorumque intellectus non omnino mundi hujus sublunaris cœno obsurduit, nec lateritio operi mens prorsus inservire cogitur, ii demum a lapsu resurgere, Deique consortio, præter animi sententiam, spontaneaque rerum meliorum disquisitione recurrere possunt.
Quæ res haud dubie nobis imperantibus usu venit. Variæ enim multiplicesque negotiorum circumstantiæ, dum ubique publica exhauriuntur æraria, passimque hostium incursionibus patet aditus, tranquillum imperii nostri statum turbaverunt: cum primumque adeo imperatoriæ majestatis fastigium conscendimus, nos a tergo illa malorum myrmecia insecuta est. Novellae Constitutiones of Emperor Alexios I Komnenos regarding Ecclesiastical Matters
I.

II.
On sacred vessels and that they should never thereafter be converted to profane use.
Those who are poorly in body and need healing treatment, if they but perceive that they are suffering from an illness, are much more easily cured by physicians; but those who do not perceive the gravity of the malady from which they suffer, and indeed do not even know that they are ill, undoubtedly provide no small amount of trouble both for themselves and for their doctors. This same thing is certainly clear also in the illnesses of the soul; and among us who sin, some, having fallen into a depth of evils, are heedless, and, being overcome by the magnitude of our transgressions, do not even perceive that we are sinning, as that wicked habit often passes over into our very nature. But those upon whom God sheds some saving light, and whose faculty of reasoning is not altogether confounded by the mire below, nor the mind forced to serve utterly under the brick-making [of this world], these are able to recover from their fall; and, having often been thrust out from the fellowship of God against the intent of their soul, they are able to return to Him, not without the diligent and spontaneous searching for better things.
This, without a doubt, has indeed happened to us as sovereigns. For the various and manifold circumstances of affairs, and the depletion of the public treasuries on every side, and the opening of outlets for expenditures on every side, have troubled the tranquility of our state; and at the same time as we ascended to the pinnacle of imperial majesty, those swarms of evils followed us from behind.
