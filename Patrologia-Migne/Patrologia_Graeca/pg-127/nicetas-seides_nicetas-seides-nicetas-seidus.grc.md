Regarding the Pascha
. R. Our Lord Jesus Christ was born in the year 5506 from the creation of the world, as I have also said before. Thus also the admirable lord Nicetas, called Seidus, says in the treatise composed by him on this subject.
FROM THE TREATISE OF THE BLESSED LORD NICETAS SEIDUS ON THE PASCHA
Cardinal Angelo Mai, in his notes on the passage of Theodore Meliteniotes where the celebration of the Pascha is discussed, displays an excerpt from the work of Nicetas Seidus, previously lauded by Allatius, prefacing it thus: "Because in this place mention of the Pascha occurs, there came to my mind the notice handed down by Allatius in his diatribe on the Nicetas, no. XI, that Nicetas Seidus, a man of the twelfth century, wrote a treatise on the Pascha. In truth, in the Greek codex Vat. 1059, f. 212, there occurred to me a not-to-be-despised excerpt of the aforementioned treatise, which it is worth the labor to append here, both because it is useful of itself, and also that we may gather the writings of the Nicetases according to our strength. No one will wonder at the title 'Blessed' given to a man who was a schismatic and who even declaimed against the dogmas of the Latins; for the Greek writer of the codex himself was credibly a schismatic, and the term 'blessed' used to be applied by the ancients to any dead person, whether blessed or otherwise; and even now it is used in some regions in the same sense."
Our Lord Jesus Christ was born in the year 5506 from the creation of the world, as I have also said before; for it cannot be otherwise, even if someone were to labor at investigating it to infinity. But his saving passion occurred in the year 5559, when the lunar cycle was the tenth, and the solar was the twenty-third. Which being conceded, it is discovered truly that the legal Pascha—that is, the fourteenth of the moon—was celebrated at that time on the twenty-third day of the month of March, on the sixth day of the week, when the Lord also suffered. For after the burial of the Savior, the irrational lamb was sacrificed toward evening. But as the Sabbath began to dawn, or the twenty-fourth day of the month of March, the solemnity of Unleavened Bread was celebrated by them, as the law in Leviticus ordained; but on the twenty-fifth day of the same month, the bright-shining resurrection of Christ shone upon the whole world. The Lord was born, then, as we all the orthodox believe, in the year of the world 5506, in the first indiction, and in the forty-second year of the reign of Caesar Augustus, on the twenty-fifth of December, on the third day of the week.
