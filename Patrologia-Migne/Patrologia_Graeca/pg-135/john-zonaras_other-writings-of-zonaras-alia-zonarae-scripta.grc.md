Furthermore, I easily persuade myself that these observations made by us concerning the site of the Hebdomon will not be unwelcome to readers, and especially to those who apply themselves to investigating the History of Constantinople; indeed, I have thought it worthwhile to describe them here, not so much to refute the opinion of the author of the dissertation (whose learning and keenness of intellect I have always respected) regarding this controversy, as to propose our own to learned men once more: yet not with such confidence that I might wish it to be taken as indubitable, lest if by chance the reader judges me to be wandering from the purpose, he should immediately say what the aforementioned Henry Valesius said of Casaubon (in his notes to the *Excerpta* of Dio), who he claims saw nothing in explaining a certain passage of Suetonius, and that at the time when he seemed to himself to have seen more than others: so much so that a treatise which relies on conjectures should be composed without stubbornness, as Xenophanes used to say according to Varro. Indeed, the same Valesius, easily the prince of the scholars of our age, when discussing this difficulty proposed in this Disquisition with us, thought it arduous and obstructed, so much so that he would voluntarily confess he did not know to which side to incline, since the places which are said to be situated in the Hebdomon were far removed from the City; having not sufficiently pondered the authorities of the writers, he scarcely judged it, contenting himself with having touched upon this with a few lines and a light hand in his annotations to Ammianus and the *Ecclesiastical History*. Truly, if the things we have brought forward in this Disquisition to strengthen our opinion concerning the site of that same Hebdomon do not please the learned man at all, let us both say with Augustine (lib. *contra Epist. fundam.*), "Let no one of us say that he has already found the truth; let us seek it as if it were unknown to both of us."

JOANNIS ZONARAE, MONK AND FIRST SECRETARY, IN SANCTISSIMAM DEIPARAM CANON
*Whose acrostich signifies: The last tone weaves the last melody.*
(Ex Joan. Bap. Cotelerii Monum. Eccl. Gr., tom. III.)
Tone IV. Plagal. Aurigam Pharaonis.
Arius.
Most Holy, who didst bear God, of the same nature as the Father, the only-begotten Word, co-eternal, of equal honor; even though the senseless Arius thought Him a creature, alien to the essence of the Father; entreat Him that we may be saved.
**Sabellius.** You received in your womb, O Queen of all, the Son, who is truly one of one divinity having three hypostases; even though Sabellius, by a wickedly devised confusion, has basely contracted the divine Trinity into one person of three names.
**Macedonius.** We glorify the uncreated Spirit of God, who overshadowed you, O Immaculate one, consubstantial with the Word and the Father, and proceeding from Him; we bid all to deem Macedonius a madman, for he says that He is a creature and a work.
**Apollinarius.** The Lord, descending from heaven into your womb, O chaste Virgin, and assuming flesh endowed with mind and soul, saved our nature. But let him who says that the Lord was created with a soul lacking a mind lament and suffer punishment, for he is a madman, namely Apollinarius.
Ode 3. The roof of the celestial vault.
**Nestorius.** May he who does not call you truly Theotokos be part of the portion of Nestorius, O Queen of all; he who declared Christ to be a mere man, and called you, who are the Mother of God, only the mother of Christ.
**Marcion.** May God, who from your blood assumed flesh, O Lady, not in appearance alone—as the impious Marcion supposed, who foolishly introduced two [or three] principles—strengthen your flock with his own faith.
**Eunomius.** By your supplications, O Virgin, deliver your flock from eternal punishments, which Eunomius, most impious and most wretched, foolishly raved were written only as a threat to frighten us.

**Eutyches.** The heresy of the childishly-minded Eutyches spoke of one nature in Christ after the union; he does not think, O Virgin, that the body which the Lord assumed is of the same substance as us, along with Severus; whose opinion we reject.
**Manes.** The hand of your Son, O Immaculate one, fashioned me, who also constituted the whole world; even though the raving Manes introduced two principles, and taught two gods, the one indeed good and the other evil, and divided the creatures between them.
**The same.** This wretched man, O pure one, also affirmed the incarnation of your Son to be in appearance, not thinking it true, and impiety taught that He suffered only in appearance, introducing one nature, that of the divinity alone. May we, therefore, be delivered from his madness.
**Apollinarius.** Apollinarius affirmed the body of Christ to be soulless.
Arius, since the divinity sufficed for him in place of a soul, hastening deceitfully to attribute to the divinity whatever words the Word spoke in a human manner, in order to show the Son to be lesser than the Father, O Queen of all.
Ode 5. Why hast thou cast me away from thy face? 
**Origenes.**
I am subject to eternal punishment, O Virgin, help me; for I do not accept the opinion of Origen, who teaches that the terrible punishments of hell are temporary, for the purification of sins.
**Didymus. Evagrius.**
With Didymus, Evagrius again teaches the pre-existence of souls, having received this strange opinion from Origen. But may the pious dogma be preserved for us unharmed, by thy intercessions, O Queen of all.
**The Same.**
It is the opinion of the same, again, that even the demons are to receive restoration, and that men who have done lawless things will, with the angels, complete the nine orders (1), which they fabulize with a corrupt mind.
Ode 6. Be merciful to me, O Savior. 
**Marcion.**
Marcion impiously denied the resurrection of the dead; and he gave a triple baptism to those who fall. But we, O Mother of God, have been taught one baptism, and also resurrection.
**Novatus.**
Direct my paths, O venerable one, toward repentance, which Novatus denied to us who fall even after baptism, having thought this unworthy of the goodness of God.
**Encratites.** 
Those who are called Encratites forbid marriage; and they loathe the eating of animals. But do thou strengthen us all in the decrees of thy Son, O Mother-Virgin.
Ode 7. God’s condescension. 
**Massalians.**
The Massalians say that Satan impiously rules the minds of men, and that in them the spirits of wickedness dwell personally, O Mistress. But by thy supplications, O Virgin, may God dwell in us.

Ode 8. Sevenfold the furnace.
Aerius.
Thinking wrongly, Aerius used to say that one should not offer sacrifice for the dead. For he claimed that they reap no benefit from this. Insanely, he also said that one should not fast on the fourth and sixth days of the week. He was also of the same mind as Arius regarding the faith. O Queen of all, rescue me from his impiety.
Paul of Samosata.
From the impious dogmas of Paul of Samosata, O Bride of God, deliver your flock. For when he said the Son, who is without beginning, was a prolative word, and that God is the Creator, he wrongly claimed that He used the Spirit as an instrument, and that He received a beginning from your womb.
Sergius [and] Pyrrhus.
Following the decrees of the most sacred doctors, we acknowledge two wills and a double operation in Christ; and we leave Sergius, Pyrrhus, and the rest of the same opinion to rave, O Theotokos, since they foolishly say that the flesh of the Lord is devoid of will and has no operation.
Apelles.
Apelles indeed proclaims one principle, O Queen of all, and confesses one supreme God; but he says that that one created another, whom he posits to have become the evil creator of the world; since he himself is evil, and an ungrateful creature, worthy of fire alone.
