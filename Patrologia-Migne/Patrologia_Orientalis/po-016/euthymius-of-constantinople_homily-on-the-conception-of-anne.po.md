3. Whence indeed we adore the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one essence, one inaccessible and ineffable divinity, one God in three persons; for as it befits servants and the orthodox, we serve the most holy and omnipotent Trinity. One is God in three properties, and these three, in which is divinity, are one. For we divide in the manner of theologians, but indivisibly; and we join again, yet not without distinction. We do not contract into one, we do not confuse the substances of the Son, we do not introduce separate persons. But, as we have said, we both confess one God and do not deny three hypostases or persons: for God is Father, God is Son, God is Holy Spirit. Avoiding Judaism and Sabellianism, and anathematizing Arianism thereafter, we speak of divinity, we profess the whole divinity; but we proclaim one person, whether of the Father, or of the Son, or of the Spirit, noting the property of each separately. For not one person is three, as some impious men babble, but one essence and three distinct, immutable characters.

7. This one, I will say again, Fathers and brothers and children, Christ today gave for redemption through the most just Joachim and Anna. Should we not therefore accept this day as the first of all feasts? Should we not embrace it as one that will reconcile the sum of goods to us? Shall we not leap with great exultation, singing a triumphal song against the tortuous and fraudulent serpent? Where is death, your sting? Where, O hell, your victory? For it is expedient for us that we modulate a trophy against him, like some adventitious song, exhibiting the signs of his defeat and moving our strongest shield, namely the cross, on which the Son of the [Virgin] himself and God incarnate from her, stretching out his hands, nailed our sins, and drove away from us the wicked army of invisible and dark enemies with blows.

For Christ was born of a Virgin, that he might teach us virginity; he was laid in a manger of brute beasts, that he might instruct us to seek humble things, and that we might not think highly of uncertain riches—For if riches abound, do not set your heart on them. The author of life had nowhere on earth to lay his head, but traveled on foot for our salvation, and slept upon the earth, using no pillow or anything else for resting, that he might teach us poverty and render us heavenly.
