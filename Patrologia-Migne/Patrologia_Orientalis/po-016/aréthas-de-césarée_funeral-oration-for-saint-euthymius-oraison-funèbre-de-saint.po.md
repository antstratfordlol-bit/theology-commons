1. What is this, holy assembly, sanctified inheritance, divinely inspired people? What is this new gathering of yours and this unusual solemnity, in which no sign of joy appears, nor all those things which are wont to decorate a festival; but whatever is sad and gloomy, whatever is lamentable and tearful, and all those bitter and harsh things which the greatest calamities are accustomed to bring to men, this illustrious gathering is abundantly affected by today, so that it seems to be singing a funeral dirge? What then is such a great plague? Or what is this so vehement pain, which has occupied your souls, that it does not permit you to lift your head at all, nor allow your eyes to be entirely free from the sadness of tears? Surely Euthymius, that great pontiff of God, was the cause of this lamentation for you, an innocent man treated injuriously, driven from his seat, condemned to exile by the impious hand of criminals, now subjected to death, and handed over to this tomb, which you see.

You, however, make those things which ought to effect perfect joy an occasion for lamentations. Yet neither are these things pleasing to God, nor do they befit the one lying there. For although he seemed to the eyes of the foolish to die, as the prophet says, and his departure was considered an affliction, and his departure from us a crushing, nevertheless he is now in peace, since God, to continue to praise the words of sacred eloquence, tested him and found him worthy of himself; as gold in the furnace he proved him and as a holocaust of sacrifice he accepted him. And this also: in the time of his visitation he will shine when all the just will shine like the sun, joined to the most clear and pure light — darkness and gloom will surround those who will be on the left side; and the just will stand against those who have distressed him, if one is to believe the divine prophet, describing the retribution of future vengeance.

The feast of Bacchus was not yet finished when he was miserably carried up into his bedchamber, having met with a fall no better than that of Herod, who, after he had slain James the son of Zebedee to please those who had killed Christ, and had attacked Peter the leader himself, received his deserved reward, for in the midst of the solemnity, surrounded by a royal retinue of guards, he departed this life through a most grievous swelling and an incredible burning of worms. But what of this man of ours? You surely know of his most miserable funeral and the haste to the tomb, the royal chair in which his body was carried having broken, just as if it had borne this most wicked burden with difficulty. That unhappy body stank, emitting a most foul stench that confounded every scent of aromatics. Moreover, it had swollen in a wondrous manner. And such was the state of his inner parts while he was still alive: his body already melting into putrefaction, they say his decaying flesh flowed out through the latrine, which indeed resulted in his being barely carried to the intended tomb and shamefully cast aside. 8. Let those things which happened to this man now be compared with ours, if you please, and let it be shown what a religious and pious man is, and what, on the contrary, a contemner of God and an impious man is, and how the end of a holy man is honored. For in addition to what we have said, there is also the fact that he did not obtain the customary funeral rites, nor was royal pomp employed, except for a very few, and those commoners, who would have performed the customary service for anyone from the crowd—and yet he had many who were devoted to him, and no gathering had been forbidden—if you except those who must attend the emperors without delay. But it was fitting that that contemner should obtain such a burial, which would be a symbol of the future punishments owed for the crimes committed by him. For the groaning of the poor will not perish forever, and the choir of young women, of our churches, I say, will sing a song of victory, with the new and arrogant Pharaoh drowned in the sea along with his chariots and horsemen; since such an end is prepared for them by their own harshness of character, hardness, and implacability. But let us leave these things aside, and pursue those which were proposed to us, adding these to what has already been said. 9. O power of the pontiff of God, who together with those who have suffered injuries for the sake of virtue, exult in joy! Together with Abel you are subject to envy, together with that ancient Jacob you are vexed by rivals, together with Zechariah you are slaughtered, together with James the son of Zebedee you are put to death for the favor of the Jews, together with the divine Athanasius you are expelled. Together with Paul you are honored with the shackles of exile, just as this man is honored with confession; together with the golden John you are delivered to death in exile; together with Nicephorus and Photius, most illustrious men, you are adorned with flights and deaths; the tomb did not cover you, but revealed you to all the lands that are under heaven. The earth did not hide you, but manifested you to all parts that are everywhere as a pontiff worthy of the God who had created you, a worthy herald of His wonders, a worthy man who attained the end of his sacred labors.

Nevertheless, you who have already received the rewards of your labors, and are one with the leader and master Christ, reconcile Him to us from there, that He may still the storm, restrain the tempest, calm the tumult of the sea, and pay the deserved rewards to those who have been afflicted with injuries just as we have, whether so that their judgment may be turned into victory, or that the authors of the injuries may recognize that they have sinned, hoping for a worthy retraction.
