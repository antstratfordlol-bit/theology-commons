6. But that saying: 'And a sword will pierce your own soul,' most clearly foretells what happened to the Virgin at the very cross. For, as if by a sword, at that time, her soul was divided into two parts, while she revolved in her mind the words spoken to her by the angel at the Annunciation, and how she had the conception without seed and underwent that birth without detriment to her virginity, while she recalled the miracles performed by him and in which, as a mother, she gloried. All these things the Virgin was again fashioning within herself in one thought; in the other, however, she saw him as a man affected by insults, struck with blows, scourged, struck on the head with a reed, crowned with thorns, led to the cross, fixed with nails and hanging, and enduring these things with evildoers, pierced in the side with a lance, given vinegar and gall to drink, and laid in the tomb. On both sides, therefore, there was sufficient cause for the soul of that most pure one to be dissected as if by a sword. Well, therefore, did Simeon say to her: 'And a sword will pierce your own soul.'
