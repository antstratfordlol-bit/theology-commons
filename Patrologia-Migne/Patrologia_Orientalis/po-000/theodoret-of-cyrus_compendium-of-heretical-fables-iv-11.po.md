But they say that the indwelling demon exits through mucus and the excrements of spittle. Deceived by this reason, the most wretched men undertake no work, for they call themselves spiritual, but truly giving themselves to prayer, they consume the greatest part of the day in sleep. Then, deceived by the demon agitating them, they affirm that they see revelations and attempt to predict future things, but by the things themselves they are argued of lying. Moreover, they boast that they see the Father and the Son and the holy Spirit with bodily eyes and receive the sense of the arriving Spirit. But they are prompt to deny and, if they are urged, they immediately anathematize those who say any of these things. When, therefore, they were called into court before the most celebrated Flavian, bishop of the Antiochians, they called the accusations brought against them slanders. But the most wise man perceived the hidden heresy. For appearing to be incensed at the accusers and to take them ill, as if they had fabricated lies, he ordered Adelphius to sit by him, — for he was in charge of those who were accused of heresy, — a man old and advanced in age and otherwise close to the gates of death. Addressing him, the most blessed Flavian said that the accusers were ignorant as being more coarse in their spiritual way of living. 'But we,' he said, 'who have grown old, have perceived this knowledge in time. Tell me, however,' he said, 'candidly, what are these things which are said?' Deluded in this way, the old man, thinking he had found a companion in his dogmas, confirmed the accusation by his confession. Having been taught these things

A divine man from the borders of the East drove them out. But they dare many other frenzied deeds. For they suddenly leap and boast that they are leaping upon demons, and with their fingers they express the shape of shooting, affirming that they are shooting at demons. And they do other things full of similar madness, from which they have obtained the name of Enthusiasts. This heresy was led by Sabbas, Adelphius, Daodes, Symeones, Hermas, and certain others. The most praiseworthy Letoius, bishop of Melitene, wrote letters against them, having examined the heresy. But the best of all, Amphilochius, ruling the church of Iconium, most accurately refuted the heresy, inserting their very words into the Acts he composed, which clearly declare the difference in doctrine.
