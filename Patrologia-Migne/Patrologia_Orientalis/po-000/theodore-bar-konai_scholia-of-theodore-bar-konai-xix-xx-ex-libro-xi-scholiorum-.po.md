They are also said to hold this opinion: that every human being, as if by a succession of nature, receives a demon who, they say, remains with him throughout his life and is not expelled by any other means except prayer—that is, by the furious movements which they call 'the complement.' Regarding baptism, they assert that it confers nothing upon the one who possesses it, nor does the grace of the Spirit which is given by its aid; nor do the holy mysteries profit the one who receives them, but they become an auxiliary to the prayer which is offered by them to the demon that appears to them. Now, those who were seized by this infirmity were monks who, having been cast out from their places near Edessa and from the regions of the East, sought Lycaonia. For they did not separate themselves from the members of the church by communion, since they thought it was neither a loss nor a gain to participate in the life-giving mysteries or to abstain from them.
