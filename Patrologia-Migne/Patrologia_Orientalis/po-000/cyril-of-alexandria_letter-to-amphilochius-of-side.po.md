St. Cyril, Archbishop of Alexandria, Letter to Amphilochius, Bishop of Side. I indeed know that your most prudent piety will hold nothing more than charity and brotherly love. Nevertheless, you will consider it to be of your prudence in all matters to be conducted to have the best method of acting. Therefore I write lovingly to your piety and,

what it knows how to effect, I ask, since it is manifest that one must not use too many subtleties with those who wish to repent and, having left the impious heresy of the Messalians or Euchites, approach the right dogmas of the Church. For it suffices for those who have entered this path to profess: "I anathematize the heresy of the Messalians or Euchites." For if anyone deals with them more minutely and at the same time sometimes thrusts readings from books upon him, he disturbs him who does not possess sharpness of mind; for very many are uneducated and are not able to understand so as to condemn eloquently all things that are worthy of condemnation. It suffices, therefore, that he has anathematized the heresy and that names are not at all brought forward, lest the litigious spirit of some, out of ignorance, be driven away from the will of believing rightly. What I write, I do not write to gratify anyone, but knowing that prudence is best in these matters; for subtleties, as is generally wont to happen, disturb very many, even the wisest themselves.
