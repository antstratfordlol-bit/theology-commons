# Prologue to the Dialogue Against the Pelagians

**Author**: Jerome the Priest  
**Series**: PO Tome PS  
**Columns**: pp. 1–1  

[p. 1] {#po.0.p1}
[THE PROLOGUE OF ST. EUSEBIUS HIERONYMUS TO THE DIALOGUE AGAINST THE PELAGIANS, WRITTEN AROUND THE YEAR 415.] Having already written the letter to Ctesiphon, in which I answered the questions asked, there was frequent expostulation from the brothers as to why I was delaying the promised work, in which I pledged that I would answer all the little questions of those who preach *apatheia*. For there is no doubt that this contention belongs to the Stoics and Peripatetics, that is, the old Academy, in that some of them assert that *pathe*, which we can call perturbations—sickness, joy, hope, fear—can be eradicated and extirpated from the minds of men; others that they can be broken, ruled, and moderated, and restrained like unbridled horses with certain bits. Tullius explains these opinions in his *Tusculan Disputations*, and Origen attempts to mix them into Ecclesiastical truth in his *Stromata*, to say nothing of the Manichaean, the Priscillianist, Evagrius the Iberian, Jovinian, and the heretics of almost all of Syria, whom they call in the gentile tongue *diestrammenos* Massalians, and in Greek *Euchites*; the opinion of all of whom is that human virtue and knowledge can arrive at perfection and, I will not say at the likeness, but at the equality of God: so that they assert that they cannot sin even in thought and ignorance, when they have ascended to the summit of consummation.
