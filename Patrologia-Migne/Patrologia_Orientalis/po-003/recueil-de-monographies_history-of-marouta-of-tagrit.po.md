II. Youth of Marouta. - Saint Mar Marouta belonged by his family to the land of Beit Nehoudrâ in the vicinity of Nineveh, to the village of Sourzaq. His parents were faithful (Jacobites), just, good, and pious men; they possessed without reserve a good reputation for the things of God as well as for the wealth, goods, and honor of this world. They were notables, leaders of the whole village whose inhabitants were pious Christians thanks to the teaching and direction of the parents of the saint who led them all to imitate their virtues. They had no children and preferred the love of God to that of children, an affinity near Him * to brothers and servants, and to be His heirs to heirs and inheritances. They loved this new homeland to come, heaven and the heavenly Jerusalem which is the city of the living God, more than this visible city. They did not love this world, in order to dwell and sit in the one that must last forever; for they learned from Our Lord, from James his brother, and from John the Evangelist: Do not love the world nor anything that is in it, and: He who wants to be a friend of this world will be the enemy of God.

When by the will of almighty God they begat this blessed one and brought him into the world, they gave him the education that befitted parents truly friends of God and children. From his smallest size after babbling, they entrusted him to God who had given him to them and surrendered his existence to Him. They knew indeed, these sages in the Lord, that there is no * precaution comparable to that and that there is no guardian more powerful than the Lord. They imitated in this the blessed Anne and Elizabeth and the other holy personages who offered their children to the Lord. There was, not far from their village of Sourzaq, a holy monastery, named after Mar Samuel the Mountaineer, raised on a height, on the bank of the Tigris opposite the monastery of Saint Mar Sergis which is near Balad. They gave him from then on to this monastery to be raised and instructed in the divine letters from his childhood. Divine grace followed him as for the holy prophets Samuel, Jeremiah, and John, preacher of truth. From his tenderest childhood germinated and appeared his meekness, his intelligence, the beauty of his excellent conduct, and his ardor for the sciences.

When they saw the child's meekness, his gentleness, his humility, and his knowledge, they loved him; he grew up before them and they prophesied about him and said: “What could well become of him?” and many things which came to pass.
