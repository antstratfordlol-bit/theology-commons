Christians are divided into several sects. The first is that of the Melkites: these are the Greeks, and they are so called for the sole reason that the emperor (malik) of the Greeks follows their cult. It alone extends over the empire. The second is that of the Nestorians, whose name is linked to Nestorius, the founder of their system, in the year 720 and some of Alexander. The third is that of the Jacobites. These are their most important sects. There are also among Christians, regarding the principles—that is to say, the hypostases, the divinity and humanity of Christ, and the union in the person of Christ of these two natures—divergences of views that separate them. Thus was born among them the sect of the Arians; the notion that the Arians have of Christ is closer to that held by the Muslims, and departs from what the generality of Christians profess. There are many other sects, but this is not the place to mention them: the books containing dogmatic treatises, the exposition of systems, religious questions, and the refutation of these heterodox doctrines have exhausted the subject, and have visited it in its corners and mysteries. The Melkites and the Nestorians are the most numerous: indeed, the Greek empire with the countries that border it is entirely populated by Melkites, and the populations of Syria,

Kanun II (January). 1. C. of Basil, and feast of the Calends. Calends is a word that means: It is good. On that day the children of the Christians gather, go around the houses, and leave one house to enter another, shouting and singing: Kalendas, Kalendas! They are served food in all the houses, and they are poured goblets of wine. It is claimed that the reason for this custom is that the year of the Greeks begins on that day, which is also the end of the week of the birth of Mary; others allege this reason: when the system of Arius appeared, and he had acquired followers, he seized one of the churches of the Christians. The faithful declared war on him, then they came to agree and to make peace on the condition that the door of the church be closed for three days in a row, then that they come together; and that they recite their prayers in turn before this door: those before whom the door would open would be those who would have the right to the church. They did what was agreed; the door did not open before Arius, it opened for the Christians (on that day). It is claimed, therefore, that this custom of the children is meant to recall the good news that the Christians circulated on that day. - 2. C. of Moses, son of Amram, may peace be upon him! - 6. Feast of Mount Tabor. Here is what the Gospel reports on this subject: the prophets Moses, son of Amram, and Ilia, who is Elijah, appeared to Christ on Mount Tabor. And there were with Christ three of his companions: Simon, James, and John, who were sleeping. When they woke up and saw this apparition, they were seized with fear, and said: "May our Master (they meant Christ) permit us to raise three tents, one for you, and the other two for Moses and Elijah." They had not finished when three clouds above them spread their shadow over them; Moses and Elijah entered into the mist, and disappeared. Moses had been dead, at that moment, for centuries; as for Elijah, he was alive, and still is (this is indeed their belief), but hidden and stolen from the gaze of men. - 7. Al-Ma'mun ibn Ahmad as-Salami al-Harawi relates that he saw in Jerusalem a pile of stones at the gate called Bab el-'Amoud piled up to the height of a mound or a hill. It was explained to him that they had been thrown on the blood of John, son of Zechariah, and that the blood was always rising higher, bubbling. This lasted until Nebuchadnezzar made the massacres that one knows, and spilled the blood of the victims on that of John; and from that day its movement stopped. Now, there is nothing like that in the Gospel, and I do not know what to think: indeed, Nebuchadnezzar invaded Jerusalem something like 445 years before the murder of John; and the second pillage of the city was accomplished by Vespasian and Titus, emperors of the Greeks. Perhaps the inhabitants of Jerusalem gave the name of Nebuchadnezzar to every destroyer of their city; otherwise, I heard a historian say that this king in question is Joudharz, son of Sa'abour, son of Afquorchah, king of Achkania. - 30.
