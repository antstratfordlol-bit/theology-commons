### ON THE FEASTS OF THE COPTIC CHRISTIANS IN THE EGYPTIAN PROVINCES
ذكر أعياد القبط من النصارى بديار مص روى يونس عن عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه انه قال اجتنبوا عيد البهود وانصارى فانَ السخط ينزل عليهم فى مجامعهم ولا تتعلموا رطانتهم فتخاقوا ببعض خلقهم وعن ابن عباس ! فى قوله تعالى والذين لا يشهدون الزور واذا موا باللغو موا كراما قال اعياد المشركين فقيل له اوما هذا فى الشهادة بالزور فقال لا انما آية شبادة الزور ولا تقف ما ليس لك به علم انّ السمع والبصر والفؤاد كل اولئك كان عنه مسشولا. اعلم أن نصارى مصر من القبط ينتحلون مذهب البعقونية كما تقدم ذكرة وأعادهم الآن التى هى مشبورة بديار مصر أرعة عشر عيدا فى كل سنة من سنيهم القبطية
Yunus reports that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (may God be favorable to him) made this recommendation: "Avoid the feast of the Jews and the Christians, for anger descends in their assemblies; do not learn their languages, for fear that you might contract some of their habits." According to the same author, Ibn 'Abbas, commenting on this word of the Most High: Those who do not want to be witnesses of imposture, but who, when they pass by falsehood, pass (turning away from it) nobly, affirms that it concerns the feasts of the Christians; and, as he was asked: "Does this not concern rather false testimony?" he replied: "No, the verse that targets false testimony is only this one: Do not pursue that of which you have no knowledge; the hearing, the sight, the heart, you will be held accountable for all that." Know that the Coptic Christians of Egypt profess the Jacobite doctrine, as we have explained previously. are called great feasts and the other seven are the small feasts. The great feasts are: the Feast of the Annunciation, the Feast of the Olive, the Feast of Easter, the Feast of the Thursday of the Forty Days, the Feast of the Fifty Days, the Feast of the Nativity, and the Feast of the Immersion. The small feasts are: the Feast of the Circumcision, the Feast of the Forty Days, the Thursday of the Promise, the Saturday of the Light, the Sunday of the Limits, the Transfiguration, the Feast of the Cross. They also have non-canonical feasts established by custom. Such is, for example, the day of Nauruz. I will give in this book information on these feasts that one would not find gathered elsewhere; I have indeed collected them from the books of the Christians and the histories of the Muslims.

1. The Feast of the Annunciation. - This feast of the Christians has its origin in the Annunciation by which Gabriel made known to Mary the birth of Christ (salutation to Him). The Christians call Gabriel Ghabrial; Mary, Marta Mariam; and they call Christ Yashu'a (Jesus); often they say: the Lord Yashu'a. The Christians of Egypt celebrate this feast on the 29th day of Barmahat.

4. The Thursday of the Forty Days. - The Syrians call it the Mislaq; it is also called the feast of the Ascension. It falls on the forty-second day after the end of their fast. The Christians claim that, forty days after his resurrection, Christ (salutation to Him!) went to Bethany with his disciples, stretched out his hands, blessed them, and went up to heaven. He was then thirty-three years and three months old. The disciples returned to Jerusalem. Christ had promised them the propagation of their doctrine and other advantages that the Christians cite. Such is their belief on the manner in which Christ left this world. But who is more truthful than God concerning traditions? 5. The Feast of the Fifty Days. - It is the feast of Pentecost (al-Ansara) which they celebrate fifty days after the Resurrection. They affirm that ten days after the Ascension and fifty days after the Resurrection of Christ, the disciples being gathered on Mount Zion, the Holy Spirit manifested itself to them in the form of tongues of fire. They were filled with the Holy Spirit, spoke all languages, and operated a large number of prodigies. The Jews persecuted them and put them in prison. But God delivered them from their hands;

6. The Feast of the Nativity. - They affirm that this is the day on which Christ was born. It was a Monday. They keep vigil on the evening of the night of the Nativity and are accustomed to lighting a great number of lights in the churches and decorating them. This feast is celebrated in Egypt on the twenty-ninth day of Koiahk. It has remained until now one of the most solemn feasts of Egypt. Under the Fatimids, on that day, they would distribute to high officials, grand stewards, emirs wearing the collar, secretaries, and others, bowls of Cairo pastries, cakes made with fine flour, skins of julep, dishes of fritters, and the fish called mullet. We have seen Christmas in Cairo, in Misr, and throughout all of Egypt, be the occasion of a magnificent solemnity. One sold there candles illuminated with pretty colors and ravishing images for considerable sums. There was no one, among the highest placed as among the humblest, who did not buy some for his children and his family. These torches were called faouânis, in the singular fânoûs. One hung enormous quantities of them, and of great beauty, in the shops of the bazaars. A true fever reigned to drive up their price, so much so that I saw a lantern, the workmanship of which cost a thousand or five hundred silver dirhams, rise to more than seventy mithqâl of gold. The street beggars themselves took part in these festivities; they asked God to grant them a lantern and one bought for them small lamps for a dirham or thereabouts. Later, the troubles of Egypt, among the luxury customs they caused to disappear, ruined the manufacture of lanterns for the Christmas feast, of which little remains.

The same author reports that in the year 388 (998), "the feast of the immersion was celebrated. Tents and pavilions were set up and seats were placed in several places on the edge of the Nile. Divans were established for the Raïs Fahd ibn Ibrahim the Christian, secretary of the ustâdh Barjaouân, and torches and lanterns were lit in his honor, and singers and mimes were brought in. He sat with his family and began to drink until the hour of the immersion. He then took his bath and retired." He adds: "In the year 401 (1011), the twenty-eighth day of Jomâda I, which coincided with the tenth day of Touba, the immersion was forbidden to the Christians and not one of them plunged into the river."
