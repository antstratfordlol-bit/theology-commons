### HOMILY VIII
ܬܘܙ̈ܓܡܐ ܕܬܡܢܝܐ ܕܐܦܝܬܪ̈ܘܦܘ ܕܥܠ ܛܠܵܝ̈ܐ ܗܢܘܢ ܕܡܢ ܗܪܘܕܝܣ ܐܬܩ̈ܛܠܘ ܒܒܝܬܠܚܡ ܩܕܝܫܐ ܡܪܝ ܣܐܘܪܘܣ ܦܐܛܪܝܪܟܐ
Translation of the eighth of the Cathedral (homilies), on the children who were massacred by Herod in Bethlehem, by Saint Mar Severus, Patriarch.
ܡܬܕܡܪ̈ܝܢ ܐܢܬܘܢ ܡܢ ܟܠ ܦܪ̈ܨܘܦ ܒܦܘܩܕܢܐ ܕܫܢܝܘܬܐ ܕܗܪܘܕܝܣ ܕܠܘܩܒܠ ܫܒܪ̈ܐ ܗܿܢܘܢ ܕܠܐ ܥܕܠܝ ܇ ܡܛܠ ܕܟܝܬ ܕܐܦ ܥܕܟܝܠ ܬܚܝܬ ܬܕܐ ܐܝܬܝܗܘܢ ܗܘܘ ܇ ܕܦܣܩܐ ܚܪܡܐ ܕܡܘܬܐ ܡ̇ܝܬܐ ܗܘܐ. ܐܠܐ ܠܐ ܬܬܕܡܪܘܢ. ܕܫܢܝܘܬܐ ܓܝܪ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܗܘܐ. ܡܢ ܗܪ̈ܟܐ ܟܬܒܐ ܐܠܗܝܐ ܘܟܗܢܝܐ ܕܐܘ̈ܢܓܠܝܐ ܇ ܟܕ ܠܠܐ ܡܠܝܠܘܬܐ ܕܗ̇ܘ ܕܦܩܕ ܡܝܚ̈ܘܐ ܐܡܼܪ ܆ ܐܬܚܿܡܬ. ܘܐܘܣܼܦ ܣ̈ܓܝ. ܚܡܼܬܐ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇ ܗܘܬ ܐܡܪ ܇ ܗܺܝ ܕܐܦܼܩܬ̇ ܠܓܙܪ ܕܝܢܐ ܆ ܘܚܡܬܐ ܕܐܚܼܕܬ̇ ܠܡܚܫܒܬܐ. ܡܢܐ ܗܟܝܠ ܬܐܠܕ ܗܘܬ ܚܡܬܐ ܇ ܐܠܐ ܐܢ ܫܢܝܘܬܐ ܘܐܫܝܕܘܬ ܕܡܐ ܇ ܘܡܪ̈ܚܘܬܐ
1 You are completely stunned by Herod's senseless order against the innocent little children, since in truth they were still at the breast, (an order) which brought an execrable death sentence. Do not be stunned, however, for (this order) stemmed from madness. That is why the divine and sacred book of the Gospels, showing the lack of reason of the one who had ordered it, says: He became angry, and he added: Greatly. It is anger, he says, that pronounced the sentence, and (it is) anger that took hold of his thought. What could anger therefore give birth to, if not madness, the shedding of blood, and the fury of wild beasts?
ܕܚܝ̈ܘܬ ܫܢܐ ܘܚܝܘܬܐ. ܟܕ ܗܠܝܢ ܕܝܢ ܫܿܡܥ. ܟܒܪ ܢܐܡܪ ܐܢܫ ܆ ܕܛܒܐܝܬ ܡܿܢ ܟܕ ܠܡܠܬܐ ܘܠܥܠܼܬܐ ܕܩܛܘܠܘܬ ܛܠ̈ܝ̈ܐ ܕܗܪ̈ܘܕܝܣ ܐܬܬܼܒܥܬ̇ ܇ ܠܠܐ ܡܠܝܠܝܘܬܐ ܕܡܢ ܪ̈ܘܓܙܐ ܝܗܼܒܬ. ܒܥܐ ܐܢܐ ܕܝܢ ܆ ܕܡܛܠ ܡܢܐ ܫܒܼܩ ܡܫܝܚܐ ܇ ܕܛܠܝ̈ܐ ܕܠܐ ܥܕܠܝ ܟܢܝܫܐܝܬ ܢܬܩ̇ܛܠܘܢ ܇ ܘܥܒ̈ܕܐ ܕܫܢܝܘܬܐ ܕܗ̇ܘ ܢܗܘܘܢ ܇ ܡܐܠܠܘܢ ܕܝܢ. ܥܒܿܕܐ ܕܠܐ ܒܘ̈ܠܝܬܐ. ܠܡܫܝܚܐ ܡܿܢ ܓܝܪ ܟܕ ܒܿܥܐ ܗܘܐ ܆ ܛܒ ܓ݂ܠܝܐ ܕܠܡܫܟܚܘ ܠܐ ܡܿܨܐ ܗܘܐ ܇ ܠܗܘ ܕܒܟܠ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܠܐ ܡܬܕܪ̈ܟܢܐ ܘܥܣܼܩ ܠܫܟܚܬܐ ܇ ܘܕܨܒܝܢܐܝܬ ܡܛܠ ܦܘܪܩܢܐ ܕܝܠܢ ܐܬܬܕܪܟ ܘܐܨܛܠܒ ܇ ܐܡܬܝ ܕܙܿܕܩ ܗܘܐ ܘܐܡܬܝ ܕܨܒܐ ܗܘܐ. ܚܠܦ ܕܝܢ ܒܥܳܬܐ ܕܡܛܠ ܗܿܘ ܆ ܩܛܼܠܐ ܗܘ ܕܛܠܝ̈ܐ ܗܼܘܐ ܫܘܡܠܝܗ
2 But, at these words, someone will perhaps say: "It is a good (answer) indeed, that after having been questioned about the motive and cause of the massacre of the children by Herod, you have given the madness (resulting) from anger. But I seek why Christ allowed innocent children to be massacred en masse and for this to be the result of this man's madness, or rather an iniquitous work. Indeed, on the one hand, when Herod was looking for Christ, it is quite evident that he could not find the one who is absolutely elusive and difficult to find and who voluntarily for our salvation was seized and crucified, when it was necessary and when he wanted it. On the other hand, rather than a search for (Christ), this massacre of the children was the outcome of a design of vengeance.
ܕܚܘܫܒܐ. ܐܠܐ ܗܕܐ ܆ ܕܬܪ̈ܥܝܬܐ ܗܺܝ ܕܙܥܘܪ̈ܝܬܐ ܚ̈ܙܝܐ ܆ ܘܕܒܥܠܡܐ ܗܢܐ ܐܣܝܪ̈ܐ ܐܝܬܝܗ̇ ܇ ܗܺܝ ܕܥܠ ܩܛܸܠܐ ܗ̇ܘ ܕܛܠ̈ܝ̈ܐ ܬܫܬܼܓܫ ܇ ܐܝܟ ܗܽܘ ܕܐܬܥܼܘܠ ܒܗܘܢ. ܐܪ̈ܐܓܝܖ ܆ ܐܠܘ ܐܢܫ ܟܕ ܐܝܬܝܟ ܐܣܝܪ̈ܐ ܘܒܒܝܬܐ ܕܠܐ ܙܗܪܐ ܚܼܒܝܫ ܗܘܝܬ ܆ ܘܒܟܦܢܐ ܡܕܡ ܢܓܝܪ̈ܐ ܘܡܬܝܚܐ ܡܬܥܵܨܪ ܗܘܝܬ ܆ ܐܘ ܒܕܘܟܐܦ ܒܢ̈ܓܕܐ ܕܥܠܝ ܡܬܒܿܣܒܣ ܗܘܝܬ ܆ ܡܫܬܘܕܐ ܗܘܐ ܠܟ ܕܒܝܕ ܚܕܐ ܢܟܣܬܐ ܠܘܬ ܐܬܪܐ ܢܗܝܪ̈ܐ ܡܫ̇ܢܐ ܗܘܐ ܠܟ ܆

(with) a food not subject to corruption, but incorruptible and inexhaustible, to satiety, (in a place) rich in spiritual plants, in crowns blooming and developing at every hour, and never withering, would you not eagerly incline your neck under the sword, in order to go to this place, and would you not call benefactor, and not murderer, the one who had struck you?
ܬܐܙܠ ܗܘܝܬ ܆ ܘܡܥܕܪ̈ܢܐ ܘܠܘ ܩܛܘܠܐ ܩ̇ܪ̈ܐ ܗܘܝܬ ܆ ܠܗܽܘ ܕܡܥܝܟ. ܡܢܐ ܗܟܝܠ ܡܫܚܼܠܦ ܥܠܡܐ ܗܢܐ ܡܢ ܒܝܬܐ ܚܫܘܟܐ ܇ ܗ̇ܘ ܕܠܗ ܡܛܠ ܚܛܝܼܬܐ ܡܢ ܕܘܒܪ̈ܐ ܗ̇ܘ ܢܨܝܚܐ ܕܦܪ̈ܕܝܣܐ ܢܚܬ̣ܢܢ ܇ ܘܒܟܘ̈ܒܐ ܗܢܘ ܕܝܢ ܒܐܣܘܖܪ̈ܐ ܕܡܼܪ̈ܢܝܬܐ ܙܒܢ̈ܝܬܐ ܡܬܥܪܙܠܝܢܢ ܟܝܬ ܘܡܬܩ̇ܒܥܝܢ ܚܢܢ ܇ ܘܟܕ ܐܟ ܗ̇ܘ ܕܡܢ ܡܚܘܬܐ ܡܬܪ̈ܕܦܝܢ ܚܢܢ ܬܒܕܘܥܬܐ ܕܐܦ̈ܐ ܠܚܼܡܐ ܐܟܠܝܢܢ ܐܝܟܢܐ ܕܒܝܕ ܡܪܕܘܬܐ ܥܡܝܼܠܬܐ ܐܟ ܗܕܐ ܡܝܬܪ̈ܐ
4 Now, in what does this world differ from an obscure house, this world into which, after the radiant life of paradise, we have descended as a result of sin, where we are in truth entangled and embarrassed in thorns, namely in the bonds of temporal cares where, as if overwhelmed by blows, we eat our bread by the sweat of our brow in order to acquire virtue through such a painful discipline?
ܢܗܘܐ. ܡܢܐ ܕܝܢ ܐܣܬ̇ܓܦܘ ܛܠܝ̈ܐ ܆ ܕܟܕ ܗܪ̈ܘܕܝܣ ܝܗܼܒ ܬܒܥܬܐ ܕܡܛܠ ܡܣܝܒܘܬ ܩܛܠܐ ܗܺܝ ܕܝܠܗ ܘܝܿܗܒ ܝܬܝܪ ܩܫܝܐܝܬ ܇ ܗܢܘܢ ܒܝܕ ܚܕܐ ܡܚܘܬܐ ܡܢ ܚܝ̈ܐ ܕܕܘܘܢܝܐ ܐܓ݂ܗܝܘ. ܘܠܘܬ ܫܘܠܡܐ ܗ̇ܿܘ ܛܘܒܬܢܐ ܘܕܠܐ ܫܘܠܡ ܫܘܼܪܘ ܥܒܼܪ̈ܘ. ܐܠܘ ܡܢ ܗܟܝܠ ܥܕܡܐ ܠܗܠܝܢ ܕܩ̇ܪܺܝܒܢ ܩܝ̈ܡܢ ܗ̈ܘܝ ܗܠܝܢ ܕܝܠܢ ܆ ܙܒܢܐ ܗܘܐ ܠܢ ܕܐܝܟ ܦܘܠܘܣ ܢܐܡܪ ܆ ܕܐܢ ܒܗܠܝܢ ܚܝ̈ܐ ܒܠܚܘܕ ܡܣܿܒܪ̈ܝܢܢ ܒܡܫܝܚܐ ܆ ܝܬܝܪ̈ ܡܬܚܢ̈ܢܐ ܐܝܬܝܢ ܆ ܛܒ ܡܢ ܟܠܗܘܢ ܒܢܝ̈ܢܫܐ. ܐܢ ܕܝܢ ܡܫܝܚܐ ܩܡ ܡܢ ܒܝܬ ܡ̈ܝܬܐ ܆ ܪ̈ܝܼܫܝܬܐ ܕܗ̇ܿܢܘܢ ܕܫܼܟܒܘ ܆ ܘܦܘܪ̈ܥܢܝܐ ܕܡܝܬܪܘܬܐ ܘܡܣܡ ܒܪ̈ܝܫܐ ܕܒܝܼܫܘܬܐ ܇ ܘܗ̇ܝ ܕܐܝܟ ܕܫ̇ܘܝܐ ܠܟܠܢܫ. ܒܡܣ̈ܐܬܐ ܘܒܡܬܩ̈ܠܐ ܕܝܬܝܪ ܟܐܢܝܢ ܕܥܠܡܐ ܕܥܬܝܕ ܢܬܬܟܝܠܘܢ ܆ ܠܐ ܐܬܥܘܠܘ ܛܠܝ̈ܐ ܡܢ ܡܫܝܚܐ. ܐܠܐ ܒܕܠܩܘܼܒܠܐ ܐܦ ܐܬܥ̇ܕܪ̈ܘ ܇ ܟܕ ܠܩ̈ܛܠܐ ܡܶܢ ܕܗܪܘܕܝܣ ܐܫܬܠܡܘ ܇ ܠܟܠܝ̈ܠܐ ܕܝܢ ܕܣܗܕܘܬܐ ܘܼܛܪܘ. ܗܢܘܢ ܕܠܗܘܢ ܡܢ ܒܬܪ ܕܘ̈ܥܬܐ ܣܓܝ̈ܐܬܐ ܐܫܼܬܘܝܘ ܇ ܦܛܪ̈ܘܣ ܡܿܢ ܗܘ ܪܝܫܐ ܕܫܠܝ̈ܚܐ ܟܕ ܒܬܪ ܪܝܫܗ ܐܨܛܠܒ ܇ ܦܘܠܘܣ ܕܝܢ ܘܝܥܩܘܒ
5 In what, therefore, have they been harmed, these children who, while Herod, because of the infamous massacre (which he had ordered), underwent a punishment and underwent it more harshly, were they, in reality, at one stroke, delivered from a miserable life, and passed in a bound to a happy and eternal end? If therefore this condition of ours stopped at this present (world), it would be the moment for us to say like Paul: If it is for this life only that we hope in Christ, we are the most miserable of men; but if Christ is risen from the dead, he is the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep and the rewards of virtue, as the punishments of iniquity—according as it will be fitting for each—will be measured with the more equitable measures and weights of the future world; in that case, the children were not treated unjustly by Christ, on the contrary they were even favored, by being delivered to Herod's massacre and by weaving the crowns of martyrdom; (crowns) which were earned only after much sweat by Peter, the chief of the apostles, when he was crucified head down; Paul and James, when they were struck down by the sword; and Stephen, the first martyr, when he was stoned with stones.
ܟܕ ܒܣܝܦܐ ܐܬܪ̈ܡܼܝܘ ܇ ܣܛܦܐܢܘܣ ܕܝܢ ܗܘ ܩܕܡܝܐ ܕܣܗ̈ܕܐ ܟܕ ܒܟܐ̈ܦܐ ܐܬܪܲܓ݂ܡ. ܠܝ ܕܝܢ ܆ ܠܡܬܕܡܪܘ ܐܬܺܝܬ ܠܝ ܇ ܒܡܘ̈ܗܒܬܐ ܦܐܝ̈ܬ ܠܐܠܗܐ ܕܡܐܬܝܬܐ ܕܒܒܣܪܖ̈ ܕܡܫܝܚܐ ܇ ܕܡܝܥܢ̇ܘܐ ܐܦ ܒܝܕ ܢܟܣܬܐ ܕܫܒܪ̈ܐ ܗܠܝܢ ܇ ܕܠܓܢܣܐ ܐܢܫܝܐ ܗ̇ܘ ܕܢܼܚܬ ܠܘܬ ܩܘܡܬܐ ܠܐ ܡܫܡܠܝܬܐ ܘܫܒܪ̈ܝܬܐ ܇ ܡܥܿܠܐ ܡܿܣܩ ܠܘܬ ܡܫܡܠܝܘܬܐ ܗܺܝ ܕܡܢ ܫܘܪ̈ܝܐ.
6 For me, I happen to be in admiration before the gifts, so worthy of God, of the coming of Christ in the flesh, because he shows also, through the immolation of these children, that this human race, descended to an imperfect and childish age, he straightens and makes it rise back up to the perfection of the beginnings. fact, at the beginning, he formed Adam as a perfect being who was not brought to growth by successive ages; for immediately, he directly entrusted him with the exploitation of paradise, after having fixed for him as a wage the grace of immortality. But, because (Adam) descended to death and, by the deception (of the serpent), fell, was put out of paradise, and rejected perfection far from him, the way for us to come into existence through the commerce of marriage was then introduced. Then, from the fact that man is born a small child, he shows by this inferiority the lack of perfection he has in the beginnings; then, by successive growth and the progress of ages, he shows that we are destined for corruption.

7 However, the Emmanuel, the author and physician of our nature, on the one hand appropriated this imperfection which is ours, having become without change a small child because of his charity; on the other hand, to small children and to our race, he grants the perfection that existed at the beginning, or rather that which is his and which is entirely divine, for he summons them to the combats of martyrdom, valiant and worthy of men. That is why he also cries out in the Gospels, making it known that he came for this: Be therefore perfect, you, he says, as your heavenly Father is perfect.

9 Indeed, if (God) himself were ashamed to be called a small child, he would blush first—and quite rightly—to have become man without change because of us. But if, on the one hand, he takes upon himself, in view of the Economy, the imperfection of the small child, and on the other hand he makes small children participate in the perfection which is his and gives them the perfection of martyrs, when they testify for him, as God—for their blood cries out with a voice louder than the blood of Abel—how do you, because of this childish age, dishonor the one who, even at this age, does marvelously what is worthy of God, and (how) do you cut this Unique one into two natures, and say to him in blaspheming that he is a small child and that he is the Lord of the small child, when you grant to these two natures an adhesion of affinity and present one as Lord and the other as that over which this Lord has authority?

12 To this, what does the impious Eutyches answer us, he who drank the murky dregs of the impious Valentinus, of the Manichaeans, and of Apollinaris? For, if it is not a body consubstantial with us, with the exception of sin, possessing an intellectual soul, to which the Word of God himself united himself hypostatically, the departure for Egypt was indeed superfluous. For it would have been necessary that, as in the case of hallucinations, he be invisible to deceive the sight of those who saw (him) and escape the hands of Herod, if in truth he appeared to us in imagination and conferred upon himself a celestial or aerial body, and not the one which is consubstantial with us. But this impure and atheistic opinion was chased and rejected far away by the angel who stood by Joseph during his sleep, saying at one moment: Arise, take the child and his mother, and flee to Egypt; and at another moment: Arise, take the child, and go into the land of Israel, for those who sought the child's life are dead.
