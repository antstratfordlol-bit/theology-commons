The aforementioned truth becomes clear according to the principle of divine justice, which leaves nothing that happens in disorder unpunished, and which, according to the measure of the transgressions, as the sayings state, established the manner of the blows. Since, therefore, for every sin man draws upon himself a certain stated punishment, if he does not pay this in the present age, the order of divine judgment seeks that he may make satisfaction in the future; otherwise, he would remain unpunished. But if someone dies broken, he will not pay this penalty in Hades, according to 'The sinner, at whatever hour he groans, shall live a life and shall not die'—which must be understood concerning the death in Hades. But in heaven he will not pay such a punishment; for this is contrary to the purity of the heavenly dwelling; for through it, as Wisdom says, nothing defiled enters, nor does any unclean person pass through; which dwelling is one of rest, not of punishment, of joy, not of mourning. It remains, therefore, that outside of paradise and Hades another place be set apart, where this purification may happen: upon which occurring, each one, departing from being unclean and defiled, becomes pure and is immediately opened to the contemplation and enjoyment of God.
