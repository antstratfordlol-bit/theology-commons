# Legend of Abraham, Master of Barsoma

**Author**: Recueil de monographies  
**Series**: PO Tome 5  
**Columns**: pp. 767–774  

[p. 767] {#po.5.p767}
ܡܢ ܬܫܥܝܬܐ ܕܩܕܝܫܐ ܡܪܝ ܐܒܪܗܡ ܕܛܘܪܐ ܕܪܡ ܢܨܝܚܐ ܡܪܝ ܐܒܪܗܡ ܐܝܬܘܗܝ ܗܘܐ ܒܓܢܣܐ ܕܐܢܫ̈ܝܐ ܡܫܝܡܗ̈ܐ ܘܒܥܘܬܪܐ ܘܡܗܝܡܢܘܬܐ ܡܠܝܐܝܢ ܗܘܘ. ܘܐܝܬܝܗܘܢ ܗܘܘ ܡܢ ܡܕܝܢܬܐ ܕܩܘܣܛܢܛܝܢܘܦܘܠܝܣ ܘܠܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܠܗܘܢ ܒܪܐ ܠܒܪ ܡܢܗ. ܘܟܕ ܐܠܦܘܗܝ ܣܦܪܐ ܘܐܬܚܟܡ ܘܝܪܒ. ܡܟܪܘ ܠܗ ܐܢܬܬܐ. ܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܡܛܠ ܕܢܦܫܗ ܨܒܐ ܗܘܐ ܕܢܡܟܘܪ ܠܡܫܝܚܐ: ܟܕ ܗܘܐ ܠܗ ܦܠܥܐ: ܫܒܩ ܐܒܗܘ̈ܗܝ ܘܐܢܬܬܗ: ܘܝܬܒ ܒܐܠܦܐ ܘܡܛܐ ܠܣܘܪܝܐ. ܘܣܠܩ ܠܗ ܠܛܘܪܐ ܚܕ ܕܡܬܩܪܐ ܕܪܡ ܘܐܫܟܚ ܒܗ ܡܥܪܬܐ ܕܥܡܪ ܗܘܐ ܒܗ ܝܚܝܕܝܐ ܚܕ ܕܫܡܗ ܐܒܘܣ. ܘܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܥܡܗ ܝܚܝ̈ܕܝܐ ܥܣܪ̈ܝܢ ܘܥܠ ܠܘܬܗܘܢ ܛܘܒܢܐ. ܘܗܢܘܢ ܕܝܢ ܩܒܠܘܗܝ ܚܕܝܐܝܬ. ܪܝܫܕܝܪܐ ܕܝܢ ܟܕ ܚܙܐ ܠܥܠܝܡܐ ܕܡܛܟܣ ܘܡܟܝܟ: ܚܕܝ ܒܗ ܐܝܟ ܕܒܣܝܡܬܐ ܡܝܬܪܬܐ. ܘܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܛܘܒܢܐ
(EXTRACT) FROM THE HISTORY OF ABRAHAM OF THE HIGH MOUNTAIN 1. His birth and his vocation. - The illustrious Mar Abraham drew his origin from renowned parents, filled with riches and (full) of faith. They were * from the city of Constantinople and had no other child than him. When they had taught him to write, when he had become wise and had grown up, they made him marry a woman. But he, who only wanted to be betrothed to Christ, abandoned his parents and his wife as soon as he found the opportunity, boarded a ship and arrived in Syria. He went up a mountain which is called "High" and found there a cave inhabited by a solitary named Abas (Abous) and there were twenty solitaries with him. The blessed one entered near them and they received him with joy. When the head of the monastery saw that this young man was orderly and humble, he rejoiced in him as in an excellent treasure and the blessed Abraham was the admiration of all for his sublime conduct.
ܐܒܪܗܡ ܒܕܘܒܪ̈ܘܗܝ ܡܥܠ̈ܝܐ ܠܟܠܢܫ ܡܬܕܡܪ ܗܘܐ܀ ܒܬܪܟܢ ܚܣܡܘ ܒܗ ܐܚ̈ܐ ܘܐܬܟܢܫܘ ܠܘܬ ܪܝܫܕܝܪܐ ܘܐܡܪܝܢ. ܗܢܐ ܐܚܐ ܠܐ ܡܫܘܐ
2. Later, the brothers envied him; they went to the head of the monastery and said to him: "This brother does not associate with us; you must (choose) either him or us in the monastery."

ܢܦܫܗ ܥܡܢ. ܐܘ ܚܢܢ ܐܘ ܗܼܘ ܒܕܝܪܐ. ܟܕ ܚܙܐ ܪܝܫܕܝܪܐ ܛܢܢܐ ܕܐܚܝ̈ܐ ܩܪܝܗܝ ܠܛܘܒܢܐ ܘܐܡܪ ܠܗ: ܗܐ ܐܚܝ̈ܐ ܛܢܝܢ ܒܟ. ܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܟܕ ܫܡܥ ܣܡ ܩܕܡܝܗܘܢ ܡܝܛܢܘܝܐ ܘܢܦܩ ܡܢ ܕܝܪܐ. ܘܢܦܩ ܥܡܗ ܪܝܫܕܝܪܐ ܟܕ ܒܟܐ ܘܐܡܪ. ܙܠ ܒܫܠܡܐ ܒܪܐ ܕܬܪܒܝܬܐ. ܐܒܪܗܡ. ܘܥܗܕܝܢܝ ܒܨܠܘܬܟ. ܘܐܬܒܪܟܘ ܡܢ ܚܕ̈ܕܐ.
When the superior saw the jealousy of the brothers, he called the blessed one and said to him: "Behold, the brothers are jealous of you." And he, when he learned this, asked their forgiveness (metanoia) and left the monastery. The head of the monastery went out with him weeping and said to him: "Go in peace, O son of beautiful promises Abraham, and make mention of me in your prayers," and they gave each other their blessing.
ܘܟܕ ܐܪܚܩ ܛܘܒܢܐ ܩܠܝܠ ܡܢ ܕܝܪܐ. ܟܪܥ ܒܨܠܘܬܐ ܘܐܡܪ. ܐܠܗܐ ܕܒܪܝܢܝ ܘܬܪܘܨܝܢܝ ܠܐܝܟܐ ܕܡܢܚ ܨܒܝܢܟ. ܡܛܠ ܕܠܟ ܒܠܚܘܕܝ ܚܡܝܬ ܡܢ ܟܠ ܪܚܝܡܬ. ܘܠܒܟ ܫܒܝܠܐ ܚܕ ܘܡܛܐ ܠܪܝܫ ܛܘܪܐ. ܘܐܫܟܚ ܬܡܢ ܕܘܟܬܐ ܕܫܦܝܪܐ ܘܡܪܓܐ ܚܕ ܘܡܥܝܢܐ ܕܡܝ̈ܐ ܒܗ. ܘܟܕ ܗܘܐ ܪܡܫܐ. ܫܡܫ ܬܫܡܫܬܐ. ܘܠܩܛ ܠܗ ܥܩܪ̈ܐ ܘܐܣܬܝܒܪ ܘܕܡܟ. ܘܐܬܓܠܝ ܠܗ ܐܠܗܐ ܘܐܡܪ ܠܗ. ܐܒܪܗܡ ܕܘܟܬܐ ܗܕܐ ܠܟ ܛܝܒܬܗܿ. ܐܬܟܫܪ ܒܗܕܐ ܘܠܐܝ. ܕܬܐܣܒ ܟܠܝܠܐ ܕܠܐ ܡܬܚܒܠ. ܥܬܝܕ ܗܘ ܓܝܪ ܕܢܬܟܪܙ ܫܡܟ ܒܟܠܗ ܥܠܡܐ. ܘܐܣܥܘܪ ܒܐܝ̈ܕܝܟ ܐܬܘ̈ܬܐ ܘܬܕܡܪ̈ܬܐ. ܩܘܡ ܐܣܘܪ ܚܨܝܟ ܐܝܟ ܓܢ̈ܒܪܐ. ܘܒܢܝ ܗܪܟܐ ܕܝܪܐ. ܗܐ ܓܝܪ ܬܠܡܝ̈ܕܐ ܛܝܒܬ ܕܢܐܬܘܢ ܠܘܬܟ. ܐܬܚܝܠ
3. He founds a monastery. - When the blessed one had moved a little way from the monastery, he knelt down to pray and said: "O God, lead me and bring me where it pleases your will, for I have loved you alone, above all else." He took a path and arrived at the summit of the mountain. He found there a beautiful place with a meadow in which there was a spring of water. When evening came, he performed the office (he prayed), gathered * roots, satisfied himself, and slept. God appeared to him and said: "Abraham, I have prepared this place for you; progress here (in virtue) and work to receive the incorruptible crown, for it will come to pass that your name will be proclaimed by all the world and that I will perform signs and wonders through your hands. Arise, gird your loins like a strong man and build a monastery here, for I have decided for disciples to come to you; be courageous and strong and I will be with you."
ܘܐܬܥܕܪܢ. ܘܐܢܐ ܐܗܘܐ ܥܡܟ. ܘܒܬܪ ܬܪ̈ܝܢ ܝܘ̈ܡܝܢ. ܐܬܘ ܠܘܬܗ ܐܚ̈ܐ ܬܪ̈ܝܢ ܘܒܢܐ ܠܗ ܛܘܒܢܐ ܒܝܬ ܚܒܘܫܝܐ.
4. At the end of two days, two brothers came to him and the blessed one


[p. 770] {#po.5.p770}
ܘܢܙܪ ܢܦܫܗ ܡܢ ܠܚܡܐ ܘܡܢ ܟܠܡܕܡ. ܘܡܣܬܝܒܪ ܗܘܐ ܡܢ ܥܩܪ̈ܐ ܘܡܢ ܝܪ̈ܩܘܢܐ. ܘܨܐܡ ܗܘܐ ܡܢ ܚܕ ܒܫܒܐ ܠܚܕܒܫܒܐ ܘܠܒܝܫ ܗܘܐ ܟܘܬܝܢܐ ܕܣܥܪܐ ܘܥܪܩܬܐ ܕܦܪܙܠܐ ܥܠ ܚܨܘ̈ܗܝ ܠܓܘ ܡܢ ܟܘܬܝܢܗ. ܘܡܢ ܒܬܪ ܬܠܬ ܫܢ̈ܝܢ: ܢܚܬ ܡܢ ܛܘܪܐ ܗܼܘ ܘܣܛܦܐܢܘܣ ܬܠܡܝܕܗ ܘܐܙܠܘ ܠܘܬ ܐܦܝܣܩܘܦܐ. ܘܗܘܐ ܛܘܒܢܐ ܩܫܝܫܐ ܘܬܠܡܝܕܗ ܡܫܡܫܢܐ ܘܢܦܩ ܛܒܗ ܒܟܠ ܐܬܪ. ܘܐܬܝܢ ܗܘܘ ܠܘܬܗ ܟܪ̈ܝܗܐ ܘܐܠܝܨܐ ܡܢ ܟܠܕܘܟ ܘܡܐܣܐ ܗܘܐ ܠܗܘܢ: ܐܣܘ̈ܬܐ ܕܝܢ ܘܚܝ̈ܠܐ ܕܣܥܪ ܛܘܒܢܐ. ܥܒܪܝܢ ܠܡܫܘܚܬܐ. ܒܨܠܘܬܗ ܕܝܢ ܘܫܗܪܗ ܘܨܘܡܗ ܠܟܠܗܘܢ ܚܝܠܘܬܐ ܫܡܝܢ̈ܐ
built himself a place of seclusion; he deprived himself of bread and everything, and he nourished himself with roots and vegetables. He fasted from Sunday to Sunday, he wore a tunic of (camel) hair and an iron belt on his loins underneath the tunic. At the end of three years, he descended from the mountain with his disciple Stephen: they went to find the bishop, and the blessed one was made a priest and his disciple a deacon; his fame spread in every place. The sick and the afflicted came to him from everywhere and he healed them. The healings and wonders that the blessed one performed exceed (all) measure. By his prayers, his vigils, and his fasting, he rejoiced all the heavenly powers.
ܡܚܕܐ ܗܘܐ܀ ܓܒܪܐ ܕܝܢ ܚܕ ܡܢ ܐܬܪܐ ܪܚܝܩܐ ܕܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܠܗ ܕܡܝ̈ܐ ܫܢܝ̈ܐ ܬܪ̈ܬܝܢ ܟܕ ܐܬܐ ܠܘܬ ܩܕܝܫܐ ܨܠܝ ܥܠܘܗܝ. ܘܢܦܩ ܡܢ ܬܚܝܬ ܫܦܘܠܘ̈ܗܝ ܒܝܕܡܝܘܬܐ ܕܗܢܕܘܝܐ. ܕܛܥܝܢ ܥܠܘܐ ܕܡܝ̈ܐ. ܘܐܙܠ ܟܕ ܡܝܠܠ ܘܐܡܪ. ܘܝ ܠܝ ܡܢܟ ܐܒܪܗܡ ܛܘܪܝܐ ܗܐ ܟܡܐ ܠܝ ܒܟܪܣܗ ܕܥܠܝܡܐ ܗܢܐ ܘܠܐ ܐܢܫ ܐܫܟܚ ܢܦܩܢܝ. ܘܐܢܬ ܒܚܕܐ ܥܬܐ ܪܕܦܬܢܝ܀
5. His miracles. - A man from a distant land who had had urinary retention for two years (came to find him); when he arrived near the saint, he prayed over him and there came out from him below like a Hindu (negro) who was carrying a waterskin (full) of water and who came out complaining and saying: "Woe to me because of you, Abraham the mountaineer! It is a long time that I was in the belly * of this young man and no one could make me come out, and you have driven me out in an hour."
ܬܘܒ ܐܬܐ ܠܘܬܗ ܥܠܝܡܐ ܐܚܪܢܐ ܕܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܒܗ ܕܚܘ̈ܐ ܣܓܝ̈ܐܐ. ܘܒܝܐܣܘܪ̈ܐ
6. Another young man also came to him who had many demons; he was bound with ropes and chains, but he broke them all

ܘܒܫܫܠ̈ܬܐ ܡܬܐܣܪ ܗܘܐ. ܘܠܟܠܗܘܢ ܡܦܣܩ ܗܘܐ ܘܥܪܩ. ܘܩܡ ܛܘܒܢܐ ܒܚܪܡ ܒܨܠܘܬܐ ܡܟܒܟܝܐ ܣܓܝܐܐ. ܘܚܬܡܗ ܒܨܠܝܒܐ. ܘܓܥܪ ܒܗ ܒܪܗ ܕܝܘܐ. ܘܟܕ ܫܡܥ ܗܘ ܠܓܝܘܢ ܫܡܗ ܕܡܫܝܚܐ. ܢܦܩ ܡܢܗ ܘܐܬܠܡ܀

7. He receives Barsoma. - Christ appeared to the blessed one and said to him: "Abraham, Abraham, your conduct has pleased God. Arise and go to the city of Samosata, there is there an orphan young man named Barsoma; take him with you, for he is a chosen vessel for me." The blessed one made this divine grace known to the brothers and set out on his way. He arrived at Samosata and found near the bank of the river a (child) who was walking and weeping. He ran to him, took his hand, and said to him: "Greetings." And (the child) lifted his cap from his head, bowed, and was blessed by him. The blessed one said to the young Barsoma: "My son, where do you come from and where are you going?" He answered: "Lord, I am an orphan: my father is dead and my mother has (taken) another man; I want to go away to be the servant of Christ." He said to him: "Where do you plan to go?" (The child) answered him: "To the desert of Scetis." The blessed one said to him: "You are young and the desert is very harsh, you could not endure it; but if someone did you the favor of leading you to a monastery, would you flee?" He answered: "I would not flee." The blessed one took him

and he went * with him to his monastery and remained there six years. He left it then, led by divine grace; he traveled through the lands and performed great wonders and countless miracles.

8. As for the blessed Abraham, seventy brothers gathered near him. His disciple Stephen - the one who wrote (this) history and who was honored with the episcopal office - saw, by (the effect of) the divine Spirit, three eagles that were descending from the sky and gathering in the monastery of the blessed one, while another eagle came out of the monastery to meet them, and they (then) ascended to heaven. These three eagles were Simeon, Thomas, and John, perfect ascetics, who were sent by God for the death of the saint.
