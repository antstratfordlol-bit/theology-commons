The perfection of ten, for its part, is sung even by profane philosophers, who went so far as to consider it a god, as the tetrad of Pythagoras signifies, because it puts an end to units and is recognized as being the unit of decades. Added successively to itself, it is also the base of the numbers that follow it. But the law of Moses sings of it even more clearly: Noah appears tenth after Adam and constitutes a second root for his species, like the decade. For their fame has spread over absolutely the whole earth and the remarks that one makes about them have reached the ends of the world (cf. Ps 18:5). We will understand indeed that the Psalmist does not sing this about the apostles alone, but about all those who have sought to equal their immortal example. That is why we too were previously only the listeners of their grace, like all those who find themselves at a great distance from their sanctuary; but at present, we have become also the spectators of the truth of what we had heard: we had gone to find them because of an eye disease, as it was said previously, and after having gathered health, we see other men harvesting healings. But may the prophet grant his sacred lyre and say through his songs for the Jerusalem of old: "as we have heard, so we have seen" (Ps 47:9)! As for us, let us say to the martyrs: the things that we have learned about you through our ears are much smaller than those that we have seen with our eyes; we were moistened by droplets that the rumors, imitating the clouds and rising from the martyrial ocean of your healings, made rain.
