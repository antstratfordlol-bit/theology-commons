Another (hymn) in memory of the Greek doctors, composed by the priest Saliba (Slîbâ), son of the priest David, son of the priest Mégbél, from the blessed village of Mansouryah, which is in the land of Gezirtâ; he composed it according to the history of Mar Nestorius.

(σχολαστικοί), what the heretics did against the pure one and the chief of the just, and the illustrious among all * the valiant. See (these people) worthy of all torment, how they persecuted the just one and introduced fraud and schism into the salvific economy. See the rebellious priests, who gave satisfactions to the Satans and offered sacrifices to the demons in the hope of defeating the chief of the illustrious men. They had besieged him with all ruses, they had hidden all the traps from him, they had dug all the underground passages under him and they had opened all the pits. As for him, - like a tower solidly founded and like a wall which supports the constructions, whose houses (δόμος) are placed by the artist on the foundation of the son of the dove, - he was not moved * by their tyrannies (τύραννος), was not frightened by their assaults, did not fear their threats, and did not associate himself with their blasphemies. It is our glorious father, the chief of all the sublime ones, who made of his soul a temple to the Lord, Mar Nestorius the Greek.

This chosen one was from the land of Germanicia, from the city which is called Mar'as in the Syriac language and he was raised in the schools (σχολάς), before this contemplative (θεωρητικός) doctor Theodore, the interpreter of the Testament (διαθήκη). We must report in a few words the wonders that were done by that one, Theodore the interpreter, master of the just Nestorius. This remarkable man was from the very land of Syria, from near the city of Antioch, son of illustrious princes. His parents were known men, they were pagans and even bad, and they led him, in their ignorance, to the school of impure and fetid things. For fifteen years, after he had become a young man, he was instructed in the pagan science of books filled with futilities. He studied them in fullness, instructed himself therein as much as possible, meditated on them sufficiently and learned eloquence (ῥήτωρ) there. The thought then came to him and the desire was born in his heart
