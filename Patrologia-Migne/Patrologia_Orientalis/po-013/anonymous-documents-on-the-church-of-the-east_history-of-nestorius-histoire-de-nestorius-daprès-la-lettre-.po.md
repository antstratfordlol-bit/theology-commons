# History of Nestorius (Histoire de Nestorius d'après la lettre à Cosme)

him; to say it in a word: (we have seen) that his memory was disappearing as he himself has disappeared: therefore we, whose names are written above, because you conjured us much in your second letter to learn the mode of his deposition, and because Eutyches, the bearer of your letter, also begged us on this subject, we have judged it good to give an answer to your letter by a common opinion, to make known to your piety some reproaches that were addressed to Nestorius.

Some, of the party of Arius, thought to build by force an oratory in the city, thanks to the support of the emperor; Nestorius burned it, and there was trouble and bloodshed in the city without

ܘܬܘܒ ܩܠܝܪ̈ܝܩܘ ܕܡܢ ܐܠܟܣܢܕܪܝܐ ܐܬܘ ܠܘܬ ܡܠܟܐ ܟܕ ܡܬܒܥܝܢ ܥܠ ܐܦܝܣܩܘܦܗܘܢ. ܕܐܬܬܚܡܘ ܡܢܗ ܠܐ ܘܠܝܐܝܬ. ܘܥܕܪ ܐܢܘܢ ܢܣܛܘܪܝܣ ܒܕܝܢܐ ܕܩܕܡ ܡܠܟܐ. ܘܗܕܐ ܒܩܝܢܕܝܢܘܣ ܪܒܐ ܐܩܝܡܬܗ ܗܘܬ܀ ܘܬܘܒ ܡܠܟܬܐ ܦܘܠܘܟܪܝܐ ܕܝܪܝܬܐ. ܗܝ ܘܒܢ̈ܬ ܕܝܪܗ̇ ܒܚܕܒܫܒܐ. ܒܝܬܐ ܐܦܝܣܩܘܦܝܘܢ ܡܫܬܪ̈ܚܢ ܙܘ̈ܥܝ ܒܬܪ ܡܣܒܐ ܕܩܘܪܒܢܐ: ܢܣܛܘܪܝܣ ܕܝܢ ܠܐ ܩܒܠ. ܘܡܛܠܗܕܐ ܪܘܒܐ ܪܒܐ ܐܬܬܥܝܪ ܥܠܘܗܝ ܡܢ ܩܠܝܪ̈ܝܩܐ ܘܡܢ ܟܠܗ ܒܝܬܐ ܡܠܟܝܐ܀ ܘܬܘܒ ܨܠܡܗ̇ ܕܩܘܪܐ ܦܘܠܘܟܪܝܐ ܐܨܝܪ ܗ̱ܘܐ ܠܥܠ ܡܢ ܡܕܒܚܐ. ܠܚܝܗܝ ܢܣܛܘܪܝܣ. ܘܗܕܐ ܒܨܝܪ ܩܠܝܠ ܐܦܩܬܗ ܗ̱ܘܬܐ ܡܢ ܥܕܬܐ܀ ܘܬܘܒ ܐܣܛܘܠܗ̇ ܕܦܘܠܘܟܪܝܐ. ܕܒܙܒܢ ܡܬܦܪܣܐ ܗ̱ܘܬ ܥܠ ܡܕܒܚܐ ܒܙܒܢܐ ܕܩܘܪܒܢܐ: ܘܒܙܒܢ ܠܒܫܐ ܗ̱ܘܬ ܠܗ̇.
4. Furthermore, clerics from Alexandria came to the emperor and complained of having been censured illegally by their bishop; Nestorius helped them in the judgment before the emperor, and this had put him in great peril. 5. Furthermore, the Empress Pulcheria, a nun, and her nuns used to dine on Sunday at the episcopal palace after having received communion; Nestorius did not admit it, and a great outcry resulted against him from the clerics and the whole court. 6. Furthermore, Nestorius had the image of Pulcheria, which was painted above the altar, erased; this almost got him driven from the church. 7. Furthermore, Nestorius had the stole (στολή) of Pulcheria removed, which was sometimes spread on the altar at the moment of communion and sometimes worn by her.
ܠܗܕܐ ܐܥܒܪܗܿ ܢܣܛܘܪܝܣ܀ ܘܬܘܒ ܒܥܐܕܐ ܪܒܐ ܕܦܨܚܐ: ܥܝܕܐ ܐܝܬ ܗܘܐ ܠܡܠܟܐ. ܕܒܩܕܘܫ ܩܘܕܫܝ̈ܢ ܢܣܝܒ ܩܘܪܒܢܐ. ܘܐܬܪܓܪܓܬ ܦܘܠܘܟܪܝܐ ܘܐܦ ܣܬܡܬ ܠܣܝܣܝܢܘܣ ܐܦܝܣܩܘܦܐ. ܘܥܡ ܡܠܟܐ ܦܩܕܐ ܗ̱ܘܬ ܩܘܪܒܢܐ ܒܩܕܘܫ ܩܘܕܫܝ̈ܢ. ܢܣܛܘܪܝܣ ܕܝܢ ܠܗܕܐ ܠܐ ܩܒܠ. ܐܠܐ ܒܚܕ ܡܢ ܝܘ̈ܡܝܢ ܟܕ ܥܐܠܐ ܗ̱ܘܬ ܐܝܟ ܥܝܕܗ̇ ܠܩܕܘܫ ܩܘܕܫܝ̈ܢ. ܚܙܗ̇ ܢܣܛܘܪܝܣ ܘܟܐܠ ܕܡܢܘ ܗܢܐ. ܘܓܠܝܗܝ ܗ̱ܘܐ ܠܗ ܠܣܘܥܪܢܐ ܦܛܪܘܣ ܐܪܟܕܝܩܘܢ. ܘܪܗܛ ܗ̱ܘܐ ܢܣܛܘܪܝܣ. ܘܐܪܥܗ̇ ܒܬܪܥܐ ܕܒܝܬ ܩܕܘܫ ܩܘܕܫܝ̈ܢ ܘܟܠܝܗ̇. ܘܠܐ ܫܒܩܗ̇ ܕܬܥܘܠ. ܘܐܬܬܦܝܪܬ ܠܩܘܒܠܗ ܡܠܟܬܐ ܩܘܪܐ ܦܘܠܘܟܪܝܐ ܘܐܡܪܬ ܠܗ. ܐܪܦܢܝ ܐܥܘܠ ܐܝܟ ܥܝܕܝ. ܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܐܡܪ ܠܗܿ. ܠܐ ܡܦܣ ܐܬܪܐ ܗܢܐ ܠܢܫܬܐ ܐܠܐ ܐܢ ܠܟܗ̈ܢܐ. ܗܝ ܕܝܢ ܐܡܪܬ ܠܗ. ܠܡܢ ܝܠܕܬ ܠܘ ܠܐܠܗܐ ܝܠܕܬܝ. ܗܘ ܕܝܢ ܐܡܪ ܠܗܿ. ܐܢܬܝ ܠܣܛܢܐ ܝܠܕܬܝ. ܘܕܚܩܗ̇ ܡܢ ܬܪܥܐ ܕܒܝܬ ܩܕܘܫ ܩܘܕܫܝ̈ܢ. ܘܐܙܠܬ ܟܕ ܡܪܝܪܐ ܠܗܿ. ܘܥܠܬ ܠܩܕܡ ܡܠܟܐ. ܘܐܡܪܬܗ ܠܣܘܥܪܢܐ. ܘܡܠܟܐ ܐܡܪ ܠܗܿ. ܠܐ ܚܝܝ̈ܟܝ ܚܬܝ. ܘܠܐ ܟܠܝܠܐ ܕܒܪܝܫܟܝ. ܕܠܐ ܦܠܐ ܐܢܐ ܘܡܬܢܩܡ
8. Furthermore, on the great feast of Easter, the emperor was accustomed to receive communion in the Holy of Holies; Pulcheria desired (the same privilege), she convinced the bishop Sisinnius, and she received communion with the emperor in the Holy of Holies. Nestorius did not admit this, but one day when she was heading according to her custom toward the Holy of Holies, Nestorius saw her and asked what this meant; the archdeacon Peter explained the matter to him; Nestorius ran, met her at the door of the Holy of Holies, stopped her, and did not permit her to enter. The Queen Pulcheria was irritated against him and said to him: "Let me enter according to my custom." But he said to her: "This place must be trodden only by priests." She said to him: "Why * did I not give birth to God!" He said to her: "You, you gave birth to Satan," and he drove her from the door of the Holy of Holies. She left irritated, went to find the emperor, and told him the thing. The emperor said to her: "By your life, my sister, and by the crown that is on your head, I will not cease until I have taken vengeance on him." From that day he had no more credit with the emperor.

9. From then on all calamities fell upon this man, his soul weakened and his body (prosôpon) languished: He was young, red-haired, with large eyes and a beautiful face, one would have said a second David: the timbre of his voice was also pleasant. He said various things at the moment of communion, and many who came to the church did so only to hear the sound of his voice. They took him and placed him next to the corpse, and one of the chiefs said to him: "O cursed soul! is it you who did this?" And he swore (saying): "I do not know what you are saying." Then they pulled the corpse from the reeds under which it was hidden, and this chief said to him: "Do you believe now that your eyes see, or do you still persist in your impudence?" When John saw what it was about, he said to this chief: "I am therefore responsible, sir, for the blood of the dead man, but, because the weather is bad and the heat is burning, let us enter the monastery until the temperature of noon cools down. Behold I am in your hands, do to me what will seem good in your eyes." They believed him because they were also suffering, and they entered the monastery carrying the dead man. John asked them to put the corpse on the tomb of Nestorius, and they placed it where he said, then they sat down to eat. While they were eating, Our Lord, to whom everything is easy, visited his servant, resurrected the dead man, and delivered the oppressed (from the hand) of his oppressors. I was not an eyewitness of it, for I heard it said. But I was an eyewitness of the rest.

We did not want these things to be known, because there was no one who thought of this man; but now that you inquire about these things, behold we have announced to your holiness a part of what happened. Although speech is powerless to recount things as they are, however, because of your precious prayer and Eutyches, bearer of your letter, we have written to your holiness a part of what we have seen and heard; first to answer the insistence of your request, and then because such things are precious to us also, when we record them in our letter * by the rule of friendship. Be and remain in good health, as well as all your house, in Our Lord. Amen. End of the letter.
