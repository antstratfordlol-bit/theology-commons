This spiritual athlete (ἀθλητής) was also from the land of Ma'alta; he was a relative of Mar Narsai, he was also of his family and even of his village. His father was named Bar-Sahde. At the age of fifteen, he was moved, by divine operation, to abandon all the delights of this world and to occupy himself with spiritual work. When he learned where Mar Narsai was and what he was doing, he asked his father to lead him to him. When his father had agreed, had led him to Nisibis, and Mar Narsai had learned that it was his relative, he (Narsai) asked his name. His father replied: "He is called Narsai like you." Immediately he changed his name and called him Abraham, and he said: "There will not be two Narsais in one cell"; but, as it seems to me, this took place by prophecy: just as Abraham, by the change of his name, had become "the father of many nations," this one, likewise, was charged to be so for all of Persia, by spiritual birth.

After he had been there a little time and had attempted to advance from day to day in the teaching of piety and the ardor of his charity, he showed great zeal for science. His master also showed greater care for him and, in a short time, he surpassed, by his instruction, most of those of his age. He also had such conduct that the whole community called him, because of his humility, a second Moses. Because the Lord knows those who are his, he therefore chose him in advance and loved him, for the reading and meditation of the Books, more than the plant (the castor-oil plant) of Jonah.

That he gives the name of Spirit to the grace of the Spirit because it belongs to the Spirit, it is evident from what the blessed Paul said: Do not quench the Spirit, do not despise prophecies. It is not of the substance of the Spirit that he was saying: Do not quench, for no man nor even all the creation together can quench it, since it is * without end. John the evangelist also said: The Holy Spirit was not yet, because Jesus had not been glorified until then; but it is not of the substance of the Holy Spirit that he would ever have said: He was not then. 27.
