LXVII. - Hours of prayer. Upon rising in the morning, pray; pray at the third hour; pray at the sixth hour; pray at the ninth hour; pray at the fall of night and at the cock's crow: in the morning, because God enlightens us, makes the night disappear, and gives us the day; at the third hour, because it is the hour when Pilate condemned the Lord; at the sixth hour, because it is the hour when the Lord was crucified; * at the ninth hour, [because it is the hour when] He gave up the spirit, when all nature was troubled, when from His pierced side came blood and water, when, in the presence of the crucified Lord, all creation was seized with fear and trembling because of the crime of the impious Jews, and could not bear the spectacle of its Master mocked. Night come, give thanks, because He grants it to you to rest from the fatigues of the day; and at the cock's crow, because you have received the announcement of the coming of the day, during which you will be able to attend to your occupations and do the works of light.

It is not necessary, as we have said, for the bishop to sit to collect taxes, but he must give himself entirely to the affairs of his Church; otherwise, he must abdicate the episcopate, for the Lord has decreed that no one can * serve two masters.
