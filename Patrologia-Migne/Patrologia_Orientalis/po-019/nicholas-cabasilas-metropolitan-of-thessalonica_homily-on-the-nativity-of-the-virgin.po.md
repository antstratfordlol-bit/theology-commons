OF OUR MOST HOLY LADY THE MOTHER OF GOD. 1. First indeed let us invoke God here, not that we may attain a speech equal to the things and sufficient for the purpose—for this altogether surpasses human hope—but from this, that from those things which are at hand, praise may be accomplished in some way, and we may not appear much inferior to others because of the multitude of those who have preached; but from that—which is better—that we may take utility from the study, and gain some sanctification of words, as if performing a certain sacred act. Therefore, with a suppliant voice I will ask for those things which I know to prefer the Praised one to all, and what, before other things, she desires for her praisers, namely, the utility of the soul; for both from her benefits toward us, and from the thanksgivings which she deigns to receive from us for the benefits, she herself seeks this very thing in all.
