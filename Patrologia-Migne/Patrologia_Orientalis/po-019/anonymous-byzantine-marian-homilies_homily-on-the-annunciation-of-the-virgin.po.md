And God indeed did not warn Adam nor persuade him about his side, from which Eve was to be built, but He took away his sense, and thus snatched away the member; but He first instructed the Virgin, and awaited her faith, before He proceeded to the work. And about the creation of Adam He converses with the Only-Begotten: Let us make man, He says. But when it was necessary to introduce this admirable counselor, namely the Firstborn, into the earth as Paul says, and to form the second Adam, He takes the Virgin as a participant in this purpose. And this great counsel, as Isaiah says, God indeed decreed, but the Virgin confirmed. And the Incarnation of the Word was not only the work of the Father, and of His Power, and of the Spirit—the former approving, the latter arriving, and the other overshadowing—but also the work of the will and faith of the Virgin. And she did not pass over the address without examination, nor did she suffer any levity, nor, because of the excess of the proclamations, was she poured out in joy, but she attentively considered the salutation.
