Matter by itself is indivisible; for as it is without quality, so it is also without quantity; but that which lacks quantity is indivisible; but that which cannot be divided cannot be distinguished; but that which by its own nature is not divided nor distinguished, how could it provide a cause of distinction to another? But that which does not admit diversity (for any matter is indifferent to any matter by its own reason), how will it be the cause that other things are diverse from each other? Furthermore, if a body consisting of matter is divided into parts of quantity according to the matter itself, of which each one is taken and placed separately to show that this is the same substance, but not that other one, that is called material distinction. From that, however, it is by no means brought about that matter is the cause of distinction (for how, since it is indistinct by itself?), but material division arises, from which quantity itself comes to those things which are distinct.
