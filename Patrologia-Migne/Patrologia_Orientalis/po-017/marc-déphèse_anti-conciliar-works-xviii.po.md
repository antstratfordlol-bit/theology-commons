And rightly so. For any king is called a king before he is crowned king, especially when he is already approaching to receive the crown, increased with attendants and honors; a king is also called the image of the king, and likewise is honored and adored. By the same reasoning, the divine gifts, before they are transformed, are said by us to refer to the type and image of the Lord's body and blood. Whence also the great Basil in the holy mass calls them the antitypes themselves. 'Offering,' he says, 'the antitypes of the precious body and blood of your Christ.' What foolish things, therefore, do we do, while we bring forward and receive with honor those things which, having already been offered and dedicated to God, have become both a sacrifice and a victim, and are offered to Him to be consecrated through the coming of the Holy Spirit? But those who strain at a gnat but swallow a camel will perhaps accuse us regarding the holy images, that we adore them, since they are not the archetypes themselves, but examples of them. That would be truly worthy of their folly.
