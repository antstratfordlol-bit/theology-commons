### XIX
MARK OF EPHESUS' LETTER TO THE PATRIARCH OF CONSTANTINOPLE.

### MARK TO THE OECUMENICAL [PATRIARCH]
ΜΑΡΚΟΥ͂ ΠΡῸΣ ΤῸΝ ΟἸΚΟΥΜΕΝΙΚΟΝ. Παναγιώτατέ μου δέσποτα καὶ οἰκουμενιχὲ πατριάρχα, πέποιθα εἰς Θεὸν τοῦ ὑγιαίνειν τὴν μεγάλην ἁγιωσύνην σου κατὰ τὴν αἰσθητὴν καὶ ὁρωμένην ὑγίειαν εὐφραινομένην ἐν Κυρίῳ πάντοτε᾽ καὶ αὐτὸς ἐλέει Θεοῦ μιχρὸν ὑγιαίνω τῷ σώματι.
Most holy lord and oecumenical patriarch, I trust in God that your exceptional holiness is well and always enjoys bodily and visible health in the Lord. As for me, by the mercy of God, I am moderately well in body.

We have heard by report what has been done there, and it has not grieved us lightly; nevertheless, we give thanks to the merciful God and bestow the greatest praises, because He has given you courage in the present crisis, and we ask Him again and again that you may become stronger day by day in the face of dangers and remain unshaken, so that as many of us as emulate the orthodox faith may have you as a harbor, a refuge, and a fortress. And if indeed the evils are warded off, everyone will consider you blessed and worthy of praise; but if God permits, according to those judgments which He alone knows, for the evils to worsen, you will become stronger and more experienced through the endurance of sufferings, just as iron heated and plunged into water is daily incited and sharpened by dangers. For nothing so overcomes the tormentor as the alacrity of the one who suffers. Let us not, I pray, be more cowardly than those seven youths, I mean the Maccabees, who, having endured every kind of torture for the sake of mere swine's flesh, carried off the crown of the contest; but let us also say with them, 'What then? Even if we do not die at this time, shall we not die at all? Shall we not fulfill the duty owed to our birth?'

What is necessary, let us do gloriously; let us learn to die; let us make what is common our own; let us buy life with death. Let none of us be desirous of this life, none cowardly and timid. Let the tyrant despair of the rest, and let him be the first to show the way to others, and the last to be the seal of the contest.' For had there been no persecution, neither would the martyrs have shone, nor would the confessors have obtained the crown of victory from Christ, by confirming and gladdening the Catholic and Orthodox Church with their rewards. For there must also be heresies, according to the divine Apostle, so that those who are approved may become manifest. And if we understand this and struggle in this way, we shall surely obtain the same rewards ourselves, and we shall become heirs of the same glory, and we shall enter into the joy of the Lord, fearing nothing, dreading nothing, neither foreign enemies nor those who move among us, the pseudo-Christs and enemies of the Spirit. And would that until our last breath we might profess with the utmost confidence the excellent deposit of the holy Fathers, that faith which we sucked with milk from childhood, which we first pronounced, and with which I would wish to depart at the last, carrying away from here this, if nothing else: the right faith.
