81 Homily of consolation and joy pronounced by Anba Timothy (Timatäwuüs) patriarch of Alexandria, son of the [father] patriarch Dioscorus (Disqurus) who struggled in the pains of the apostles against the impure Chalcedonians, whose prayer brought death upon Marcian (Margïän) before his time, left Pulcheria (BulhärTya) without fruit and cast Juvenal (Yünälïyüas) into the depths of hell, threw Justin (?) (Wüstä) the Chalcedonian into the abysses, caused the death of Leo (Läwün) [the impostor], Theodoret (Täüdüratus) and Mäylhäwüs (?), they through whom he had endured the fatigue of exile, I mean Saint Dioscorus, then he concelebrated with the angels in the abode of the living. Already his soul had rested upon Timothy, who had become for him a very dear son in his old age and obtained the see of Alexandria after the death of his father Dioscorus in exile. He composed this homily in the land of Egypt at the time when he was invited to consecrate the church of Pbow (Qäàw) to dedicate it in the name of the holy Anba Pachomius (Bahüm), the father of holy cenobitism. He pronounced this homily in the presence of many bishops who accompanied him and said some words of praise and veneration for the pure Virgin My Lady Mary, because of the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ into the land of Egypt. This happened at the arrival of Anba Timothy on this rock, the one that bears the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, a mark of his bodily coming into [the land of] Egypt. [His pride, his dignity and his glory, he raised them to the highest]

87 When the only-begotten Son of the Father saw the mountains overturned and the rocks shaken, and the apostles who, out of fear, had fallen on their faces, he stretched out his hand toward the mountains, saying: "Stand still." They ceased their trembling and their dizziness. [He] placed his hand on the stones and the rocks; they calmed down and stopped trembling.
